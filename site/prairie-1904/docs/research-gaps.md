# Remaining work, ranked by reconstruction value

1. Resolve the 91 tentative map frontages, three 1904 corrections and directory entries into versioned parcel/building entities. Check ambiguous labels, 1700/1702 shared structure and 1916/1930 alias; enumerate unnamed rear stables separately. This is the missing denominator behind any claim of “every building.”
2. Fully transcribe the six acquired Glessner HABS drawings and report; acquire the remaining captions, 17-photo series and field notes FN-128. Reconcile them with Harvard's Richardson originals, the 1888 Inland Architect plate, family photographs and the 1892 changes. Keep exact dimensions tied to sheet and phase.
3. Visually verify the 1904 Blue Book pages, then use the full Lakeside directory and the 1900/1910 census schedules to identify owners, tenants, boarders and domestic workers. No census households have yet been transcribed. Elite social lists omit much of the neighborhood population.
4. Read address-indexed UIC permit reels and American Contractor notices. Distinguish permit fees from construction costs; acquire alteration/demolition permits, not only initial construction.
5. Acquire dated street/yard photographs and civic plans for curb heights, sidewalks, paving, trees, railings, gas/electric lighting, drainage, hydrants, wire routes and Illinois Central shore works. A citywide paving report does not identify a particular block's surface.
6. Resolve recorded chronology conflicts: Shortall/Gregory, Fernando Jones, Rees, Field Jr. phases, Robbins 1904/1905 and uncertain losses. Retain both readings until primary documentation decides them.
7. Georeference the original sheets and publish a control-point residual report before tracing measured footprints. Then digitize roofs, service wings, stables and fences with evidence per attribute.

This research package supports the existing Prairie scene tickets; it does not close their acceptance criteria. Source leads and acquisition actions are in the dossiers, rather than a false “complete” status.
