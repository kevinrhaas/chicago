A plank walk over the slough mouth, and along the river into town.

**The owner, 2026-08-20, at the State Street slough where it meets the river:** *"the
pedestrian plank sidewalk bridge crossing it close to the river should exist and run along
the river towards the town."*

Two things, and only the first exists today:

- **The crossing.** The **Slough Log Bridge** is a committed structure — its deck sits at
  E +805…+813, N +14, and T-0005 carved the channel under it so that it finally spans
  water instead of prairie (bed −1.8 ft, ~1.3 m of clearance). What the owner is asking
  for is that the thing a person actually walks — a **plank footway**, not a log deck read
  from a distance — is there and reads as a sidewalk where it crosses.
- **The walk along the bank, which does not exist at all.** From that crossing the planks
  should carry on **along the river towards town**, as a riverside footway. Nothing on the
  frontage layer runs there now.

This is the same layer and the same argument as **T-0069** (fences line the streets, and
plank sidewalks run beside them) and the walks already built at the Green Tree (T-0082)
and the Sauganash (T-0090) — reuse that machinery rather than inventing a second kind of
plank. Reconstructed tier: the owner's 2026-08-18 standing ruling covers it (*"you are
totally fine to be liberal with adding reconstructed items when i ask for things, you can
just label and mark them as such"*), with a `docs/LIBERTIES.md` entry stating what bounded
the run — the bank line, the bridge's committed deck ends, and where the frontage the walk
serves begins.

**It must be walkable.** T-0045's deck-walking machinery is what makes a plank surface
something a visitor steps onto rather than through; the walk and the crossing both ride on
it, and the walker keeps standing on the heightfield everywhere else.

**Wait for T-0118 if both are in flight** — that ticket moves the slough's last reach and
may move the mouth, and a walk pinned to the old bank line would have to be redrawn.
Whichever runs second reads the other's result first.

**Acceptance:** from the owner's stand, a plank footway crosses the slough at the bridge
and continues along the river bank towards town, walkable end to end, carded reconstructed
with its bounds; the crossing reads as planks underfoot rather than a distant deck. Gates
green.
