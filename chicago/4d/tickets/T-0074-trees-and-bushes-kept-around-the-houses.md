Trees and bushes kept around the houses.

**The owner, 2026-08-18, of the circa-1833 view looking east, verbatim: "see the fences,
lining the streets, and property that should be what it looks like, with more trees and
greenery around the houses like people would have put or kept some trees and bushes
around their houses."**

The reference (`data/sources/assets/owner_brief_2026_08_18/README.md`, image 12): trees
and bushes stand **close around the houses** — kept deliberately, shading the dooryard —
and the fenced ground carries garden green. The committed Kinzie plate agrees (its row
of Lombardy poplars was planted, not wild). Today the flora layer is prairie-driven:
distribution follows the land, not the households, so houses stand on bare open grass.

Add a dooryard planting pass: near dwellings, inside or at the fence line, place kept
trees and shrubs — settlers both **kept** standing native trees when they built (oaks,
cottonwoods already in the species list) and **planted** quick fashionable ones
(Lombardy poplars, attested by the Kinzie plate; fruit trees; lilac and currant bushes
at the door are period-universal). Bound species to the committed flora lists plus the
plate-attested poplars; density and placement reconstructed, one LIBERTIES entry.
Respect T-0067's ground treatments — a garden gets its bushes, a working yard stays
trodden.

**Acceptance:** houses across the town show close-set dooryard trees and bushes distinct
from the prairie scatter — visibly, from the walker, on ordinary house lots and not just
one showcase — carded reconstructed. Gates green.
