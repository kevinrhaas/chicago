Fourth group of T-1184's tool, driven by T-1164 as well as the order book.
The eight taverns are attested and stay eight (the census says eight; the tool refuses a ninth).
What is missing is the boarding-house economy that housed 8.2 people per dwelling — the programme
carries 42 boarding-house roofs (H1–H3) and 4 stand; every one needs a KEEPER and a household —
and the river trades: four forwarding houses are attested, the warehouses (F1–F4, 20 roofs) mostly
anonymous, the drays, the ferry at the forks (attested), a livery (3 in the register), the lumber
yards the boom implies, the harbour works of 1834–35 (the piers, the cut through the bar) with a
contractor and a gang, and the vessels in port on 1 July (schooners and the steamboat traffic
the *American* reports — a bounded, dated bracket, crews reconstructed in T-1175).

**This group's quota:** boarding houses to the lodging model's bed count (keepers often widows or
couples — the sex rule stated); forwarding/warehouse firms only where the occupation model shows
a gap beyond the attested four; drays/teamsters as no-premises employments; a lumber yard per the
saw-mill and the building rate; the pier-works contractor as an `employment` business with a
gang; vessels as `vessel` locations (not structures) with masters and crews.

**Naming:** boarding houses by keeper (`Mrs. Hale's Boarding House`), warehouses by firm
(`Jones & Clark, Forwarding and Commission`), vessels by period schooner names drawn from the
lake lists the press prints (attested names first; invented ones flagged).

**Acceptance:**

- Records build to quota; `--check`; LIBERTIES scope; the lodging model's beds vs keepers table
  prints with zero unkept boarding house.
- Every standing H-family and F-family roof (`recon_1835_*_h*`, `*_f*`) has, or is scheduled in
  T-1197 to have, a business behind it.
- Visible: the Businesses view; the building cards of adopted boarding houses name their keeper.

**Stop condition:** the lodging/river/transport buckets read filled and the bed count is kept.
