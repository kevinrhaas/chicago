Is the Democrat's 'A. Sweet' of 4 June 1834 Alanson Sweet or the Alon[s]on Sweet of the same column.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

**Reached by T-1155, 2026-09-17.** Before the square-bracket supplies were kept, the
corpus's second Sweet — printed `Alon[s]on Sweet` — split to the forenames `alon` and
`on`, which no initial fits, and the Democrat's `A. Sweet` of 4 June 1834 folded onto
Alanson Sweet unopposed. With the supplied letter back that printing reads as the full
forename Alonson, the identity ladder sees TWO full forenames the initial A fits, and R3
refuses the reading to both rather than choosing. `A. Sweet` therefore comes off Alanson's
card and stands on a container of its own, `hh_sweet_a`.

The cluster `sweet` is filed `undecided` under rule U1 in
`data/residents/card_merge_rulings.json` and referred here. It is not merged, because
merging it would assert a choice between two printings that nobody has made.

**The question is a reading of the page, not a rule.** Is `Alon[s]on` a second man, or is
it the Democrat's compositor transposing Alanson? Settle it on the printings themselves —
the 4 June 1834 column and every other Sweet the newspapers, the 1833 tax roll and the
1834/1835 poll lists carry — not on the initial.

**Acceptance:** rule the cluster `merged` or `separate` in `card_merge_rulings.json` with
the page evidence quoted; if merged, `hh_sweet_a` folds into `hh_sweet_alanson` and L220's
scope count goes back down by one; if separate, say what distinguishes the two men and
leave both cards standing. Either way, restate L220's count and record the reading.
