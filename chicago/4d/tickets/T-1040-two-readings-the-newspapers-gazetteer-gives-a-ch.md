Two readings the newspapers gazetteer gives a Chicago person belong to somebody else: 'Col. Samuel Miller, Michigan City' is Carver's Indiana agent and sits on a Chicago resident card, and person_w_montgomery carries Loton W.'s shoemaking beside the auctioneer's own trade.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- **Miller.** `person_col_samuel_miller` (Democrat, 25 February 1834) and
  `person_samuel_miller` (4 March 1834, occupation `agent`) are ONE advertisement printed
  twice — David Carver's storage, commission and lumber notice — whose closing sentence is
  "Calvin Britain esq. at St. Joseph, and Col. Samuel Miller, Michigan City, are his Agents".
  The Colonel is Carver's agent at Michigan City, Indiana. Say which pass joined those two
  readings to `miller_samuel`, a Chicago card otherwise resting on `tax_1833_075` and an 1832
  Black Hawk enrollment, and either give the person the place its own notice gives it — the
  shape T-0694 settled for the FIRM M'Cormick & Moon of Detroit and asked be checked "for
  every other out-of-town house the papers advertise for" — or split the readings off the
  card. Whichever, the card must not read as a Chicago man with a trade when its trade is
  another state's.
- **Montgomery.** `person_w_montgomery` carries `boot and shoe maker` among its occupations
  beside `auctioneer`, `commission merchant`, `land agent` and `newspaper agent`. The
  shoemaker is Loton W. Montgomery, who signs `L. W. MONTGOMERY` in the same paper and whom
  Fergus 1843 and Norris 1844 print under his own name; the auctioneer signs `W. MONTGOMERY`
  and `W. MONTGOMERY, Auc'r.`. Two signatures, separated on the page, on one person record.
  Either the readings split or the note says on what ground one man holds both trades.
- both repairs re-run the derived resident files in the same pass, and `bash tools/check.sh`
  is green;
- the ruling that finds a THIRD instance of this shape adds it here rather than filing again.

## WHERE THIS CAME FROM

T-1034 cohort C2. MILLER SAMUEL was refused partly BECAUSE of the Michigan City reading —
strip it and the card is two bare names — and MONTGOMERY WILLIAM was upheld in spite of the
shoemaking, on the auctioneer's own signature. Neither ruling repaired the card: a ruling
refuses a purchase and does not rewrite a person. T-0694 met the same shape at the firm level
and its last acceptance line asked for exactly this check.
