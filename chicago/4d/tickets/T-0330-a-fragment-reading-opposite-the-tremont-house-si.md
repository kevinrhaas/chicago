In every December 1834 printing of Matthias Mason's blacksmithing advertisement the
sentence ends `various branches, on ` and the street word is cut away. Four to six lines
away in the SAME segmenter column stands the fragment `site the Tremont Hous` — that is,
`[oppo]site the Tremont House`, a placement good enough to put a shop on the ground.

T-0294 refused to give it to Mason (1834-12-10 c007, placement `none`), for two reasons
stated there: the lines the fragment sits among are a dry-goods list — English flannels,
sattinets, muslins, ginghams, calicoes, merinos — belonging to the OTHER physical column
woven into this one, and assigning a placement across an interleave is exactly the mistake
the schema's `interleaved` flag exists to prevent.

But refusing it does not answer it. One of two advertisements in that column is anchored
on the Tremont House and the reconstruction is missing a frontage either way. Note that
the copy date `Nov. 26, 1833` sits under both and is for that reason recorded as neither's.

The columns: 1834-12-03 page 4 column 4 lines 4261-4275; 1834-12-10 page 4 column 4 lines
4419-4430; 1834-12-17 page 4 column 4 lines 4218-4234; 1834-12-24 page 4 column 4 lines
4350-4369.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- The page image says which advertisement carries `opposite the Tremont House`, and the
  claim that should have it gets it — with its own verbatim offset text.
- If it is the dry-goods advertiser, that advertiser is identified and minted; if it is
  Mason, his `placement` moves from `none` to `relative` and his street is stated.
- Either way the OTHER advertisement's placement is stated too, or recorded as absent.
