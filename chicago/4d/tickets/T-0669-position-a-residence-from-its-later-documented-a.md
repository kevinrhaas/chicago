Position a RESIDENCE from its later documented address: the six the directories print as res or bds, and the rule that governs a home rather than a shop.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

Filed by T-0633, which wrote the business half and refused this one by name.

`docs/ADDRESS-BACK-PROJECTION.md` clause 2 refuses a directory address the volume itself
prints as a RESIDENCE — its own `res` or `bds` — because that policy positions businesses
and reading a home address as a shop door would be answering a question nobody asked.
There are **six** of them today, all with a trade attested in 1835 and no `works_at`:

- J. H. Collins, attorney, *res 15 Lake* (1843)
- J. K. Botsford, tinsmith, *res 171 Wabash ave* (1843)
- J. B. Cook, baker, *res Jefferson, bet W. Randolph and Washington* (1843)
- L. W. Montgomery, shoemaker, *res Market* (1843)
- George W. Snow, surveyor, *res 244-6 State. s.-w. cor Jackson* (1843)
- Giles Spring, attorney, *res 62 Adams near State* (1843)

Half of them name a street the 1835 grid does not hold, so clause 3 would refuse them a
second time and the yield here is small — Lake, Market and State are the three that could
survive. The question this ticket has to answer first is whether a home is the same claim
as a shop at all: a `lives_at` reaches 20 households against `works_at`'s 50, the town's
own residence evidence is thinner, and a directory printing where a man slept in 1843 is
further from where he slept in 1835 than a shopfront is from a shopfront.

**Acceptance:** the rule for a residence is written down and is either the business rule
or an argued departure from it; all six are adjudicated with their outcome recorded on the
card as T-0633's are; and `lives_at`'s real-value count is stated before and after.
