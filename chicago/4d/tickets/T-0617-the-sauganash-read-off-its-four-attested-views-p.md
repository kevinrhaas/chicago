## A FIFTH view arrived, and it may be of this very building

The owner deposited it on 2026-09-03 — *"a great pic of the shore along there and
the eliza chappel school at clark and lake"*:

`chicago/reference/images/chicago/eliza-chappel-school/21617595_10203558686525015_5452300313452439832_n.jpg`

**What it actually shows.** A one-storey **LOG** building at the right, gable roof,
a door and two windows on the near face, a woman in a white apron standing in the
doorway and a crowd of children — girls in bonnets and aprons — streaming along the
bank toward it. To the left, the **shore**: one canoe afloat with paddlers, two more
drawn up on the beach with figures around them. In the middle distance a **white
lighthouse tower** on a low point, and behind it a scatter of small gabled
buildings across a flat prairie horizon.

**The caption and the picture disagree, and that is the useful part.** Clark and
Lake is inland, and the 1834 destination of Chappell's school was the FRAME First
Presbyterian Church on that corner — not a log building, and not on a shore. So
this is very unlikely to be the Clark and Lake schoolroom. Two readings are open:

- **(a) It is Chappell's FIRST school, September 1833** — the *"small log house
  formerly used as a store"* of the Chappell source, which the Beaubien source
  identifies as Mark Beaubien's own original cabin beside the Sauganash at Lake and
  Market. That corner stands on the south branch's bank, which is a shore with
  canoes on it. **If this reading holds, the picture is a FIFTH view of the very log
  annex this ticket is measuring** — and the most informative one for it, because it
  shows the building nearly square-on: log course count, door and window placement
  in the face, roof pitch and eaves, and the whole thing scaled against a
  schoolmistress and two dozen children rather than against a distant street.
- **(b) It is some other log schoolhouse**, in which case it leaves this ticket and
  goes to whichever record it does depict.

**Settle it before spending it.** The lighthouse is the control: it fixes the view
direction toward the river mouth and puts the scene at or after the harbour light's
construction, and the tree already holds `chicago_harbor_lighthouse_1838.jpg` to
compare the tower against. Read the bank's bearing, the lighthouse's position and
the building's orientation together and say which corner of the town this can be
standing on.

**Provenance first.** The file arrives with a social-media filename and no artist,
title, date or repository. It is an undated retrospective drawing until someone
establishes otherwise, so file a source record for it at the tier that supports —
and do NOT let it move an attributed plate (Petford, Braunhold) on any axis where
the two disagree. Where it agrees with them it corroborates; where it is alone it
is a single weak view, graded the way T-0092 graded the shutter leaves.

The shore half of this picture — the bank profile, the beached canoes, the low
buildings on the far side — is evidence for the river-edge work rather than for
this ticket; **T-0453** carries the bank tracing and is pointed at it.
