The Democrat of **1834-02-25** carries two advertisements over J. K. Botsford's name,
eighty lines apart in the same column run, and they give two different addresses:

| claim | address, verbatim | placement |
|---|---|---|
| `chicago_democrat_1834_02_25` c001 | `Corner of Dearborn an[d] Lake-sts.` | `corner` |
| `chicago_democrat_1834_02_25` c008 | `next.door to Graves' Tavern.` | `relative` |

The second address also ran a week earlier, at `chicago_democrat_1834_02_18` c003, over the
same `Feb. 17` copy date. So it is not a stale insertion the man forgot to pull: both are
current on 25 February 1834.

**Why it matters more than one grocer's frontage.** Graves' Tavern is one of the town's
fixed names and THREE storefronts in February 1834 are placed off it — Botsford next door,
and Matthias Mason & Co.'s blacksmith shop "back on **Main-street**, nearly opposite" it
(`chicago_democrat_1834_02_25` c006, the fuller witness; `1834_02_18` c011 is the cut one).
The tavern is doing the work of a survey point without having a location of its own. If
Botsford's two addresses are one frontage, the corner of Dearborn and Lake places Graves'
Tavern, and Main-street with it, and three storefronts come onto the ground together.

T-0311 refused to make that step and the refusal is the point: `next door` and `corner of`
are both Botsford's own frontage, and nothing in the paper says they are the same frontage.
**What the later corpus already says, and it narrows this a long way.** Botsford is at the
corner of Dearborn and Lake continuously from the following summer: `1834_07_09` c026 is a
TIN FACTORY headed `CORNER OF DEARBORN AND LAK[E]-STREETS`, `1834_07_09` c027 puts
Fullerton & Botsford's pork `[at the] corner of Dearborn and Lake-sts.` over an April 1834
copy date, `1834_08_13` c015 wants ginseng at `the Tin, Sheet Iron and Copper Factory,
corner of Lake and D[e]arborn-streets`, and the September read (T-0291) recorded the tin and
stove manufactory on the same corner. So the corner is his settled address by spring 1834
and the tin manufactory he promises here for "the opening of navigation" was built.
`next door to Graves' Tavern` appears only in February and does not survive into the summer
corpus. That is evidence for reading 3, and it is not proof.

**And the anchor next door moves too.** Matthias Mason & Co.'s forge is `on Main-[s]treet,
n[early] [o]pposite Grave[s]' Tavern` in January (`1834_01_07` c008) and February
(`1834_02_25` c006), and `on Main-stro[e]t, oppo[sit]e the Tremont House` by September
(`1834_09_10` c013). The forge has not moved. Either **Graves' Tavern was renamed the
Tremont House** in 1834, or a second house went up across the same street. A D. Graves is
running the Chicago Bakery by September (`1834_09_10` c016) and a D. Graves sits on the
Chicago Academy's board in February (`1834_02_25` c002), so the man is still in town under
his own name either way.

Three readings are open on Botsford and the February evidence alone does not choose —

1. **One shop, two descriptions.** A corner store is both "on the corner of Dearborn and
   Lake" and "next door to Graves' Tavern" if the tavern is the adjoining lot.
2. **Two premises.** He advertises as a grocer in one notice and as a tinner in the other,
   and says he will "commence the manufacturing of Tin, in all its branches" at the opening
   of navigation — a manufactory is not a grocery counter.
3. **A move between 17 and 25 February**, with the older copy still running.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — one demonstration, never weakened to pass)

- The three readings are tested against the rest of the corpus — the March-onward Democrat
  as later months are read, the 1835 issues already read, and any Botsford or Graves record
  in `data/sources/` — and the one the evidence supports is stated with what supports it,
  or all three are reported as still open, which is a real answer.
- If and only if the evidence closes it, Graves' Tavern gets a placement record with the
  confidence the evidence earns — `inferred` with the reasoning written out, never
  `documented`, because no source puts the tavern on a corner directly.
- **Main-street is not identified with a platted street on the strength of this alone.**
  That the town used the name in 1833-34 is what the corpus holds; which line on the plat it
  names is a separate question and wants its own evidence.
