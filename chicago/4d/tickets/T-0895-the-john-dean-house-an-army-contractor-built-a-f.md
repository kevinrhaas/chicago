The John-Dean house: an army contractor built a five-room house at the foot of Randolph Street in 1815, Beaubien bought it in 1817 for $1,000, and this project models nothing there.

**Found by T-0718, 2026-09-06.** Andreas scan p. 183, read directly off the scan:

> In 1815, a short time before the rebuilding of the fort, an army contractor named **Dean**, built
> a house **on the lake shore, at the mouth of the Chicago River, near where is now the foot of
> Randolph Street**. In 1817, Mr. Beaubien purchased this house, which was **a low, gloomy building
> of five rooms, for $1,000** — a large sum for those days. After this purchase he lived in the Dean
> house for several years, his son Alexander being born there. He used the old cabin after this for
> a barn.

That is more than this project holds for most of what it has built: a builder, a year, a site named
by a street this town already draws, a price, a room count and an occupant with a child born there.
Wentworth's 1881 address names it again — *"what was before known as the John-Dean house"* — and
Andreas p. 191 has Varnum boarding in *"the old John Dean house, with J. B. Beaubien, then its
owner"*. **Nothing in this dataset stands there.**

**The question this ticket must answer first: was it still standing on 1835-07-01?** Beaubien lived
in it "for several years" from 1817 and had moved to the factory building by 1822 or soon after.
Nothing reached follows the building past that. A five-room house at the foot of Randolph Street in
1835 would sit beside `T-0759`'s watering place, which this town drew on 2026-09-06 — so if it
stood, the scene is missing a building at a spot a visitor already walks to; and if it did not, the
refusal belongs on the record in writing rather than as an absence.

**Whether contractor Dean and Hubbard's United States factor John Dean are one man is not
established** (T-0718 says so in as many words) and does not need to be for this ticket.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — one demonstration, never weakened to pass)

- The building is either placed with a stated grade and its own note, or refused in writing with
  the reason on a record a reader can find — no silent omission.
- Its survival to the scene date is argued from evidence, not assumed from its construction date.
- The "foot of Randolph Street" is read against this town's own Randolph Street and the traced
  1834 shoreline, as `T-0759` did for the watering place, rather than by eye.
- Any size taken from "five rooms" says out loud that a room count is not a dimension.
