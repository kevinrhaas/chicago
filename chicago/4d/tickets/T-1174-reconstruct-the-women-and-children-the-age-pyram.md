The owner: *"a fair number of missing women and children."* The 1840 sheet says children under
10 were 27% of the town and women 20–29 the second-largest band; the known layer names 15 women.
After T-1171 most of that gap closes inside families; this stage (`women_children`)
fills what the buckets still show short — because the sources under-record exactly the households
without a male head, and a family-drawing rule keyed to male heads cannot create them.

**Rules:** the order book's female and child buckets (sex × age × division) after families →
new households of the types the model gives them: widow-headed (with her children, a trade —
boarding, washing, sewing, keeping school — from the female-trade rows of the occupation model),
a single woman in service (seated in an employer household or a hotel by T-1175/T-1189),
a schoolmistress (one per school the civic band leaves unstaffed), older children as apprentices
and servants where the model places them outside their parents' house. Names from the pools;
every attribute reconstructed with basis and seed.

**Acceptance:**

- The female and child buckets read filled; the sex ratio and age pyramid printed against the
  model within bracket; female-headed household share printed against the model.
- The female-trade rows of the occupation model read filled (laundress, domestic, seamstress/
  dressmaker/milliner, schoolmistress, boarding-house keeper) — attested first, then these.
- LIBERTIES entry; visible in the People view and the card.

**Stop condition:** the town's women and children are as numerous as the model says, and every
one of them says she is reconstructed and why.
