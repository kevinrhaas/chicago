The 1840 census images 26-50: the nine continuation sheets, paired to their left sheets by printed page.

Piece 6 of 7 of **T-0495 — The 1840 census deposit is 75 page images and 210 heads on seven printed pages are the only names read from it: images 26-50**, split because the parent needed more than one run's demonstration to be done. The parent keeps the full ask and its links; this ticket owns one slice of it.

**What this piece is.** The continuation (right) sheets of group 26-50 that carry entries:
`33S7-9YYJ-FJ`, `33S7-9YYJ-K2`, `33S7-9YYJ-L3`, `33S7-9YYJ-V4`, `33S7-9YYJ-VJ`, `33S7-9YYN-3CF6`,
`33SQ-GYYJ-5H`, `33SQ-GYYJ-9CZ` — eight of them, plus `33S7-9YYJ-V2` if T-0529 finds it is an
ordinary continuation sheet after all. `33S7-9YYN-3CNQ` is struck through corner to corner and
`33SQ-GYYJ-7L` (printed 236) is empty; both stay declared and unread.

**The problem this piece has to solve, and it is the interesting part.** A continuation sheet carries no
name. A household is one ruled line spanning a left sheet and a right sheet, so pairing is by printed
page and line position — and most of these right sheets carry NO printed page number where the contact
sheet could read one. State the pairing rule, demonstrate it on a page whose left sheet is already
transcribed, and record any sheet the rule cannot pair as unpaired rather than guessing.

**Acceptance:** (one demonstration, never weakened to pass)
- Each right sheet either paired to a left sheet with the evidence for the pairing, or recorded unpaired.
- The industry, pensioner and schools columns read per line for every paired sheet.
- No household figure attached to a name on a pairing the evidence does not carry.
