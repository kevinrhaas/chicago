`D. WEAVER` advertises the same building for sale or to let in two consecutive numbers and
the two transcriptions disagree about which lot it stands on:

| issue | column | reads |
|---|---|---|
| 1834-12-17 | page 3 column 3, line 2958 | `building on Lot 9, in block 1, on Norh` |
| 1834-12-24 | page 3 column 3, line 3379 | `THE building on Lot 2, in block 1, on Nort` |

Both columns are Vision-set, not the Tesseract fallback, so neither is the obviously worse
witness; and 2 and 9 are not a confusable pair in clean type, which means one of the two
transcriptions is simply wrong rather than both being uncertain readings of a damaged
glyph.

T-0294 claimed BOTH printings — 1834-12-17 c006 and 1834-12-24 c012 — each quoting its own
digit, and neither amended to agree with the other, because an agreement manufactured
between two transcriptions is not evidence.

This matters more than a one-digit correction usually would. `block 1, North Water street`
is inside the modelled town on the north bank, and a lot number is as close to a map
reference as this corpus ever gets: it is the difference between a documented building
standing at one place on the river frontage and standing at another.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- The lot number is read off at least one of the two page images and stated.
- The losing claim keeps its verbatim quote — the transcription is not edited — and gains a
  note saying which reading won and on what evidence.
- If the images cannot settle it, that is recorded as the answer, with the reason.
