The Wolf Tavern's pole sign, from its documentation.

**The owner, 2026-08-18, of the sign close-up, verbatim: "its almost like a flag, you
should be able to get documentation of what this sign/flag was and put it at the tavern
correctly."**

The brief holds a close-up (`data/sources/assets/owner_brief_2026_08_18/README.md`,
image 4): beside the log tavern stands a **very tall pole — mast-tall, well above the
ridge — with a cross-arm at the top, and a board sign hanging from the arm, painted
with a wolf.** Flag-halyard proportions, exactly as the owner reads it.

Do the documentation first: Andreas (1884) describes the Wolf Tavern and its sign — the
painted wolf is the tavern's name-source in the standard accounts. Pull what the sources
actually say (Andreas v1, chicagology's tavern pages) into the record, then build the
pole, arm and painted board at the tavern per the engraving: `inferred` for the sign's
existence and form if the text attests it, reconstructed for dimensions and paint. The
wolf is painted flat on a board — it is signage, not a creature; L1 is untouched.

**Acceptance:** the tall cross-armed pole sign with its painted wolf board stands at the
Wolf Tavern, and its card cites the documentation found (text and plate), each claim at
its honest tier. Gates green.
