Signboards carry the location's name, and no two are alike.

**The owner, 2026-08-18, verbatim: "you can and should put the name of the location on
the sign board. the sign boards should have variation in color and style and signage font
and color, some signs may hang from an awning and others may be on the building or painted
on the face of the building. you need to add more signage and be period correct and it is
fine if they are reconstructions."**

This widens T-0039 (done), which raised boards only where a sign was attested. Now:

- **Every signboard names its business** — the name the record gives the location, painted
  on the board (the inspector card and the sign must agree).
- **Variation is required**: board colour, lettering style and colour, shape — no two
  alike on a street. Period letterforms (signwriter/stencil, gold-on-black, dark-on-white,
  ochre grounds — the range the trade actually used).
- **Vary the mounting**: post-hung (the Green Tree's corner post, brief image 6/7),
  awning-hung, building-mounted boards, and names **painted directly on the facade**
  (the Tremont street scene, brief image 5, shows painted fronts across the row).
- **More signage overall** — businesses without an attested sign get a reconstructed one,
  labelled as such.

The Wolf Tavern's pole sign is its own ticket (T-0072) — leave it to that one.

**Acceptance:** walking a core street, the named businesses carry name-bearing boards in
visibly varied styles and mountings, including at least one awning-hung and one
facade-painted example; each unattested sign cards as reconstructed. Gates green.
