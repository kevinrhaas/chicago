More fences: the town encloses its property.

**The owner, 2026-08-18, verbatim: "i think there should be more fences."** And the brief
shows why (`data/sources/assets/owner_brief_2026_08_18/README.md`, image 12 — Chicago
circa 1833 looking east): **split-rail and board fences line the roads and enclose every
property in view.** Fences are the norm of an 1830s town lot, not the exception.

Today only a handful of enclosures exist (the T-0050/51/52 set). Extend enclosure
coverage across the improved lots: board and picket fencing in the built core, split-rail
toward the edges and along the outlying lanes, following lot lines (the plat gives them).
Reconstructed tier — the fences are invented per-lot but bounded by image 12, the Kinzie
plate's garden fences, and universal period practice; one LIBERTIES entry for the scheme.

Street-LINING fences (the continuous runs at the road edge, with the sidewalks) are
T-0069's half — this ticket owns the property enclosures. Watch the triangle budget:
T-0056 (the enclosure layer's LOD cost) is open and this multiplies it — coordinate,
don't collide.

**Acceptance:** improved lots across the town read as fenced — from a street or the fly
camera, enclosure is the norm on built lots, in varied period types — carded
reconstructed. Gates green.
