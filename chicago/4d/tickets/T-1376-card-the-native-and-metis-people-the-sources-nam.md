Piece 2 of 4 of **T-1177 — Reconstruct the under-documented cohorts within their evidence: the Native and Métis people, households and businesses in and around the town, the free Black residents, families and Black-owned businesses, and the Irish and German Catholic town the register implies — every one identified, tiered and reviewable**, split because the parent needed more than one run's demonstration to be done. The parent keeps the full ask and its links; this ticket owns one slice of it.

**Acceptance (stated before working, 2026-09-19):** every Native and Métis person the
committed corpus NAMES, and whom the town does not already carry, is on a card — at the
grade its own evidence allows, with `review_required` and `touches_removal` and the
sentence that says which subject it is held for; every row the roster offered and this
stage did not card carries a stated reason rather than a silence; the counted-but-unnamed
remainder is reconstructed within a bracket WHERE A COMMITTED SOURCE COUNTS, and refused in
writing with what would retire the refusal where none does; and
`docs/RESEARCH/native_and_metis_1835.md` lists every source read and the review still owed.

**Demonstrated:** the Illinois State Archives index of Black Hawk War enrollments prints 134
men enrolled at Chicago in 1832 in two companies — forty under `G KERCHEVAL`, ninety-four
under one the roll heads `INDIAN`. The town carried **twenty of Kercheval's and none of the
others**, because the borderline roster's R6 rule routes a reading with a community term in
it into a class T-1172 was not licensed to spend. Stage `underdocumented` / sub-stage
`native_and_metis` (`tools/reconstruct_underdocumented.py`) cards **87** of them under R3's
own licence — the same persistence model, lag and seeded draw — 69 present and 18 absent on
the scene date; 15 rows were already on a card and 29 are withheld, each with a named reason.
No nation is written on any card: the roll says `INDIAN` and says no more, so `community` is
`native`, a term added to the vocabulary for exactly that. The remainder is REFUSED: no roll,
census or estimate in this corpus counts Native people at Chicago before 1 July 1835, and the
August gathering is six and a half weeks after the scene and is not staged.

**Filed, not spent:** the St Mary's register is the only source here in which a contemporary
states an Indigenous identity for a named person at Chicago, and the two women it so
identifies — *Marianne (sauvage)*, wife of Antoine Aspam, and *Jaespquaa (sauvage de Green
Bay)*, wife of Paul Vieaux — are the ONLY adults on their own entries that the roster ruled
ineligible. The town holds their husbands and their children and not them. A baptism is not a
residence and that ruling is the research layer's; this is a reading owed its own run.
