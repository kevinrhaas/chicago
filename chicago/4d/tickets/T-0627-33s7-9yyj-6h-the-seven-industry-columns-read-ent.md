33S7-9YYJ-6H: the seven industry columns read entry by entry and closed against the enumerator's own footings.

Piece 1 of 2 of **T-0548 — The 1840 census image 33S7-9YYJ-6H read line by line and closed against its own printed column totals**, split because the parent needed more than one run's demonstration to be done. The parent keeps the full ask and its links; this ticket owns one slice of it.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — one demonstration, never weakened to pass)

**Why T-0548 was split, measured rather than judged.** T-0548 inherited T-0541's ask
for `33S7-9YYJ-6H` whole: the twelve slave columns, the family TOTAL, the seven industry
columns, pensioners, the ten deaf/dumb/blind/insane columns and the seven schools columns,
per ruled line, every column closed against its own printed footing. Its sibling sheet
`33S7-9YYJ-5V` is the measured price of that ask and it came to THREE tickets — T-0547 read
the eight middle columns against the footings, T-0564 finished them, and T-0565 fitted the
line index and read the TOTAL column against it. 6H is the harder leaf of the two: its
exposure is pale enough that the column-finding tool refused the sheet outright until this
run's instrument fix, and its body carries paper creases that the ink mask reports as
entries. Splitting is the rule the queue states for a ticket that needs more than one
demonstration, and this is the same cut 5V already took.

**Acceptance:** every one of the seven industry columns is read entry by entry off the
image, at magnification, and set against the figure the enumerator wrote at the foot of
his own column. A column with a written footing either CLOSES or records its residual;
a column with no written footing says so and records what was read. No entry is adjusted
to make a total come out, and no line index is asserted — the row grid is T-0628's work,
and the entry y positions this ticket commits are the anchors it will fit to.
