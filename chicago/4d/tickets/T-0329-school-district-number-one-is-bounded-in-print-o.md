The Democrat of 1834-12-10 prints the metes and bounds of **School District Number One**,
together with the notice of a meeting of the district's citizens called at the Mansion
House for 16 December. It is the town's first school district described as a closed
circuit on real ground: Willis, block 101, section 16, the Chicago river and the Lake
shore are all features this reconstruction already carries.

It is also the worst-cut passage of the month. The notice stands in page 3 column 2, where
the segmenter has woven it line by line through a Winnebago-massacre despatch and a
chancery order, and better than half of every line is gone with the column edge. T-0294
claimed it (1834-12-10 c001) from FIFTEEN surviving lines out of a cited range of
fifty-four, and the normalized reading is a skeleton with brackets at every joint — the
compass words that survive are kept, the ones that do not are marked absent, and nothing is
supplied.

So the boundary cannot be drawn from what is on `dev` today. The page image would recover
it in one pass, and a district boundary that can be drawn is a thing the walkthrough can
actually show.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- The boundary description is read from the page image of 1834-12-10 page 3 and the claim's
  `normalized` reading is completed, or the parts still illegible are named individually.
- The claim's `quote` is unchanged — it is verbatim transcription and stays so.
- Whether the circuit closes on the modelled ground is stated as a yes or a no, with the
  features it runs on named. If it closes, say what it would take to draw it.
