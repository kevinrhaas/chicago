The 16-by-30-foot house at Lasalle and Lake is a documented Chicago building with a corner and a footprint, and the town places it nowhere.

T-0412 took this house OFF `P. Pruyne & Co.` — he signs the notice as its vendor, and the
address is the house he is selling, not the ground his store stands on. That was the
correction. What it leaves is a building the corpus documents better than most of what the
town stands on, and nothing anywhere builds it.

**What the paper gives** (`chicago_democrat_1834_05_21` c001, and the printed copy is dated
Ap'l. 22): "[W]E offer for sale the House on [the corner] of Lasalle and Lake streets. [It]
is 16 by 30 feet, and possession can [be had on] the first day of next month." A named
intersection the plat can find, a plan dimension in feet, and a date — offered 22 April,
still standing and untenanted on 21 May 1834, possession on the first of June. Four hundred
and eighty square feet is a small two- or three-room dwelling. **WHICH of the two dimensions
runs along Lake is NOT stated**, and the claim already refuses to infer it; a build has to
say what it assumed and grade it.

**Why it is worth a roof.** The reconstruction's West Division and Lake-street blocks deal
their house sizes from ARCHETYPES. This is a real one, from the paper, at a corner. The
claim's own note says as much: "the single most directly buildable line in the month".

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- The house stands at the Lasalle-and-Lake corner the notice names, on committed lots, with
  its 16-by-30-foot footprint — or the ticket states, on the ground, why that corner cannot
  take it and what it took instead.
- The frontage orientation is a stated assumption with its confidence, never a silent one:
  the printing does not say which dimension runs along Lake.
- It is NOT attributed to P. Pruyne as occupant. He is the vendor; the occupant on the
  scene date is unknown and the record says so.
- Baked in the same commit and published (`needs_bake` is set for that reason).
