The 1 April 1834 Chicago letter list is 179 names and nineteen lines of debris stand where more did.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

The list of letters remaining in the Post Office at Chicago on 1 April 1834 is minted from
the transcriptions in T-0313: 163 names from the printing of 1834-04-01, plus 16 more from
the reprint of 1834-04-08 that the first printing's OCR crushed. **179 is a floor, not the
printed length.**

Nineteen lines across the two printings carry debris where a name stood and are left unread
rather than guessed at:

- **1834-04-01, page 3 column 3** (the A-J half): lines 2869, 2881, 2882, 2893, 2914, 2915,
  2916, 2921, 2922, 2923 and 2925 — eleven lines, reading things like `is Bet i`,
  `Rowercee ee, i`, `Hag, pitsrerall` and `Atyere Rovler 2 ore`.
- **1834-04-01, page 3 column 4** (the J-W half): lines 3039-3045, seven lines where the
  interleaved editorial took the column instead. The 1834-04-08 reprint shows what stood
  there — Isaac Merrill, Chas. Manning, James Mulford, Philo C. Mills and James McKee — and
  those five ARE minted, from that reprint. What else may have been in those lines is not
  known.
- **1834-04-08, page 3 column 4**: the segmenter cuts this half further left than 04-01's
  and strips the leading letter off most names (`lsaac`, `lomes`, `ingston`), so it cannot
  be used to audit 04-01's second half name for name.

**What would close it:** the page images for Vol. I Nos. 18 and 19, read at name level, the
way `data/sources/chicago_democrat_1833_11_26.json` was read for Vol. I No. 1. That is a
`scan_verified` pass and it outranks both transcriptions (ruling 2). Until it happens the
cohort is a floor and the coverage note says so.

**Sibling to T-0316**, which asks the same question of the 1 January 1834 list (97 names,
also a floor). If the page images are fetched once, both should be read in the same pass.
