The Chicago Democrat's bookseller advertises through the whole of December 1834 and the
firm's name survives in exactly ONE of the four printings: 1834-12-03 page 3 column 4,
where it sets as `RUISAL & CLUPR,`. The printings of 1834-12-17 (page 3 column 3) and
1834-12-24 (page 3 column 4) both open at `[B]ooks & Stationary` with the heading cut away
above it, and 1834-12-10 carries only the word `Stationary`.

T-0294 recorded the claim (1834-12-03 c004) with the name inside `[uncertain: Ruisal &
Clupr]` rather than guess at it, so the gazetteer now carries a business key with an
uncertainty marker in it. That is the honest state and it is not a good permanent one.

Two questions, and one page image answers both:

1. **What is the firm called?** The advertisement is a substantial one — Miscellaneous,
   School, Classical, Theological and Medical Books out of Boston, New-York and
   Philadelphia; almanacs for 1835; Maynard & Noyes' inks; beaver and nap hats; agents for
   Morison's Vegetable Pills; Silsby's cast steel axes — so this is a real shop and it
   deserves a real name.
2. **Is it the firm already in the gazetteer?** `business_chicago_wholesale_and_retail_
   book_stationary_store` stands there from an earlier read with no proprietor. If it is
   the same house, the two entries must be joined — and T-0304 records that the gazetteer
   has NO declared merge rule for firms, only for persons, so joining them is not yet
   something the tool can do honestly.

**Acceptance:** (state it before working — the definition of done, never weakened to pass)

- The name is read off the page image of 1834-12-03 page 3, or the reading is recorded as
  irrecoverable with the reason, and the claim's `[uncertain:]` marker is resolved either way.
- The relation to `business_chicago_wholesale_and_retail_book_stationary_store` is stated:
  same firm, different firm, or undecidable — with the evidence.
- If they are the same firm, T-0304 is either already closed or this ticket says plainly
  that the merge waits on it. No hand-merge.
