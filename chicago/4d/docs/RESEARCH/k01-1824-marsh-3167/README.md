# T-3167 (piece 2 of T-1887) — 1824 Prairie onto K01

It refines the district draft (T-2329) in place under `marsh_house_1824_prairie`. T-3166 (piece 1)
put 1828 next door onto K01; T-3168 (piece 3) owns what the pictures show at 1824 and 1828 that is
not built yet, the cameras still to solve, and the match records' `matches`.

## The house

Samuel B. Pomeroy's Italianate villa of 1857, the avenue's second house (the marker; the Glessner
House Museum blog, "The early history of Prairie Avenue", June 2020: "an elaborate bracketed cornice,
front and side porches, and a large cupola"). George B. Marsh bought it in 1880 and "soon after"
gave it "an entirely new stone façade" (the same post). The 1911 sheet prints it "2½B; rear2B brick
with stone front".

## The pictures

- **The Pomeroy photograph** (before about 1880): source record
  `glessner_blog_1824_pomeroy_italianate`, library `a18-gh-pomeroy-house-italianate` and
  `cgn-1824-pomeroy-italianate-copy` (the same photograph). It shows the house before the stone
  front, so it is read only for what that front did not change: two tall storeys over a raised
  basement, the deep bracketed cornice, the low hip, the lower two-storey block beside the main one,
  the stacks, round-headed flank windows under hoods.
- **The automobile photograph of 1828** (about 1897-1906; `chicagology_1828_shipman_automobile`):
  its right third shows the next house north. Read here, in order from 1828's porch: a light wall
  carrying a frieze of small panels between brackets under a cornice, with the street trees'
  shadows on it (the south side of 1824 and the street face of its rear range); then a stone front
  with a **canted bay through two storeys** between panelled pilasters with incised blocks, one tall
  flat-headed window with a transom bar to each face per storey, a deep belt between the storeys
  and a cornice at its head; then, at its north end, **the entrance in a porch on columns under a
  balustraded balcony**, a stoop between stone cheeks, and stone posts and an iron fence before the
  house. That composition is the 1911 sheet's stone strip below: the bay at the south part of the
  front, the stone piece before the north part.
- **The partial view of 1828** (`glessner_blog_1828_shipman_partial`): its right half shows the
  same south flank of 1824 receding, with round-headed second-storey windows under hoods, the frieze
  with its panels and brackets, and a one-storey projection low on the flank.

**What the automobile photograph does not do:** fix sizes. T-3166's camera was fitted to 1828's
front alone, and through it the sheet's 1824 front lands about 250-350 px right of where the print
shows the bay (the sheet puts the front's south corner 8.8 m north of 1828's north wall; the print,
read at 1828's scale, about 3 m). Either that camera's eye or the reading of the bay is off by that
much, and nothing in this piece settles which, so the front's composition is read off the picture
and its sizes off the sheet. Solving a camera of its own for 1824 on this print is T-3168's.

## Rulings

- **The cupola: not built.** The Pomeroy photograph shows a square cupola on the hip before 1880, and
  the blog calls it "a large cupola". No picture after the stone front shows the roof (the
  automobile photograph's top edge is the bay's cornice; the partial view's right edge is torn), the
  1911 sheet prints no lantern or observatory, and its "½" storey is met by the attic behind the
  villa's deep frieze. A cupola is a feature a reconstruction should not add without a picture of it
  after the 1880s work, and K01 has no cupola part; so 1904 is built without it, and the ruling is
  recorded as uncertain (docs/LIBERTIES.md, L-k01-1824-marsh-1887). A picture of the roof after
  1880 overturns it.
- **The rear range's roof** is decked flat in K04's tin: the Pomeroy photograph shows a low hip, and a
  pitched K01 wing is slated, which K04's slates allow only from 45 degrees.
- **The bay on the rear range's south wall** is not built: a K01 wing's wall carries a K08 tower or a
  full-height projection, not a bay.

## What is open (for T-3168)

The flank windows' round heads under hoods (K01 cuts a flank's windows flat); the frieze's panels
between the brackets; the flanks' light painted or stuccoed finish (K01 lays brick); the bay's
pilasters with their incised blocks; the porch's columns and balcony read to a camera; the rear
range's low hip and its south bay; the one-storey projection low on the south flank in the partial
view; and a camera for 1824 on the automobile photograph and on the partial view.

No generator rule was changed: every part is the K01, K02, K06, K07, K08, K09, K10, K11 and K15 kits
as they stand.
