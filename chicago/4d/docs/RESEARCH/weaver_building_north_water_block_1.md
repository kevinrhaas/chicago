# D. Weaver's building — Lot 2 or Lot 9, block 1, North Water street, and the day the copy was dated

*The disagreement, and the reading taken from it. AGENTS.md § Honesty rules: "Where
sources disagree, record the disagreement in `docs/RESEARCH/<id>.md` and pick the
best-attested reading **with reasoning** — never silently choose." T-0328 (PR #510) made
the choice inside the claim notes; this is the dossier that rule asks for, written after
it so it describes what the corpus actually holds. **T-0350 (below) carries the second
question the same notice raised — the day of its dateline — and it found two more printings
while answering it, so the counts in the section above this line are the counts T-0328 had.**
Where the two disagree about how many printings there are, seven is right and five is what
was known on 2026-08-29 before T-0350 ran.*

**Question.** Two printings of the same advertisement gave two different lot numbers. A
lot and a block are as close to a map reference as this corpus ever gets: it is the
difference between a documented building standing at one place on the north-bank river
frontage and standing at another.

**Answer. Lot 2.** Four printings to one when T-0328 read it, and five to one now that
T-0350 has found two more — on five separate settings of the type, with a sixth printing
no digit at all.

## The witnesses

The notice is a standing advertisement, run in **seven** consecutive numbers of the
*Chicago Democrat* from Vol. I No. 50 into the fourth number of Vol. II. All seven stand on
issue page 3. T-0328 knew five of them; T-0350 found the two earliest.

| issue | page/col | claim | the line, verbatim |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1834-11-12 | 3 / 1 | `c015` | `[uncertain: Tl ae etce Apry tolock 1, on Nort]` — no lot digit |
| 1834-11-19 | 3 / 3 | `c012` | `building on Lot 2, in bl` |
| 1834-11-26 | 3 / 3 | `c010` | `ling on Lot 2, in block 1, on North` |
| 1834-12-03 | 3 / 3 | `c025` | `SuE building on Lot 2, in block 1, on North` |
| 1834-12-10 | 3 / 3 | `c012` | `on Lot 2, in block 1, on North` |
| **1834-12-17** | 3 / 3 | `c006` | `building on Lot 9, in block 1, on Norh` |
| 1834-12-24 | 3 / 4 | `c012` | `THE building on Lot 2, in block 1, on Nort` |

Five of the seven read Lot 2, one reads Lot 9, and 1834-11-12 prints no digit at all. The
majority T-0328 took is widened by the two later finds and is not disturbed by them.

Only the last two had been claimed before T-0328. The November read (T-0293) and the
December read (T-0294) both passed over the notice in the earlier issues — in
1834-11-26 and 1834-12-10 it survives inside an alternating pair of physical columns,
woven line by line, which is the shape those reads found hardest. **That is a fact about
the instrument, not only about this advertisement**, and it is recorded in the November
range of `coverage.json` so the same miss can be looked for elsewhere.

## Why this is evidence and not a tidy-up

T-0294 claimed both of the printings it found, each quoting its own digit, and amended
neither to agree with the other. That was right, and the reading does not undo it:
**no transcription has been edited.** Every printing keeps its own verbatim quote,
including the one that reads 9, and the losing reading stays visible in that claim's
`normalized`. What changed is the number of witnesses.

The notice is **reset weekly** — its line breaks, its capitals, its dateline and even its
column all move between numbers — so the four agreeing printings are four compositors'
settings and four passes of the transcriber over different type, not one reading counted
four times. That is the whole warrant: a majority of independent settings, not a
manufactured agreement between two.

## The losing column, on its own record

1834-12-17 page 3 column 3 is the least reliable of the seven, and demonstrably so from
inside its own three lines:

- it sets `Norh` for *North*;
- it loses the word `Water` to the alternating column entirely;
- it sets the advertisement's own dateline as `3, Nor. 13, 131`.

**And that last one also refutes the premise the ticket was opened on.** T-0328 argued
that "2 and 9 are not a confusable pair in clean type, which means one of the two
transcriptions is simply wrong rather than both being uncertain readings of a damaged
glyph". The premise is sound and the conclusion followed, but the unstated assumption —
that this type is clean — does not hold: the same advertisement's copy dateline is set
**12, 12, 12, 12, 13 and 19** across the seven weeks, with one week illegible. A column
that sets a day three different ways
is not a column whose digits can be taken at face value, and the reading rests on the
count of independent settings rather than on any one of them being trustworthy.

## The dateline — T-0350's question, and its answer

**Answer. 12 November 1834.** Four printings to one to one, and the notice's own insertion
key decides it without the count.

| issue | claim | the dateline, verbatim | reads |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1834-11-12 | `c015` | `[uncertain: Sap Nr. 12.1831 -5)]` | Nov. **12** |
| 1834-11-19 | `c012` | `o, Nor. 12, 1884.—50` | Nov. **12** |
| 1834-11-26 | `c010` | `ine. 12, 1881-50` | Nov. **12** |
| 1834-12-03 | `c025` | `Chicago. Nor. 12, 1831. SO WEAVER.` | Nov. **12** |
| 1834-12-10 | `c012` | `e, 1631.—00` | no legible day or month |
| **1834-12-17** | `c006` | `3, Nor. 13, 131-D WEAVER.` | Nov. **13** |
| **1834-12-24** | `c012` | `Chicago, Nor. 19, 1834-SD. WEAVER.` | Nov. **19** |

### The key is the stronger instrument, and it is not a count

Every legible setting tails its dateline with the figure **50**: `1834.—50`, `1881-50`,
`1831. SO`, `1631.—00`, `1834-SD`, `1831 -5)`. That figure is not part of the date. It is
the number of the issue the copy is keyed to — **the first number published on or after the
dateline** — and the same columns prove it on other advertisers:

| the notice | its dateline | its key | the issue of that number |
|---|---|---|---|
| J. Bates, blacksmith's tools | `Nov. 5, 1834.—49` | 49 | Vol. I No. 49, **5 November** |
| a For Sale notice, 1834-12-24 | `Nov. 26, 1834-52` | 52 | Vol. I No. 52, **26 November** |
| Clybournsville, 1834-12-24 | `Dec. 11, 1834.-3` | 3 | Vol. II No. 3, **17 December** |
| Jeremiah Smith, 1834-12-24 | `Dec. 16, 1834.-3` | 3 | Vol. II No. 3, **17 December** |

Vol. I No. 50 was published on **12 November 1834**. A key of 50 therefore admits only a
dateline falling after 5 November and on or before 12 November. **Nov. 13 and Nov. 19 fall
outside that window and would both key 51.** Nov. 12 is the only one of the three readings
the notice's own key allows, and it is the reading four of the seven settings print.

Stated carefully, because one control notice breaks the looser version of the rule: the key
is a function of the **dateline**, not an assertion about first appearance. Bates's
`Nov. 5, 1834.—49` does not stand in No. 49 at all; it first appears in No. 50. For
Weaver's the two coincide anyway — the notice is absent from No. 49 of 1834-11-05 and from
No. 48 of 1834-10-29 (the `For Sale or Rent` standing in both is G. Blanshard's, a different
advertisement), and it first stands in No. 50.

### What this disposes of

T-0350 weighed 12 against 19 partly on the ground that a Nov. 12 dateline would sit a
fortnight before the notice's first appearance where Nov. 19 would sit a week. Both halves
rested on 1834-11-26 being the first appearance. **It is the third.** Dated 12 November the
notice is dated the day it first ran — the same-day pattern `Nov. 5, 1834.—49` and
`Nov. 26, 1834-52` show.

It also disposes of the coincidence that made the ledger wrong in the first place: the two
printings T-0328 inherited were the LAST two of seven, and they are the only two that do not
read 12. A notice that had been standing six weeks is the worst place to read its copy date.

### What the claims now say

All seven agree, and no quote is edited. `1834-12-17 c006` and `1834-12-24 c012` carried
`ad_copy_date.iso: 1834-11-19` and now carry `1834-11-12`, each keeping its own verbatim
dateline and showing the losing reading in `normalized`. `1834-11-26 c010` and
`1834-12-03 c025` gain the field T-0328 deliberately left empty. `1834-12-10 c012` still
carries none, and says why: `verbatim` is a field for that setting's own type, and that
setting prints no day and no month at all.

## Status of the reading

`transcription_mediated`, not `scan_verified`. T-0328's acceptance asked for the lot
number to be read off a page image; **the page images are not in the repository** — the
deposit at `chicago/reference/newspapers/` holds transcriptions only. The digit is settled
on the corpus's own text, which is a weaker instrument than a scan and a stronger one than
the two-witness deadlock it replaces. A scan read would still outrank it, as
`data/sources/chicago_democrat_1833_11_26.json` outranks the transcription for the first
number.

## What it means for the model

`block 1, North Water street` is inside the modelled town, on the north bank at the east
end of the frontage. Nothing stands for this building yet; placing it is T-0263 / T-0306
territory. When it is placed, **Lot 2**.
