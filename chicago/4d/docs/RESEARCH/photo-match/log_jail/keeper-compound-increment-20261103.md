## Historical prior phase change_note — verbatim, superseded where it asserts a sole roof

The log jail the citizens put up on the north-west corner of the court-house square in the fall of 1833, because the estray pen at the other corner of the same block had until then been the town's only place of confinement. Standing on 1835-07-01. No change of fabric is attested inside the phase. THE ONLY COUNTY BUILDING STANDING ON THE SQUARE ON THIS DAY, and the square held no third thing (T-1687). Andreas's summary of the new city reads 'A court-house, a jail, and an engine-house adorned the present square', and two of those three are later than 1 July 1835: the one-story and basement brick court-house was erected on the north-east corner 'during the fall of the year (1835,)' and the county's offices only moved into it toward the end of October, and the engine house was contracted to Levi Blake on 30 December 1835 and was still not fairly completed in February 1836. So on the scene date this jail is the one roof the block carries, with the roofless estray pen at the corner diagonally opposite and nothing else between them - the rest of the square is open, unimproved prairie. No worship or school building stands on it either: all four of the town's worship roofs and both its school roofs are named records elsewhere in the town (Andreas I scan pp. 299, 305, 369, 463; docs/RESEARCH/civic_public_buildings_1835.md).
