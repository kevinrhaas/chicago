# Where the Cook Circuit Court sat, May 1834 to October 1835 — and the May 1835 term refused

**Ticket:** T-1700 · **Outcome: REFUSED IN WRITING.** No source this project holds or reached
names the room the May 1835 term of the Cook Circuit Court sat in. Nothing is placed, no record
is built and no record is cited.

**What asked the question.** A chancery order of the Cook Circuit Court, sealed 8 December 1834
and printed in the *Chicago Democrat* of 24 December 1834
(`data/research/newspapers/extracted/chicago_democrat_1834_12_24.json#claims/c006`), requires the
defendant Michael P. Welch to appear at the next term, "to be holden in the [Co]urt House at
Chicago, on the fourteenth day [of] May next". The only court-house record the town holds is
`cook_county_courthouse_1835`, the one-storey-and-basement brick building on the north-east corner
of the square that Andreas puts up "during the fall of the year (1835)" (vol. I p. 176) and that
`compile_scene` excludes from 1835-07-01 by date. T-1591's ruling on c006
(`data/research/spend_rulings.json`, rule `the_place_is_named_and_the_town_holds_no_record_of_it`)
refused to write the order onto that record and filed the question of the earlier room as T-1700.

## 1. Every term from 1834 to 1835, and the room each source gives

| Term | Dates | Judge | Room | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| May 1834 | returnable 14 May 1834 | R. M. Young | **"the unfinished loft of the old Mansion House, just north of where the Tremont now stands"** | Caton, quoted by Andreas vol. I p. 421 ("First Chicago Divorce") |
| Fall 1834 | "the first day of October, 1834" (Caton) | R. M. Young | **"an unfinished store, about twenty feet by forty, on Dearborn Street, between Lake and Water"** | Andreas vol. I p. 421 ("First Murder Trial") |
| **May 1835** | **25 May – 9 June 1835** | **Sidney Breese**, by exchange with Thomas Ford | **not named** | Andreas vol. I p. 427 ("Circuit Court, 1835-36") |
| Fall 1835 | first Monday of October – 11 October 1835 | Stephen T. Logan, by exchange with Ford | not named | Andreas vol. I p. 427-428 |

Andreas gives the terms one paragraph each and names a room for the two 1834 terms only. For the
spring 1835 term he gives the judge, the dates, the growth of the docket and the first meeting of
the Chicago bar (on Chief Justice Marshall's death, 6 July 1835), and no room.

  archive.org text of `historyofchicago01andr`. **Silent on the room.**
- **Moses & Kirkland, *History of Chicago* (1895)**, both volumes, committed at

## 3. Why neither 1834 room is carried forward to May 1835

There are two candidates, and the town holds a record for one of them (`mansion_house`). Neither
is adopted:

1. **Nobody says the court went back to either room.** The court sat in two different rooms in
   its first two Chicago terms. That is evidence that it had no fixed room, and it is no
   evidence that it returned to either one.
2. **The Mansion House changed hands in 1835.** Andreas p. 635 has Haddock keeping it "until 1835
   when it passed into the hands of Abram A. Markle". A tavern loft lent once in May 1834 is not
   evidence that the next keeper lent it a year later.
3. **The Dearborn Street store was "unfinished" in October 1834.** A store finished over the
   winter is a store, let to whoever took it. The town also holds no record
   that can be identified with it. "About twenty feet by forty, on Dearborn Street, between Lake
   and Water" names a block face and a size, and no lot.
4. **The order's phrase does not pick between them.** "The Court House at Chicago" in December 1834
   was written two months after the October term sat in the Dearborn store, and while the county
   had no seal (the clerk sealed the order with his private seal). It most plausibly names
   whatever room the court was using. It is not evidence of a building called the court house,
   and it does not say which room the next term would use.

Writing either candidate onto a record would be an identity ruling no source makes. Under rule
2 of AGENTS.md the gap is recorded as a gap.

## 4. Two things the reading turned up, recorded and not resolved

- **The term did not open on the day the order named.** The order summons Welch for "the
  fourteenth day of May next", Thursday 14 May 1835. Andreas opens the term on Monday 25 May. The
  *American*'s OCR reads "the 26th", which may be a misreading of 25. The likeliest cause is the
  January 1835 act that moved Cook County into Ford's new Sixth Circuit, under which this was "the
  first [term] in Chicago after it became part of the Sixth Circuit". The act's text is not held here,
  so that cause is stated as the likeliest and not as fact.
- **Caton and Andreas disagree on the year of the first term.** Caton (1893) dates the first term
  of the Cook Circuit Court to May 1833. Andreas, quoting Caton's own recollection of "that term in
  May, 1834", and Moses & Kirkland (vol. II p. 153: "tried in the spring of 1834") put it in 1834.
  This bears on no record here. It is noted because the next reader of Caton will meet it.

## 5. What would reopen it

The Cook County Circuit Court record book for the May 1835 term, which burned in 1871 (Moses &
Kirkland vol. II p. 153), or a contemporary notice that names the room. The places to look are a
summons or a notice to jurors in the May 1835 issues of the *Chicago Democrat*, which are not
committed here, and the *Chicago American* of 6 June 1835 or earlier. Either one would put a named
room in front of `mansion_house` or a Dearborn Street record, and it would decide this. Until
then the c006 unit stays spent in the aggregate, as T-1591 left it.
