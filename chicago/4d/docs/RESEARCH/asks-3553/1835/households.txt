# Questions for the women, young people and children of Chicago on 1 July 1835: wives, ladies,
# servants, laundresses, needlewomen, boarding-house keepers, the schoolteacher, the little ones.
# Reconstruction, liberty L-tales-3553.

## a35-tw-arrive
roles: townswoman
q: What did you think when you first saw Chicago?
meet: kinzie_juliette

I cried. I will not pretend otherwise. We came in on the schooner in a grey rain and there was the fort, and a few dozen houses, and mud as far as the eye could see. My husband said it was the finest town in the West. I asked him where it was.

?? Do you like it now?
I like the people. Every woman in this town came from somewhere else and left somebody behind, so we look after each other. Mrs. Kinzie, the agent's wife, came out in thirty-one and wrote home that it was a wilderness, and now she gives the best suppers in Chicago. Go and see her. She will tell you how she crossed the prairie in winter on horseback.

?? What do you miss?
My mother's garden in Connecticut. Apple trees. A paved street. A church with a bell. A shop with a proper ribbon counter. Silence. You cannot get any silence here: hammers all day and fiddles all night.

## a35-tw-prices
roles: townswoman, lady
q: How much does it cost to keep house here?

Everything comes on a boat and the boat sets the price. Flour twelve dollars the barrel this spring, when the boats were late; eggs dear; butter dearer, and half of it rancid by the time it gets here. A woman with a cow and a hen yard is a rich woman, whatever her husband is.

?? What do you buy?
Calico, tea, sugar, candles, pins, and whatever the store has that it did not have last week. Mr. Gale has books and paper and, once, a parcel of Boston magazines that every lady in town read in a single week and passed round until they fell apart. Everybody watches the landing for the next lot.

## a35-tw-washing
roles: townswoman, laundress, domestic
q: How do you do the washing here?

Carry the water from the river or the lake, heat it in a copper over the fire, scrub on a board with soft soap, rinse, wring, and hang it out where the mud will not splash it, which is nowhere. Monday is wash day. Monday is also when everybody's husband brings home the mud of a week.

?? Is the water clean?
Clean enough for washing if you take it from the lake, not the river. The river has the slaughterhouse and the tannery in it, and everything from the town. A girl who boils the river water for drinking is wiser than one who does not.

## a35-tw-church
roles: townswoman, lady
q: Is there much society here?

More than you would think. There are sewing circles at the churches, a ladies' society for the poor, suppers at the Kinzies', a ball now and then at the Tremont or the Sauganash. In winter, sleigh rides on the river ice and on the prairie. A girl can dance until two in this town and be up to milk the cow at five.

?? Who leads it?
Mrs. Kinzie, for the old families. The ministers' wives, for the churches. Mrs. Porter, who was Miss Chappel and ran the school until she married the minister last month. She has more friends than anyone and fewer enemies.

## a35-tw-husband
roles: townswoman
q: What does your husband do?

Whatever pays, this year. He came as a carpenter, then he was a clerk, then he took a contract on the harbour, and now he is in land. I married a carpenter. I am not sure what I have now. He comes home talking about lots on Wolf Point and Wabash Avenue as if they were cows we owned.

?? Are you worried?
I keep a little money of my own in a stocking in the flour barrel, in silver. If the land goes up I will spend it on a new dress. If it goes down I will spend it on bread. Either way I sleep better.

## a35-tw-sick
roles: townswoman, lady, domestic
q: What do you do when your children are sick?

Pray, and send for the doctor if we can pay him, and if we cannot, ask a neighbour. There are good doctors here now: Kimberly, Egan, Maxwell at the fort. For ague, quinine; for a cough, a mustard plaster; for the summer flux, boiled water and arrowroot and a great deal of sitting up at night.

?? Have you lost anyone?
A baby, in our first summer. Most women here have. We put the little ones in the ground on the lake shore and then on the new burying ground and we go home and cook supper. That is what a woman does. You do not stop.

## a35-tw-lady-house
roles: lady
q: Is it hard to find a servant here?
meet: rc_flynn_mary

The hardest thing in Chicago. A good girl comes off the boat in the morning and by night she has three offers of marriage and a better position. We pay twice what they pay in New York, and still they leave. My Irish girl is the fourth this year, and I would sooner lose my husband than her.

?? Where do they come from?
Ireland, mostly, by Quebec or New York, and some Yankee farm girls from the East, and a few from the old French families. Mary Flynn is the best girl in town. She keeps a family's silver as if it were her own. Go and ask her how she likes it here. She may tell you more than she tells her mistress.

## a35-tw-lady-dress
roles: lady, needlewoman
q: Where do ladies get their dresses?

From the dressmakers in town, of whom there are now three or four, and the best is a French girl, Archange Laframboise, whose family has been here since before the soldiers. She makes over the New York fashions from the magazines, with sleeves as big as hams. You cannot go through a door sideways in them.

?? Are they practical?
Not at all. A lady in Chicago lifts her skirts six inches at every crossing and loses a slipper in the mud every week. But she will not be outdressed by a lady in Detroit. Miss Laframboise will measure you before you can say no.

## a35-tw-needle
roles: needlewoman
q: How's business for a dressmaker?

More than I can do. Every boat brings ladies who need gowns let out from the journey, and widows who need mourning, and brides who need everything at once. I sew from six in the morning till the candle gives out. My fingers are like a pincushion.

?? Who is your best customer?
The land men's wives. A man makes a thousand dollars on a lot and his wife wants a silk the same week. I sew silk for the rich and calico for the rest and mend for everybody. Whatever the town is doing, it shows up first in the sleeves.

## a35-tw-boarding
roles: boarding_keeper
q: How many people live in your house?

Twenty-two this week, and nine more on the floor when the boat came in Tuesday. I cook three meals a day for all of them, with one girl to help and my husband to carry the water. They pay two dollars a week, when they pay. Some of them leave in the night with their bags out the window.

?? What do you feed them?
Pork, potatoes, bread, beans, coffee and pie. Fish when the boys catch it. Whatever is cheap at the landing that morning. They complain, and they come back for more, and they tell their friends. A full table is the best advertisement.

?? Who's your strangest boarder?
A gentleman who says he is a poet and pays in verses. I have accepted two. They were not very good. He is behind four weeks now, and I have stopped reading them.

## a35-tw-domestic
roles: domestic
q: Do you like your work?
meet: rc_flynn_mary

It is work. I rise at five, light the fires, carry water, cook breakfast for the family, wash, scrub, iron, cook dinner, mend, cook supper, wash up, and go to bed in the little room behind the kitchen. On Sunday afternoon I walk out. On Thursday evening I may go to Mass.

?? Will you stay?
Until I marry, and a girl here can marry any week she likes. Mary Flynn says she will stay with her mistress because her mistress trusts her with the silver. I say a girl should trust herself first. Go and ask Mary. We disagree about everything, and we are best friends.

## a35-tw-irish-girl
roles: irish
sex: f
q: Where's home for you?

Galway, by way of Quebec and a summer in Buffalo. My brothers are on the canals in New York. My mother is still in the cottage with the little ones. I send her a dollar in every letter. The postmaster knows my writing by now.

?? Do you miss it?
I miss the sea and the singing and the Sundays. Here the Mass is in a little wooden church, Saint Mary's, and the priest is French, Father St. Cyr, and half the people are French. But the prayers are the same, and that is something. Go and hear him say Mass. He has a lovely voice, though his English makes us laugh.

?? Will you marry here?
If the right man asks. There are ten men in this town for every girl, so the asking is not the trouble. The choosing is.

## a35-tw-laundress
roles: laundress
q: Whose washing do you take in?

The fort's, some of it, and the Tremont's sheets, and a few gentlemen's shirts. I have a copper, two tubs, a board, a mangle I bought with my first year's money and a line strung right across the yard. On a good drying day I can do six baskets.

?? Is it good money?
Better than service, because I am my own mistress. Worse for the hands. Look at them. The lye eats the skin and the cold water cracks it, and in the winter they bleed. But nobody tells me when to get up, except the washing.

## a35-tw-teacher
roles: teacher
q: How many pupils do you have?
meet: watkins_john

Thirty or forty in the winter, when there is nothing else for the children to do, and a dozen in the summer, when there is everything. Ages five to fifteen, in one room, on benches, with slates and whatever books their parents brought with them. I teach the alphabet in one corner and fractions in the other at the same time.

?? Who was the first teacher here?
Mr. Watkins, in thirty-two, in a little log room, with a dozen scholars, half of them French. Then Miss Chappel, who married Mr. Porter. Now there are schools all over town. Go and ask Mr. Watkins what the first day was like. He says the children taught him more than he taught them.

?? What's the hardest part?
Keeping the boys in school when a boat comes in. They hear the whistle and they are gone, out the window and down to the landing to carry bags for pennies. I have learned to stop the lesson and go with them. We do our arithmetic on the bags.

## a35-tw-youth-girl
roles: youth
sex: f
q: What do girls do for fun here?

Walk out on the lake shore on a Sunday afternoon in our best bonnets and pretend not to look at the officers from the fort. Pick strawberries on the prairie in June. Go to the singing school at the church on Thursday. In winter, skate on the river, and sleigh, and dance until the fiddler falls asleep.

?? Is there someone you like?
I am not telling you! He is a clerk at a store on Lake Street, and he has red hair, and that is all you will get out of me. My mother says he has no prospects. Every clerk in this town has prospects. That is what this town is for.

## a35-tw-youth-boy
roles: youth
sex: m
q: What's the most exciting thing that's happened here?

When the Potawatomi danced through town at the treaty, painted and singing, going down Lake Street and past the houses, and everybody standing in the doorways not sure whether to be afraid. That was a story they still tell about two summers ago, at the treaty. People say there will be another when the payment comes.

?? Were you there?
I was at the treaty, two years ago, when I was small, and I remember all the people camped round the town, thousands, and the smoke, and the horses. My father said it was the last time we would see them like that. Go down by the river and look. Some of them are here now.

## a35-tw-singing
roles: townswoman, youth
q: Is there music in town?
meet: beaubien_mark

Every night, somewhere. Mark Beaubien plays the fiddle at the Sauganash and anybody who can dance does. There is a singing school at the Presbyterian church for the hymns. The soldiers at the fort have a drummer and a fifer. And the French families sing old songs at their doors in the evening that nobody else understands.

?? What's the best dance?
A reel at the Sauganash, when Mark is in the mood and the floor is full and somebody's husband has to hold the candles because there is no room for a table. They say he never plays the same tune twice. They say he never stops till the last guest falls down.

## a35-ch-mud
roles: child
q: Have you ever got stuck in the mud?

Every day! Yesterday I lost a shoe on Clark Street. It is still there. My brother says someday they will dig it up and put it in a museum. Once a pig got stuck in front of our house and the whole street came out to watch.

?? Did they get the pig out?
Three men and a plank and a rope. The pig got out and the men fell in. My mother said not to laugh. I laughed until I fell down. Then I was stuck too.

## a35-ch-boats
roles: child
q: Do you watch the boats come in?

When the bell goes or somebody shouts sail, everybody runs to the river. I can tell the schooners apart by their sails. When a steamboat comes, it blows smoke and has a big wheel on the side that splashes, and all the dogs bark.

?? What comes off them?
People, mostly, with trunks and babies and bundles, looking at the mud. And barrels and boxes and a piano once. They carried it up the street on poles and a lady played it right there on the landing. Everybody clapped.

## a35-ch-school
roles: child
q: Do you go to school?

I did, to Miss Chappel, but now she is Mrs. Porter and she is married so she is not my teacher any more. Now I go to a man who smells of tobacco and has a stick. I miss Miss Chappel. She gave us a ribbon if we said our lessons right.

?? What did you learn?
My letters, and to count to a hundred, and the names of the states, and a hymn. And that the earth goes round the sun. My grandfather does not believe it. He says he would feel it.

## a35-ch-indians
roles: child
q: Do you know any Potawatomi children?

Some come with their mothers to sell berries and baskets. One girl showed me how to make a doll from corn husks. We could not talk, so we did it with our hands. Then her mother called her and they went back to the camp by the river.

?? Are they leaving?
My father says they have to go west across the big river because of the treaty. I asked why. He said because the government says so. I asked why again and he said go and play. So I am asking you.

## a35-ch-pets
roles: child
q: Do you have any pets?

A cat, a dog, a pig that is not supposed to be a pet but is, and a crow that my brother found with a broken wing. The crow says one word. It sounds like Mother. My mother does not think that is funny.

?? What's the pig's name?
Andrew Jackson. My father named him. My mother says it is not respectful. My father says the pig does not mind.

## a35-ch-fort
roles: child
q: Have you been inside the fort?
meet: greene_john

Once, with my father, to see the soldiers drill. They all move at the same time like one big animal. There is a cannon on the blockhouse and when they fire it at sunset everything shakes and the birds all fly up.

?? Who is in charge?
The major. He has a sword and a horse and he does not smile. My father says he is a good man really. My father says the fort will go soon, because the town does not need protecting any more. I asked from what. He did not say.

## a35-ch-strawberries
roles: child
q: What's the best thing to eat here?

Wild strawberries on the prairie in June, so many your hands go red. And plums in the fall. And maple sugar from the Potawatomi in spring. And fish my father catches off the pier. And the pie at the Sauganash, but only once, because it costs too much.

?? Where do you find the strawberries?
Out past the last houses on the prairie, where the grass is low. You lie down on your stomach and eat them right off the plant. My mother sends us with a pail and we come home with a pail half full and our stomachs very full.

## a35-ch-french
roles: child, canadien
age: -15
q: Do you speak French?

My grandmother speaks only French and Potawatomi, and my father French and English, and I speak all three, but my English is best because of school. At home we say grace in French. At school I am not allowed to.

?? Teach me a word?
Bonjour. That is hello. And bouette, that is mud, the way my grandmother says it, and it is the most important word in Chicago. She says it ten times a day when we come in.

## a35-ch-wolf
roles: child
q: Have you seen a wolf?

I heard one! At night, from our house at the edge of town. It went ow-ow-oooo and then another one answered. My mother shut the window. My brother said it was coming to eat me. I hit him. Then I could not sleep.

?? Did you ever see one?
In the daytime, far away on the prairie, a grey thing running. My father said it was a prairie wolf and it was more afraid of us than we were of it. I do not think he is right. It did not look afraid.

## a35-to-boat
roles: toddler
q: Do you like the boats?

Boat! Big boat. It goes toot. I was on a boat. A long time. I was sick.

?? Where did you come from?
From far. From grandma. She is not here. She is on the other side of the water.

## a35-to-mud
roles: toddler
q: Why are you so muddy?

I was jumping. In the big puddle. Splash! My mother is cross.

?? Was it fun?
Yes! I want to do it again. Do not tell.

## a35-to-dog
roles: toddler
q: Is that your dog?

He is mine. He licks my face. He is big. Bigger than me.

?? What's his name?
Bowser. No, Rowser. No. I do not know. He is Dog.

## a35-to-bell
roles: toddler
q: Do you hear the bell?

Ding ding! At church. It is loud. I put my hands on my ears.

?? Do you go to church?
I go. I sit. I have to be quiet. It is hard.

## a35-to-cake
roles: toddler
q: What's your favourite thing?

Cake. And my blanket. And Papa. Papa is first. Then cake.

?? Did you have cake today?
No. Tomorrow. It is a birthday. Not mine. But I can have some.

## a35-to-cat
roles: toddler
q: Can you count?

One, two, five, ten! I can count cats. One cat. Another cat. Two cats.

?? How many cats do you have?
One. But she is going to have more. My mother says. In the box.
