# Questions for the garrison of the second Fort Dearborn, the Potawatomi waiting on the 1833
# treaty's payment and removal, and the old French and Metis families, on 1 July 1835.
# Reconstruction, liberty L-tales-3553. Nothing is said that the scene could not yet know.

## a35-so-pay
roles: soldier
q: What does a soldier earn here?
meet: greene_john

Six dollars a month and rations, paid when the paymaster comes, which is when he feels like it. A carpenter in town makes a dollar and a half a day. You can do the sum. Every man in this barracks does it every night.

?? Why stay, then?
Because a man who runs is a deserter and they put his name in the papers with a reward. Because the army feeds you when the town does not. Because I signed. The major knows what the town does to his men. He cannot pay us more and he cannot build a wall high enough. Go and ask him how many he lost this spring.

?? What would you do instead?
Carpentry. I can frame a wall as well as any man in Chicago. My enlistment is up next year. Then I shall be in town with a hammer and a lot of my own, if there are any lots left.

## a35-so-beaubien
roles: soldier, officer
q: Is it true someone bought the fort?
meet: beaubien_jean_baptiste

Colonel Beaubien went into the land office this spring and entered the whole fort reservation in his name, as the man living on it, for less than a hundred dollars. He has a receipt. The major has a fort. Washington has a headache. The lawyers in town have never been happier.

?? Can he do that?
He says the law lets a settler buy the land he lives on, and he has lived on it longer than any of us have been soldiers. The major says a fort is not a farm. I say I would like to see the Colonel try to evict a company of infantry. Go and ask him how he means to do it. He will laugh and offer you a glass.

## a35-so-sentry
roles: soldier
q: What do you watch for on sentry at night?

Fire, mostly. A whole town of wooden houses outside the walls, and every one of them has a stovepipe through a roof. After that, soldiers coming back from the taverns over the pickets, and townsmen trying to sell whiskey through the gate. The enemy is not out on the prairie any more. He is on Lake Street with a jug.

?? Is it dull?
The lake at night is not dull. You stand on the blockhouse and the water is black and the light at the harbour mouth is the only thing out there. On a calm night you hear the waves on the sand and the frogs in the slough and the fiddle at the Sauganash. On a stormy one you hear nothing but the wind.

## a35-so-flag
roles: soldier
q: How many stars are on that flag?

Twenty-four, one for every state, Missouri the last. Thirteen stripes. We raise it at dawn and strike it at sunset with the gun, and the whole town sets its clocks by us. It is the one thing about this fort that nobody has bought yet.

?? Will there be more stars?
Michigan wants one, and Arkansas, and they say Michigan and Ohio nearly went to war this spring over a strip of land at Toledo. Imagine it: two states fighting over a swamp. Out here we would sell them a better swamp for less.

## a35-so-black-hawk
roles: soldier
q: Were you here for the Black Hawk war?

I came in with General Scott's army in thirty-two, on the steamboats from the East, and the cholera came with us. We lost more men to it on the way here and in this fort than the whole war lost to the Sauk. They buried them by the lake.

?? Did you fight Black Hawk?
I never saw him. By the time we were well enough to march, the militia had chased him across Wisconsin to the Mississippi and it was over. I saw his people afterwards, though, and that was enough. Starving women. Children with no shoes. I do not boast about that summer.

## a35-so-fishing
roles: soldier
q: What do you do off duty?

Fish off the pier. Whitefish, perch, pike, now and then a sturgeon big enough to feed the company. The officers hunt ducks in the slough. The men play cards and drink if they can get it, and walk out with the town girls if they can get that. Mostly they can get the fish.

?? Any good spots?
The end of the north pier, early, when the water is flat. A sergeant showed me. He has fished this river mouth since the fort was rebuilt, and he swears the perch know his voice. Bring a line and come at dawn and I will show you.

## a35-so-girl
roles: soldier
q: Do the soldiers marry town girls?

Some do. A private needs his captain's leave and he will not get it unless the girl can wash for the company. There are more men in this town than women by three to one, so a girl can choose, and she does not choose a private at six dollars a month if she can get a carpenter.

?? Have you anyone?
There is a girl at the boarding house on Lake Street who gives me an extra potato. That is all I am saying. In Chicago an extra potato is practically a proposal.

## a35-so-mackinac
roles: soldier
q: Where were you posted before?

Fort Brady, at the Sault, at the top of Lake Huron, where the winter lasts eight months and the snow comes over the pickets. Before that Fort Howard at Green Bay. This is the softest post I have had: a town at the gate, fresh beef, and the boats in all summer.

?? Do you prefer it?
I prefer it to the Sault. But at the Sault a man knew what he was there for. Here the fort guards nothing but itself. The Potawatomi are going west, the town has three thousand people and its own sheriff. The word is that they will close the fort and send us off before long. I believe it.

## a35-so-band
roles: soldier
q: Who plays the drum at the fort?

A boy of sixteen from Ohio and a fifer who used to be a sailor. They play the calls all day and the town knows every one: reveille, breakfast, drill, dinner, retreat, tattoo. A merchant on Water Street told me he does not need a clock any more, he has the fort.

?? Do they ever play for fun?
On the Fourth of July they play everything they know, in the square, and the town dances. That is three days from now. Come and see it. There will be a salute from the gun and speeches from every lawyer in town and the soldiers will be very thirsty by evening.

## a35-off-greene
roles: officer
q: What's it like being an officer here?
meet: allen_lieut_james

Strange. I have a commission, a sword, and a company of men who would rather be carpenters. The town invites me to every supper and every ball because an officer in a blue coat makes a room look respectable. The rest of the time I write reports nobody reads.

?? Who is the busiest officer?
Lieutenant Allen, who builds the harbour. He is the only one of us doing something the town will remember. He is out on the pier every morning with his pole, measuring the depth of the channel. Go and ask him how deep it is today. He will know to the inch.

## a35-pm-treaty-side
roles: potawatomi_man
q: What did your people get in the treaty?
meet: caldwell_billy

Land west of the Mississippi, as much as we give up here, they say. Money every year for twenty years. Goods. Blankets. A payment for the traders our people owe. And the promise that we go west within three years. Billy Caldwell and Alexander Robinson and the other chiefs signed for the united bands two summers ago.

?? Was it fair?
Ask a man who has lost his country if a price was fair. The Americans would have taken it anyway. That is what the chiefs said: take what you can get, while you can get it. Some of our people say Caldwell sold us. Others say he saved what could be saved. Go and ask him. He will tell you his side.

?? Will you go west?
This autumn or next spring. My father's grave is on the Des Plaines. My wife's mother is buried by the lake. My son was born at the Calumet. I will take them with me in my mouth, as names, since I cannot take them any other way.

## a35-pm-payment
roles: potawatomi_man, potawatomi_woman
q: Why are so many camps by the river?

People have come from all the villages for the payment, for the money and the goods the treaty promised. They say it will be soon. We have been waiting a long time. While we wait, the whiskey sellers come to the camps every night, and the traders come with their books to say what everybody owes.

?? Do they owe a lot?
More than they ever had, by the traders' books. Every trader in Chicago has a claim, and the claims are paid out of our money before we see it. A man may come away from the payment with a blanket and a hangover and nothing in his hand. That is how it was at the last one.

## a35-pm-town
roles: potawatomi_man, potawatomi_elder
q: What do you think of the town?

When I was young there were the fort, the Kinzie house, the Ouilmette house, a few French cabins and a great deal of grass. Now there are houses as far as you can see, and noise, and every man walking very fast. The river is full of boats. The prairie is full of men with chains measuring it.

?? Is anything the same?
The lake. The lake does not care. It was here when the Miami had this country and it will be here when the Americans have gone. In the morning I go down to the shore and look at it, and for a while the town behind me is quiet.

## a35-pm-shabbona
roles: potawatomi_man
q: Is there a chief who is a friend to the settlers?

Shabbona, of the Ottawa and Potawatomi, from the Fox River country. In the Black Hawk war he rode through the settlements day and night to warn the settlers that Black Hawk's men were coming, and many lived because of him. Some of our own people have never forgiven him.

?? Was that right?
He thought so. He had fought beside Tecumseh once, and he had seen what war against the Americans brought. He chose the settlers' lives over Black Hawk's war. A man can be right and still lose his friends. Shabbona knows that better than anyone.

## a35-pm-horses
roles: potawatomi_man
q: Will you take your horses west?

Every one. A man without a horse in that country is a man who walks behind other men. I have three: a mare for my wife, a strong one for the packs, and my own, who is fast and does not like Americans. He bit a trader last week. I was very proud of him.

?? What's the country like out west?
I have not seen it. Some went to look last year and came back saying it is grass with no trees and no sugar maples and the Sioux and the Osage close by. Some say it is good land. They always say the next land is good land.

## a35-pw-baskets
roles: potawatomi_woman
q: What do you sell in town?

Baskets of ash splint and sweetgrass, mats, moccasins with beadwork, maple sugar in birch-bark cones, berries in the season. The town women buy them and pay with flour or cloth or a few coins. Some of them are kind. Some of them count the coins twice before they give them.

?? Is it enough to live?
Not by itself. With the hunting and the corn and the payment, yes, barely. When we go west there will be no town to sell to for a long while. I am making as many mats as I can, to take with us. A lodge needs mats wherever it stands.

## a35-pw-daughter
roles: potawatomi_woman
q: Do your children go to the town school?

My daughter went for a while, to the little school by the church, to learn the Americans' letters. She can write her name and read a little. The other girls stared at her. The teacher was kind. I wanted her to be able to read the papers the Americans make us sign.

?? Will she keep it up?
In the west, who will teach her? I will ask her to teach her brothers. A people who can read the treaty can argue with it. That is something.

## a35-pc-town
roles: potawatomi_child
q: Do you like coming to town?

The town is loud and smells bad and has too many people. But there are things to see: the boats, the soldiers drilling, the horses, the store with the shiny things in the window. The boys throw things at us sometimes. Sometimes they share their candy.

?? What's your favourite thing in town?
The bridge that goes up. We watched it go up for a boat and everybody on both sides was shouting. My brother says it is a trick. I think it is the best thing in the town.

## a35-pc-west
roles: potawatomi_child
q: Are you going on a journey?

My mother says we are going far, across a big river, to a new place. We will walk and ride for many days. I asked if my dog can come. She said yes. Then I asked if the lake can come. She did not answer.

?? Are you sad?
I do not know. My grandmother is sad. My father is quiet. My cousins say it will be an adventure. I will be sad if there are no strawberries.

## a35-me-between
roles: metis
q: Which world do you belong to?
meet: robinson_alexander

Both, and so neither, completely. My father was French, my mother Potawatomi. I can sit in council with the chiefs and at table with the agent. The treaty made a list of people like me, the mixed bloods, and gave us money and reservations of our own. Some people in the camps say that was our price.

?? What will you do?
Stay. I have a house and a reservation and a trade. Alexander Robinson is staying on his land on the Des Plaines, and he signed the treaty, and his people still come to his door. Go and ask him how it feels to stay when your relatives are going. He will tell you honestly. It will not make you feel better.

## a35-cd-voyageur
roles: canadien
q: Were you a voyageur?
meet: beaubien_jean_baptiste

Twenty years with the brigades, from Mackinac to the Illinois, paddling and carrying and singing. A voyageur's back is gone at forty, but his voice is the last thing to go. Now I work for Colonel Beaubien and sing in the evenings outside his door.

?? Who is the Colonel?
Jean Baptiste Beaubien, a militia colonel and the oldest trader in the town, after the Kinzies. He has two families and twenty children and the whole fort reservation, if the government lets him keep it. He is the most generous man in Chicago and the noisiest. Go and see him. He will kiss you on both cheeks.

## a35-cd-french-families
roles: canadien
q: Are there many French families left?

The Beaubiens, the Laframboises, the Ouilmettes, the Mirandeaus, a few more. Before the Yankees came we were the town. Now we are the old part of the town. They cannot say our names and they buy our land at prices that make us laugh, and then they sell it at prices that make us cry.

?? Do you mind?
I mind that they think nothing was here before them. My grandfather had a cabin by the river when this was a portage and a few lodges. The Yankees call it a wilderness. It was full of people. They were just not Yankees.

## a35-cd-songs
roles: canadien
q: What are you singing?

À la claire fontaine, the song every Frenchman on these rivers knows. My father sang it paddling. His father sang it paddling. I sing it now with a hammer. It is about a man who has lost his love for a rose he did not give her. All our songs are about love, even the ones about work.

?? Can you sing it in English?
Ha! It does not go into English. The English is all corners. In French it is round, like water. Listen to it once in French and you will understand it without the words.
