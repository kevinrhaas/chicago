# The people of 1812 answer for themselves, on the evening of 14 August. Each is asked first
# what only they could tell; their own stories (tales-3553/1812/personal.txt) are not retold.
# Reconstruction, liberty L-tales-3553: what is known of each life, in a voice of our making.

## a12-kinzie-silver
for: kinzie_john_elder
q: Is it true they call you the silversmith?
meet: kinzie_eleanor

Shawneeawkee. I came to this country as a boy apprentice to a silversmith in Quebec, and I ran off from that, but the trade stayed in my hands. When I first came among the Potawatomi I made brooches and ear bobs and arm bands, and they named me for it. I still make a brooch now and then, for a wife or a daughter in the camps.

?? Do you still have your tools?
In a chest in the boat. My wife packed them herself and did not let me see where. She says I would spend the whole journey polishing something instead of watching the shore. She is right, as usual. Go and ask her what else she packed. You will be surprised.

?? Which brooch are you proudest of?
One I made for Black Partridge's wife, years ago, a big round one with a ring of little stars cut through it. I saw her wearing it at the council two days ago. Silver lasts longer than friendship, it seems. I hope not.

## a12-kinzie-house
for: kinzie_john_elder
q: Why is your house on the north side of the river?
meet: ouilmette_antoine

Because it was there when I bought it. A Frenchman named Le Mai had it, and before him Point de Sable, the man from Saint-Domingue, the first who ever really lived here. I bought the house and its poplars and its bake oven in 1804, the same year the soldiers came to build the fort across the water.

?? Did you know Point de Sable?
I met him, a tall man with a big laugh and a Potawatomi wife, Catherine. He had a house, a mill, a smokehouse, a dairy, cattle, all of it here before any American. He sold out and went to Peoria and then further down the river. If you ever hear someone say the Americans founded this place, tell them about him. Antoine Ouilmette knew him well. Go and ask.

?? Will you come back to it?
I will come back to this house if I have to crawl. My wife's garden is here. My trade is here. The army can leave Chicago. I cannot.

## a12-heald-wound
for: heald_nathan
q: What will you do if they attack on the march?
meet: wells_william

Form the men, hold the ground, protect the wagons, and make a bargain if a bargain can be made. That is all an officer can do with fifty-four regulars, twelve militia, and a column of families on a beach between sand hills and the lake. Wells and his Miami will ride ahead and tell me what is coming.

?? Why not stay in the fort?
Because General Hull ordered me out. Because the stores would last longer than the friendship of the camps, and when the friendship ran out we would be a hundred people in a box with no help coming. Detroit has troubles enough. Nobody is marching to relieve Chicago. I may be wrong to go. I would certainly be wrong to stay.

?? What do you think of your wife's uncle?
That he knows these people better than any American alive, and that he does not think we will get through. He has not said so. He did not need to. He rode a hundred and fifty miles for his niece. Go and talk to him. If he says anything to you that he has not said to me, come back and tell me.

## a12-heald-kentucky
for: heald_nathan
q: Where are you from, Captain?

New Hampshire, a long way from here. I came into the army young, and went west because the army did. I married Rebekah Wells in Kentucky last year and brought her here on horseback through the woods. She thought it was an adventure. She still thinks so, I believe, though she says less about it.

?? Do you like this posting?
Chicago is a good post for a man who likes quiet and fishing. Until this spring I would have said I could serve here twenty years. Now I would give a good deal to see my wife safe in Kentucky, and I mean to do it by the shortest road there is.

## a12-rebekah-uncle
for: heald_rebekah
q: What is your uncle really like?
meet: wells_william

He is the most stubborn, gentle, frightening man I have ever known. When I was a girl in Kentucky he used to tell me stories about the Miami villages and his wife Sweet Breeze, and then stop in the middle and go out and walk around the yard by himself. I did not understand then that he was homesick for a place he had chosen to leave.

?? Did he say why he came?
He said: because you are here, girl. Then he asked my husband for the rolls and the stores and the plan of march, and they talked until midnight. I think he came to bring me out whether the army comes out or not. Go and look at him. He has been very quiet since he arrived.

?? What will you wear tomorrow?
My riding habit, the dark blue one. My rings and my money are sewn into my stays, and a little purse of gold is in my pocket for bargaining if it comes to that. My aunt taught me that. A woman in this country carries her ransom on her.

## a12-rebekah-cicely
for: heald_rebekah
q: Who is the woman who keeps your house?
meet: cicely

Cicely. My father gave her to me when I married, as he would give a horse or a featherbed. She came with us from Louisville and she has never once complained in my hearing. She has a little boy. I do not know who his father is, and I have not asked.

?? Is she frightened?
I have no idea what Cicely feels. I have lived with her a year and she has never told me anything she did not need to. That is the truth, and it does not make me proud. Go and talk to her if you want. She may tell you more than she tells me.

## a12-wells-name
for: wells_william
q: Why do the Miami call you Apekonit?
meet: heald_rebekah

It means wild carrot, for the colour of my hair when I was a boy. I was twelve or thirteen when a Miami war party took me from my family's cabin in Kentucky, and a chief adopted me, and by the time I was a man I was Miami in everything except my hair.

?? Did you ever want to go home?
My brother found me, years later, when I was grown with a Miami wife and a child. He talked to me for days. In the end I went back to Kentucky with him, and then I came back to the Miami, and then I went to the Americans for good. A man can belong to two peoples. What he cannot do is fight on both sides at once. I have tried.

?? What do you think will happen tomorrow?
I think I will paint my face black tomorrow. You know what that means, among the Miami? It means a man expects to die. I am telling you so you are not surprised. Do not tell my niece.

## a12-wells-sweet-breeze
for: wells_william
q: Tell me about your wife.

Wanagapeth, Sweet Breeze, Little Turtle's daughter. She died some years ago, at Fort Wayne. We had children together who are in school in Kentucky now, learning to be Americans. My second wife is the daughter of a Kentucky militia colonel. I have lived three or four lives, and in every one of them somebody I loved was on the other side of something.

?? Do your children know you are here?
I wrote to them before I left Fort Wayne. I told them I was bringing their cousin Rebekah home. I did not tell them anything else. A father does not need to say everything in a letter.

## a12-partridge-friendship
for: black_partridge
q: Why have you always been a friend to the Americans?
meet: kinzie_john_elder

Not always. When I was young I fought the Americans like everybody else. At Greenville I saw that we had lost the war, and I chose to keep the peace I signed. For seventeen summers I have kept it. I have eaten at the trader Kinzie's table and he has eaten at mine.

?? What will you do tomorrow?
What a man can do who has given back his medal. I cannot stop the young men. I can be close by. If there are people who can be saved, I will try to save them. Kinzie knows this. Go and tell him I said it again, so he hears it twice.

?? Why tell me?
Because a stranger who hears it might remember it later, when people ask what Black Partridge did. Somebody should remember the truth.

## a12-partridge-peoria
for: black_partridge
q: Where is your home?

At the lake the French call Peoria, on the Illinois River, many days south of here. My village is there, and my fields. I came up the Illinois and the Des Plaines for the council and for the goods, and because Kinzie and I are old friends and I wanted to see him before whatever comes.

?? Is it peaceful there?
The Americans in the south are building more cabins every year, and their militia go riding through our country looking for horse thieves. A people can only be called horse thieves so many times before some of them decide to steal horses. My village has been quiet. That is my doing, and it is getting harder.

## a12-winnemeg-road
for: winnemeg
q: How did you get through the woods with the letter?
meet: heald_nathan

Alone, on foot and in a canoe, from Detroit round the lake, sleeping in the day where the brush was thickest and moving at night where the trail was good. The letter was sewn inside my leggings. If anybody had found it, I would not be here talking to you.

?? Did anyone stop you?
Two young men from a village on the St. Joseph asked me where I was going. I told them I was going to visit my wife's mother. Every man in the world understands why someone would go slowly to his wife's mother. They let me pass laughing.

?? What did you tell the captain?
I told him: go now, tomorrow, before they gather. Or stay and fight from inside these walls. Either is better than waiting. He thanked me and waited. Go and ask him why. I asked and did not understand the answer.

## a12-topenebe-st-joseph
for: topenebe
q: Where are the St. Joseph villages?
meet: kinzie_john_elder

East of here, round the bottom of the lake, on the St. Joseph River, where the French had a fort and a mission long ago. My people have lived there for many lifetimes. It is good country: rich bottoms, sugar maples, sturgeon in the river in spring.

?? Who are your relatives among the traders?
William Burnett, the trader on the St. Joseph, married my sister Kakima. Their children are my nephews and nieces, half trader and half Potawatomi, like so many children on these rivers. Kinzie was Burnett's friend before he was mine. Go and ask him about the St. Joseph in the old days. He will tell you he was young and handsome. Both are lies.

?? Are you angry with the Americans?
I am old. I am angry with everyone and no one. The Americans take land and the British give presents and the young men want glory and the old men want peace. I have lived long enough to know that all of them will be disappointed.

## a12-waubansee-fox
for: waubansee
q: Why do people say you are careful?
meet: chandonnai_jean_baptiste

Because I count before I act. A war leader who is not careful soon leads nobody, because they are all dead. I have a small band. I cannot afford to lose ten men for the honour of being first into a fight.

?? Will you be in a fight tomorrow?
I have not decided. I am listening. Chandonnai came to my fire last night and talked for a long time about the trader's family. I listened to him too. Go and find him if you want to know what he said. He will tell you more than I will.

?? Is it true about the geese?
Ha! Who told you that? The year I was a young man, the geese came down on the marsh by the portage so thick that the branches of the trees cracked under them and we had to shout to hear each other. My uncle said it was a sign. Of what, he never said. Of a good dinner, I thought.

## a12-keepotah-harrison
for: keepotah
q: You met General Harrison?
meet: kinzie_eleanor

Years ago, when he came west as governor, before he was a general against us. A tall, quiet man who wrote everything down. He asked about my village and my family and my hunting grounds, and he wrote that down too. I did not know then that a man who writes down your hunting grounds is counting land.

?? What will you do tomorrow?
I said I would stand by the Kinzies' boat, and I will. Mrs. Kinzie gave my wife a kettle when ours cracked in the winter. A man remembers a kettle.

## a12-naunongee-corn
for: naunongee
q: What happened in your cornfield?
meet: hays_otho

Some of the officers' wives came walking through my corn in the spring, before the trouble, laughing, pulling the young ears to look at them. I told them in my own language what I thought. One of them asked the interpreter what I had said. He told them something polite.

?? What did you really say?
Something a man says when he is angry, about which of them he would like to see in his lodge. I am not proud of it. The soldiers made a great story of it. Words said in anger in a cornfield have followed me all summer.

?? Do you know Sergeant Hays?
He mended my gun last winter and gave me a blanket, and I gave him a pair of moccasins. He is a decent man. He does not understand anything about us, but he is decent. Go and see him. Tell him I have not forgotten the gun.

## a12-blackbird-ask
for: blackbird
q: Why do you hate the Americans?
meet: main_poc_runner

I do not hate them. I want them gone. There is a difference, though perhaps not tomorrow. My people are from the Milwaukee River. We have seen the American surveyors with their chains. We have seen what happened to the Delaware and the Shawnee in Ohio. We know what comes after the chains.

?? Will you lead tomorrow?
I am one of the war leaders here. The young men are listening to me, and to Main Poc's runner, and to the British message from Mackinac. Ask the runner what Main Poc wants. Then you will know what tomorrow wants.

## a12-nuscotnemeg-name
for: nuscotnemeg
q: Why are you called the Mad Sturgeon?

Because the sturgeon is the biggest fish in the river and when he is angry he jumps right out of the water and comes down so hard you hear it a mile away. My people named me for it when I was young. I have done nothing since to make them change their minds.

?? What makes you angry?
The Americans at Fort Wayne gave out whiskey to buy land. The Americans in Indiana hanged two of my relatives for killing a man who had stolen their horses. The Americans here poured our whiskey into the river last night after promising it to us. Pick any one of those. I have more.

## a12-margaret-mother
for: helm_margaret
q: What is it like to be the trader's stepdaughter?
meet: kinzie_eleanor

I am Mrs. Kinzie's daughter by her first husband, so the trader is my stepfather, and Johnny and the little ones are my brothers and sisters. I grew up at Detroit and came here as a girl. I learned Potawatomi from the women who came to sell corn and French from the engagés. My English is the best of the three, my mother says, which is a pity because it is the least useful here.

?? Are you close to your mother?
We are alike, which means we argue. She was a captive among the Seneca when she was a girl. When I told her I would ride with my husband, she did not argue at all. That frightened me more than anything else this week. Go and see her. She is in the house, packing silver into a flour sack.

## a12-linai-wife
for: helm_linai
q: Are you worried about your wife?
meet: helm_margaret

Every minute. She is seventeen and she thinks a good horse and a pistol make a person safe. I told her to go in the boat with her mother. She looked at me as if I had told her to go in the boat with the baggage. We were married last year. I am still learning which arguments I can win.

?? What do you think of the captain's plan?
I think we should have stayed or gone the morning the letter came. I have said so, in the captain's quarters, with the door shut. Now it is said and he has decided, and I will march where I am put. The men watch the officers. They will not see me doubt.

## a12-eleanor-boat
for: kinzie_eleanor
q: How will you keep the children calm on the boat?
meet: laframboise_josette_1812

Josette will tell them stories in Ottawa and French until they fall asleep, and I will give the baby sugar on a rag, and John will sit in the stern and look as if nothing is the matter. Children believe a calm face more than any words. I learned that among the Seneca. When I was small and frightened, the women there would just keep working and humming, and after a while I stopped crying.

?? Do you ever think about the Seneca?
Every day this week. The old chief who adopted me was a kind man, and when my family bought me back I did not want to go. I know what it is to be on the other side of the fence from your own people. That is why I am not as afraid of the camps as I should be. That may be a mistake. Go and see Josette. She will tell you I am braver than I am.

## a12-johnny-word
for: kinzie_john_h_1812
q: What is the secret word Josette taught you?
meet: laframboise_josette_1812

I am not supposed to tell. It is a Potawatomi word and if a stranger comes near the boat I say it loud and he will know I am a relative of somebody. Josette made me say it a hundred times. I can say it in my sleep. My sister says I do say it in my sleep.

?? Are you excited about the boat?
I am going to sit right behind Jim in the bow and watch for snags. Father says the boat will go round by the lake shore and we will see the soldiers marching on the beach. I wanted to march with them. Father said no in the voice that means no. Go and ask Josette about my word. Maybe she will tell you. Maybe not.

?? What do you want to be?
A trader like Father. Or an interpreter like Chandonnai. Or a soldier with a sword. Mother says I have to learn to read properly first. I can read. I just do not like it.

## a12-chandonnai-languages
for: chandonnai_jean_baptiste
q: How many languages do you speak?
meet: waubansee

Potawatomi from my mother, French from my father, English from the trader's books, Ottawa and Ojibwe because they are almost the same, and enough Miami and Winnebago to buy a horse. Six, if you count the language of a man who owes you money, which is universal.

?? What did you tell Waubansee last night?
That the trader's family has fed his people for years and owes them nothing and is owed a great deal. That if the Kinzies are harmed, the traders will not come back to this river for a generation. That a careful man thinks about next winter as well as tomorrow. Go and ask him if he was listening. He was.

## a12-cicely-louisville
for: cicely
q: Where were you before Chicago?
meet: heald_rebekah

In Kentucky, at the Wells place near Louisville, in the kitchen, from the time I was a girl. Then Miss Rebekah married the captain and I was part of what she brought with her, like the linen and the silver. We came north on horseback through the woods. I carried my baby the whole way on my hip.

?? Is there anyone you miss?
My mother is still in Kentucky, in the same kitchen. I have not seen her since I left. Nobody thought to ask if I wanted to say goodbye. Miss Rebekah would have let me, I think, if I had asked. I did not ask. You learn not to.

## a12-black-jim-boat
for: black_jim
q: How do you know the lake so well?
meet: kinzie_john_elder

From rowing it for Mr. Kinzie, years now: up the shore to the Grosse Pointe and down round to the St. Joseph with trade goods. The lake looks flat and kind on a morning like this, and by afternoon it can stand up and throw you on the sand. You watch the sky over the water and you listen to the birds. When the gulls go inland, you go in too.

?? What is the boat like?
Heavy, flat, built for goods, not people. Tomorrow it will carry the whole family and Josette and the little ones and the trunks. I will be in the bow with an oar and, under the seat, something else. Mr. Kinzie will be on the shore by the boat at first, and then, he says, with the column. I wish he would stay in the boat. Tell him I said so. He listens to me more than he admits.

## a12-hays-sergeant
for: hays_otho
q: Why did you become a soldier?
meet: naunongee

I needed the bounty and the pay and the land warrant at the end. My father had seven sons and one farm. A sergeant came through our county with a drum and a promise of a hundred and sixty acres, and I signed before the drum stopped. That was a year ago last April.

?? Do you regret it?
Not the men. The men are good, mostly. I regret the stripes some days, because a sergeant has to make other men do things he would not want to do himself. Tonight I have to tell the privates which of them walk on the lake side of the column. Nau-non-gee would understand that. He has to tell his young men things too.

## a12-burns-orders
for: burns_thomas
q: What did the captain tell the militia?
meet: griffith_william

To stay with the wagons, keep together, and not fire unless fired on. Twelve of us, farmers and boatmen and a cooper, with muskets from the store. Griffith there was one of us until he signed on as a regular in April. He knows our company and the regulars both.

?? Will your family be safe?
My wife is in the wagon with our new baby and her other children. I will walk on the lake side of it so that anything coming from the sand hills has to come round me. That is the plan of a man who has never been in a battle. Go and ask Griffith what he thinks of it. He will be honest.

## a12-mrs-burns-baby
for: burns_mrs
q: How old is your baby?
meet: kinzie_eleanor

Only days. She came in the night, with Mrs. Kinzie holding the lamp and Mrs. Heald boiling the water. I am not strong yet. The surgeon says I must be carried tomorrow, so I will go in my own bed, lifted into the wagon by four soldiers like a queen in a story.

?? What will you call her?
I have not decided. My husband wants a Bible name. I want something for this place, though I do not know what. If we get to Fort Wayne I will think of one. Go and thank Mrs. Kinzie for me, will you? I do not think I said it properly at the time.

## a12-lee-farm-ask
for: lee_charles
q: Will you ever go back to Hardscrabble?
meet: kelso_john

People call it Hardscrabble now, because the ground is so hard to break. I call it Lee's farm. I built the house and the barn and fenced the fields and drove the first plough on the South Branch. In April I lost two men there and I have not been back.

?? What would you do if you went back?
Bury what the army did not bury. Look for my cattle. See if the barn stands. Then sit down on the doorstep for a while. A man ought to say goodbye to a place he built, even if he cannot stay. Kelso could tell you how it looked when he ran. I cannot ask him. Go and ask him for me, gently.

## a12-kelso-reenlist
for: kelso_john
q: Why did you rejoin the army?
meet: hays_otho

Because I could not sleep. Out of the army I was a man alone on a farm with nothing between him and the dark. In the army I am a private with a musket and a sergeant and a wall. It is not much, but it is something. Sergeant Hays saw me sign and did not ask any questions.

?? Was Lee's boy all right?
He was all right in his body. In the night he still sits up and says something about the gate. I sit up too, sometimes, in the barracks. Go and ask Sergeant Hays how many of us do. He does the rounds. He knows.

## a12-ronan-academy
for: ronan_george
q: What did they teach you at West Point?
meet: helm_linai

Mathematics, engineering, gunnery, French, and how to stand very straight while being shouted at. There are not many of us yet, maybe a hundred officers in the whole army who went there. We are supposed to be the new kind of officer: educated, professional, not some politician's nephew.

?? Do you disagree with the captain?
I disagree with marching out at all, and I have said so. If we march, I want it done with the men in good order and the drums going. If we must go, let us go like soldiers and not like a herd being driven. Lieutenant Helm and I are of one mind, though he says it quieter. Ask him.

## a12-van-voorhis-chest
for: van_voorhis_isaac
q: What's in your medicine chest?
meet: burns_mrs

Calomel, jalap, laudanum, quinine bark, some lint and bandages, a set of instruments I bought in New York, and a little book of remedies. It is not much against what may come. I spent the afternoon cutting bandages from a bolt of the factory's linen, and I have rolled them all and put them where I can reach them.

?? Who needs you most now?
Mrs. Burns, who is only days past her lying-in and must be carried. And the children with the summer flux. And every man in the garrison who has stopped sleeping. I cannot give a man sleep. I can tell him the medicine chest is ready, which is almost the same.

## a12-griffith-linden
for: griffith_william
q: What do the linden birds mean?
meet: black_partridge

It is what the chief said, when he came to me this summer: the linden birds have been singing in my ears. I did not understand until the interpreter told me. He meant he had heard something he could not say aloud, and he was telling me without telling me. A man can warn you in a way that he can deny later.

?? Did you tell the captain?
I told the captain, and the captain said that every chief in the country was hearing birds this summer. He was not wrong. But a friend's birds are different. Go and find the chief if you can. Tell him I heard.

## a12-corbin-wife
for: corbin_fielding
q: Why does your wife say she will never be taken?
meet: corbin_mrs

Because she has heard the stories of women carried off to the camps and never seen again, and because she is the kind of woman who says a thing once and means it forever. I married her for that. Tonight I wish she were a little less certain about everything.

?? Is your child born yet?
Not yet. Soon. She works as if she were not expecting at all: washing for the whole company, scrubbing the shirts on the board by the river. Go and see her, but do not tell her to rest. Three people have told her today. None of them will try again.

## a12-ouilmette-garden
for: ouilmette_antoine
q: What is growing in your garden?
meet: kinzie_john_elder

Corn, squash, beans, tobacco, a few rows of potatoes, and some French things my mother would recognise: sorrel, chives, a pear tree I grew from a pip I carried from Montreal in my pocket. It has borne pears for three years. This year they will be ripe in September, and I intend to be here to eat them.

?? Is Kinzie your friend?
I paddled his goods over the portage for years, and his before that for others. I do not always like him. He is quick with his fists and slow with his wages. But he is my neighbour, and on this river a neighbour is a relative. Go and tell him I will watch his house while he is gone. He knows. Tell him anyway.

## a12-archange-relations
for: ouilmette_archange
q: How many relatives do you have in the camps?
meet: ouilmette_antoine

Count the lodges. My mother's people are Potawatomi and they married into every village from here to the Kankakee. When I walk through the camps, every other woman calls me sister or aunt or daughter. That is why my husband thinks we are safe. He is right, I think, mostly.

?? Have they told you anything?
They tell me to stay home tomorrow and keep the children close and not go to the beach. They do not tell me why. They do not need to. I have told the Kinzies. What else can I do?

?? What are you cooking?
A kettle of corn and venison for whoever comes to my door tonight. Soldiers, relatives, strangers. Everyone is hungry on a night like this. Sit down. My husband will tell you about his pears. He tells everybody about his pears.

## a12-josette-ottawa
for: laframboise_josette_1812
q: How did you come to work for the Kinzies?
meet: kinzie_eleanor

My mother's people knew Mrs. Kinzie, and when she needed someone to mind the children who could speak to them the way the children of this river speak, they sent me. I am the girl in the house who is half French and half Ottawa and wholly in charge of the little ones.

?? Do the children mind you?
Johnny minds me when his father is watching. The little ones mind me always, because I tell the best stories. Mrs. Kinzie says I spoil them. Go and ask her if that is a complaint. She smiles when she says it.
