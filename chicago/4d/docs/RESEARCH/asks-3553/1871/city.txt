# Questions for the people of Chicago on Sunday afternoon, 8 October 1871 (T-3690). Reconstruction,
# liberty L-tales-3553. Nothing here is said that the city could not yet know.

## a71-gm-business
roles: gentleman, tradesman
q: How is business?

Never better, if you believe the Board of Trade, and they believe it themselves, which is what matters. The grain comes in on the railroads from the whole West and goes out by the lake, and every bushel of it pays somebody in this city a cent.

?? Will it last?
They said the same in fifty-seven, and the banks broke. I keep a little gold in the house. But a city of three hundred thousand that was a swamp forty years ago is not going to stop now.

?? What would stop it?
A war, a panic, or the Lord's own hand. I do not expect any of them before Christmas.

## a71-gm-dry
roles: gentleman, tradesman, workman, driver
q: Why is everything so dry?

No rain worth the name since the beginning of July. The wells are low, the grass is brown, and the wind has been blowing hot out of the southwest for a week. The leaves fell in September without turning.

?? Is that dangerous?
In a city built of pine? The sidewalks are pine, the houses are pine with tar roofs, and there are lumber yards and planing mills and hay barns everywhere. There was a fire nearly every night this week. Last night's burned four blocks on the West Side.

?? What do the firemen say?
That they are tired. They worked all night and half the day on that fire and some of the hose was burnt. They have seventeen steam engines for a city this size. I wish they had thirty.

## a71-gm-palmer
roles: gentleman, lady, tradesman
q: What is the new hotel?

The Palmer House, at State and Monroe. Mr. Potter Palmer built it and opened it two weeks ago. Seven or eight storeys, and a room for every day of the year and then some, with marble everywhere and gas in every room.

?? Who is Mr. Palmer?
The man who owns State Street, or most of it. He made his money in dry goods, sold the store to Field and Leiter, and put his money into lots. He married a young lady from Kentucky last year, very handsome. The hotel was his wedding present to her, people say.

## a71-lady-dress
roles: lady, needlewoman
q: What is the fashion this year?

Everything drawn back. The skirt is flat in front and all the fullness goes behind, over a bustle, with an overskirt looped up on top and flounces at the hem. Colours are strong this year: purples, deep greens, the browns they call bismarck. A small hat tipped forward, with flowers.

?? Is it comfortable?
Comfortable! You sit on the edge of a chair and you go through doors sideways. But it is very becoming when you walk, and walking is what Sunday is for.

?? Who makes them?
A dressmaker on Clark Street makes mine, from a picture in the Bazar. She grumbles about the yards of trimming and charges me for every one of them.

## a71-lady-calls
roles: lady
q: What do ladies do on a Sunday afternoon?

Walk, and call. You leave cards at the houses of the people you know, and receive the people who know you. You talk about the sermon, the weather, the new hotel, the price of coal, the minister's wife's bonnet.

?? Anything else?
Today, the fire. Everyone saw the glow last night and everyone has an opinion on the Fire Department. Mrs. Jones's husband says it is all the fault of the city council. Mr. Jones says that about everything.

## a71-ts-car
roles: tradesman, youth, woman, workman
q: How do I get across the city?

Take a horse car. The State Street cars run north and south from Lake Street to the south end, the Madison Street cars run west, the Randolph Street cars too, and on the North Side the Clark Street cars go up to Lincoln Park. Five cents.

?? And across the river?
Over the bridges, unless one of them is swung for a boat, in which case you wait, or go round by the tunnel at LaSalle Street or Washington. You will get used to it. Every Chicagoan has lost a quarter of his life at a swung bridge.

## a71-ts-where
roles: tradesman, workman, woman
q: Where are you from?

From the old country, like everybody. You will not meet many in this city who were born in it, except the children. Half of us came from across the water and the other half from New York and New England.

?? Why Chicago?
Because there was work, and land you could buy by the month, and a man was what he could do, not who his father was. That is the theory. It is not always the practice. But it is truer here than anywhere I have been.

## a71-wm-work
roles: workman, driver
q: What do you do for work?

Whatever pays. This year I am in a lumber yard on the South Branch, carrying boards off the schooners. Last winter I was in a packing house. Before that I dug the sewers when they raised the streets.

?? Is it good pay?
A dollar and a half a day, two in the busy season. Rent is eight dollars a month for three rooms. My wife takes in sewing. We get by, and we have put by a little, in the savings bank on Clark Street.

?? And on a Sunday?
On a Sunday I sit. I have earned it.

## a71-wm-fire
roles: workman, driver, irish, german
q: Did you see the fire last night?

From the roof. It was over on the West Side, by Canal and Clinton, a planing mill and then everything round it, lumber and sheds and houses. The sky was red from one end to the other. You could read a newspaper by it on the North Side.

?? Was anyone hurt?
Not that I heard. Some firemen were burnt about the face, and they lost hose. Four blocks gone. It is out now, but they say it is still smoking in the ruins.

?? Could it happen again?
It happens every week. The trick is it happening where the engines can get at it.

## a71-ir-church
roles: irish, woman, servant
q: Which church do you go to?

Holy Family, on Twelfth Street, the Jesuits' church. It is the biggest church in the city. There is a Mass every hour on a Sunday morning, and still they stand at the back.

?? Is it far?
For the West Side Irish it is round the corner. For my cousin on the North Side it is a long walk, but she goes to St. Patrick's when her feet hurt.

## a71-ge-garden
roles: german, tradesman, woman
q: Is there music somewhere this afternoon?

In every beer garden on the North Side. A band plays, the families sit at the long tables under the trees, the children run about. You drink a glass of lager, you eat a sausage, you sing if you know the words.

?? Is that allowed on a Sunday?
The Yankee ministers say no. The law says the saloons should close. The police say nothing, because half of them are drinking in the garden. It is a quiet glass with your family. What harm is there in it?

## a71-sv-place
roles: servant
q: Do you like your place?

It is a good place. The mistress is fair and the master is hardly home. There is a cook, and me, and a girl who comes in to do the washing. I have a room under the roof with a window that looks at the lake.

?? What do you do on your afternoon?
Walk with the other girls, and look in the windows on State Street, and buy a paper of sweets. On a fine day we go to the lake front and the young men try to talk to us. We let them, sometimes.

## a71-yo-plans
roles: youth, child
q: What do you want to be?

A fireman. Or a driver on the horse cars. Or a clerk in a big store on State Street with a black coat and a gold watch chain. My father says a clerk, my mother says a priest, and my sister says a fool.

?? Have you seen the fire engines?
Last night I ran all the way to the West Side to see them, and my father gave me a whipping when I got home. It was worth it. You should hear the engines when they are going full: they shake.

## a71-ch-play
roles: child
q: What are you playing?

Fire company. Tom is the engine and I am the horses and the little ones are the fire. You have to put them out.

?? Who wins?
The engine, always. That is the rule.

## a71-dr-horse
roles: driver
q: Is that your horse?

The company's horse, but I feed him and water him and talk to him all day, so he is mine in every way but the paper. He is called Dan. He is eleven years old and he knows the round better than I do.

?? Does he mind working on a Sunday?
He minds the dust. Look at his eyes. This summer has been hard on everything that breathes.

## a71-bl-work
roles: black_resident
q: How long have you been in Chicago?

Since before the war. I came up the river from Missouri and stayed, because there was work in the hotels and there were people here who would not send me back. Some of them hid people in their cellars when it was a crime to.

?? Is it different now?
We vote now. Last year was the first time. I walked to the polls with my hat in my hand like I was going to church. Nobody stopped me. That is a different country from the one I was born in.

## a71-sc-ships
roles: scandinavian, workman
q: What brought you here?

Ships. I was a sailor out of Bergen, and the lake schooners want men who know the work. In summer I sail lumber down from Michigan, in winter, when the lake freezes, I work in a planing mill on the North Branch.

?? Do you miss Norway?
In the winter, never. It is colder here than Bergen. In the summer, when it is like this, hot and dry and the dust in everything, yes.

## a71-bk-pilsen
roles: bohemian, workman, woman
q: Who are your neighbours?

Bohemians, mostly, on our street, and Irish round the corner, and Germans on the next street over, and every one of us thinks the others are a little strange. The children do not care. They play together and fight together and speak English to each other.

?? Do you speak English?
Enough for the market. My children speak it better than Czech now, which their grandmother says is a sin.

## a71-nw-work
roles: needlewoman, woman
q: How did you learn your trade?

From my mother, who learned it from hers, and then four years with a dressmaker in New York, threading needles and pressing seams and never being allowed to touch the good silk.

?? Is it a good living?
For a woman, it is one of the few. I keep myself, which is more than many married women can say they do. My eyes are going, though. The gas is not good to sew by.
