# Fort Dearborn — the parade ground

> Part of the Fort Dearborn complex. The plan source, the scale derivation, the garrison chronology, the wrong-fort guards and the three corrections to `docs/research/04-structures-south.md` are all in **`docs/RESEARCH/fort_dearborn.md`**, which this memo does not repeat.

## Two sources on one page, differing by a factor of two

- **1855 photograph key**: "C is the parade-ground (80 × 200 ft)."
- **Fergus, of 1850**: "about 80 feet wide, and extended from the river bank south, the full
  length of the enclosure — say 400 feet."
- **The 1830 plan**: the court between the four ranges is about 71 × 109 ft.

`docs/research/04-structures-south.md` records only the first, as `[DOC]`. It is graded
**`inferred`** here with the disagreement written on the footprint. The full reconciliation is
in `docs/RESEARCH/fort_dearborn.md` § 5; the short form is that the **width** agrees across all
three and the **length** does not, and both later figures most likely measure the compound after
the stockade came down.

## The drill ground outside is not modelled

Kinzie, inside the stockaded fort in 1831: the parade-ground "extended south of the pickets".
Nothing gives its extent.

## The sun-dial

Fergus's, at his dimensions — "an 8-inch piece of square timber, imbedded in the earth, placed
upright, about 2 feet high, upon the top of which was a brass plate on which had been a
sun-dial" — carried back from 1850 on the record's own note, standing in **our** position,
because his knoll is on ground this record does not model.
