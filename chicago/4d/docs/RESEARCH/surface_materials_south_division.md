# The sixth warning: the soil profile, and the page that carries no citation at all

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Subject:** `chicago_architecture_history_115` opened at its own
locator, and the `documented` grade on ground `surface_materials.south_division` ·
**Outcome:** the source is correctly tiered and the VALUE is over-graded. The page is its own
document and stays at rung 4; it prints no footnote, endnote or reference of any kind; the one
document it does reprint is quoted without attribution and attests half of what this project
grades on it. `documented` → `inferred` is queued with the Blender bake a confidence costs. No
value moved in this slice and no mesh is stale.

## 1. The queue this discharges

`tools/validate.py`'s evidence ladder has warned since 2026-08-10 that six `documented` values
rest on no source at tier 3 or better. `docs/ROADMAP.md` § S5 tracked them one at a time and
said of this one, in the same sentence for four days: *the sixth
(`surface_materials.south_division`) has not been opened.*

It is opened now. The other five are settled — four `drloih` values that re-tiering cannot save
and ground `water`, established over-graded on 2026-08-11 (`docs/RESEARCH/swearingen_1803.md`).
This memo is the last of the six to be read, and it lands on the same side as `water`.

## 2. What was fetched, and how it was checked

Ken Schroeder, *"1.15 Improving the Mouth of the River,"* **Chicago Architecture History**,
published 9 February 2022. Retrieved at its own URL on 2026-08-11, and again from the Internet
Archive snapshot of **6 December 2025**
(`web.archive.org/web/20251206131901/…`), which is now the record's `archived_url`.

The two copies were compared on the passages this project stands on rather than eyeballed. Every
quoted phrase below is present in both, character for character, and **both carry the same
apparatus, which is none**: zero `<sup>` markers, zero footnotes, zero endnotes, zero numbered
references, and exactly one outbound link in the article body — the author's own email address.

That matters because the record's `access_notes` said, in as many words, *"Not re-fetched at its
locator by this parcel."* It had been read only through `docs/research/01-terrain-hydrology.md`,
which quotes it. The dossier is a finding aid; it is not the page. Two of the three findings
below are invisible from the dossier.

## 3. What the page says, and in whose voice

The whole of this project's soil claim comes from one paragraph, printed in the author's own
voice with no quotation marks and no citation:

> Chicago's geology presented two problems to would-be builders. The original soil consisted of
> a foot of black loam followed by a three- to four-foot layer of "quicksand," that was finally
> supported by an eight- to twelve-foot depth of impervious blue clay. Complicating matters
> further, the elevation of the original topography east of State Street lay from nine to ten
> feet above the surface of the lake, whereas to the west of State Street, it sloped down to the
> river in a level plain elevated only two to three feet above the river, hence natural surface
> drainage was practically nil.

That is a 2022 secondary synthesis by an architect-historian writing about why early Chicago was
hard to build on. Nothing on the page says where any of those five figures came from.

## 4. The one document on the page, and what it does not carry

Three paragraphs later the article stops paraphrasing and quotes. **John Mills Van Osdel** —
named on the page as Chicago's first architect — is block-quoted describing the ground he built
in:

> This sand in wet seasons became saturated with water, which could not pass downward into the
> clay, nor laterally as there was no inclination of the strata. There was nothing left but
> evaporation, which at times was a very slow process in rendering the soil firm and dry. In
> digging post-holes or trenches for foundations, the water would fill such excavations full to
> the surface of the ground… **A majority of the earlier frame buildings rested on posts sunk
> through the quicksand to the clay.**

This is the most interesting thing on the page and the dossier does not mention it. It is a
named witness, in his own words, on the exact subject of this claim. What follows from it is a
partition, and the partition is the finding:

| the claim's parts | who says it |
|---|---|
| the ORDER — saturated sand lying on impervious clay, in that vertical relation | **Van Osdel**, from having sunk posts through one to reach the other |
| that the sand will not drain, in either direction | **Van Osdel**, explicitly |
| a top layer of **black loam** | Schroeder alone; Van Osdel's passage never mentions it |
| **1 ft** of loam · **3–4 ft** of quicksand · **8–12 ft** of blue clay | Schroeder alone; Van Osdel gives no thickness for anything |

So the material value this project records — `black_loam_over_quicksand_over_blue_clay` — is
one-third attested by a witness and two-thirds a 2022 restatement of an unstated source. The
thicknesses, which are the only quantities in it, have nobody behind them at all.

**And the quotation is unattributed.** No publication, no date, no page. Van Osdel wrote and
lectured on early Chicago building in the 1880s and this is very likely from that body of
recollection, but *very likely* is not a citation and this project does not write one down. That
is precisely why the record cannot declare `transcribes`: the schema requires a `work` and a
`date` for the document carried, and the record's tier is then the best rung it declares. A page
that reprints a witness while refusing to say which document he is being reprinted from has not
made its rung a judgement about a document — which is the whole point of that field.

Recorded here so it is looked for once rather than discovered twice: **locating the Van Osdel
original would be worth a rung.** If the passage is run down to its own printing — the *Inland
Architect* series of the 1880s is the obvious place to start, and is a guess rather than a
finding — then the ORDER half of this claim gains a near-primary witness with a citation, and
`transcribes` becomes the right instrument. Until somebody opens that document, it is not.

## 5. The verdict on the source: rung 4, and nothing lifts it

The record is **correct at tier 4** and this slice does not move it.

It is outside the transcription-declaration rule by construction: `date` is `2022-02-09`, the
article's own publication date, so the record dates a document rather than a retrieval, and the
rule reaches only pages that date their own fetch. The rule is right to leave it alone. A 2022
essay IS its own document; the question is only what the document is worth, and a synthesis with
no reference apparatus is later scholarship, which is rung 4.

What the reading adds to the record is not a number but a limit that was previously an
inference:

- The old note said the page *"gives no citation for the two-to-three-feet figure."* That was
  read off the dossier and was true of one figure. **The article gives no citation for anything**
  — the entire reference apparatus is a closing "Further reading:" line naming Andreas's
  *History of Chicago*, plus two image captions crediting Sprague (JSAH, December 1981) and
  Mayer and Wade. That is now established from the page instead of assumed from a quotation.
- `verified` was `false`. It is `true`.
- `archived_url` was **a Wayback wildcard search URL** — `…/web/20240416000000*/…` — which
  resolves to a calendar of captures, not to a capture. Under this project's own standard (*a
  citation that cannot be re-read is not a citation*) that is not an archive link at all; it
  cannot fail a check, because nothing checks what an `archived_url` resolves TO, and it was the
  only such URL in `data/sources/`. It is a dated snapshot now, verified against the two
  quotations above.

## 6. The verdict on the value: over-graded, and the regrade is queued

`data/terrain/epochs/e1834_harbor_cut/terrain_spec.json`,
`surface_materials[zone=south_division]`, is graded **`documented`** on this source alone.

It should be `inferred`. The strongest grade this project awards means a period document, an
eyewitness, or a compilation from testimony says so. What says so here is a 2022 essayist with
no footnotes, and the witness his page quotes attests the stratigraphic order without a single
one of the numbers. The two sibling entries citing the same source for the same profile —
`north_division` and `west_division` — are already `inferred`, on a narrower argument (the
dossier's grouping of zones), which leaves the dataset in the odd position of grading the
zone with the *most* scrutiny highest.

**The regrade is not in this slice, and the reason is mechanical rather than editorial.** A
`confidence` is a mesh input: `generators/terrain_inputs.py` strips prose and `sources` from the
terrain's staleness hash and deliberately does not strip `confidence`, so changing this one word
re-stales the committed ground GLB and `tools/check.sh` fails until a Blender bake lands with
it. There is no Blender on the improve runner. This is exactly the position ground `water`
reached on 2026-08-11 and it takes the same route: **both regrades are one slice on a runner
with Blender**, together with the four `drloih` values, which are the same shape one domain
over.

What ships instead, today, is the finding where a visitor can read it. `note` IS stripped from
the terrain hash — that was the point of STATUS § 34 — so the block now carries the partition in
§ 4 above in the Evidence panel's *The ground you are standing on*, under a chip that is
currently stronger than the sentence beneath it. That is uncomfortable and it is the honest
intermediate state: the alternative is a visitor reading `documented` with nothing at all beside
it for as long as the bake is queued.

## 7. A correction to this project's own dossier

`docs/research/01-terrain-hydrology.md` § surface-material table, the row that produced the
grade:

> | Black loam ~1 ft over "quicksand" 3–4 ft over blue clay 8–12 ft | zones 8, 9, 18, 19 |
> **documented** (§1.15) |

The **documented** tag is the dossier's, made without opening the page, and it is the direct
ancestor of the grade in the spec. It is wrong for the reason § 6 gives. The same report calls
§1.15 *"source of the best two elevation figures in this report"* — which is true, and is a
statement about how thin the alternatives are rather than about how good this page is; the
report's own § "Bank heights at the forks are not directly documented" already says the second
half out loud.

Third dossier correction found by opening a page rather than by a check (`north_branch_bridge`
§ 6 was the clearance tag, `hogan_store` § 3 was the post-office chronology), and the third time
the lesson has been the same one: **a table row is a finding aid, and a finding aid is not the
page.** Per this project's rules the dossiers are committed verbatim and are not edited, so the
correction lives here, and the spec's own note now carries the operative half of it.
