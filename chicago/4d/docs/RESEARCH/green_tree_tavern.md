# Green Tree Tavern — research dossier

**Record:** `data/structures/green_tree_tavern.json` · **Scene status:** standing and in
operation on 1835-07-01 · **Milestone 1, west bank**

The best-documented building in the dataset, and documented from the inside out — which is the
opposite of the usual situation here. The Sauganash is known from a single sentence about its
exterior; the Green Tree is known from people who slept in it describing the rooms.

The reason is survival. It stood until 1902, long enough to be photographed in 1859 and long
enough for its old landlords and guests to be asked in print what it had been like.

---

## 1. Identity and sequence

| period | event |
|---|---|
| **1833** | built by **James Kinzie** — "the first structure ever erected in the place for hotel purposes"; carpenter disputed, see §5 |
| 1833 | opened by David Clark (also given as Clock) |
| **1834–1837** | **Chester Ingersoll**, landlord — covers the scene date; Martin M. Ford associated |
| 1837–1841 | David Cox; John Gray (April 1838–1841) — Gray is one of the witnesses in §3 |
| by 1844 | renamed the **Chicago Hotel** under George W. Rogers |
| **c. 1859** | **photographed at its original corner** — see §6 |
| 1880 | moved to 35 and 37 Milwaukee Avenue for the Jones & Laughlin works |
| August 1902 | collapsed and demolished |

Name said to derive from a solitary tree nearby; the species is variously given and is not
resolved.

## 2. Location — and a 146-metre residual doubt

**North-east corner of Lake Street and Canal Street**, agreed by both detailed sources:
chicagology gives "Northeast Corner of Lake and Canal Streets"; DRLOIH gives "the N.E. corner of
what is now Lake and Canal Streets".

Placed by the Sauganash method: modern intersection centre from OpenStreetMap
(E 446913.0, N 4637287.4), offset 12.2 m east and 12.2 m north — half an 80 ft platted street in
each direction — so the west face sits on the Canal frontage and the south face on the Lake
frontage of a north-east corner lot. Working uncertainty ~20 m, the georeference's.

**The residual doubt is larger than the georeference's, and it is worth stating plainly.**
DRLOIH's article title gives the corner as "Lake and **West Water** Streets" and glosses West
Water as "now Canal Street". Historically West Water Street ran along the west bank and Canal
Street lay one block further west — and the modern geometry agrees: modern Canal at Lake is about
**146 m** back from the modern west-bank line, a full block. So "Lake and West Water" and "Lake
and Canal" are two different corners, and the sources have conflated them.

**Canal is adopted** because both detailed sources name Canal in modern terms and one of them
says "what is now". If the tavern actually stood on the riverbank street, it belongs ~145 m east
of where this record puts it. **The check that settles it is the lot geometry on Wright 1834 or
Hathaway 1834**, which is precisely what those sheets are good for and precisely what they were
verified to supply.

## 3. The interior — three named witnesses

This is why the record exists in the shape it does. `chicagology_prefire127` transcribes
first-hand recollections rather than compiling anonymous secondary text:

| witness | testimony |
|---|---|
| **F. H. Taylor** | "The fornt was on Canal street and this side was on Lake" *(the page's own typo)* |
| **John Gray**, landlord 1838–41 | "A hall divided the house and a stairway in this hall led to the second floor"; the entrance "on the Lake street front about the middle of the long side"; the bar-room occupying a whole end; "There should also be a small attic window in the gable end, for we used the attic, too"; "There should also be a low one-story addition to the right, the same as on the left" |
| **Edwin O. Gale**, guest | a chamber "about 12×12, with two windows 6×8", whose "walls, ceilings, and board partitions had evidently received a coat of whitewash when the house was built" |
| editor's note | "Seven and one-half feet is rather low for a hotel ceiling" |

**Two limits on all of it.** The witnesses are recalling the building from the late 1830s and
1840s — a few years *after* the scene date. Gale's "when the house was built" is what ties the
fabric he describes back to 1833; without that phrase none of it could be carried to 1835.

## 4. Two corrections this dossier makes

**The whitewash is interior.** The Green Tree is described as "whitewashed" in secondary
summaries, including this project's own research digest (`docs/research/03-structures-north.md`
§2.5, and again in its §7 synthesis: "the two-story whitewashed Green Tree"). Gale's sentence is
about **walls, ceilings and board partitions** — the inside. **No source describes the exterior
finish.** The record carries `paint: unpainted`, `inferred`, and states why. This matters
visually: it is the difference between a white building and a weathered one standing a block
from the Sauganash, whose white paint was remarkable precisely because its neighbours were not
painted.

**The roof form is documented, not typological.** "A small attic window in the gable end" is a
direct attestation of a gable roof — one of very few in this dataset that is not an inference
from period practice. With the front on Canal and the long side on Lake, the gable ends face east
and west and the ridge runs east–west.

## 5. Conflicts recorded

| question | readings | resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame, or a weatherboarded log core?** | Two sources: "two-story frame building". Against: **W. H. Stow** — the builder of the Western Hotel two blocks south — "did claim that the Green Tree was originally of logs and it was not until years afterward that it was covered with weatherboarding and given the appearance of a frame building" | **Frame adopted.** Both detailed accounts say frame, and the interior the witnesses describe is a framed interior — board partitions, a stair hall, chambers on a 12 ft module. The log-core claim is one man's recollection, recorded second-hand, about a rival's house. Carried in the record as `log_core: false`, `inferred`, so it is not lost |
| **Carpenter** | chicagology: "Philo Cobb". DRLOIH: **Silas B. Cobb** | Silas B. Cobb is the historically real person; "Philo Cobb" is probably a conflation with Philo Carpenter. Not modelled either way |
| **Date of the move** | chicagology's Green Tree page: 1880. Its Town of Chicago page, written 1883: "removed two years ago" = 1881 | Immaterial to 1835; the record uses 1880 and notes the other |
| **Which corner** | see §2 | Canal adopted, doubt recorded |
| **Landlords** | chicagology: Ingersoll 1834–37. DRLOIH: "initially by David Clark and then by Edward Parsons", undated | **Compatible, not contradictory** — Clark opened the house in 1833, before Ingersoll |

## 6. The 1859 photograph — and a caveat that is usually stated wrongly

The Green Tree "survived to be photographed", and the photograph is the closest thing to direct
visual evidence for **any** Wolf Point building: a plate of an actual structure rather than a
retrospective drawing of one. Catalogued as *Green Tree Tavern, Chicago, 1859*, Chicago History
Museum **ICHi-040230**, showing the building at the north-east corner of Canal and Lake.

**The caveat in circulation — that the photograph shows the building "after ~47 years of
alteration and a move" — is wrong for this plate.** 1859 *predates* the 1880 move; the tavern is
still standing where it was built. What lies between 1833 and 1859 is **twenty-six years of
alteration** — a succession of landlords, a rename to the Chicago Hotel by 1844, and the low
one-storey side additions Gray recalls. The 47-years-and-a-move caveat belongs to the post-1880
photographs (the c. 1876 view is also pre-move; the 1902 image is of the ruins).

**It has not been seen for this record.** picryl returns 403 to automated fetchers, the IDA
Illinois catalogue record renders its metadata in JavaScript, and the Chicago History Museum's
own record was not opened. The title, date, corner and accession number all come from aggregator
and library metadata, so `chm_green_tree_1859` is tagged `verified: false`.

**It is the single highest-value open lead in this parcel.** One look settles the overall
dimensions (from the proportions of a building whose ceiling height is known), the exterior
finish, and the gallery.

## 7. The footprint — derived, not invented, not measured

The only footprint in the parcel with a textual basis. In the original units:

- **Depth ≈ 25 ft = 7.62 m** — two 12 ft ranks of chambers plus board partitions.
- **Length ≈ 40 ft = 12.19 m** — a hall of about 8 ft with the entrance "about the middle of the
  long side", flanked by two end blocks each holding a 12 ft room plus partitions. The bar-room
  occupied one whole end, which is consistent.

**What is attested is the module, not the count.** No source gives an overall dimension. A plan
with three rooms rather than two on one side gives ~52 ft. Treat the length as ±20% and the depth
as firmer. Tagged `inferred` with the arithmetic in the note.

The polygon is the **two-storey core only**. Gray's "low one-story addition to the right, the
same as on the left" is carried as `side_additions: true`, `inferred` — inferred rather than
documented because Gray describes the building three to six years after the scene date, and
nothing dates the additions. They are deliberately excluded from the geometry; recorded in
`docs/LIBERTIES.md`.
