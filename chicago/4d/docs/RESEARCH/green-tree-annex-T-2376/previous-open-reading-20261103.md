PRIOR RESEARCH RECORD:
THE BEST-DOCUMENTED BUILDING IN THIS DATASET, and it is documented from the inside out — which is the opposite of the usual situation here and is worth understanding. The Green Tree survived until 1902, long enough for people who had slept in it to be asked what it was like in print: John Gray, who kept it 1838-41, Edwin O. Gale, who was put up in a chamber 'about 12x12, with two windows 6x8', and F. H. Taylor, who fixed which street the front was on. From them come the room module, the 7 1/2 ft ceilings, the board partitions, the interior whitewash, the dividing hall and its stairway, the bar-room at one end, the attic used for lodging, the gable-end attic window and the low one-storey additions at each end. What none of them gives is an overall dimension, so even here the footprint is a derivation from the room module rather than a measurement. IT ALSO SURVIVED LONG ENOUGH TO BE PHOTOGRAPHED, about 1859, at its original corner — the closest thing to direct visual evidence for any Wolf Point building. Note the caveat carefully, because the version in circulation is wrong: the 1859 plate PREDATES the 1880 move and shows the tavern where it was built, so what stands between it and the scene date is twenty-six years of alteration, not forty-seven years and a move. That plate has not been seen for this record and it is the single highest-value open lead in the parcel: it would settle the overall dimensions, the exterior finish and the gallery in one look. CONFLICTS RECORDED: frame throughout versus a log core weatherboarded later, a claim attributable to W. H. Stow personally; the carpenter, Philo Cobb versus Silas B. Cobb; the move dated 1880 versus 1881; and, the largest in metres, whether 'Lake and Canal' or 'Lake and West Water' names the corner, which are a full block apart.
