# John Miller's tannery — research dossier

**Record:** `data/structures/miller_tannery.json` · **Scene status:** standing on 1835-07-01 on a
continuity argument; whether it was still tanning is unattested · **north bank at the forks**

The log house John Miller built in 1831 just north of his brother Samuel's tavern and used as a
tannery — "**Chicago's first recorded factory**". Two sources, one sentence each.

---

## 1. The two sentences

DRLOIH, *The Beginning of Wolf Point*:

> "**In 1831 John Miller built a log house near his brother's tavern that he used as a tannery,
> Chicago's first recorded factory.**"

Andreas, *History of Chicago* vol. 1, in the list of residents of the new town of 1833:

> "**John Miller, brother of Samuel, the landlord, came in 1831, and run a tannery just north of
> Miller's tavern**; Benjamin Hall, tanner, a partner of John Miller, who came in 1832."

The second is the more useful of the two and this project's dossier did not have it:
`docs/research/03-structures-north.md` § 3.1 carries only "near his brother's tavern". Andreas
gives a **direction**, which is what let the building be placed at all, and a **partner**,
Benjamin Hall.

## 2. Position

Placed against another record rather than against the ground: 11.5 m north of the north face of
this project's `miller_house`, on the same easting, so its south wall faces the tavern across a
yard. That inherits every uncertainty the Miller house carries — no surviving intersection
locates it, its coordinate comes from the datum origin and modern bank geometry, and its
along-bank axis is the softer one — and adds an invented separation. **Working uncertainty about
40 m.**

North is also the only direction with ground in it. The modelled 1834 waterline puts the North
Branch about 40 m west and the main stem about 15 m south of the tavern; the point is narrow.
A tannery is a nuisance trade — bark, lime, hides, and a great deal of water — and putting it
behind the tavern rather than between the tavern and the water is consistent with that, but no
source says so and the record does not offer it as evidence.

## 3. What is invented

| attribute | state | what would upgrade it |
|---|---|---|
| `footprint` | `conjectural` — 7 × 5.5 m, an ordinary single-pen log house | any dimension |
| `position` | `inferred` — direction attested, separation invented | a lot, a corner, or a plat annotation |
| `form.stories`, `wall_height_m`, `roof_type`, `roof_pitch_deg`, `chimneys` | `inferred` from type | any description |
| `documented_range.to` | `inferred` — the honest minimum | see below |

**What is not modelled at all:** the tanyard. A tannery of this date is a bark mill, a row of
lime and tan pits, vats, and drying sheds, and the record models the log house the sources name
and nothing else. Nothing in the mesh distinguishes it from a dwelling.

## 4. The survival argument, and its weakness

Nothing reached follows this building past its founding. The trade appears twice — Miller in
1831, Hall in 1832 — and then drops out of the record. Samuel Miller, the brother whose tavern
fixes this building's position, "sold the tavern and moved away following the death of his wife
in 1832" (DRLOIH); this project's `miller_house` record has the tavern in secondary store or
residential use well before the scene date. So the anchor had moved on three years before
1835-07-01 and the tannery's survival rests on the ordinary continuity argument.

**What would settle it:** the *Chicago Democrat*. A tanner advertising hides, bark or leather in
1835 would fix the trade to the year; a to-let or sale notice would fix the building.
