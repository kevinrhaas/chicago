# The last four pages that could be opened

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Subject:** `chicagology_first_post_office`, `chicagology_lastwardance`,
`chicagology_prefire274`, `chicago_temple_history` · **Outcome:** two regrades on the document
(4 → 2, twice), one rung confirmed where it stood, one page read and found to reprint nothing, a

that could not be re-read made re-readable. **No value moved and no mesh is stale.** The

§ S5 has always said cannot be solved this way.

Four pages, four different answers. That is again the finding.

## 2. `chicagology_first_post_office` — tier 4 **confirmed**, and the paragraph that is not Currey's

**What the record said it was.** A transcription of J. Seymour Currey, *"U. S. Government and
Post Office Buildings,"* **Fort Dearborn Magazine, December 1922** — plus, in the same sentence,
*"the only statement reached that gives the Thompson 1830 plat's street width as a uniform 66
feet."*

**What it is.** The first half is exactly right, and this is **the first of these pages to be
read and left where it stood**. Currey's article is printed under its own attribution line, it
is a 1922 historian's compilation ninety years after the events, and it names its authorities
inline wherever it has them — Governor Bross's history, M. H. Putney's *Historical Notes*,
Mrs. Neville on Green Bay, E. O. Gale's reminiscences. For the post-office facts this dataset
uses it names none. **Later scholarship, rung 4**, outranked by Andreas at 3 exactly as the
record already had it, including at the one point the two disagree (Currey moves the office to
Franklin and South Water in November 1832; Andreas keeps it at Lake and South Water until about
July 1834, twice, in two independently written passages, and Andreas is followed —
`docs/RESEARCH/hogan_store.md` § 3).

That outcome is worth naming, because three consecutive rounds of this work have found a record
wrong about its own page and the habit that forms is to expect it. `docs/ROADMAP.md` § S5 wrote
these six down as *"unread rather than wrong"*. One of them was simply unread.

**The finding is the other paragraph.** Between Currey's 1832 sentence and his 1837 sentence the
page prints this:

> The Illinois and Michigan Canal Commissioners hired James Thompson, a surveyor from Kaskaskia
> in downstate Randolph County, to create Chicago's first plat in 1830. He laid out the town
> with straight streets uniformly 66 feet wide (the length of a surveyor's chain) with alleys 16
> feet wide bisecting each block.

That is the 66 ft dissent — the rival to the 80 ft module every platted placement in this
dataset is offset from. **It cannot be read off this page as Currey's**, on three grounds, none
of which is decisive alone and which together are:

1. **It interrupts a chronology that is otherwise strict.** The article runs 1831 → 1832 →
   *[this]* → 1837. Nothing else on the page steps outside that order.
2. **Its subject is a survey, not a building.** The article is titled *U. S. Government and Post
   Office Buildings* and every other paragraph is about the office, its keepers, or the mail
   that reached it.
3. **It is the one paragraph naming no authority, and it does not sound like 1922.** Currey
   attributes constantly. This paragraph attributes nothing, and *"downstate Randolph County"*
   is a modern Illinois usage while *"(the length of a surveyor's chain)"* is a modern gloss for
   a modern reader.

So it is left **undeclared**. Unattributed website prose sits off the ladder altogether, rather
than at the rung of the article it happens to have been set beside — the same apparatus rule
that left the *Chicago Tribune* notice on `chicagology_prefire276` undeclared, applied in the
other direction: there it withheld a rung the project had not earned, here it withholds a rung
the project had been granting.

**What that costs, and what it does not.** It costs the dissent nothing it had not already lost.
The 66 ft figure was **excluded by measurement on 2026-08-10** — eight platted corridors read off
both 1834 sheets, block boundary to block boundary, every one between 75.7 and 92.8 ft, none
within 9 ft of 66 (`docs/RESEARCH/street_module_1830.md` § 8). No number in the dataset moves.

What changes is *what was being disagreed with*. `data/traces/street_control.json` said **"Currey
states"**, which reads as a named historian contradicting a survey annotation — the kind of
disagreement a later agent might reasonably reopen. It is a website sentence nobody can source
contradicting a survey annotation, and a measurement. The note is corrected there, and
`what_it_does_not_supply` on the source record now says the page does not supply the street
module at all. The figure **stays recorded**, in all three files that carry it: a source is not
deleted for turning out to be wrong, and a dissent that vanishes is a dissent that gets
rediscovered.

## 3. `chicagology_lastwardance` — tier 4 → **2**, and the record was under-reading its own page

**What the record said it was.** *"The page is a later compilation of recollections, unfootnoted,
and is tiered accordingly."*

**What it is.** The page prints one attribution line above its body — **Chicago Tribune, August
14, 1910** — and the article names its own witness in its last paragraph:

> This description of the last great Indian war dance in Chicago was left for coming generations
> by one who saw it in his youth and who in old age wrote it out. The school children of Chicago
> owe to the late Judge John Dean Caton their gratitude for this Indian story of their city.

**John Dean Caton** reached Chicago in 1833 and was in the town in 1835. So the route this
dataset stands on is not a compiler's digest of other people's recollections. It is one
identified eyewitness's own written account, printed by a newspaper after his death.

**Rung 2, and deliberately not rung 1.** The Tribune neither dates Caton's writing nor prints it
as a document, and it glosses him in 1910's voice: *"the 'North Branch bridge' (which stood where
the railroad bridge stands now)"* is the newspaper locating the crossing for its readers, not the
witness describing it. No placement in this dataset uses that gloss, and
`what_it_does_not_supply` now says so.

**What is deliberately not declared.** Below the Tribune article the page prints a second block
headed *"Account of the Last War Dance from an Old Book"*, and it is first-person and nearer the
event than Caton's paragraph — *"immediately opposite our house"*, *"it frightened me
dreadfully"*, a child watching from the New York House on Tuesday, 18 August 1835. Declaring it
could only raise this record's rung, which is exactly why it is not declared: **the page does not
name the book.** An undeclarable document is apparatus, no claim in this dataset rests on it, and
a rung must be a judgement about a document that can be named.

**This is the fifth source record found wrong about its own page** and the second wrong in the
under-reading direction (`chicagology_prefire278` was the first, in the opposite one). The class
is stable enough now to state plainly: a source record's prose is written when the citation is
written, and nothing re-reads it afterwards unless a person does.

**What the regrade does and does not do.** The bridge's claims are unchanged. `north_branch_bridge`
takes exactly three things from this page — the crossing standing six and a half weeks after
1835-07-01, carrying a crowd on foot, and the route of two streets — and every one of them is a
`phase`/`survival` statement rather than a geometry input. Its `documented` clearance still rests
on `old_settlers_bridges_1883`, its span on the traced 1834 waterlines. Nothing went stale.

**The standing constraint is untouched by any of this.** The subject of that page is the final
removal of the Potawatomi from Chicago, which `AGENTS.md` places under consultation this project
has not sought. Nothing in this dataset depicts, stages or narrates it; nothing taken from the
page reaches past the built environment. Reading the article more carefully did not change that
and was not allowed to.

## 4. `chicagology_prefire274` — tier 4 → **2**, on the topography and on nothing else

**What the record said it was.** *"Fort Dearborn." Chicagology* — no document named at all.

**What it is.** *"Fort Dearborn I"*, transcribing **Chicago Magazine, March 1857**, printed under
its own attribution line. That is the installment *before* the one `chicagology_prefire276`
carries, in the serial that took `chicagology_prefire273` to rung 2 on 2026-08-10.

**The rung is earned by the topography and by nothing else on the page**, and that limit is the
whole of this record's judgement. What this project cites from it is a description of ground the
writer could still walk in 1857 and of a channel filled inside his readers' memory:

> the old channel … flowing southerly, parallel with the lake shore, for nearly half a mile, till
> at last it lost itself in the lake, its mouth being choked by sand bars

> It stood upon a flattened mound, formed by the curve of the river at its base on its three
> sides … It was as high as any other point

That is recollection at twenty-odd years — **rung 2**. The page's declared *subject*, the fort of
1803–1812, is not: nobody writing in 1857 remembered 1804, that account is compiled, and no claim
in this dataset rests on it. `what_it_does_not_supply` states it, because a record at rung 2 with
a fort narrative on it is an invitation.

**One inference is stated rather than hidden.** The serial's method — written from what *"several
of the oldest inhabitants, who are now permanent residents among us"* told the writer — is printed
in the May installment and **not** in this one. The March issue is therefore graded on the same
method **by continuity of the serial**, not on a statement of its own. That is an inference and
the record says so.

**And it disagrees with this project's epoch boundary.** The page dates the channel through the
bar to *"the Engineers of the government in 1838"*. `data/terrain/epochs.json` opens
`e1834_harbor_cut` on 1833-07-01 and has the February 1834 storm scour it, on Andreas.

**Andreas is followed**, for stated reasons rather than by default: he is nearer the works, this
project's pier chronology is already built on him, and 1838 most plausibly dates the harbour's
completion rather than the cut — the piers were extended for years after the channel opened, and
a writer in 1857 summarising a decade of federal harbour work in one clause has every reason to
give its end date. But the magazine is **not made to agree**. The disagreement is recorded on the
source record, and it costs nothing either way: the epoch boundary was set before this page was
read, and neither date is cited by it.

Worth noticing for its own sake: this is the first source in the dataset that had to be graded
**by which part of it you are standing on**. Every earlier regrade moved a whole page. Here the
same document is rung 2 for the landform and no better than rung 3 for the fort, and the only
thing keeping the two apart is a sentence in a record.

## 5. `chicago_temple_history` — reprints nothing, and the finding is where it sends you

**What was looked for.** What the west-bank / north-bank disagreement over `walker_meeting_house`
actually needs: a period or near-primary account of the 1834 log meeting house — a trustees'
minute, a circuit rider's journal, a conference record, a dated quotation from any of them.

**What is printed there.** Five hundred words of modern congregational narrative running 1831 to
1924, unfootnoted end to end, no bibliography, no further-reading link, no archive named anywhere
on the site's About tree. The only quoted document on the entire page is a **1922 sermon by the
Rev. John Thompson** about the decision to build the present skyscraper — ninety years after the
cabin and not about it.

So the record declares `carries_no_document`, the third state beside `transcribes` and silence,
and its rung cannot rise: there is no document here for a rung to be about.

**One thing the full read adds, and it cuts against the page.** The paragraph is inconsistent with
itself before it ever meets this project's witnesses. The congregation is founded in 1831 and
worships in members' homes; the cabin is built in 1834 *north* of the river; it is floated across
*"four years later"*; the brick church replacing it on the Washington and Clark site is dated
1845. Nothing in that sequence is tied to a document, so the 1834 cannot be checked against
anything — which is precisely why the west-bank reading rests on the two near-primary witnesses
instead, and why this page remains the recorded competing reading rather than the adopted one.

**And the citation is re-readable now.** On 2026-08-09 no Wayback snapshot existed and a Save
Page Now request produced none, so `archived_url` was deliberately left absent rather than
fabricated — the right call, and it left a standing validator warning. A snapshot dated
**2026-06-05** was found on 2026-08-11 and verified to carry both quotations this record depends
on, character for character. `archived_url` is recorded and the warning is gone. The verbatim
quotations stay in the note regardless: an archive is a second copy, not a reason to stop keeping
the first.

## 7. The method, after three rounds of it

Three memos have now run the same procedure across ten pages, and the pattern in the failures is
consistent enough to write down as a rule rather than as a habit:

- **The error is almost never the tier. It is the sentence naming what the page carries.** Six
  records have now been found describing their own page wrongly. Four over-read (naming a period
  document the page only quotes at second hand), two under-read (`prefire278`, and this round's
  `lastwardance`).
- **A page is not one document.** Of the ten, five carry a transcription plus apparatus, and on
  three of those the apparatus is the more tempting half — a dimensioned woodcut caption, a
  period newspaper notice, an unnamed first-person book. Declaring what a rung rests on is what
  keeps the tempting half out.
- **Read the paragraph, not the page.** This round produced the first source that is two different
  rungs depending on which of its claims you take, and the first paragraph found to be the
  *website's own prose interleaved into a transcription*. Neither is visible from a citation, a
  URL, or a search hit. Both are visible in about ninety seconds of reading.
