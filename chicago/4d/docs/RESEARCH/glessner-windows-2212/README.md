# T-2212 — Glessner courtyard, bow, bay, turret and dormer window profiles

Glessner exterior audit GA-11B, part B of T-2211. The audit found the courtyard windows drawn as
repetitive dark fields: one pane behind one flat frame with a bar across its middle, the same on
every courtyard face, the hall bow and the dining bay.

## The picture read

HABS photograph 05 (c. 1923, the court from its south-west corner; the record's
`habs_glessner_photo_05_court_c1923`) is the only view of the courtyard faces. Its rights are
unknown, so it is READ — sash types counted off it — and nothing is traced or derived from its
pixels. It shows:

- **North range, hall bow, dining bay first floor:** one-over-one double-hung sash.
- **East wing, the four lights over the underpass:** two-over-two (a vertical bar in each sash) in
  three of them; the fourth (north, lower) has its blind drawn and takes its neighbours' pattern.
- **North range garden-level lights:** vertical iron bars.
- **Dining bay glazed band:** fixed lights in two rows; **stair tower lantern:** two-by-three
  fixed lights; **dormers:** one undivided light. All three were already built that way.

## What changed (generator: `generators/archetypes/masonry_house_v4_sash.py`)

- **Courtyard sash in two planes.** Every courtyard window is now T-2211's double-hung (the upper
  sash outside the lower, each with stiles, rails and glass), on a rock-faced limestone sill under
  the rough lintel the court already had. The record marks the underpass lights
  `"sash": "two_over_two"` (`openings_court`), which the params pass through.
- **Barred garden lights.** The court's garden-level lights (heads under 1.8 m) stand behind
  vertical iron bars.
- **Bow and bay sash.** `OnFacet` carries the same sash onto the hall bow's curved face and the
  dining bay's canted facets (`facet_window(..., sash=True)`); their stone surrounds are unchanged.
- The light (mobile) tier keeps the single pane: it has under 3,000 triangles of its 200,000 left.
  `docs/LIBERTIES.md` → `L-glessner-sash-2212` records what is read and what is reconstructed.

## The opening schedule

`opening-schedule.md` (and `.json`) lists all 44 courtyard openings by face, and 16 rows for the
hall bow, the bow terrace, the dining bay (first floor, garden lights, glazed band), the stair
tower's slits and lantern, the dormers and the stable's alley dormer, each with what the generator
draws and the record entry's sources and confidence. With T-2211's 62 street and stable openings
it covers every opening on the house. It is generated by the same tool:
`python3 tools/glessner_opening_schedule.py` writes both schedules, `--check` (in `check.sh`)
fails when either is stale.
