# Glessner's Prairie wall at the entry: the courses and the header stone (T-2913)

T-2912 left this piece of T-2853: the course lines either side of the front door and the
header stone over it, against HABS sheet 5's east (Prairie Avenue) elevation. The ticket
quoted the sheet's lines at about 7.1, 5.8, 4.7, 3.8, 2.5 and 1.0 ft over the door datum,
the build's at 7.3, 6.7, 6.1, 5.5, 4.4, 3.6, 2.3 and 0.8, and the header stone's joints at
S 27.7 and 36.0. Reading the whole sheet changes what those numbers mean.

## 1. The crop's feet are the door's, not the sheet's

`../glessner-entry-2228/habs5-entry.png` is rectified to 100 px a foot by making the door
5 ft wide and 7.75 ft high (sheet 6's door). On the 400-dpi scan of the whole sheet
(`chicago/prairie_1904_v1/research/public/glessner-habs-sheet-5.png`) the door's outer
frame is 494 px wide (x 6293-6787) and 751 px high (head y 5077, sill y 5828): at the sheet's
own scale, about 98.8 px a foot, a 5.0 by 7.60 ft door. So the crop stretches the sheet
about 3 % upright, and every height read off it runs 3 % tall.

## 2. The Prairie elevation draws the printed string from its own base line

The bed joints either side of the door, read on the crop (each a pair of lines a joint
apart; the mid-line, both sides averaged), then put back to the sheet's scale (x 0.981):

| bed | crop, ft over the sill | sheet scale | printed string from -0.54 | error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base line | -0.55 | -0.54 | -0.54 | - |
| 1 | 0.94 | 0.92 | 0.96 | 0.04 |
| 2 | 2.45 | 2.40 | 2.46 | 0.06 |
| 3 | 3.77 | 3.70 | 3.75 | 0.05 |
| 4 | 4.65 | 4.56 | 4.54 | 0.02 |
| 5 | 5.81 | 5.70 | 5.67 | 0.03 |
| 6 | 7.07 | 6.94 | 6.88 | 0.06 |
| 7 (the door head) | 7.73 | 7.58 | 7.50 | 0.08 |

The printed string is sheet 5's own, printed down the 18th Street entrance's elevation:
18, 18, 15.5, 9.5, 13.5, 14.5, 7.5 ... in (`ashlar_courses_in`). The Prairie elevation draws
no string of its own, but from its base line through the door head it draws that one to
within a reading. Above the head the drawn courses run long against the printed inches
(about 0.3 ft by bed 12), and so does the 18th Street elevation against the very string
printed beside it (its bottom 36 in are 299 px, its whole 271 in about 2,400): the
draftsman's upper courses are not to scale. The printed inches stay the course heights.

The 1888 photograph `a18-gh-glessner-front-with-keith` agrees. Scaled on the door (head
y 1062, sill y 1248), the joints beside it fall at about 0.8, 2.4, 3.75, 4.6, 5.8, 7.08 and
7.79 ft over the sill: the sheet's drawn courses, the door head on the seventh bed, and
the header stone a course of its own above it.

## 3. What the build has, and why it is not moved here

The build lays the same string from grade, 0.74 ft under the door datum (sheet 6's walk), so
its beds stand at 0.76, 2.26, 3.55, 4.34, 5.47, 6.68 and 7.30 (the ticket's 6.1 was not a
bed): 0.20 ft under the sheet's at every bed, and the door head 0.45 ft up the eighth course.
The heights are right; the base is not.

Raising it was built and taken back in this run. One coursing serves every granite face, and
on 18th Street the apertures are set on the printed string's sums above north grade (L305's
north correction: 111-173.5 in and 231-267 in). Raising every bed 0.20 ft pulls them off
those windows; raising the Prairie face's alone puts a 0.06 m step in every bed at the NE
corner, whose returns must line up. Read as level courses round that corner, the two
elevations say something the record does not know: the 18th Street grade stands about
0.20 ft over the Prairie walk, where `grade_datum` takes the two as level ("not measured by
any sheet"). Setting that offset moves every `ng` height on the house with the courses, so it
is its own piece, **T-2955**.

## 4. The header stone

Sheet 5 draws the stone over the door as one course from the door head to the spring,
its end joints at 5.09 ft left and 5.08 ft right of the door's centre line (x 378 and 1395
on the crop, whose horizontal feet are the sheet's). That is a stone about 10.17 ft long,
centred on the door: S 27.8 to 38.0 against the build's 27.9 to 37.9. Nothing on the sheet
falls at S 36.0. Built: S 27.8 to 38.0. The period photograph's stone is a perspective
read and does not refine this.

## 5. What is left

- **T-2955**: the 0.20 ft, as the north-grade offset, with the coursing and the north
  apertures carried together.
- **T-2950**: sheet 6 gives the door 7.75 ft, sheet 5 draws it 7.60, and the 1888
  photograph's door is about 1.49 times as high as it is wide (7.4 ft on 5 ft). Sheet 5 and
  the photograph put the head on the seventh bed. Settling the door's height moves the leaf,
  the header and the camera this view is solved on (7.75 ft), so it is its own piece.

habs5-entry keeps both items open until they land.
