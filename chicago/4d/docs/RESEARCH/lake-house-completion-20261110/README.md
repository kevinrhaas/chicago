# Lake House: a dated worksite, with a finite picture ruling

The July 1835 default is an active hotel-building project. The held *Chicago
Democrat* notice of 10 June 1835, p.2 col.5 (`chicago_democrat_1835_06_10#c001`),
is the pre-scene evidence. Its transcription-mediated reading reports a new
house building in Kinzie's Addition, understood to be named Lake House. It does
not measure the plan or progress. Choosing a bounded construction stage is

## Text and map evidence that bounds the chosen state

- **June 10, 1835 project notice:** active named project before the scene;
  transcription-mediated, with bracketed supplies. No original newspaper
  facsimile newly read here. No stage, dimension or measured schedule.
- **Andreas, 1884, vol.1:** the completed brick hotel, three storeys and a
  basement, nearly $100,000; autumn 1836 opening; Kinzie/Rush/Michigan streets,
  fronting Michigan. These are later accounts, not a July progress report.
- **Bonnell, quoted by Andreas:** “in course of construction” after the arrival
  dated August 25, 1835 in his letter. Its forty-years arithmetic implies 1836,
  and Andreas's lead-in says 1837. That disagreement remains declared. It cannot
  produce a measured July wall height.
- **Martineau, June 1836, quoted by Andreas:** unfinished hotel interior with
  benches and planks on trestles. This does not establish July 1835 roof absence.
- **Fergus, 1839 recalled in 1876, p.37:** the directly checked printed address
  “Rush and North Water sts”. It disagrees with Andreas's street account. The
  existing reconstructed position stays on the north-side Michigan/Hubbard
  interpretation; neither the retrospective print nor the modern Wrigley gloss
  moves a survey position. The newly read 1869 Sanborn reproduction places the later hotel west of Rush,
  strengthening that later west-half-block lead. It is not a July construction
  survey or a hotel-lot deed; no new contemporary July lot plan was recovered.
- **Chicagology and drloih compilations:** the editorial 1835 groundbreaking is
  not Andreas's quotation. No independent July stage is supplied. The separate
  June notice is the stronger project-date evidence.
- **The earlier bounded 28-issue primary search:** retained as transcription/OCR

## What now changes in the scene

The earlier model was a uniform four-metre rectangular magazine skin, with one
painted door surface. The `construction_site` opt-in replaces that with:

- **Real masonry thickness and openings:** 0.46 m walls; seven selected north
  work bays, with unglazed voids. Only three bays have brickwork bridged overhead, supported by timber lintels
  bearing 0.22 m into each pier. Lintels are reconstructed too.
  The central opening starts at grade; this is no claim of an interior walkthrough.
- **Uneven working lifts:** north-side wall heights of 4.0, 3.28 and 2.44 m;
  side walls step toward a rear lift of 1.2–2.08 m. A two-metre break in the rear
  lift allows the worksite to read as material access rather than a closed shed.
- **Two internal timber working stages:** standards, ledgers and separate deck
  planks support work at the higher lift. Their 1.65 m decks and all stock and
  positions are reconstructed.
- The existing **four brick stacks, three timber piles and two stone heaps**
  remain the independently declared yard layer. This increment does not invent
  a supplier transaction or imply they came from a particular yard.

Everything above is **reconstructed**. It fits the existing 24 × 15 m plan, stays
at or below the existing 4 m maximum, and adds no finished roof, cupola, glazing,
nameboard, beds, cellar excavation or exact construction-date claim. The salmon
brick recipe remains the existing separately declared reconstruction. The main
position and its uncertainty remain unchanged.
