# The three pages that looked like the same case

**Date:** 2026-08-11 · **Subject:** `chicagology_prefire062`, `chicagology_prefire276`,
`wikipedia_chicago_river` · **Outcome:** two regrades on the document, one page read and found
to reprint nothing, a third source record wrong about its own page, and the `documented` water
claim's real support identified. No value moved and no mesh is stale.

## 1. The queue this discharges

`docs/RESEARCH/evidence_tiers_chicagology.md` § 9 ends with a sentence that names its own
unfinished half:

> Nine pages remain undeclared at tier 4 or weaker … `chicagology_prefire062`,
> `chicagology_prefire276` and `wikipedia_chicago_river` all *look* like the same case, and
> none of them was opened here. Looking like the case is what this memo exists to stop being
> sufficient.

This memo is those three pages opened. The test is the one § 2 of that memo set: can the
document carrying this dataset's claim be named, dated and placed on the ladder in
`data/source.schema.json` on its own terms? Each page was fetched live on 2026-08-11 and read
in full, and the transcribed documents were identified from **the page's own printed
attributions**, not from the citation this project had already written.

The answer came back differently for each of the three, which is the finding.

## 2. `chicagology_prefire062` — the frog pond · tier 4 → **3**

**What the record said it was.** *"Quoting the Chicago American, 9 July 1836."*

**What it is.** A transcription of **A. T. Andreas, *History of Chicago*, Volume I (1884)** —
the page sets it under that heading in as many words — carrying the chapter on how Chicago's
streets were graded, turnpiked and eventually planked. The frog pond appears inside Andreas's
own prose, mid-paragraph:

> As late as July 9, 1836, the American calls attention to a pond of water on Lake Street,
> corner of La Salle, inhabited by frogs. "It smells strong now, and in a few days will send
> out a horrible stench."

**So the citation was wrong in the way two of the three were wrong last time.** It named the
newspaper as though the page reprinted it. The page reprints Andreas; Andreas quotes the
newspaper. That is the third source record in this dataset found misdescribing its own page
(`prefire273` said it compiled Andreas and the Fergus series; `prefire278`'s record described
only the 1833 town code and not the Western Hotel material four claims rest on).

**And the rung is 3, not 1, which is the judgement worth writing down.** The temptation is to
read "a period newspaper of 1836" and declare rung 1. The declaration refuses it: the document
on this page is Andreas, a compilation from pioneer testimony, and the *Chicago American* of
9 July 1836 has never been opened by this project. Promoting the record to 1 would let a future
value stand on a newspaper nobody here has read, laundered through a compilation — which is the
`drloih` fault (§ 8 of the previous memo) arriving from the respectable direction.

**One date correction.** `docs/STATUS.md` § 44 wrote this page as "quoting the *Chicago
American* of 9 July 1835". It is **1836** — the record had it right and the status entry did
not. One year after the scene date, in the same week of the year, which is what makes it usable
for the July phenology of a standing pond at all.

**Not declared:** a paragraph on the 1848 pavements quoted from "Brass's History" (Bross, as
the page prints it), and Sanborn and photographic plates of 1862-1869. No claim here rests on
either; they are apparatus.

**Nothing in the dataset cites this page today.** It is queued for S2 parcel (c) — the pond at
Lake and La Salle — and the regrade is what will let that claim be graded honestly when it is
written.

## 3. `chicagology_prefire276` — Fort Dearborn II · tier 4 → **2**

**What it is.** The *Chicago Magazine* of **15 May 1857**, printed under its own attribution,
followed by a further passage attributed to *Chicago Magazine*, May 1857. It is the same
publication and the same kind of reading that took `chicagology_prefire273` to rung 2 on
2026-08-10: written twenty-five to forty years after the events, from what the oldest residents
told the writer, and it says so — *"From the description given to us by several of the oldest
inhabitants, who are now permanent residents among us…"*

What this project draws from it is the fort's occupation:

> Again on the breaking out of the Sauk, (Black Hawk) war, in 1832, it was re-occupied … and
> its occupancy continued till the removal of the Indians in 1836.

That is the corroboration, independent of Andreas and of Wentworth, behind the settled finding
that Fort Dearborn was an occupied post on 1835-07-01 (`docs/RESEARCH/fort_dearborn.md` § 3).

**What is deliberately NOT declared is the more instructive half.** Two other things are printed
on this page, and both are period documents:

| printed on the page | rung it would carry | declared? |
|---|---|---|
| *Chicago Magazine*, 15 May 1857 | 2 | **yes** |
| *Chicago Tribune*, 27 March 1856 — proposals for purchase of the government buildings | 1 | no |
| Woodcut after Hesler's 1855 photograph, with a dimensioned lettered key | — | no |

Declaring the Tribune notice would derive this record to **rung 1**. Nothing in this dataset
rests on it, and nothing is going to: the sale and demolition of the fort in 1856 is not a claim
about 1835. Declaring it would put the record's rung on evidence the project does not stand on,
which is precisely the apparatus rule the declaration field was written with.

**And leaving the woodcut caption undeclared is now a mechanism rather than a warning.** The
record has carried a paragraph in capitals since it was written: the three dimensions on this
page (*the parade-ground, 80x200 ft.*; the commandant's quarters *brick, about 25x50 ft.*; the
officers' quarters *wood, about 30x60 ft.*) are in a caption chicagology does not attribute,
the original has not been found, and Andreas vol. 1 does not carry it. A true sentence, and
until now nothing could see it. The caption is not among the declared documents, so nothing on
this page lends it a rung — and the fort parcel, when it reaches for a dimension, has to answer
for that rather than remember the warning.

## 4. `wikipedia_chicago_river` — read, and it reprints nothing

**This is the one that was not the case, and it took a new state in the schema to say so.**

The Swearingen material is **one sentence of Wikipedia's own prose** with a footnote on the end:

> Lieutenant James Strode Swearingen, who led the troops from Detroit to Chicago to establish
> the fort, described the river as being about 30 yd wide and upwards of 18 ft deep at the
> place where the fort was intended to be built; the riverbanks were 8 ft high on the south
> side and 6 ft on the north.[^] *Journal of Lieutenant James Strode Swearingen reproduced in
> Quaife 1913, pp. 373–377.*

Not a word of the journal is printed. A **citation is not a transcription**, and the difference
is the whole content of the previous memo: a rung is a judgement about a document, and there is
no document on this page for the judgement to be about. It stays at tier 4, and the eight other
things this dataset takes from it (the harbour-works chronology, after Holland and Andreas) are
the same shape.

**The footnote is the prize.** This record has asked, since the day it was written, that someone
*chase Swearingen to a primary printing before promoting anything derived from it*. The printing
is now named, dated and paginated: **Milo Milton Quaife, *Chicago and the Old Northwest,
1673–1835* (University of Chicago Press, 1913), pp. 373–377.** Five pages, and the 1803
soundings stop resting on an encyclopedia. That is research, not modelling, and it is queued
rather than done here — see § 6.

**And the flat-water claim turns out not to rest on Swearingen at all.** This is the finding
with a consequence. `terrain_spec.json` grades the ground `water` claim **`documented`**, citing
this record alone, and it is one of the six values `check_evidence_ladder` warns about. Its own
note says what it is claiming: *"Flat. Pre-reversal flow was sluggish and outward into the lake
with a near-zero surface gradient."* Swearingen gives a width, a depth and two bank heights — no
gradient, and all of it measured at the fort, 1.2 miles downstream. The sentence that actually
supports the claim is elsewhere on the page, in the *Reversing the flow* section:

> By the time Europeans arrived, the Chicago River flowed sluggishly into Lake Michigan from
> Chicago's flat plain.

It carries no footnote of its own. The nearest one is to a 1998 Illinois State Geological
Survey paper about the ice-age ridge, a mile inland, and it is attached to the sentence before.

So § 43's ambiguity — *either the value is over-graded or the source is under-tiered* — is
settled for this one, and it is the first of the six to be settled in the **over-graded**
direction: the source is correctly tiered at 4, and a `documented` grade on unfootnoted
encyclopedia prose is not defensible. What that costs is stated in § 6, because a confidence is
a mesh input.

## 5. The state the vocabulary did not have

A page nobody has opened and a page opened and found to reprint nothing **declare exactly the
same thing: nothing**. The validator's note has been counting both as "unread rather than
wrong", and after this memo that sentence would have been false about a page that had been read
carefully — a true sentence describing something no check could see, which is the fault this
whole family of gates exists to end, arriving inside the gate written to end it.

`carries_no_document` is the third state: not a flag, the reading itself — what was looked for,
what is printed there instead, and where the page sends you. Two rules hold it:

- **It is mutually exclusive with `transcribes`.** A page either reprints a document or it does
  not, and both fields are readings of the same page.
- **A record declaring it may not be graded at or above the testimony rung.** There is no
  document on the page for the rung to be a judgement about. The self-test's discriminating case
  is exactly that: every field well formed, the reading honestly recorded, and the number
  claiming a judgement about a document the page does not contain.

The counter now reports the three states separately: **6 records derive their rung from the
document they carry, 1 page was read and reprints none, 6 remain undeclared** — down from nine,
and the six that are left are genuinely unopened.

## 6. What is queued, and what it costs

- **Quaife 1913, pp. 373–377.** Fetch the reprinting of Swearingen's journal and give the 1803
  soundings a source record of their own at their own rung. Free of any bake *unless* it is then
  cited from `terrain_spec.json`, whose source ids are inside the terrain's staleness hash.
- **The ground `water` claim is over-graded.** `documented` → `inferred` is the honest grade for
  a flat surface argued from an unfootnoted encyclopedia sentence plus this project's own datum
  definition. A confidence is a mesh input, so it stales the ground and lands with a Blender
  bake, which this runner does not have. Queued in `docs/ROADMAP.md` § S5 beside the four
  `drloih` values, for the same reason and at the same price.
- **Six pages still undeclared**: `chicago_temple_history`, `chicagology_first_post_office`,
  `chicagology_lastwardance`, `chicagology_prefire274`, `drloih_hotels`, `drloih_wolf_point`.
  The last two are the ones that matter and they are not solvable this way — both are
  unfootnoted blog compilations whose own records say *never as sole evidence*, and neither has
  a web archive.

## 7. What this does not settle

- **Whether a rung is the right rung.** Unchanged from the previous memo. Declaring makes the
  judgement re-derivable, not correct.
- **Whether a declared document says what its note claims.** A human read put it there and a
  human read is what would overturn it.
- **Under-declaration is still invisible.** Both pages here carry documents deliberately left
  out on the apparatus rule, and no check can distinguish that from having missed them. § 3's
  table is the mitigation: the undeclared documents are written down beside the declared one, so
  the choice can be argued with.
- **The ladder gate is per-value and the declaration is per-record.** A source lends its rung to
  every attribute that lists it, so neither regrade here improves the evidence for any
  particular claim — and neither page is cited by anything today.
