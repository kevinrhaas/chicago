# Fort Dearborn — block-house

> Part of the Fort Dearborn complex. The plan source, the scale derivation, the garrison chronology, the wrong-fort guards and the three corrections to `docs/research/04-structures-south.md` are all in **`docs/RESEARCH/fort_dearborn.md`**, which this memo does not repeat.

## What is evidence

- **The south-west corner, three times.** Andreas p. 84: "The block-house was in the southwest
  corner." Hubbard 1827: "the block-house was located at the southwest". The 1830 plan draws a
  heavy hollow block projecting outside that angle — the only element drawn that way.
- Wentworth records that when the reservation was sold in 1839 "the land whereon the block-house
  stood … was not parted with", so it was still a landmark a decade after the army left.

## The guard

The widely repeated "two blockhouses, one on the south-east corner, the other at the
north-west" is the **first** fort. Its source passage ends "Such was the old Fort previous to
1812". This fort has one block-house and two bastions, and the bastions are part of
`fort_dearborn_palisade`.

## The measurement, and why it was not quietly reduced

Off the plan at the derived scale it comes out **about 48 × 32 ft**, which is large for a
period block-house — 20 to 30 ft square is the type. It is either an unusually large one or,
more likely, the plate drawing the block-house together with the return of the curtain round it
as a single heavy block. The measurement is recorded rather than shrunk to the expected size,
because shrinking it would substitute a typology for the only plan that exists.

## What is ours

Nearly everything else: height, the jetty of the upper storey, the loopholes, the pyramid roof
and the stack — **L42**. Nothing reached describes this block-house beyond the words
"block-house" and "southwest".
