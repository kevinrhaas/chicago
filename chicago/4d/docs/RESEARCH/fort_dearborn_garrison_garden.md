# Fort Dearborn — the garrison garden

> Part of the Fort Dearborn complex. The plan source, the scale derivation, the garrison chronology, the wrong-fort guards and the three corrections to `docs/research/04-structures-south.md` are all in **`docs/RESEARCH/fort_dearborn.md`**, which this memo does not repeat.

## What is evidence

- **The 1830 Harrison plan** draws a large diamond south-west of the fort and labels it
  "**Garden for the Garrison**", with the Big Barn with Cupola on the river bank beyond its
  north-west corner and Charles Jouett's grave beyond its south-east one.
- **Kinzie, 1831**: "Beyond the parade-ground, which extended south of the pickets, were the
  **company gardens, well filled with currant-bushes and young fruit-trees**."

## The fence is read from a drawing convention

The plate draws two of the plot's sides as a continuous **zigzag**. A zigzag boundary on a
period American survey is the convention for a **worm (snake) rail fence**, which is the fence
a garrison with axes and no post-hole digger builds. The convention is the reading; the sheet
never says "fence". So `wall_kind` is `inferred` and every dimension of the fence is
`conjectural` — **L45**.

## The planting is documented and not built

`data/flora/` has no cultivated zone and no garden species, so the record carries a documented
chip over an empty rectangle of ground. The single most evocative detail anyone recorded about
this place is in the data and invisible in the model. `geometry: absent`, **L45**.

## A guard

"The ground on the south side was enclosed and cultivated as a garden" is the **first** fort's
garden — its passage ends "Such was the old Fort previous to 1812". This record does not rest
on it.
