## New primary text read: close-dated Chicago workwear for 1904

**Sears, Roebuck and Co., Catalogue No.111, Spring1902**. Metadata https://archive.org/metadata/sears-roebuck-catalogue-111 gives creator Sears, date **1902-03**, 704-page illustrated trade catalog. Actual full OCR read at https://archive.org/stream/sears-roebuck-catalogue-111/Sears%20Roebuck%20Catalogue%20111_djvu.txt . Header within the relevant text identifies the Chicago firm and catalog111. These are printed US1902 commercial descriptions: reasoned US public-domain basis by publication date, **not** an explicit rights field in IA metadata. This pass read text only; catalog illustration geometry and page-number location remain unverified. Stable exact locators are section and article numbers below.

Section **“MEN’S AND BOYS’ OVERALLS, WORKING PANTS AND JACKETS”**, item **41R702 Everett Overalls**: black/gray striped denim, double-sewn throughout, continuous fly, patent buttons, front/watch/hip pocket description. OCR is noisy; button construction and exact pocket count/shape require page inspection before modeling. Item **41R706 Hercules Overalls** describes nine-ounce York denim with double sewing/reinforcement. Numeric weights are OCR readings, not measured fabric specifications.

Section **“MEN’S JACKETS”**:

- **41R738/41R740**, Amoskeag blue-and-white check jackets, with different material grades.
- **41R742**, black-and-white hairline checked denim coat, described for grain-elevator men, grocers and lumbermen; buttons to neck with turn-down collar and multiple pockets. This is unusually useful occupational evidence for a generic work adult, but does not identify a teamster's actual purchases.
- **41R746**, blue-denim jacket described as eight-ounce, cut full **28in long**, buttons to collar, sleeve buttons and outside pocket. The 28in description is a catalog size/design statement, not a requirement to make every adult's jacket 0.7112m regardless of size.
- Adjacent **Railroad Jacket41R734**, extra-quality twilled black sateen, closes to neck, diagonal watch pocket, pencil/outside pockets, removable eyelet buttons; described for baggagemen, firemen, brakemen, grocerymen and similar work. This is a distinct garment option, not an invitation to give every civilian railway uniform.
- Adjacent **41R736**, conductor/mail-agent bib overalls of the same goods. Their occupational label must not be silently generalized to every wagon driver.

This securely precedes July1904. It gives a real Chicago-commercial workwear alternative to dressing all transport workers in formal top-hat livery. It does not justify branded uniform lettering, recovered historical colors from photographs or a named face. The separate archive `catalogueno11200sear` normalizes to an ambiguous `190-` imprint; it was inspected as a lead but **not** used as exact1904 evidence.
