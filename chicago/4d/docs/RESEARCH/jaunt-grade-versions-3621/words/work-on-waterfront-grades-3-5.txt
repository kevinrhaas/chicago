== premise
Read the town's notices asking for workers. Ask about jobs at two of Newberry and Dole's warehouses. Leave with a work slip, or a good idea of where to try next.

== opening
JAUNT: WORK ON THE WATERFRONT. It is morning, and you need a job. The good news is that Chicago in 1835 needs everybody. Buildings go up as fast as boards come in. Every boat needs unloading. The papers have notices that say workers are wanted right away. The bad news is that new people keep coming by land and by water. Half of them want work too.

Choose what you can do. Read the notices. Ask at two of Newberry and Dole's buildings. Then plan your next step over breakfast.

== stop notices
The Democrat newspaper wants a young helper to learn the printing business. On 17 June a hardware store printed a notice too. It wanted a copper worker and a tin worker right away. A town growing this fast needs printers for its notices. It needs tin workers for its stovepipes. Since 1833, every stovepipe going through a roof must have tin or iron around it, or the owner pays a five dollar fine.

== stop packing
A history book puts George Dole's 1832 warehouse and meat yard near Lake and Dearborn Streets. Dole started the town's meat packing business here. It grew into one of the giant businesses that made Chicago famous. But packing meat is winter work. Pigs were brought in to be packed in December. By 1834 that work had moved to the South Branch of the river. In July there is nothing to pack.

== narrative packing
Ask them to remember you, or go on to the river?

== label packing remember
Ask to be remembered for the packing

== consequence packing remember
A promise to ask again when there is packing to do.

== label packing go-on
Go on to the river

== consequence packing go-on
There is nothing to ask about here in July.

== stop forwarding
Newberry and Dole have put ads in the paper since November 1833. They take in goods and send them on. In June they were named the town's agents for the steamboat Michigan. In July 1834, a big sailing ship called the Illinois came in through the new harbor. People cheered her at this firm's dock. Every ship since then has needed workers to unload her. Nobody puts ads in the paper for those jobs.

== narrative forwarding
Ask about work, or watch the dock?

== label forwarding offer
Offer a day's carrying

== consequence forwarding offer
You get a work slip for a day of carrying when the next ship unloads. No pay is written on it.

== label forwarding watch
Watch the wharf and ask nothing

== consequence forwarding watch
You watch how goods come off the water, and move on.

== stop breakfast
Have breakfast at the Exchange Coffee House at Lake and Wells Streets. Mark Beaubien built it, and the Murphys have run it since August 1834. Long ago the county set the price of breakfast at twenty-five cents. Think about your morning. You saw two notices for skilled workers, a meat yard waiting for winter, and a dock that never stops.

== narrative breakfast
Where will you ask first tomorrow?

== label breakfast answer
Answer a printed notice

== consequence breakfast answer
You will apply where a notice asked for workers.

== label breakfast boat
Wait on the wharf for a vessel

== consequence breakfast boat
You will be at the river when a sail comes in.

== label breakfast around
Keep asking around town

== consequence breakfast around
You will visit the stores and yards and ask in person.

== leg 0
Take your skill to the warehouses.

== ending apprentice
You will answer the newspaper's notice for a young printing helper. It was printed six weeks ago, so someone may already have the job. Ask anyway, and ask early.

== ending tin-and-copper
You will take your tin and copper skills to the hardware store. Its notice wanted both kinds of workers right away. But that was two weeks ago, and someone may have answered first. Go before your breakfast gets cold.

== ending no-notice-for-a-back
No notice you read asked for a strong back. The papers ask for skilled workers. Carrying jobs are found on the dock, by asking in person. Tomorrow, try the river first.

== ending chit-for-the-boat
You have a work slip for a day of carrying when the next ship unloads. No pay is written on it. Be on the dock when a sail comes in.

== ending remembered-for-the-winter
You asked them to remember you when there is packing to do. That is a winter promise, with no pay, no name and no date. Hold on to it until December, when the pigs come in.

== ending a-days-work-in-prospect
No work today, but a plan for tomorrow. You saw two notices asking for skilled workers, a meat yard waiting for winter, and a company that moves goods by the boatload. A day's work is coming.

A made-up note about a morning spent looking for work. It shows the notices that asked for workers, and where to ask again. No pay or job offer is written on it.
