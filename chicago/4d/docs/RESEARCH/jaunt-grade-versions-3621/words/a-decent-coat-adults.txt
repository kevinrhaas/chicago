== opening
JAUNT: A DECENT COAT. You came west to make something of yourself, like everyone stepping off the boats this summer, and so far you have made a bed-share in a log boarding house and a coat gone grey with road dust. Tonight there is a call to pay at the Sauganash. Tomorrow there is work, if the work holds.

Chicago does not much care what you wear. At a ball here in 1834 the New York writer Charles Hoffman found 'a complete medley of all ranks, ages, professions, trades and occupations': 'a veteran officer in full uniform balancing to a tradesman's daughter, still in her short frock and trousers', while 'a pair of Cinderella-like slippers would chassez across with a brace of thick-soled brogans'. A town that mixed, and judged, all at once.

Still, first impressions are currency in a place where nobody knows your family. Walk South Water Street, weigh what you can afford against what you want to be taken for, and decide.

== stop ready-made
Start one door from Dearborn at John Holbrook's. On 10 June 1835 he advertised clothing, boots and shoes, wholesale and retail, as agent for the manufacturers, with new goods arriving 'every fifteen or twenty days'. That is the boom town's bargain: coats cut by the hundred in eastern shops, crated, shipped up the lakes and hung here for whoever has the money tonight. No tailor, no fitting, no questions. A clerk off the boat can pass for a man of property by suppertime.

== stop cloth
A block west at Clark, Harmon, Loomis and Company sell dry goods. Their winter card listed broadcloths, cassimeres, satinets, flannels and goats' hair camlet, with heavy Russia duck and canvas, and in June they still advertise dry goods, wholesale and retail. Good broadcloth is how a newcomer says they mean to stay. But cloth is a promise, not a coat: someone must cut it and sew it, and the cutting takes days you do not have and money you have not yet earned.

== stop dress
One more block west, to Mrs Rufus Brown's log boarding house behind Peck's store. The store in front was the village's first two-storey frame, built of lumber 'sawed from black walnut trees' and 'brought from Detroit by a boat'; the town has dressed itself up a good deal since, and so has every speculator in it. Lay the choice out on the bed you share with a stranger who snores and keeps his boots on. A call tonight wants polish. Tomorrow's work wants wear. One coat, and one purse, must serve both, and nobody here is going to lend you the difference.

== stop the-call
On to Lake and Market and the Sauganash, white with bright-blue shutters in Juliette Kinzie's memory, and repaired and newly fitted up this June. Its landlord, Mark Beaubien, is 'the fiddler par excellence of the town', and when he plays, Judge Caton said, he could 'set every heel and toe in the room in active motion'. The room will be hot and loud, and every man in it will be sizing up every other. Decide what you wear.

== ending ready-tonight
You go to the call in a plain made coat and keep the travelling coat for tomorrow's work. By the second tune nobody can tell a Holbrook coat from a New York tailor's, and by the third, with the whiskey going round, nobody cares. You have spent money you meant to save, and bought, for one evening, the look of someone who belongs here.

== ending brushed
You go to the call brushed and neat in the coat you came in, the dust beaten out of it on Mrs Brown's step, with a length of good broadcloth in mind for the day your wages come. In a room of slippers and brogans you fit in well enough, and your purse goes home as heavy as it came.
