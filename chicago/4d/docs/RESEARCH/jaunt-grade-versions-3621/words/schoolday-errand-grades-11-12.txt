== premise

== opening
JAUNT: A SCHOOLDAY ERRAND. Your household has a solitary question: where could a child obtain an education? In this settlement, reaching school constitutes half the lesson, and the schoolmaster Granville Sproat recalled carrying the smaller scholars on his back, "not daring to trust them on the slippery plank", and once tumbling into the mire with a child in his arms.

Ride past the locations where Chicago's earliest classes assembled, on both sides of the river, and conclude where the Democrat is printed, with a notice dated today concerning the construction of a proper school house.

== stop michigan-street
On Michigan Street, between Cass and Rush, Andreas has John Watkins, engaged in 1833, subsequently teaching in a house that Moses and Kirkland identify as Richard J. Hamilton's residence. Watkins had commenced in 1832 "in a log house about twelve feet square", with merely twelve pupils during his first quarter, so Chicago's educational system originated on a remarkably modest scale.

== stop north-bank
Down to the bank east of Clark, where Andreas reports that Hamilton and Owen constructed a school-house in 1833, the first erected specifically for the purpose, although Watkins himself did not recall it; by 1835 it functioned as the North Side's public school. South of the river, Sproat's school convened upstairs in the Baptist church, and he characterized it as "a very pleasant one, the pupils obedient and attentive".

== stop the-log-house
Across the river to State Street, adjacent to the former military reservation, Andreas situates Eliza Chappel's infant school of 1833 and 1834 in a log house, although other accounts dispute the location. In 1834 the school relocated into the Presbyterian church.

== narrative the-log-house
Where the settlement's youngest scholars initially assembled for instruction.

== stop the-notice
Proceed to South Water and Clark, where the Democrat was printed from 1833. Today's number repeats a notice: School District No. 4 convenes in the Presbyterian Church on 7 July at six, to consider constructing a school house. Funds are scarce because the school section was sold in 1833 for $38,865, and "The sale was a great mistake": retained twenty years, it would have generated a hundred times more, a costly lesson in public stewardship.

== ending the-meeting
No school is recorded yet, but the notice is transcribed: 7 July, six in the evening, the Presbyterian Church. Whether a school house materializes depends upon the meeting, and you intend to participate.

== ending a-long-way
The north-bank school is recorded, but the river intervenes between it and the commercial district, constituting an arduous journey each morning for a child of five. Somebody may have to carry the little one, precisely as Sproat did.

== ending a-scholars-walk
The north-bank school is recorded: a walk and a river crossing each morning, no considerable hardship for a child of ten. Whether accommodation exists for one additional scholar remains a question for the schoolmaster.

== ending still-asking
Nothing is recorded and nothing transcribed, so the inquiry returns home precisely as it departed. The schools you passed are authentic, the meeting is next week, and the investigation continues.
