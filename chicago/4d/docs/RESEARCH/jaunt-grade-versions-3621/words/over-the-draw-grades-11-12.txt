== premise
Cross the settlement's first drawbridge into the North Side and survey what occupied the far bank: a brickyard, the first building constructed for a school and a brick hotel under construction, with nothing to tally or transport.

== opening
JAUNT: OVER THE DRAW TO THE NORTH SIDE. On this autumn afternoon, most of the town's commerce occupies the south bank, but the North Side is where prosperity intends to reside: by 1836 "The greater portion of the families of wealth, education and high social position" lived there, and the new Lake House functioned as their "fashionable headquarters", an early instance of the city's social geography taking shape.

Cross the settlement's first drawbridge and witness the North Side's construction: a brickyard, the first building ever erected for a school, and a brick hotel ascending.

== stop the-draw
Commence at the foot of Dearborn Street, at the settlement's first drawbridge, constructed in 1834 by the ship-carpenter Nelson R. Norton. Moses and Kirkland record it as "300 feet in length, by sixteen in width", with "a double draw", whereas Andreas specifies a sixty-foot opening for craft and gallows frames at either end. Whenever a schooner ascends the river, the draw rises and all traffic waits. Cross deliberately to the northern bank.

== narrative the-draw
The solitary dependable passage across the river from the commercial district.

== stop the-brickyard
Off the draw and a short distance west along the bank, Andreas situates Tyler K. Blodgett's brickyard of spring 1833, between Dearborn and Clark, with Henry S. Lampman as the settlement's first brickmaker. The first brick building outside the fort was constructed that year for John Noble, on the lot adjacent to where the Lake House now ascends. A solitary drying shed represents the entire operation.

== narrative the-brickyard
Where the settlement's masonry originated.

== stop the-school-house
Several steps further, immediately east of Clark Street, Andreas reports that Hamilton and Owen constructed a school-house in 1833, the first building erected for educational purposes in Chicago, and that in 1835 John Watkins taught the North Side's public school here. Watkins himself did not recall the building, an understandable lapse in a settlement transforming so rapidly. His inaugural class, in 1832, assembled "in a log house about twelve feet square", with twelve pupils.

== narrative the-school-house
An educational institution standing on the riverbank.

== stop the-lake-house
East along the bank to Rush Street and the Lake House. Andreas describes the completed hotel as brick, three stories with a basement, inaugurated in autumn 1836. It "aimed to cater for only the highest class of guests", and it will become the North Side's "fashionable headquarters, where were given select parties". Presently it consists merely of walls and scaffolding. Determine how to conclude the excursion.

== narrative the-lake-house
A hotel under construction, and a choice of where to stop.

== ending on-the-north-side
You conclude the excursion on the North Side, at the Lake House by Rush Street, having crossed by the draw and surveyed a brickyard, a school-house and the settlement's most ambitious hotel under construction. Within a year this will constitute the most prestigious address in Chicago.

== ending back-on-south-water
You return westward along the bank and across the Dearborn Street draw to South Water Street, where the excursion commenced, while behind you the North Side accumulates, one brick at a time.
