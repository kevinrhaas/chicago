== opening
JAUNT: OVER THE DRAW TO THE NORTH SIDE. An autumn afternoon. Most of the town's trade is on the south bank, among the mud, the warehouses and the bar-rooms, but the North Side is where the money is going to live: by 1836 'The greater portion of the families of wealth, education and high social position' lived there, and the new Lake House was their 'fashionable headquarters, where were given select parties'. A river, and a drawbridge, between the people who do the work and the people who are invited.

Cross the town's first drawbridge and watch the North Side being made: a brickyard, the first house ever built for a school, and a brick hotel going up for the highest class of guests.

== stop the-draw
Start at the foot of Dearborn Street, at the town's first drawbridge, built in 1834 by the ship-carpenter Nelson R. Norton. Moses and Kirkland make it '300 feet in length, by sixteen in width', with 'a double draw'; Andreas gives a sixty-foot opening for craft and gallows frames at either end. When a schooner comes up the river, the draw goes up and everyone waits, the merchant and the labourer alike: the river sets the town's hours, not the town. Cross to the north bank.

== stop the-brickyard
Off the draw and a short way west along the bank, where Andreas puts Tyler K. Blodgett's brickyard of spring 1833, between Dearborn and Clark, with Henry S. Lampman the town's first brickmaker. Brick is hard, dirty work, and it is what the rich build with. The first brick building outside the fort went up that year, for John Noble, on the lot next to where the Lake House now rises. In a town of 165 new frames, one brick house was a statement. One drying shed stands for the whole yard.

== stop the-school-house
A few steps on, just east of Clark Street, Andreas says Hamilton and Owen built a school-house in 1833, the first house built for a school in Chicago, and that in 1835 John Watkins taught the North Side's public school here. Watkins himself did not remember the building, which happens in a town that changes this fast. His first class, in 1832, a subscription school paid for by the parents who could afford it, met 'in a log house about twelve feet square', with twelve pupils.

== stop the-lake-house
East along the bank to Rush Street and the Lake House. Andreas describes the finished hotel: brick, three storeys and a basement, opened in autumn 1836. It 'aimed to cater for only the highest class of guests', and it will be the North Side's 'fashionable headquarters, where were given select parties': select meaning that most of the town is not invited. For now it is walls and scaffolding, and the men on the scaffolding will never be guests. Choose how to end the outing.

== ending on-the-north-side
You end the outing on the North Side, at the Lake House by Rush Street, having crossed by the draw and seen a brickyard, a school-house and the town's grandest hotel going up. In a year this will be the best address in Chicago, and the hardest to be welcome at.

== ending back-on-south-water
You walk back west along the bank and over the Dearborn Street draw to South Water Street, where the outing began and the real work of the town goes on. Behind you the North Side is filling in, one brick at a time, for people who will rarely cross back.
