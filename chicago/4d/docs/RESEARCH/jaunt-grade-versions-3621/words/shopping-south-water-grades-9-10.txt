== premise
Fill a modest household list along the working riverfront without purchasing everything you see.

== opening
JAUNT: SHOPPING SOUTH WATER STREET. This is the town's main street, and it is hardly an attractive one: the river on one side, plank walks and mud on the other, and every store crammed with merchandise unloaded from the boats. In 1833 the Democrat quoted tea at 87 cents to $1.25 a pound and calicos "from 15 to 38 cents" a yard.

You have three dollars and a short household list: something for a cough, a staple and tea. Three stores on South Water sell them, and you will tally your purchases on Lake Street, where the town's business is heading next.

== stop remedy
Begin at Philo Carpenter's, the druggist at the Sign of the Golden Mortar. He landed in 1832 while cholera was raging at the fort, sold his drugs from a log house beside the river, and built this store the following summer. He also "prepared and circulated the first temperance pledge" in town. His June notice lists aloes, ipecac, liquorice ball and tooth powder.

== narrative remedy
Purchase the remedy now, or hope the household's cough eventually passes on its own.

== label remedy liquorice
Liquorice ball for a cough — 25¢

== consequence remedy liquorice
A small paper of it goes into the basket beside the household list.

== stop staples
A short distance east, Peck's store occupies the corner of LaSalle and South Water. His 1833 card lists dry goods, hardware, groceries and crockery, with fifty barrels of superfine flour on hand, and an 1834 notice adds flannels, ginghams and calicoes to the inventory.

== narrative staples
Choose the single staple the household needs most, or preserve the purse for later.

== label staples flour
Flour for the bin — $2

== consequence staples flour
The staple the household cannot do without, consuming most of the purse.

== label staples calico
A length of calico — $1.25

== consequence staples calico
Cloth for mending, while the bread waits for the next trip.

== stop compare
Further east at Clark Street stands Harmon, Loomis and Company, whose June 1835 notice offers Young Hyson and Souchong tea, loaf sugar, crockery and glassware. Andreas specifies the corner of Clark and South Water, although not which corner. Business is flourishing: next year the firm will erect "the Harmon and Loomis block of four-story brick stores on South Water street".

== narrative compare
Compare what is on the shelf with what remains in the purse, and choose accordingly.

== label compare hyson
Young Hyson tea and loaf sugar — $1

== consequence compare hyson
The final item on the list, with sugar to accompany it.

== label compare souchong
A smaller paper of Souchong — 40¢

== consequence compare souchong
The purse is short, so a cheaper tea substitutes for the Hyson.

== label compare bowl
A crockery bowl instead — 50¢

== consequence compare bowl
Not on the list, but the old bowl is cracked, so the tea must wait.

== stop receipt
Drive south up Clark to Lake Street, where in 1834, Moses and Kirkland write, business "began to extend to Lake street", and Thomas Church was among that year's arrivals. A modern note credits him with the first store building on this street, a two-story frame, although it names no stock, so nothing is purchased here. Tally your basket carefully.

== narrative receipt
Calculate what the basket contains and what the purse has remaining.

== leg 0
With the cough attended to, choose the essential staple the household needs most urgently.

== leg 1
Tea is the final item on the list, and the purse is considerably lighter now.

== leg 2
With nothing remaining to purchase, find a quiet spot away from the river and calculate the total.

== ending list-filled
A staple, a remedy and tea: with the list crossed off and a few coins remaining, the household is provided for this week, and you understand South Water Street better than most newcomers do.

== ending list-short
Part of the list must wait for another expedition, so you return home with whatever the purse permitted, and South Water Street, mud and all, will still be there.

== keepsake text
A fictional household list and receipt from three South Water Street stores, tallied on Lake Street.
