== premise
Marshall Field Jr. has a wife, three children, a big new wing on his house, and the biggest fortune in Chicago coming to him. On this November day he has one year and one day left.

== opening
JAUNT: ONE YEAR AND A DAY. It is a Monday afternoon, 21 November 1904, at the Field houses on Prairie Avenue.

There are four stops between two houses, the father's and the son's. Marshall Field built one of the biggest stores in America. He is the richest man in Chicago. His only living son lives two doors away. The son is 36. All his life he has waited to become the man his father is. Count the days with him. One year and one day from now, a gun will go off in the house at 1919.

Start at the son's front door.

== stop son
This is 1919 Prairie, the house of Mr. and Mrs. Marshall Field Jr. He bought it in 1890. Two years ago builders added a huge new part to it. He married Albertine Huck, and they have three children: Henry, Marshall, and little Gwendolyn. Look at them on the front walk this afternoon. The nurse holds the youngest, and the boys wear their coats. Everything a man could want is on this walk, except his own name. His name belongs to his father. All his life he has been called Junior. Walk north two doors to his father's house.

== caption son
Marshall Field Jr. and his wife come out to the walk, where the nurse is waiting with their children.

== stop father
This is 1905 Prairie, the father's house, where the son grew up. It was the first house in Chicago with electric wires in every room. There was a first son, Louis, who died as a baby. This son lived. He was raised to one day run the great store, whose motto was "Give the lady what she wants". His father is the richest and most powerful businessman in Chicago, and he does not give up control. In his will he plans to skip his son. The fortune will be saved for forty years for the grandsons playing on the walk at 1919. The son walks up for his Monday visit. Benson the butler lets him in. This house will be torn down in 1955.

== caption father
Marshall Field Jr. walks up to his father's door. Thomas Benson opens it for him.

== stop talk
Walk up the street and look back at the two houses. The street sees a good son visiting his father. But behind the curtains, people gossip. A few blocks west is a rough part of town with a famous club where rich young men go at night. Some people say Marshall Field Jr. goes there too. Nobody has proved it, and nobody ever will. A year and a day from now, a story will spread that he was really shot at that club and carried home. Other people will tell it a different way. Go back to 1919.

== caption talk
A man and a woman walk past the Field houses. The man nods toward 1919.

== stop shot
This is 1919 again, late in the afternoon. The children have gone inside. On 22 November 1905, one year and a day from now, Marshall Field Jr. will be shot in this house. He will live five more days. At the hospital he will say he shot himself with the gun by accident. The officials will agree it was an accident while he was looking at the gun. Some people will say he was at the club that night, but no one will prove it. Look at the lit windows. The son of the richest man in Chicago is home for dinner. He has one year and one day left.

== caption shot
A maid closes the curtains at 1919 as the light fades. The nurse takes the children inside.

== ending six-weeks
The walk ends here. Marshall Field Jr. died in the hospital on 27 November 1905. His father was 71. On New Year's Day the father played golf in the cold and caught a bad sickness of the lungs. Six weeks after his son, Marshall Field Sr. died too. The fortune was saved for the boys from the front walk at 1919, just as the will had planned. The son was never anything but Junior. His sons got everything.
