== premise
Observe from Wolf Point what Daniel Elston and Company's works up the North Branch manufactured, compose a short list on South Water Street, choose what to carry, and bring it home to a boarding house behind Peck's store.

== opening
JAUNT: SOAP AND CANDLES. Dusk arrives noticeably earlier now, because autumn means the long darkness is approaching: once the lake freezes, "during the long six months of winter the inhabitants, were cut off from the rest of the world". Light and soap are what carry a crowded boarding house through those isolated months.

You board in the log house behind Peck's store, so look up the North Branch toward the soap and candle works, compose a short list on South Water Street, decide what to carry, and bring it home before complete darkness.

== stop the-works
Begin at Wolf Point and gaze up the North Branch to its eastern bank, where the scene positions Daniel Elston and Company's works, although no source identifies the actual site. Their notice, printed during 1833 and 1834, offered hard and soft soap, wax and other candles, and paid cash for tallow and household ashes. Every family's kitchen fat and fireplace ashes return to it transformed into soap and light.

== narrative the-works
Tallow and ashes enter the works, while soap and light emerge for the town.

== label the-works light-first
Light for the evenings matters most

== consequence the-works light-first
Candles are placed prominently at the top of the household list.

== label the-works soap-first
Soap for washing day matters most

== consequence the-works soap-first
Soap is placed prominently at the top of the household list.

== stop the-list
Across the river to Jones's grocery on South Water Street. His November 1833 notice offered sugar and most varieties of provisions at the lowest prices, although whether he stocked soap or candles it does not specify, and only the street itself is evidence. Candles mean fire, and the town understands the danger: since 1833 every stovepipe passing through a roof must be "guarded by tin or sheet iron".

== narrative the-list
Compose the list carefully, and determine what you will carry home.

== label the-list candles
Carry candles home

== consequence the-list candles
A bundle of candles, dipped in tallow and tied together with string.

== label the-list soap
Carry a cake of hard soap

== consequence the-list soap
One cake of hard soap, practically as heavy as a brick.

== label the-list both
Carry some of each

== consequence the-list both
A considerably heavier armful, containing the entire list.

== stop lake-street
Up to Lake Street and the two-story frame store Thomas Church built, reputedly the first store building on the street. Church arrived in 1834, the year business "began to extend to Lake street". A single undated sentence constitutes all the evidence, and because it names no merchandise, nothing is purchased here.

== narrative lake-street
Shift the bundle in your arms and count what you are carrying.

== stop home
Home to the log house behind Peck's store, which Andreas identifies as Mrs Rufus Brown's, one of the first-class boarding houses. In 1834, he writes, such houses were perpetually full, occasionally with three in a bed. Nevertheless, Moses and Kirkland insist the snowbound months were "perhaps, the most enjoyable of the entire year".

== narrative home
A single candle illuminates a great deal in a crowded room.

== leg 0
With the priority between soap and light settled, the list comes next.

== leg 2
The final few steps homeward with the bundle balanced in your arms.

== ending an-armful
Soap and candles both, carried home to the log house behind Peck's, so the entire list is complete, whichever item stood at the top of it. Let the long winter come.

== ending candle-lit
Candles at the top of the list, and candles carried home, so tonight the crowded room behind Peck's has light for reading and mending, and the darkness arrives a little later.

== ending washing-day
Soap at the top of the list, and soap carried home: the shirts could wait no longer, and now they need not.

== ending the-other-errand
You set out for one item and carried home the other, which will be needed soon enough, and the list will keep until next time.

== keepsake text
A fictional note of a short list and what you carried home from it in July 1835. No real sale, price or customer of any shop is written on it.
