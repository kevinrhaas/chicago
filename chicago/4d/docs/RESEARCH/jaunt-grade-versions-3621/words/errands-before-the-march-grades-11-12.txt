== premise
Play a young runner of the settlement at Fort Dearborn during the week the evacuation order arrives, August 1812: determine who receives the intelligence from you first, and where you spend the final nights before the garrison's departure.

== opening
JAUNT: ERRANDS BEFORE THE MARCH. Monday, 10 August 1812, at the confluence of the Chicago River and Lake Michigan. You are thirteen years old, you reside in the settlement beyond the pickets of Fort Dearborn, and you are the swiftest messenger on the post: soldiers, traders and families alike entrust you with their communications. The United States has been at war with Britain since June, and last night an order arrived from the general commanding at Detroit.

This morning it will be proclaimed on the parade, and by noon every household at the river mouth will need to comprehend its consequences. Who receives it from you first, and where you are stationed during the final nights before the evacuation, depends entirely upon your decisions. The people and the locations are authentic; the messenger is your invention, and you are the messenger.

== stop the-order
10 August 1812, morning, on the parade. General William Hull's order is proclaimed, and it commences: 'It is with regret I order the Evacuation of your Post owing to the want of Provisions'. The garrison is to withdraw, every weapon and all the ammunition are to be destroyed, and the merchandise in the government's store distributed, a portion of it to 'the Poor & needy of your Post'. According to Wau-Bun, the officers' 'unanimous advice was, to remain where they were, and fortify themselves as strongly as possible.' Captain Heald nevertheless resolves to obey, and you, having absorbed every syllable from the rear of the assembly, are already maneuvering toward the gate.

== narrative the-order
Across the river, or westward to the government buildings?

== label the-order kinzies
Across to the Kinzies'

== consequence the-order kinzies
The merchant who provisions the garrison deserves the intelligence first.

== label the-order agency
Westward to the Agency House

== consequence the-order agency
The families sheltering there will expect the news before anybody else.

== stop kinzie
Across the river and up the embankment to the Kinzie residence. Wau-Bun recollects 'the dwelling-house and trading-establishment of Mr. Kinzie' on the northern bank, and 'The fort was situated on the southern bank, directly opposite this mansion'. In January Captain Heald designated 'Messrs. Kinzie & Forsyth' the fort's exclusive sutlers, the solitary merchants authorized to sell to its soldiers. Lieutenant Helm's wife Margaret is the 'step-daughter of old John Kinzie', so within this household the order constitutes family business as well. The Kinzies will not accompany the soldiers on foot: when the appointed day arrives, they intend to travel by boat.

== narrative kinzie
The merchant's residence directly opposite the fort.

== stop agency
West of the fort stand the government's log buildings: the factor's trading establishment and, adjacent to it, the Agency House, 'an old-fashioned log building, with a hall running through the centre'. Since April the settlers residing beyond the pickets have 'intrenched themselves in the Agency House'. Its piazzas 'were planked up, for greater security, port-holes were cut, and sentinels posted at night.' Captain Heald has additionally enrolled 'fifteen militiamen from the civilian population outside the fort.' Behind the factor's door remain the shelves of merchandise that the order instructs him to distribute. Before the week concludes, they will stand entirely vacant.

== narrative agency
The fortified residence west of the fort.

== stop council
12 August, afternoon, on the open ground west of the pickets, where intelligence of the order has circulated rapidly. Heald recorded that the inhabitants of the surrounding country 'came in from all quarters in order to receive the goods in the factory store, which they understood were to be given them.' Their leaders confer with him here in council, and he pledges them the merchandise. 'Captain Heald alone attended on the part of the military.' His officers remain stationed in the blockhouses commanding this ground, with the port-holes open. From the periphery of the crowd you can observe the entire proceeding while distinguishing almost nothing, and the pledge concerns the merchandise; nobody has yet mentioned the guns, the powder or the whiskey.

== narrative council
Where the clandestine labor occurs tonight, or where the road terminates?

== consequence council night-work
Something must be transported after dark, and discreet hands are required.

== label council watch-road
Maintain watch at the gate

== consequence council watch-road
Whoever approaches next, you will be the first to observe them.

== stop covered-way
13 August, evening, above the fort's north-eastern corner. Throughout the day the factory store has been systematically emptied: out went 'blankets, broadcloths, calicoes, paints'. Now, after nightfall, commences the operation Heald hopes nobody will detect. 'The same evening the ammunition and liquor were carried, part into the sally-port, and thrown into a well which had been dug there to supply the garrison with water in case of emergency'. The muskets 'were broken up and thrown into the well'. The remaining liquor proceeds 'as secretly as possible through the northern gate, the heads of the barrels knocked in, and the contents poured into the river.' Your assignment is to transport, and to maintain absolute silence.

== narrative covered-way
Where the barrels descend toward the river.

== stop the-gate
14 August, sunset, at the principal gate. The factory store stands vacant now: Heald has distributed its merchandise 'and a considerable quantity of provisions which we could not take away with us.' Captain William Wells arrives on horseback from Fort Wayne with his escort; Heald reports 'about 30 Miamies'. Wells is the uncle of Heald's wife Rebekah and has journeyed here to conduct them to safety, but Wau-Bun maintains that he arrived 'all too late': the ammunition has already been destroyed. You distinguished him on the road before anybody stationed at the gate, and tomorrow, 'at 9 o'clock in the morning', the gate will open and the garrison will march.

== narrative the-gate
The gate on the concluding evening.

== leg 0
Across the water, conveying the intelligence.

== leg 1
West of the pickets, as rapidly as your legs permit.

== leg 4
Two days afterward, following nightfall.

== leg 5
Two days afterward, at sunset.

== ending strong-grog
By morning the river will taste of whiskey, and you will comprehend the reason before anybody across the water does. You conveyed the order to the Kinzie residence on the first day and assisted in emptying the barrels on the penultimate night. On Saturday at nine the garrison departs and the Kinzies embark by boat. Whatever the fort abandons, nothing intoxicating will remain within it.

== ending news-both-ways
Your week commenced with intelligence carried out of the fort and concludes with intelligence riding into it. The Kinzie household learned of the order from you, and the gate opened for Captain Wells under your observation. Tonight the entire post remains awake, packing whatever it can transport. At nine tomorrow the band will play, the gate will open, and the column will proceed along the lake.

== ending nothing-left-behind
You informed the Agency House first, and on the penultimate night you helped guarantee that the fort would abandon neither powder nor whiskey. The sentinels on the planked-up piazzas will not be required considerably longer. On Saturday at nine the garrison and the militia depart, and the protracted, apprehensive vigil at the river mouth is finally concluded.

== ending the-last-sunset
From the Agency House on the inaugural morning to the gate at the concluding sunset, you conveyed the week's intelligence on your own feet. Captain Wells is within the walls now, the gates close behind him for the final time, and tomorrow at nine they will open again. The army will return in 1816 and construct a second fortification on this identical ground.
