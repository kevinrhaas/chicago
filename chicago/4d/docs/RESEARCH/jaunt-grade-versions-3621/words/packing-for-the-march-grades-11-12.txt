== premise
Play a young member of the civilian settlement surrounding Fort Dearborn during the final two days before the garrison's evacuation, 13 and 14 August 1812, choosing your errands and preparing your household for the march.

== opening
JAUNT: PACKING FOR THE MARCH. August 1812, and you are twelve years old. Your household occupies a log dwelling outside the pickets of Fort Dearborn, on the southern bank of the Chicago River, within the small civilian community that depends upon the garrison. The United States has declared war on Great Britain, and General William Hull, commanding at Detroit, has ordered the post's evacuation. Within two days the soldiers will march out, and your household, like the settlement's other families, will accompany them.

The locations and the documentary evidence are authentic; the young person is entirely your invention. You will not converse with the commanding officer or the chiefs, and nobody will explain the political situation to you. Observe carefully, carry what you are given, and determine which errands deserve priority.

== stop the-order
Thursday, 13 August 1812, morning, on the northern bank. Across the river stands the fort, and on this side 'the dwelling-house and trading-establishment of Mr. Kinzie', whose partnership holds the exclusive privilege of supplying the garrison. Heald's journal records that the general's order arrived on the 9th, instructing him to 'evacuate the Post of Chicago and proceed with my Command to Detroit.' According to Wau-Bun, 'The order for evacuating the post was read next morning upon parade', and the officers' 'unanimous advice was, to remain where they were, and fortify themselves as strongly as possible.' Captain Heald nevertheless intends to obey. At nine on the 15th the garrison departs, accompanied by the women and children and the twelve civilians of the militia. Your household must determine what is carried, what is distributed, and what is abandoned.

== narrative the-order
Which errand deserves priority: the government factory, or the garrison's principal gateway?

== label the-order to-factory
To the government factory, where the merchandise is being distributed

== consequence the-order to-factory
You will witness precisely what the departing garrison relinquishes.

== label the-order to-gate
To the garrison's gateway, to collect information

== consequence the-order to-gate
You will discover who is arriving before anyone officially announces it.

== stop factory
Afternoon at the factory, the government's trading establishment, one of two log houses situated a short distance southwest of the fort. Everything on its shelves is being distributed: 'blankets, broadcloths, calicoes, paints'. Heald's official report states that he delivered 'all the goods in the factory store, and a considerable quantity of provisions which we could not take away with us', although Wau-Bun dates the distribution one day earlier than his report. General Hull's order explicitly authorized it, naming 'the Friendly Indians' who might escort the garrison to Fort Wayne. It equally named 'the Poor & needy of your Post.' Your household qualifies as needy. Adjacent stands the agent's residence, its piazzas 'planked up, for greater security', with port-holes cut. You carry home a bolt of calico, and nothing additional.

== narrative factory
Whatever the departing garrison cannot transport, it distributes deliberately and conditionally.

== stop the-gate
Afternoon at the principal gateway, positioned in the middle of the southern side and flanked by the enlisted soldiers' barracks. The fortification forms a quadrangle of barracks with blockhouses at its northwestern and southeastern corners, 'inclosed within a double row of palisades'. Horsemen arrive: Captain William Wells of Fort Wayne, who according to Heald's report came 'with about 30 Miamies, for the purpose of escorting us in, by the request of General Hull.' Wau-Bun dates his arrival one day later and insists he came 'all too late', because the ammunition had already been destroyed. Wells is the uncle of Rebekah Heald, the commanding officer's wife. Nobody at the gateway pauses to explain these relationships to you. You listen attentively, and carry the information home alongside the water bucket.

== narrative the-gate
Information reaches the garrison's gateway considerably before it reaches anyone's household.

== stop covered-way
Evening, on the river side of the fort's northeastern corner, where a covered way descends to the water, 'securing thus to the garrison access in safety to the water in time of attack.' Tonight soldiers are working within it. 'The same evening the ammunition and liquor were carried, part into the sally-port, and thrown into a well'. The muskets 'were broken up and thrown into the well'. The remaining liquor departs through the northern gate, 'the heads of the barrels knocked in, and the contents poured into the river.' Heald explained that he feared 'they would make bad use of it if put in their possession.' The operation was intended to be confidential, but the knocking carries across the water, and the following morning the water tastes of 'strong grog'. Your household drinks from this river.

== narrative covered-way
Should you collect uncontaminated water upstream tonight, or postpone it until morning?

== label covered-way draw-water
Fill the household's keg upstream immediately

== consequence covered-way draw-water
Your household will possess uncontaminated water for the march.

== label covered-way keep-watch
Remain in the vicinity of the fort tomorrow

== consequence covered-way keep-watch
You will observe who arrives and who departs during the final day.

== stop river-mouth
Friday, 14 August, morning, where the river enters the lake, approximately half a mile below the fort. In 1808 Captain Whistler recorded 'John Kinzie Esq. House on the opisite side River' and declared the garrison 'perfectly secure from any ambuscade or Barrier', except that the houses opposite stood 'somewhat in the way'. Wau-Bun relates that Mrs. Kinzie's boat will descend to this river mouth tomorrow while the column marches. Your keg is filled and stoppered, and today you organize everything else: a blanket apiece, the cooking pot, the calico or the information, and the possessions that must remain behind. Tomorrow the column follows the beach southward, 'with the lake on our left, and a high sand bank on our right'.

== narrative river-mouth
The final complete day, devoted entirely to deliberate preparation.

== stop the-medal
Friday, 14 August, evening, on the parade ground within the fort, near the commanding officer's quarters on its eastern side. Black Partridge, 'a Pottawatomie chief from the Illinois River', has arrived. According to Wau-Bun he 'entered the quarters of the commanding officer' and surrendered his peace medal: 'I will not wear a token of peace while I am compelled to act as an enemy.' Quaife observes that Lieutenant Helm and Sergeant Griffith both recorded his warning, and that 'Helm alone mentions the surrender of the medal.' You witness nothing beyond a door closing across the parade, and you learn about the medal afterwards, as the entire settlement does. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens.

== narrative the-medal
An unmistakable warning, delivered during the garrison's final evening.

== leg 0
Across the river by canoe initially, then southward along the entire length of the stockade.

== leg 1
Around the stockade to the gateway where the information arrives.

== ending ready-to-walk
Night on the 14th. The bundle is tied, with the calico folded beneath the blankets and the keg stoppered beside the cooking pot, because you completed both errands and preserved your household's uncontaminated water. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens and the column marches southward along the beach. What happened there that morning is related in the jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn.

== ending the-warning
Night on the 14th. You brought home the information from the gateway, and tonight you bring home the account of the medal, which everyone in the settlement is repeating and interpreting. Nobody can unpack now: tomorrow at nine the gate opens and the column marches southward along the beach. What happened there that morning is related in the jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn.

== ending the-gift
Night on the 14th. The calico from the factory lies folded on top of the bundle, and you observed the garrison's final evening. You understand now how much was relinquished, and on whose orders. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens. What happened on the beach that morning is related in the jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn.

== ending packed
Night on the 14th. The keg is filled, the bundle is tied, and you know exactly who rode in at the gateway and which direction the column will travel in the morning. At nine the gate opens and the column marches southward. What happened on the beach that morning is related in the jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn.
