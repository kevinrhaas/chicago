== premise
Equip a rider for tomorrow's journey: boots at Holbrook's on South Water Street, a view from Wolf Point toward Miller's tannery, where the town's leather originated, the saddler at Lake and Canal, and the Green Tree to assemble everything.

== opening
JAUNT: BOOTS, LEATHER AND THE ROAD. On this spring afternoon the mud is finally drying, and you depart tomorrow. Strangers stream through this settlement "by land and by water", and the majority of them, like you, intend on "locating farther west". Before joining them you require an outfit, and an outfit fundamentally means leather: boots for the rider and tack for the horse.

Here is the complication: in this town those constitute two distinct trades, practiced in two separate establishments on opposite banks of the river. Purchase boots on South Water Street, survey the tannery where Chicago's leather manufacturing originated, consult the saddler at Lake and Canal, and assemble everything at the Green Tree tonight.

== stop ready-made
Commence one door east of Dearborn on South Water Street. In June 1835 John Holbrook advertised hats, clothing, boots and shoes, wholesale and retail, as agent for the manufacturers, with fresh additions arriving every fifteen or twenty days. These are manufactured commodities, shipped from eastern workshops and sold from inventory, because in a settlement expanding this rapidly, nobody can afford to wait for a cobbler's custom work.

== stop tannery
Across the South Branch to Wolf Point. On the far side of the North Branch, in 1831, John Miller constructed a log house on the point and operated it as a tannery, regarded as Chicago's first recorded factory, and Andreas identifies Benjamin Hall, tanner, as his partner from 1832. Before vessels delivered boots by the crate, leather here originated as hides soaking in a pit. Whether hides were still being processed this summer, no surviving source discloses.

== narrative tannery
Leather must originate as hides somewhere, and here it originated on the point.

== stop saddler
South along the western bank to Lake and Canal, where Goss and Cobb established their shop in 1833, the year Silas B. Cobb arrived, manufacturing saddles, harness, bridles, martingales and trunks to order and guaranteeing that repairing would be immediately attended to. The partnership dissolved in February 1835, and Cobb's June advertisement announces that he will continue independently. Neither printed advertisement mentions boots or shoes.

== narrative saddler

== consequence saddler bridle

== label saddler ask-boots
Ask the saddler for boots

== consequence saddler ask-boots
That falls outside his trade: his advertisement specifies saddles, harness, bridles, martingales and trunks, whereas boots and shoes were Holbrook's merchandise, back across the river.

== stop road
East along Lake to West Water and the Green Tree, which James Kinzie constructed in 1833 as the first building here erected for hotel purposes, "so called from an oak tree standing close by". Early settlers recalled Chester Ingersoll operating it from 1834 to 1837, and it endured until 1880, when it was relocated to Milwaukee Avenue. Arrange the outfit on the floor for the morning.

== narrative road
Will you depart at first light, or delay another day?

== consequence road ride

== leg 0
Whatever you purchased rides in front of you across the saddle.

== ending another-day
Another day in town, then. Whatever the outfit lacks is advertised somewhere along these streets, and the road will wait, because a day's delay is not equivalent to being unprepared.

== ending equipped
Boots from the clothier and a bridle from the saddler: leather for the rider and leather for the horse, from two specialized trades that never encroached upon each other's work. You depart at first light, equipped to travel.

== ending trunk-bound
A trunk will not ride on a saddle, so the morning commences with locating a wagon traveling your direction: the baggage is prepared, but the means of transporting it is not.

== ending half-ready
You depart with what you possess. Boots for the rider and a bridle for the horse constituted the outfit's two leathers, one from each trade, and whatever is missing, the road will inevitably demand.

== keepsake text
A fictional tally of one rider's outfit: what came from the clothier, what from the saddler, and what was left behind. No authentic sale, price or customer of either shop is recorded on it.
