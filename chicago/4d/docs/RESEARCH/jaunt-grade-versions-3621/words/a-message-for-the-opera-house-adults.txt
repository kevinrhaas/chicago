== opening
JAUNT: A MESSAGE FOR THE OPERA HOUSE. Sunday 8 October 1871, early afternoon. You are thirteen, and you earn your keep running messages for the Sherman House, the marble hotel at Clark and Randolph. Hotels keep no Sabbath. This afternoon the clerk has a folded note for Crosby's Opera House, and it is to be there before dark.

The way is yours to choose, and so is what you do with the daylight after. The streets and buildings are as the accounts give them; the messenger is invented. You know, as the city does not, what tonight will bring.

== stop sherman-house
Twenty past two at the Sherman House. The note goes inside your coat. The hotel first. It is the second Sherman House: the first, once the City Hotel, was torn down in May 1861, and the new one opened on 8 July, six stories of 'the finest Athens marble, from the quarries at Lemont', looking out over the public square. Now, its guests this week. Theodore Thomas and his sixty players are lodged here, to open ten concerts at the Opera House tomorrow night. A letter written after the fire says he 'was compelled to run for his life'. Your note goes to the same house.

== stop court-house
Court House Square, the whole block between Randolph, Clark, Washington and LaSalle, green enough to have 'the appearance of a park'. The building holds nearly everything the city and county run, 'from the jail in its basement to the mayor's quarters'. Now, the cupola. A spiral stair climbs to a balcony 120 feet up, the fire watchman's walk. Tonight it will be Mathias Schaffer up there with his spy-glass. He will sight a fire, place it wrongly near Canalport Avenue and Halsted, and the operator below will strike Box 342. When Schaffer sees his mistake, no correct alarm will follow. Out at Washington Street, and east.

== stop chamber
The Chamber of Commerce, southeast corner of Washington and LaSalle: Edward Burling's building, dedicated 30 August 1865, its great hall 'decorated with frescoes and paintings', where the Board of Trade runs the exchange that moves the city's money. Sunday, and the floor is empty. Now, the street. After 1868, Colbert and Chamberlin write, LaSalle became 'the home of Insurance Companies and commission merchants', and the banks drew close. Every one of those companies carries policies on this city, and tonight will test them. Two blocks east to the Opera House.

== stop crosbys
Crosby's Opera House, north side of Washington between State and Dearborn: five stories, a mansard roof, Painting, Sculpture, Music and Commerce in stone over the arch, the shops of Root & Cady, W. W. Kimball and H. M. Kinsley on the ground floor, and behind them 'seats for three thousand patrons.' The summer's renovation cost about $80,000, and this morning's Tribune says 'the whole house, gallery and all, is nearly sold for the first night.' You hand in the note. An hour or two before the fire, the house will be lit by gas for the first time, to see the effect. It will never open.

== stop water-tower
The Water Tower, Pine Street at Chicago Avenue, dedicated in 1867. Across the street the Pumping Station draws the city's water from a crib two miles out, clear of the 'unspeakably polluted Chicago River'. Boyington, who built Crosby's, made both in 'castellated Gothic'. Now, what the Board of Police and Fire Commissioners has been saying 'year by year': 'the water supply was insufficient', 'our fire hydrants were twice too far apart', 'wooden cornices were an abomination'. The whole city hangs on 'a single set' of pumps, under a wooden roof. It will be one of the first buildings to burn on the North Side. Home before dark.

== stop saturday-ruins
The east end of the Van Buren Street bridge. Between ten and eleven last night fire broke out in the boiler room of Lull & Holmes's planing mill on Canal Street, in a block packed with lumber, sheds and dwellings. Four blocks and close to a million dollars went. 'The piles of coal and lumber burned all day on Sunday', thousands came to look, and about a hundred families were put out of their homes. Now, the men who fought it. Many worked eighteen hours straight, until three this afternoon, and are 'nearly exhausted'. Not a drop of rain since 29 September; the southwest wind is rising, and will peak at 35 to 45 miles an hour. Home before dark.

== ending square-and-tower
Through the square and up to the Water Tower: the bell that will be rung tonight and the pumps that will fail. The note is delivered for a concert that will never be given. You walk home as the lamps are lit, the wind hard at your back, with a few hours left of the city as it is.

== ending square-and-bridge
Through the square and down to the river: the watchman's tower, then the ruins of Saturday's fire. The note is delivered. The firemen are going home to sleep, worn out, and within hours they will be called again, into the same southwest wind. Before dawn, so will much of the city.

== ending lasalle-and-tower
Round by LaSalle and up to the Water Tower: past the insurance men's offices and out to the city's one set of pumps. The note is delivered, and the Opera House is nearly sold out for a night it will not see. You reach home at sunset.

== ending lasalle-and-bridge
Round by LaSalle and down to the river, where a crowd still stares at last night's ruins and the coal piles smoke. The note is delivered. The hotel will want you again in the morning, if there is a hotel in the morning. You go home as the sun sets behind the West Side.
