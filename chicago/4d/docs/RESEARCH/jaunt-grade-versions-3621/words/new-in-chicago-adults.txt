== opening
JAUNT: NEW IN CHICAGO. Your first morning in a town incorporated less than two years ago, when 'thirteen votes were cast by the twenty-eight legal electors'. This summer it has two newspapers, a land office, more strangers than beds, and more men who mean to get rich than ways of doing it honestly. You are one of the strangers. Nobody here knows your name, your family or your debts, which is either a danger or a fresh start.

Five stops, from the forks to South Water Street: a landmark to steer by, the old mail corner, the store everyone uses, a map of the town, and a bed for tonight, if one can be had. The houses of 1834 'were always full; and full meant three in a bed sometimes, with the floor covered besides.'

== stop arrival
Begin outside the Sauganash. Recalling 1831, Juliette Kinzie described a white two-storey house with blue shutters. Moses and Kirkland call it 'the most attractive hostlery the city possessed', and its landlord Mark Beaubien, 'with his merry fiddle, the most popular of hosts'. Every newcomer learns this corner first, usually by way of its bar.

== stop mail-corner
This is the old mail corner. John S. C. Hogan, made postmaster in 1832, handed out letters 'in a room partitioned off for that purpose' in his log store. Andreas says the office moved about July 1834, and Hogan's card now offers goods 'one door below the Post Office'. Ask for your letters there, not here, and do not expect many.

== stop store
At Peck's store, on South Water at LaSalle, note a supply shop. It was the village's first two-storey frame, built of black walnut lumber 'brought from Detroit by a boat'. His own advertisements list dry goods, hardware, groceries, crockery, boots, shoes and hats. Upstairs, Peck gave the young minister Jeremiah Porter 'a lodging place and study': commerce below, conscience above.

== stop newspaper
This building keeps the Democrat's first address; by May 1835 the paper printed over Jones and King's hardware store. That spring it still ran Kinzie and Forsyth's notice of lithographed maps of the town, the School Section, Kinzie's Addition and Wabansia: a map of land for sale, printed by men selling land.

== stop lodging
Finish at Brown's boarding house. Andreas names Mrs Rufus Brown as a boarding-house keeper and places her log house behind Peck's store, which you passed a block ago. Andreas says the houses of 1834 'were always full; and full meant three in a bed sometimes, with the floor covered besides.' A spot on the floor counts as a bed, and a stranger's back against yours counts as privacy.

== ending settled-by-cost
Your route note ends with weekly board behind Peck's. Cost decided. You may share the floor and the bed, but you have an address in the fastest-growing town in the West, and the money you saved may keep you here long enough to matter.

== ending settled-by-convenience
Your route note ends with a bed a few steps from the stores. Convenience decided, at a price, and tomorrow starts close to everything, including the bars.

== ending finding-your-feet
You have a few names and corners to connect. Every old settler in this town started exactly here: new, muddy, short of money and looking for a bed. Some of them got rich. Most of them only got older.
