== premise
Rough-faced stone, deeply recessed round arches and turrets: four Romanesque fronts between Eighteenth Street and Twenty-first.

== opening
JAUNT: ROMANESQUE STYLE. A winter afternoon, 1904, with the elms bare and the stone exposed. In the 1880s a Boston architect, Henry Hobson Richardson, made heavy masonry fashionable again: rough-faced blocks, deep round arches resembling a castle gate, and towers. Chicago embraced it, and for a few years its wealthy residents wanted fortresses.

Four houses on foot, from 1800 to 2110 Prairie. Look for rock-faced stone, arches that appear to sink into the wall, and turrets.

Begin with the house that launched the trend, the one its neighbors resented.

== stop glessner
1800 Prairie, Glessner House, the street's original Richardsonian Romanesque design. Its street walls are brick faced with alternating six- and eight-inch sheets of granite, cut deeper at the openings so the entire wall appears to be solid stone, beneath a roof of unglazed red tiles. Richardson, all 370 pounds of him, insisted that the windows of a city house were "not to look out of", so the street side is nearly blank while the family rooms open onto a courtyard behind. George Pullman, across the corner, complained about "that thing staring me in the face". Across the avenue and slightly south, the style found imitators.

== stop ames
1811 Prairie, the Coleman house of 1886, designed by Cobb and Frost, who were well known for their Romanesque work. This façade is rough-faced brown sandstone. The 1888 plate displays the style's signature: a deep round arch on clustered columns above a broad stair, with a corbelled oriel projecting above it like a lookout on a castle rampart. Notice that the arch is the door itself: in this style, the entrance becomes a tunnel into the stone. Walk south past Twentieth Street for two smaller fortresses.

== stop r2011
2011 Prairie, a neighbor of the Mayer house. The district register classifies it as Romanesque: a rough ashlar façade, a round-arched entrance, and a rounded corner turret beneath a conical cap. A turret on a city lot is pure theater, a castle tower grafted onto a townhouse. Across the avenue and a block farther, the last of the four compresses the style onto one narrow lot.

== stop rees
2110 Prairie, Harriet Rees's narrow house, which the district register attributes to Cobb and Frost in 1888 and classifies as Romanesque: a limestone façade beneath a tall gable facing the avenue. Two years after their Coleman house, the same architects interpreted the style in pale stone for a woman's residence. Compare the two: the same firm, with opposite colors.

== ending finish
The Romanesque walk ends here. Rough stone, round arches and towers defined the fashion of the 1880s on this street.

Yet Richardson's fortress outlived nearly every house surrounding it, and it still stands. Walk back up to Eighteenth Street and examine it once more in the winter light.
