== premise
Tall round-topped windows, fancy roof edges on carved brackets, and high front steps: five Italianate houses from 1612 to 2031 Prairie, some of the oldest on the street.

== opening
JAUNT: ITALIANATE STYLE. It is an autumn morning in 1904. Long ago, before the steep roofs and the granite, Prairie Avenue was built in the Italianate style. The houses were dressed like country houses in Italy. That was the fashion in the 1860s, when the first big houses went up here.

Look for three things. Look for tall windows with round tops. Look for a deep edge along the roof, held up by carved brackets. And look for high front steps that lift the door above the street. There are five houses on foot, from 1612 to 2031 Prairie.

Start at 1612, the best Italianate front on the street.

== stop goodman
This is 1612 Prairie, Joseph Goodman's house. It was built in 1869, two years before the Great Fire. An old photo shows it made of stone. Every window has a round top with a long middle stone. A stone band runs across the front. At the top of the high steps there is an arched porch on columns. The high steps matter. A visitor climbs far above the street and its dust before the door opens. The house will be torn down around 1960. Walk south one block.

== stop hibbard
This is 1701 Prairie, William Hibbard's house, also built in 1869. Mr. Hibbard ran a big hardware business. His architect was William Le Baron Jenney. Look for the low roof with brackets under its edges, over a stone base. More rooms were added at the back in 1874. Later, Jenney would build the first skyscraper. This house will be torn down in 1932. Two doors south is something rare here: an Italianate house made of wood.

== stop walker
This is 1720 Prairie, the Walker house, built of wood. It has a low roof on deep brackets, a pointed part in the middle, a bay window and a porch all across the front. The brick and stone houses around it show which way the street went. In 1910 this will be the first of many Prairie Avenue houses torn down, as homes give way to offices and factories. Compare its wooden brackets with the stone ones nearby. Cross Eighteenth Street to a house that changed its hat.

== stop jones
This is 1834 Prairie, Fernando Jones's house, built in 1866. Mr. Jones was one of Chicago's first settlers. His house was built Italianate. Twenty years later, the whole street had steep French roofs. So in 1886 Mr. Jones added a steep roof too, to look in style with his neighbors. Here is an Italianate house in a newer hat. It will be torn down in 1942. Prairie Avenue was a street where neighbors tried to keep up. One more stop is past Twentieth Street.

== stop tolman
This is 2031 Prairie, the north house in a row of three built in 1869. Their fronts are made of pale limestone. Each has a flat roof behind an edge with brackets, over three floors. The three houses share one roof line, like one big Italian palace, but each has its own door. Look for where the brackets stop and start again.

== ending finish
That is the end of the Italianate walk. Count the brackets you passed. These are the street's oldest fronts, built before the fire and before the big fortunes came.

By 1904 they already look old-fashioned next to the steep roofs and the granite. Try the Second Empire or Romanesque walks to see what came next.
