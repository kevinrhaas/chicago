== premise
Three days after Arthur Caton died in a New York hotel, the whole street is whispering about his wife, Delia, and the richest man in Chicago, who lives just across the back yards.

== opening
JAUNT: THE MERCHANT AND MRS. CATON. It is Monday morning, 21 November 1904. A telegram has come from New York, and the blinds are down in a house on Calumet Avenue.

There are four stops, on foot. You go from the widow's door, through the back yards the servants share, to the front steps of Marshall Field, the richest store owner in Chicago. Everybody on Prairie Avenue knows this story. Nobody says it out loud. This morning the only people talking are the ones who carry the coal.

Start at Calumet Avenue, at the house with the blinds pulled down.

== stop widow
This is Calumet Avenue, one street behind Prairie, at the side of the Caton house. The blinds are down. On Friday Arthur Caton died at a big hotel in New York. Nobody is sure why. Some said his liver failed. Others said it was a sickness of the belly. He was a judge's son and a lawyer. He loved golf and polo, and he raised horses and dogs on the family farm. His wife, Delia, gave the best parties in town. They had no children. Look at the side yard. The news is going from the house out to the stable. Go around the back, to the yards between Calumet and Prairie.

== caption widow
In the Caton side yard a maid brings the news to the coachman. He takes off his hat. The groom stops work at the coach house door.

== stop yards
These are the back yards, by the back fence. Turn around, and the Field house is right there, with its coach house on the alley. The servants of the two houses have shared this ground for years, and they have heard the talk. People said there was a secret tunnel under the ground from the Caton house to the Field house. A man who grew up here said that was silly, and it was. But Delia did sometimes travel to Europe with Marshall Field without her husband. Now Arthur is dead, and Field's wife died eight years ago. A washerwoman from the Field house leans on the fence. Walk on to Prairie Avenue to find Mr. Field.

== caption yards
Karolina Hall comes over from the Field yard to the fence. A Caton maid is waiting there with the news.

== stop field
This is 1905 Prairie, the Marshall Field house. It was built in 1876 and cost about two million dollars. It was the first house in Chicago with electric wires in every room. Field is the richest store owner in the city. Long ago he asked his first wife, Nannie, to marry him by jumping onto her train as it left Chicago. Their marriage was not happy, and Nannie died in France in 1896. A man who knew them said Field fell in love with his friend's wife, Delia, years before he could marry her. In those days people did not get divorced, so they waited. Thomas Benson, the butler, holds the door. John Dunford, the coachman, comes around from the stable. This house will be torn down in 1955.

== caption field
Marshall Field comes out his door. He stops on the steps and looks east, toward Calumet Avenue, before his coachman reaches him.

== stop talk
From up the street, the Field house looks like the others on the row. It is not. Field did not like to make speeches. One night at a big Caton dinner, people asked him to say a few words. Delia jumped up and spoke for him. She said, "Mr. Field just wants me to remind you that the White Sale starts next Monday". It was a joke about his store. The guests never forgot it. They said it showed how close those two were. The ladies walking past this morning do not look at the house, but they talk about nothing else. Arthur Caton is not yet buried. Everyone can guess what happens next.

== caption talk
Two ladies walk past the Field house with their heads close together. They do not look at it.

== leg 0
Through the gap to the back yards: the Caton fence on one side, the Field coach house on the other.

== leg 1
Around by the side path to Prairie Avenue and the front of 1905, the house everyone gossips about.

== leg 2
North along the sidewalk to look back at the Field house, the way the neighbors do.

== ending london
The walk ends here. They waited less than a year. In 1905 Delia and Marshall Field were married in London, in a quiet wedding with only a few friends. They came home to Chicago as husband and wife. But in January 1906 Marshall Field died. He caught a bad sickness of the lungs after playing golf on New Year's Day. After thirty years of waiting, they had only a few months. Delia moved to Washington, where her house was called the Pink Palace. She lived until 1937.
