== opening
JAUNT: SHOPPING SOUTH WATER STREET. This is the town's main street, and it is not a pretty one: the river on one side, plank walks and mud on the other, and every store crammed with goods off the boats and priced for people who have no choice. In 1833 the Democrat quoted tea at 87 cents to $1.25 a pound, calicos 'from 15 to 38 cents' a yard, and 'whiskey 40 cents a gallon'. A gallon of whiskey cost less than a third of a pound of the best tea.

You have three dollars and a short household list: something for a cough, a staple and tea. Three stores on South Water sell them; tally up on Lake Street, where the town's business is heading next. Every coin you spend is one you will not have when the cough gets worse.

== stop remedy
Start at Philo Carpenter's, the druggist at the Sign of the Golden Mortar. He landed in July 1832 while cholera was at the fort and 'all ordinary intercourse had been suspended', opened his drugs in 'a small log house standing near the river', and built this store the next summer. He also 'prepared and circulated the first temperance pledge among the convivial citizens' of the town, which tells you what he thought of them. His June notice lists aloes, ipecac, liquorice ball, tooth powder, surgical instruments and trusses: purges, emetics, and something for the hernia you got hauling freight.

== stop staples
A short way east, Peck's store holds the corner of LaSalle and South Water. His 1833 card lists dry goods, hardware, groceries and crockery, with fifty barrels of superfine flour on hand; an 1834 notice adds flannels, ginghams and calicoes. Flour is the one thing a household cannot do without, and the one thing a merchant with fifty barrels on hand never has to worry about. Choose what you need most, and what you can go without.

== stop compare
On east at Clark Street stands Harmon, Loomis and Company. Their June 1835 notice offers Young Hyson and Souchong tea, loaf sugar, crockery and glassware. Andreas gives the corner of Clark and South Water, not which corner. Business is very good: next year the firm will put up 'the Harmon and Loomis block of four-story brick stores on South Water street', built on the margins of a thousand small purses like yours. Compare what is on the shelf with what is left in your hand.

== stop receipt
Drive up to Lake Street. In 1834, Moses and Kirkland write, business 'began to extend to Lake street', and Thomas Church was among that year's arrivals who became leading citizens. A modern note credits him with the first store building on this street, a two-storey frame. It names no stock, so nothing is bought here. Tally your basket, and your purse, and see which one is lighter.

== ending list-filled
A staple, a remedy and tea: the list is crossed off, and a few coins remain. The household is provided for this week, and you know South Water Street better than most newcomers, including what it costs to be poor on it.

== ending list-short
Some of the list waits for another day, and someone at home will go without. You go home with what the purse allowed, and South Water Street, mud and all, will still be there, with its prices.
