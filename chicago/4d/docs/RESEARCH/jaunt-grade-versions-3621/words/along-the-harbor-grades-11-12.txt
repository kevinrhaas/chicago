== premise
Trace commerce back to its source: a forwarding house on the river, the Dearborn Street draw, the light at the mouth, and the two piers still advancing into the lake. Depart with a route note.

== opening
JAUNT: ALONG THE WORKING HARBOR. A summer morning, and the lake constitutes the town's highway. Three years ago Chicago possessed no harbor whatsoever, merely a river obstructed by a sand bar. Then the army excavated a channel through the bar, the piers advanced into the lake, and in 1834 "the schooner Illinois, the first large vessel to enter the river, sailed into the harbor amid the rejoicings of the people". That year 180 vessels discharged cargo here.

Trace the freight back along its route: from a forwarding house on the river, beneath the Dearborn Street draw and past the lighthouse, out to the piers, both still extending into the lake as federal engineering reshapes the shoreline.

== stop the-forwarding-house
Commence at Newberry and Dole's warehouse on the river. In the Democrat of 1 July 1835 they advertise storage, forwarding and commission services as agents for the Merchants' Line, and the forwarding house is the essential intermediary of frontier commerce: goods unloaded from lake vessels land at establishments like theirs and are redistributed to every store in town. When the schooner Illinois ascended the new channel in July 1834, she was cheered at Newberry and Dole's wharf. Which bank the warehouse occupied remains contested; its commercial function does not.

== narrative the-forwarding-house
What the town cannot manufacture, the lake delivers.

== stop the-draw
East to Dearborn Street and the settlement's first drawbridge, constructed in 1834 by the ship-carpenter Nelson R. Norton. Moses and Kirkland record it as "300 feet in length, by sixteen in width", with "a double draw"; Andreas specifies a sixty-foot opening for craft and gallows frames at either end. Vessels repeatedly collided with it, necessitating repairs in 1834 and again this year. This week the American's Marine Journal enters the schooners Hiram, arriving from Michigan with lumber, Philips, Whig and Jesse Smith, and comparable schooners navigate out to the lake through this draw. Remain on the south bank.

== narrative the-draw
Where the river is crossed, and navigated.

== stop the-light
Continue along the south bank to the light at the river's mouth, the first established on Lake Michigan. The original tower collapsed in 1831 before completion; Mr. Jackson erected this replacement, forty feet high, in 1832, with a fourteen-inch reflector in its lantern. Moses and Kirkland supply an unexpected detail: "Samuel S. Lasby was the first, and Mark Beaubien the last keeper of the old light house". The settlement's fiddler and tavern-keeper evidently maintained the lighthouse as well, a characteristic accumulation of roles in a frontier town.

== narrative the-light
A mark for vessels making the river.

== stop the-piers
Proceed to the south pier's root. Across the cut stands the north pier, the weather pier that excludes the drifting sand. The Democrat reprinted the federal report: 180 vessels arrived and discharged here in 1834, and Congress has appropriated $32,800 more toward the harbor's completion. Both piers lengthen throughout the summer; by season's end the north pier will extend 1,260 feet into the lake. A vessel drawing excessive water for the bar still anchors in the offing: in May the brig Illinois lay off here while French Canadians in Mackinaw boats lightered her passengers and cargo ashore. Select the mark for your route note.

Your note reads: in by the end of the north pier, past the light, beneath the Dearborn draw. By autumn that pier will extend 1,260 feet into the lake, rendering your mark obsolete; a harbor in perpetual expansion is an enviable inconvenience for an ambitious town.

== ending a-note-that-keeps
Your note reads: in between the piers, past the light at the mouth, beneath the Dearborn draw to the forwarding houses. Of everything recorded on it, the light constitutes the solitary mark that remains fixed throughout this summer of construction.
