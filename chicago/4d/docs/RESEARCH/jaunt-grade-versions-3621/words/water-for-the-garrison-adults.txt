== opening
JAUNT: WATER FOR THE GARRISON. 14 August 1812, first light, at the first Fort Dearborn. You are twelve, from a family living outside the pickets, and you carry the garrison's water: buckets up from the river every morning. War with Britain has been declared. Tomorrow at nine the garrison marches out for Detroit, under orders, and everyone inside is going with it.

Choose your way to the river, and whose door gets the last bucket. The fort and its people are as the records give them; the water carrier is invented.

== stop the-fort
Daybreak outside the pickets. The fort first. In 1809 William Johnston called it 'the neatest and best wooden garrison in the United States': barracks round a quadrangle, a blockhouse at two opposite corners, a double row of palisades round all of it, so that a man who scaled the first row found himself in a lane swept by the blockhouses' fire. Now, the order. Hull's letter reached Heald on the 9th. When it was read on parade, his officers' 'unanimous advice was, to remain where they were, and fortify themselves as strongly as possible.' Heald chose obedience. Two ways down to the water.

== stop covered-way
The north-east corner, where the covered way runs to the river, built for water 'in time of attack'. Last night it was used for something else. The ammunition and liquor 'were carried, part into the sally-port, and thrown into a well'; the muskets were broken up and thrown in after them; the rest of the barrels were carried out by the northern gate, their heads knocked in and poured into the river. The noise gave it away. Heald wrote that he feared they 'would make bad use of it'. And the river this morning? As one man put it, 'strong grog.' Fill your buckets anyway, and up to the main gate.

== stop wharf
The wharf under the bank, where the stores come ashore. Whistler marked it in 1808 and judged the fort 'perfectly secure from any ambuscade', except for the houses across the river, 'somewhat in the way'. The biggest is John Kinzie's, straight over the water: trader, and since January, with his partner Forsyth, the fort's exclusive sutler. Whistler had a picket fence over there pulled up in case it served an enemy as a barrier. Fill your buckets, and climb to the main gate.

== stop soldiers
In at the main gate, the soldiers' barracks on both hands, two storeys of 'shingled Roofs and Galliaries fronting the parade', the 'lofty flagstaff' in the middle. Heald counts his strength at 'fifty-four regulars and twelve militia'. The men are packing for Detroit, and packing is thirsty work. A soldier takes one bucket and points you across the parade. One left.

== stop commandant
The commandant's quarters, east side of the parade. Nathan Heald took the post from Whistler in 1810, and in June 1811 brought home his bride, Rebekah, 'the niece of Captain William Wells'. In his own journal for this spring: 'On the 4th of May, 1812, we had a son born dead for the want of a skilful Midwife.' It is the house of a man who has had a hard year and is about to have a harder one. Leave the bucket at the door and go. Your round is done.

== stop officers
The junior officers' quarters, across the parade. Lieutenant Linai Helm came for 'the cheaper cost of living'; his wife Margaret is the 'step-daughter of old John Kinzie'. Ensign George Ronan is 'a young cadet direct from West Point'. The surgeon's mate, Isaac Van Voorhis, twenty-two, wrote last October that this would be 'the seat of millions yet unborn'. Heald's report of the march names Ronan and Van Voorhis among the dead and Helm among the prisoners. Set the bucket down by their door. Your round is done.

== ending covered-and-captain
The covered way and the captain's door: the safe way to the water, past the well that is now full of powder, muskets and rum. Your buckets are empty. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens, and no one will need water carried here again.

== ending covered-and-officers
The covered way and the officers' door. You filled your buckets at the river and tasted the grog in it. The officers who told Heald to stay are packing with everyone else. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens.

== ending wharf-and-captain
The wharf and the captain's door: the long way, past Kinzie's house across the water. The fort is packing all round you. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens.

== ending wharf-and-officers
The wharf and the officers' door, the long way round. Of the three men in that house, Heald's report of the march will list two dead and one a prisoner. Your round is done. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens.
