== premise
Ride from Wolf Point to the fort. Pass the homes and work places of six women named in old records of the town: a writer, two women who ran a hotel and a boarding house, a teacher, and the women of two of the first families.

== opening
RIDE: WOMEN OF 1835. It is a summer afternoon. You will ride from west to east, from the forks of the river to the fort. Most of what was written about this town back then was written by men, about men. They wrote about land, votes and boats. But if you read closely, women are everywhere. They ran the hotels and the boarding houses. They taught the first classes. One wrote the book that helps us see many of these buildings. Some held families together across two worlds.

There are seven stops and seven lives. Old records name each woman here, and each one still matters.

== stop robinson
This is Wolf Point. Across the water stand log cabins. An old book says Alexander Robinson and his wife's family lived here in 1831. His wife was Catherine. She was Potawatomi, and her grandfather was a leader. Her sister Archange married a man named Antoine Ouilmette. The town of Wilmette is named for them. The Robinson family kept their land for more than one hundred years.

== stop kinzie
This is John Kinzie's store. His wife, Juliette, later wrote a book about the early town. Her memories help us know what some buildings here looked like. In June 1835, just two weeks ago, she had a baby girl named Eleanor. One day Eleanor would have a daughter of her own. That girl, Juliette Low, would grow up to start the Girl Scouts.

== stop murphy
This is the Exchange Coffee House, Mark Beaubien's second hotel. An old book says Mr. and Mrs. Murphy took charge of this new hotel. They gave it its name. They have run it together since August 1834. It is a husband and wife business. The book names Mrs. Murphy as its keeper, right beside her husband.

== stop brown
This is Mrs. Rufus Brown's boarding house, behind Peck's store. An old book says she ran one of the best boarding houses in town. The houses were always full. Full meant three people in a bed, with more on the floor. She ran the business. But the records only keep her husband's name.

== stop chappel
This is Eliza Chappel's school. She ran the town's first school for very small children. It opened in 1833 in a little log house that used to be a store. One story says some children paddled canoes across the river to get to class. An old book says she should get special praise.

== stop church
This is the town's first Presbyterian church. Eliza was one of the twenty-six people who started it in 1833. In June 1835 she married its minister, Jeremiah Porter. Many years later, during a war, she led a group in the city that helped soldiers. She also cared for hurt soldiers far from home.

== stop beaubien
This is Fort Dearborn. The Beaubien family's home stands just south of it. Josette Beaubien was married to Jean Baptiste Beaubien. Her father was a trader. Her mother, Shawenoqua, was a Potawatomi woman. In July 1834, a woman named Josette Beaubien helped at a baby's baptism at St. Mary's. It was the town's first Catholic church.

== ending finish
Your ride ends at the fort. These are the women whose places stand in this town. Other women in the town's papers ran their own shops and schools. Their stories are still waiting to be found.
