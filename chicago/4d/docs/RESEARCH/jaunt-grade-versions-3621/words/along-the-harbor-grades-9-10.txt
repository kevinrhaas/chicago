== premise
Trace freight back to the lake: a forwarding house on the river, the Dearborn Street draw, the light at the mouth, and the two piers still advancing into the lake. Leave with a route note.

== opening
JAUNT: ALONG THE WORKING HARBOR. On this summer morning the lake serves as the town's highway, although three years ago Chicago possessed no harbor at all, only a river choked by a sand bar. Then the army cut through the bar, the piers went out into the lake, and in 1834 "the schooner Illinois, the first large vessel to enter the river, sailed into the harbor amid the rejoicings of the people". That year 180 vessels unloaded here.

Trace the freight back the way it arrived: from a forwarding house on the river, under the Dearborn Street draw and past the lighthouse, out to the piers, both still extending into the lake.

== stop the-forwarding-house
Begin at Newberry and Dole's warehouse on the river. In the Democrat of 1 July 1835 they advertise storage, forwarding and commission services as agents for the Merchants' Line: goods off the lake land at houses like theirs and continue to every store in town. When the schooner Illinois came up the new channel in July 1834, she was cheered at Newberry and Dole's wharf. Which bank the warehouse occupied remains disputed; the trade does not.

== narrative the-forwarding-house
What the town cannot manufacture, the lake delivers.

== stop the-draw
East to Dearborn Street and the town's first drawbridge, built in 1834 by the ship-carpenter Nelson R. Norton. Moses and Kirkland make it "300 feet in length, by sixteen in width", with "a double draw"; Andreas gives a sixty-foot opening for craft and gallows frames at either end. Because vessels repeatedly struck it, the bridge was patched in 1834 and again this year. This week the American's Marine Journal enters the schooners Hiram, arriving from Michigan with lumber, Philips, Whig and Jesse Smith, and schooners like them work out to the lake through this draw. Remain on the south bank for the remainder of the walk.

== narrative the-draw
Where the river is crossed by travelers, and passed by vessels.

== stop the-light
Continue along the south bank to the light at the river's mouth, the first on Lake Michigan. The original tower collapsed in 1831 before completion; Mr. Jackson erected this one, forty feet high, in 1832, with a fourteen-inch reflector in the lantern on top. Moses and Kirkland supply a surprise: "Samuel S. Lasby was the first, and Mark Beaubien the last keeper of the old light house". The town's fiddler and tavern-keeper maintained the lighthouse as well.

== narrative the-light
A navigational mark for vessels approaching the river entrance.

== stop the-piers
Out to the south pier's root. Across the cut stands the north pier, the weather pier that excludes the sand. The Democrat printed the federal report: 180 vessels arrived and discharged here in 1834, and Congress has appropriated $32,800 more toward the harbor. Both piers lengthen all summer; by season's end the north pier will reach 1,260 feet into the lake. A vessel drawing too much water for the bar still anchors in the offing: in May the brig Illinois lay off here while French Canadians in Mackinaw boats ferried her passengers and cargo ashore. Choose which landmark belongs on your route note.

== narrative the-piers
Where the federal harbor works meet the open lake.

== label the-piers mark-the-light
Mark the light

== consequence the-piers mark-the-light
The light goes on your note: it will stand precisely where you observed it.

== label the-piers mark-the-pier-end
Mark the end of the north pier

== consequence the-piers mark-the-pier-end
The north pier's end goes on your note, although it will extend further by autumn.

== ending a-note-to-redraw
Your note reads: in by the end of the north pier, past the light, up under the Dearborn draw. By autumn that pier will extend 1,260 feet into the lake, so your mark will require redrawing, and a harbor that keeps expanding is an enviable problem.

== ending a-note-that-keeps
Your note reads: in between the piers, past the light at the mouth, up under the Dearborn draw to the forwarding houses. Of everything recorded on it, the light is the single mark that remains fixed this summer.

== keepsake text
A fictional route note from a July 1835 walk: the forwarding house, the Dearborn draw, the light and the two piers. No vessel, cargo, keeper or bridge-tender is named on it.
