== premise
Ride past the places where Chicago's first classes met, on both sides of the river. Bring a made-up family's question about school. End with a notice in the newspaper dated today.

== opening
JAUNT: A SCHOOLDAY ERRAND. Your family has one question. Where could a child go to school? In this town, getting to school is half the lesson. A teacher named Granville Sproat remembered carrying the smaller children on his back. He did not trust them on the slippery board over the mud. Once he even fell into the mud with a child in his arms.

Ride past the places where Chicago's first classes met, on both sides of the river. End where the Democrat newspaper is printed. It has a notice dated today about building a real school house.

== stop michigan-street
On Michigan Street, a history book says John Watkins later taught school in a house here. Another book says the house was Richard Hamilton's home. Watkins started teaching in 1832 in a small log house. He had only twelve students at first. Chicago's schools started very small.

== narrative michigan-street
Once a classroom, now a home again.

== label michigan-street little-one
Ask about a child of five

== consequence michigan-street little-one
The question is for the youngest child in the family.

== label michigan-street scholar
Ask about a child of ten

== consequence michigan-street scholar
The question is for a student ready for reading and math.

== stop north-bank
Go down to the river bank east of Clark Street. A history book says a school house was built here in 1833. It was the first one built just for a school. Watkins did not remember it, though. By 1835 it was the North Side's public school. South of the river, Sproat's school met upstairs in the Baptist church. He said it was a very nice school, and the students listened and behaved well.

== narrative north-bank
Compare what the history books agree on.

== label north-bank write-it-down
Write down the north-bank school

== consequence north-bank write-it-down
A school we know was open in 1835. But the river is between it and the stores.

== label north-bank keep-looking
Keep looking for one nearer

== consequence north-bank keep-looking
Your note stays blank for now.

== stop the-log-house
Cross the river to State Street, near the old army land. A history book says Eliza Chappel taught a school for little children in a log house here in 1833 and 1834. Other books say different things. In 1834 her school moved into the Presbyterian church.

== narrative the-log-house
This is where the town's youngest students first met.

== stop the-notice
Go on to South Water and Clark Streets, where the Democrat was first printed. Today's paper has a notice. People will meet in the Presbyterian Church on 7 July at six o'clock to talk about building a school house. Money is tight. The town sold its school land in 1833. A history book says the sale was a big mistake. If the town had waited twenty years, it would have made a hundred times more.

== narrative the-notice
Keep a useful notice.

== label the-notice copy-notice
Copy out the District No. 4 notice

== consequence the-notice copy-notice
The meeting's day, time and place go in your note.

== label the-notice leave-it
Leave the notice to the paper

== consequence the-notice leave-it
You copy nothing. The question goes home just as it came.

== leg 0
The family's question rides with you, and so does the mud.

== leg 1
There is one more classroom to see, south of the river.

== ending two-answers
You wrote down the north-bank school, and you copied the meeting notice for 7 July. That is two answers to one family question: a school already teaching, and one people still have to argue about.

== ending the-meeting
You did not write down a school yet, but you copied the notice. The meeting is on 7 July, at six in the evening, at the Presbyterian Church. The meeting will decide if a school house is built. You plan to be there.

== ending a-long-way
You wrote down the north-bank school. But the river is between it and the stores. That is a long way every morning for a child of five. Somebody may have to carry the little one, like Sproat did.

== ending a-scholars-walk
You wrote down the north-bank school. It is a walk and a river crossing each morning. That is not hard for a child of ten. Is there room for one more student? You will have to ask the teacher.

== ending still-asking
You wrote nothing down and copied nothing. The question goes home just as it came. The schools you passed are real, the meeting is next week, and the asking goes on.
