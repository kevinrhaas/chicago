== premise
Cross the town's first drawbridge into the North Side and observe what stood on the far bank: a brickyard, the first house built for a school and a brick hotel under construction, with nothing to count or carry.

== opening
JAUNT: OVER THE DRAW TO THE NORTH SIDE. On this autumn afternoon, most of the town's commerce occupies the south bank, but the North Side is where the money intends to live: by 1836 "The greater portion of the families of wealth, education and high social position" resided there, and the new Lake House served as their "fashionable headquarters".

Cross the town's first drawbridge and watch the North Side being constructed: a brickyard, the first house ever built for a school, and a brick hotel rising.

== stop the-draw
Begin at the foot of Dearborn Street, at the town's first drawbridge, built in 1834 by the ship-carpenter Nelson R. Norton. Moses and Kirkland record it as "300 feet in length, by sixteen in width", with "a double draw", while Andreas gives a sixty-foot opening for craft and gallows frames at either end. Whenever a schooner comes up the river, the draw rises and everyone waits. Cross carefully to the north bank.

== narrative the-draw
The only dependable way across the river from the commercial district.

== stop the-brickyard
Off the draw and a short distance west along the bank, Andreas places Tyler K. Blodgett's brickyard of spring 1833, between Dearborn and Clark, with Henry S. Lampman as the town's first brickmaker. The first brick building outside the fort was erected that year for John Noble, on the lot adjacent to where the Lake House now rises. A single drying shed represents the entire yard.

== narrative the-brickyard
Where the town's brick industry originally began.

== stop the-school-house
A few steps further, just east of Clark Street, Andreas reports that Hamilton and Owen built a school-house in 1833, the first house built for a school in Chicago, and that in 1835 John Watkins taught the North Side's public school here. Watkins himself did not remember the building, which happens in a town transforming this quickly. His first class, in 1832, met "in a log house about twelve feet square", with twelve pupils.

== narrative the-school-house
An educational building standing on the riverbank.

== stop the-lake-house
East along the bank to Rush Street and the Lake House. Andreas describes the finished hotel as brick, three stories with a basement, opened in autumn 1836. It "aimed to cater for only the highest class of guests", and it will become the North Side's "fashionable headquarters, where were given select parties". For the present it consists of unfinished walls and scaffolding. Choose how you would prefer to conclude the outing.

== narrative the-lake-house
A hotel under construction, and a choice of where to stop.

== label the-lake-house end-here
End at the Lake House site

== consequence the-lake-house end-here
Conclude on the North Side, at the corner of Rush Street.

== label the-lake-house back-over
Back over the draw

== consequence the-lake-house back-over
Return west along the bank and over the Dearborn Street draw to where you began.

== leg 0
Across the river to the developing North Side.

== leg 1
Continue from the brickyard to the neighboring school-house.

== leg 2
Proceed along the riverbank to the building under construction.

== ending on-the-north-side
You conclude the outing on the North Side, at the Lake House by Rush Street, having crossed by the draw and observed a brickyard, a school-house and the town's grandest hotel under construction. Within a year this will be the most prestigious address in Chicago.

== ending back-on-south-water
You return west along the bank and over the Dearborn Street draw to South Water Street, where the outing began, while behind you the North Side is filling in, one brick at a time.
