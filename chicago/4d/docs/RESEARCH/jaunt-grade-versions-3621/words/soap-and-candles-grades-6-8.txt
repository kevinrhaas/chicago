== premise
See from Wolf Point what Daniel Elston and Company's works up the North Branch made, make a short list on South Water Street, choose what to carry, and bring it home to a boarding house behind Peck's store.

== opening
JAUNT: SOAP AND CANDLES. Dusk comes early now, because autumn means the long dark is close: once the lake freezes, "during the long six months of winter the inhabitants, were cut off from the rest of the world". Light and soap are what get a crowded boarding house through the winter.

You board in the log house behind Peck's store. Look up the North Branch toward the soap and candle works, make a short list on South Water Street, choose what to carry, and bring it home before full dark.

== stop the-works
Start at Wolf Point and look up the North Branch to its east bank, where the scene places Daniel Elston and Company's works, although no source gives the exact site. Their notice, printed in 1833 and 1834, offered hard and soft soap, wax and other candles, and paid cash for tallow and house ashes. Every household's kitchen fat and fireplace ashes come back to it as soap and light.

== narrative the-works
Tallow and ashes go in; soap and light come out.

== label the-works light-first
Light for the evenings matters most

== consequence the-works light-first
Candles go at the top of your list.

== label the-works soap-first
Soap for washing day matters most

== consequence the-works soap-first
Soap goes at the top of your list.

== stop the-list
Cross the river to Jones's grocery on South Water Street. His November 1833 notice offered sugar and most kinds of provisions at the lowest prices, but it does not say whether he kept soap or candles. Only the street is evidence, and candles mean fire, and the town knows it: since 1833 every stovepipe through a roof must be "guarded by tin or sheet iron".

== narrative the-list
Make your list, and decide what you will carry home.

== label the-list candles
Carry candles home

== consequence the-list candles
A bundle of candles, dipped in tallow and tied with string.

== label the-list soap
Carry a cake of hard soap

== consequence the-list soap
One cake of hard soap, as heavy as a brick.

== label the-list both
Carry some of each

== consequence the-list both
A heavier armful, with your whole list in it.

== stop lake-street
Walk up to Lake Street and the two-story frame store Thomas Church built, said to be the first store building on the street. Church arrived in 1834, the year business "began to extend to Lake street". One undated sentence is all the evidence we have, and it names no stock, so nothing is bought here.

== narrative lake-street
Shift the bundle in your arms and count what you carry.

== stop home
Head home to the log house behind Peck's store, which Andreas names as Mrs Rufus Brown's, one of the first-class boarding houses. In 1834, he writes, such houses were always full, sometimes with three in a bed. Yet Moses and Kirkland insist the snowed-in months were "perhaps, the most enjoyable of the entire year".

== narrative home
A single candle goes a long way in a crowded room.

== leg 0
With the choice between soap and light settled, your list comes next.

== leg 2
The last few steps home, with your bundle in your arms.

== ending an-armful
Soap and candles both, carried home to the log house behind Peck's. Your whole list is done, whichever item stood at the top of it. Let the long winter come.

== ending candle-lit
Candles at the top of the list, and candles carried home. Tonight the crowded room behind Peck's has light to read and mend by, and the dark comes a little later.

== ending washing-day
Soap at the top of the list, and soap carried home. The shirts could wait no longer, and now they do not have to.

== ending the-other-errand
You set out for one thing and carried home the other. It will be needed soon enough, and the list will keep until next time.

== keepsake text
A fictional note of a short list and what you carried home from it in July 1835. No real sale, price or customer of any shop is written on it.
