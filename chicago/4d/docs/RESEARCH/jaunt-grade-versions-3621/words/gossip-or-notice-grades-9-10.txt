== premise
Two rumors are circulating at the forks of the river; carry them past the Exchange to the town's two printing offices, read what the papers actually reported, and decide what you will carry back.

== opening
JAUNT: GOSSIP OR PRINTED NOTICE? Morning at the forks, and two rumors are already circulating: the land sale supposedly took in a million dollars, and a bank is reportedly opening in a brick house on the square. In a town this excited, rumors travel faster than the mail, which arrives "often irregular and long delayed", and when it finally comes, "some one with a good voice would mount a dry goods box" and read the news aloud.

This summer the town supports two competing newspapers, so carry the rumors to both printing offices and read what the papers actually reported before you repeat a single word.

== stop the-forks
Begin at the forks, beneath the Wolf Point Tavern's painted wolf sign, hung by about 1833. One account has William Walters keeping the house until 1836, while another says it stopped accepting guests in 1834. At the door the conversation concerns nothing but money: a million dollars supposedly collected at the land sale, and a bank opening on the square within weeks. Since the land office opened in May, nobody in town discusses anything else.

== narrative the-forks
Listen carefully to the rumors, but believe neither one until you have evidence.

== stop the-exchange
Across the river stands the Exchange Coffee House, which Mark Beaubien built at Lake and Wells and which Mr and Mrs Murphy have kept since August 1834. The rumors have outrun you here, and they have grown considerably along the way. Somebody at the door demands to know whether they are true.

== narrative the-exchange
Hear the rumors again, one river later and somewhat larger.

== label the-exchange promise
Promise to find out

== consequence the-exchange promise
You will owe an answer once you have finished investigating.

== label the-exchange just-listen
Just listen

== consequence the-exchange just-listen
Nobody is owed anything.

== stop the-democrat
At South Water and Clark stood the Democrat's first office, although by 20 May it was printing above Jones and King's hardware. Today's number admits it cannot state what the Land Office collected, and is only "led to believe" it was between five and some hundred thousand dollars; the surviving copy has lost the second figure. Whichever figure is correct, it is certainly not a million dollars.

== narrative the-democrat
Locate the first rumor in the newspaper's printed columns.

== label the-democrat note-the-guess
Note the paper's guess, doubt and all

== consequence the-democrat note-the-guess
Not a million, and not certain, as the paper itself acknowledges.

== label the-democrat skip-the-sale
Leave the land sale to the talkers

== consequence the-democrat skip-the-sale
Nothing is recorded in your notes at this office.

== stop the-american
Near the drawbridge stands the office of Davis's American, a newspaper only three weeks old. Its 27 June number reprints the Sangamon Journal: the State Bank's directors have decided on a branch at Chicago, but its officers are not yet known. The brick building on the square near the court house belongs to Springfield, not Chicago. Half of the rumor is accurate, which makes it the most dangerous and persuasive kind.

== narrative the-american
Decide what information you will responsibly carry back to the forks.

== label the-american carry-the-print
Carry back what was printed, doubts and all

== consequence the-american carry-the-print
Only what you read, with the papers' own qualifications.

== label the-american not-settled
Carry back that nothing is settled

== consequence the-american not-settled
Uncertainty, stated openly.

== label the-american say-nothing
Carry back nothing for now

== consequence the-american say-nothing
The rumors continue traveling without you.

== leg 0
Two rumors, traveling considerably faster than you can walk.

== leg 1
Find a newspaper against which to test each circulating rumor.

== ending true-to-the-page
You carry back both items exactly as printed: a land-sale total the Democrat would only estimate, and a branch bank approved with no officers named and no building in Chicago yet. It is less than the forks claimed, but faithful to the page, and before the year ends the census will count one bank in town, "a branch of the State bank".

== ending one-checked
You carry back the bank item as printed and leave the land sale alone, because you did not record it. It amounts to half a correction, although an honest and responsible one.

== ending not-yet-known
You carry back the news that nothing is settled: one paper would only estimate, and the other names no officer and no building. Uncertainty is a legitimate answer too.

== ending owed-an-answer
You promised an answer at the Exchange but carry none back, so the rumors will be repeated there without you until Saturday's American or next week's Democrat prints more.

== ending held-tongue
You say nothing at all, and the rumors continue circulating without your assistance. Saturday's American and next week's Democrat will print more, and until then, holding your tongue is a perfectly reasonable way to read the news.

== keepsake text
A fictional note of two rumors heard at the forks in July 1835, set beside what the Democrat and the American printed. No real person said either rumor, and no paper is made to settle what it left uncertain.
