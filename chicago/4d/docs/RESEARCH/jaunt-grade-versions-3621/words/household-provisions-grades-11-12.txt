== premise
Establish a cupboard of your own from a single block of South Water Street, with a concise list and a modest purse.

== opening
JAUNT: STOCK THE HOUSEHOLD. You have recently secured a share of a room at a boarding house behind Peck's store, along with two dollars to establish a cupboard of your own, a sum that will not extend far. When the Democrat commenced publication in 1833, flour cost $6 a barrel, butter 17 cents a pound, coffee 17 and loaf sugar 20.

The excursion comprises four stops, all along a single block of South Water Street, where you will determine what purpose the list serves, then fulfill it methodically at the grocer's, the druggist's and Peck's corner.

== stop the-room
Commence your expedition behind Peck's store at Mrs Rufus Brown's log boarding house, where you occupy a share of a room, and which Andreas identifies as one of the first-class boarding houses of the town. A share of a room is not a kitchen, so whatever you possess must fit upon a shelf. Determine what your concise list is intended to accomplish.

== narrative the-room
Determine whether the list principally serves the communal table or your private portion of the room.

== label the-room table
A list for the table

== consequence the-room table
Something invigorating to drink each morning, and an ingredient for the household's baking.

== label the-room room
A list for the room

== consequence the-room room
Something comfortably warm for the night, and a necessity for the washstand.

== stop provisions
West along the block to Jones's grocery and provision store, whose November 1833 advertisement guarantees "tea, coffee and sugar" and most varieties of provisions in stock. No source establishes precisely where the store stood, and even his initial, B or H, remains historically contested. The Democrat's 1833 prices quoted coffee at 17 cents a pound and tea at 87 cents to $1.25.

== narrative provisions
Purchase something to drink, or conserve the purse for subsequent necessities.

== label provisions coffee
Coffee and sugar — 50¢

== consequence provisions coffee
The morning coffee pot is adequately provisioned for the foreseeable future.

== label provisions tea
A paper of tea — 40¢

== consequence provisions tea
Tea for the evenings, with marginally more currency remaining in the purse.

== stop the-druggist
Return eastward to Philo Carpenter's, the druggist at the Sign of the Golden Mortar, who commenced selling drugs in 1832 in "a small log house standing near the river", subsequently constructed this establishment and diversified into salt, sugar and hardware. His notice of 27 June 1835 enumerates cream of tartar, lemon syrup, black lead and tooth powder.

== narrative the-druggist
Select one modest item from the druggist's extensive inventory, or decline entirely.

== label the-druggist tartar
Cream of tartar for the baking — 15¢

== consequence the-druggist tartar
A small paper of it, deposited conveniently on the cupboard shelf.

== label the-druggist tooth-powder
Tooth powder for the washstand — 20¢

== consequence the-druggist tooth-powder
A box of it, positioned conveniently beside the washbasin.

== stop the-corner
Conclude at Peck's corner of LaSalle and South Water, merely several steps from your original point of departure. His store was the village's first two-story frame structure, constructed of lumber "brought from Detroit by a boat". His advertisement enumerates dry goods, hardware, groceries and crockery, and a December 1834 notice supplements these with flannels and calicoes.

== narrative the-corner
Complete the basket, or retain whatever remains, then carry it around to the boarding house.

== label the-corner crockery
A crockery cup and plate — 30¢

== consequence the-corner crockery
Your own individual cup and plate at an otherwise communal table.

== label the-corner flannel
A length of flannel — 75¢

== consequence the-corner flannel
Considerable warmth for the chilly nights, although it consumes the majority of the remaining currency.

== leg 0
Commence by acquiring whatever the household list requires most urgently.

== leg 1
Something inexpensive for the cupboard shelf or the washstand, depending on your priorities.

== leg 2
One final acquisition, and the basket will be sufficiently full to transport home to the boarding house.

== ending table-laid
Something to drink and a cup of your own from which to drink it: your place at the communal boarding-house table is established, and the cupboard along with it.

== ending room-kept
Flannel for the nights and tooth powder beside the basin, so your share of the room is beginning to feel authentically your own.

== ending purse-kept
You purchased relatively little and conserved most of the purse, because a household can commence with patience, and the block's establishments will remain available tomorrow.

== ending basket-begun
The basket does not precisely correspond to the list with which you departed, which is characteristic of most first days, and the cupboard is commenced, although certainly not yet completed.

== keepsake text
A fictional receipt for a first basket of household goods, purchased along a single block of South Water Street and carried home to a boarding house.
