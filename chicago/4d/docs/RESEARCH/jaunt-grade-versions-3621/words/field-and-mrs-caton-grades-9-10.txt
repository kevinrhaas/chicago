== premise
Three days after Arthur Caton's mysterious death in a New York hotel, Prairie Avenue is whispering about his widow Delia and the wealthiest merchant in Chicago, whose mansion stands just across the alley.

== opening
JAUNT: THE MERCHANT AND MRS. CATON. Monday morning, 21 November 1904. A telegram has arrived from New York, and the blinds are drawn in a house on Calumet Avenue.

Four stops, on foot, from the widow's door, through the rear yards the servants share, to the front steps of Marshall Field, the merchant prince of Chicago. Everyone on Prairie Avenue knows this story, and no one acknowledges it aloud. Thirty years of dinners, transatlantic voyages and rumors are approaching their conclusion, and this morning the only people openly discussing it are the ones who carry the coal.

Begin on Calumet Avenue, at the house with the drawn blinds.

== stop widow
Calumet Avenue, behind Prairie, beside the Caton house, its blinds drawn. On Friday, 18 November, Arthur J. Caton died at New York's Waldorf-Astoria hotel, and the cause remains uncertain: "Some said he succumbed to liver failure. Others ascribed the demise to peritonitis". A newspaper in Ottawa, Illinois, will insinuate that he took his own life. He was the son of Judge John Dean Caton, one of the state's first chief justices, and a lawyer, sportsman and clubman who bred horses and dogs on the family farm. His wife Delia, a hardware heiress he married in 1876, hosted the most celebrated parties in Chicago. They had no children. This morning there is a widow, and the news is traveling from the house to the stable. Proceed around the back, to the yards between Calumet and Prairie.

== caption widow
In the Caton side yard a maid carries the news to the coachman, who removes his hat, while the groom halts his work at the coach house door.

== stop yards
The back yards, where Calumet meets Prairie at the rear fence. Turn around and the Field house is immediately before you, its coach house on the alley. The servants of both households have shared this ground for years and have absorbed every rumor. Arthur Meeker, who grew up on this street, recorded the most extravagant: "it was even absurdly asserted that an underground tunnel connected the Caton house on Calumet with the Field house on Prairie". Meeker dismissed it as absurd, and it was. Yet a society diarist noted that "Delia sometimes traveled to Europe with Marshall Field without Arthur accompanying them". Now Arthur is dead, Field has been a widower for eight years, and a laundress from the Field house is leaning on the fence. Continue to Prairie Avenue and find the man himself.

== caption yards
Karolina Hall crosses from the Field yard to the fence, where a Caton maid waits with the news.

== stop field
1905 Prairie, the Marshall Field mansion, constructed in 1876 for roughly $2 million and "the first in Chicago to be completely wired for electricity". Field is the most powerful businessman in the city. Decades ago he proposed to his first wife by leaping aboard her departing train; one account claims that "Mrs. Field led Marshall Field a hell-on-earth life", and Nannie Field died in France in 1896. Meeker stated the remainder bluntly: "he fell in love with his friend's wife years before he was able to marry her", and "in the Nineties, divorce was unthinkable: they had to wait for him (Arthur Caton) to die, which, distressingly, he didn't do until 1904". That was Friday. Thomas Benson, the Irish-born butler, attends the door; John Dunford, the English coachman, approaches from the stable. The mansion will be demolished in 1955.

== caption field
Marshall Field emerges from his door and pauses on the steps to gaze east, toward Calumet Avenue, before his coachman reaches him.

== stop talk
From farther up the street, the Field house resembles any other on the row; it is not. Field disliked public speaking, and one evening, at one of the Catons' lavish dinners, he was called upon to say a few words. Delia leapt to her feet and answered on his behalf: "Mr. Field just wants me to remind you that the White Sale starts next Monday". It was a joke about his department store, delivered by another man's wife at her husband's own table, and the guests who repeated it afterward described it as the evening everyone recognized how intimate those two had become. The ladies passing the Field house this morning carefully avoid looking at it, and they are discussing nothing else. Arthur Caton is not yet buried; everyone can predict what follows, and nobody dares to say it.

== ending london
The walk ends here. They waited less than a year. In 1905 they married at St. Margaret's, Westminster, in London; the license recorded Delia as forty-six and Field as seventy, and "the ceremony was quiet, and attended only by a few friends". They returned to Chicago as husband and wife, and in January 1906 Marshall Field died of pneumonia, contracted on a New Year's Day golf course in a foursome that included Robert Todd Lincoln. After three decades of waiting, they had a few months together. Delia inherited $1 million and the Prairie Avenue mansion, relocated to Washington, where her residence became known as the Pink Palace, and lived until 1937.
