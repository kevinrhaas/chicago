== premise
Play a young runner of the settlement at Fort Dearborn during the week the evacuation order arrives, August 1812: decide who receives the news from you first, and where you spend the final nights before the gate opens.

== opening
JAUNT: ERRANDS BEFORE THE MARCH. Monday, 10 August 1812, where the Chicago River meets Lake Michigan. You are thirteen years old, you live in the settlement outside the pickets of Fort Dearborn, and you are the swiftest runner on the post: soldiers, traders and families all entrust you with their messages. The United States has been at war with Britain since June, and last night an order arrived from the general commanding at Detroit.

This morning it will be read aloud on the parade, and by noon everyone at the river mouth will need to understand what it means. Who hears it from you first, and where you find yourself during the final nights before the march, is your decision. The people and the places are genuine; the runner is you.

== stop the-order
10 August 1812, morning, on the parade. General William Hull's order is read aloud, and it opens: 'It is with regret I order the Evacuation of your Post owing to the want of Provisions'. The garrison is to withdraw, every weapon and all the ammunition are to be destroyed, and the goods in the government's store distributed, some of them to 'the Poor & needy of your Post'. According to Wau-Bun, the officers' 'unanimous advice was, to remain where they were, and fortify themselves as strongly as possible.' Captain Heald nevertheless decides to obey, and you, having heard every word from the back of the crowd, are already edging toward the gate.

== stop kinzie
Across the river and up the bank stands the Kinzie house: Wau-Bun recalls 'the dwelling-house and trading-establishment of Mr. Kinzie' on the north side of the river, and 'The fort was situated on the southern bank, directly opposite this mansion'. In January Captain Heald appointed 'Messrs. Kinzie & Forsyth' the fort's exclusive sutlers, the only traders permitted to sell to its soldiers. Lieutenant Helm's wife Margaret is the 'step-daughter of old John Kinzie', so in this household the order is family business as well. The Kinzies will not march alongside the soldiers; when the day arrives, they intend to travel separately by boat.

== narrative kinzie
The trader's house directly across the river from the fort.

== stop agency
West of the fort stand the government's log buildings: the factor's trading store and, beside it, the Agency House, 'an old-fashioned log building, with a hall running through the centre'. Since April the settlers living outside the pickets have 'intrenched themselves in the Agency House'. Its piazzas 'were planked up, for greater security, port-holes were cut, and sentinels posted at night.' Captain Heald has additionally enrolled 'fifteen militiamen from the civilian population outside the fort.' Behind the factor's door wait the shelves of goods that the order instructs him to distribute. Before the week is over, every one of those shelves will stand empty.

== narrative agency
The fortified house west of the fort.

== stop council
12 August, afternoon, on the open ground west of the pickets, where news of the order has travelled quickly. Heald wrote that the people of the surrounding country 'came in from all quarters in order to receive the goods in the factory store, which they understood were to be given them.' Their leaders meet him here in council, and he promises them the goods. 'Captain Heald alone attended on the part of the military.' His officers remain in the blockhouses overlooking this ground, with the port-holes open. From the edge of the crowd you can observe the entire council while hearing almost nothing, and the promise concerns only the goods: nobody has yet mentioned the guns, the powder or the whiskey.

== stop covered-way
13 August, evening, above the fort's north-east corner. Throughout the day the factory store has been emptied: out went 'blankets, broadcloths, calicoes, paints'. Now, after dark, begins the work Heald hopes nobody outside the pickets will notice. 'The same evening the ammunition and liquor were carried, part into the sally-port, and thrown into a well which had been dug there to supply the garrison with water in case of emergency'. The muskets 'were broken up and thrown into the well'. The remaining liquor goes 'as secretly as possible through the northern gate, the heads of the barrels knocked in, and the contents poured into the river.' Your responsibility is to carry whatever you are handed, and to stay completely silent.

== narrative covered-way
Where the barrels travel down to the river.

== stop the-gate
14 August, sunset, at the main gate. The factory store is empty now: Heald has distributed its goods 'and a considerable quantity of provisions which we could not take away with us.' Captain William Wells rides in from Fort Wayne with his escort; Heald reports 'about 30 Miamies'. Wells is the uncle of Heald's wife Rebekah and has come to bring them out safely, but Wau-Bun says he arrived 'all too late': the ammunition has already been destroyed. You spotted him on the road before anybody at the gate did, and tomorrow, 'at 9 o'clock in the morning', the gate will open and the garrison will march.

== ending strong-grog
By morning the river will taste of whiskey, and you will understand the reason before anyone across the water does. You carried the order to the Kinzie house on the first day and helped empty the barrels on the second-to-last night. On Saturday at nine the garrison marches out and the Kinzies depart by boat. Whatever the fort leaves behind, there will be nothing in it left to drink.

== ending news-both-ways
Your week began with news carried out of the fort and concludes with news riding into it. The Kinzie household learned about the order from you, and the gate opened for Captain Wells while you watched. Tonight the entire post is awake, packing whatever it can carry, and at nine tomorrow the band will play, the gate will open, and the column will march out along the lake.

== ending nothing-left-behind
You informed the Agency House first, and on the second-to-last night you helped ensure that the fort would abandon no powder and no whiskey. The sentinels on the planked-up piazzas will not be required much longer. On Saturday at nine the garrison and the militia march out, and the long, anxious wait at the river mouth is finally over.

== ending the-last-sunset
From the Agency House on the first morning to the gate at the final sunset, you carried the week's news on your own feet. Captain Wells is inside now, the gates close behind him for the last time, and tomorrow at nine they will open again. The army will return in 1816 and construct a second fort on this very ground.
