== premise
Begin a cupboard of your own from one block of South Water Street, with a short list and a modest purse.

== opening
JAUNT: STOCK THE HOUSEHOLD. You have just secured a share of a room at a boarding house behind Peck's store, along with two dollars to begin a cupboard of your own, which will not stretch far. When the Democrat began publishing in 1833, flour cost $6 a barrel, butter 17 cents a pound, coffee 17 and loaf sugar 20.

The jaunt includes four stops, all along a single block of South Water Street, where you will decide what purpose the list serves, then fill it at the grocer's, the druggist's and Peck's corner.

== stop the-room
Begin behind Peck's store at Mrs Rufus Brown's log boarding house, where you have a share of a room, and which Andreas names as one of the first-class boarding houses of the town. Because a share of a room is certainly not a kitchen, whatever provisions you keep must fit on a single shelf. Decide what your short list is intended to accomplish.

== narrative the-room
Choose whether the list principally serves the shared table or your portion of the room.

== label the-room table
A list for the table

== consequence the-room table
Something refreshing to drink every morning, and an ingredient for the household's baking.

== label the-room room
A list for the room

== consequence the-room room
Something comfortably warm for the night, and a necessity for the washstand.

== stop provisions
West along the block to Jones's grocery and provision store, whose November 1833 advertisement promises "tea, coffee and sugar" and most varieties of provisions on hand. No source identifies precisely where the store stood, and even his initial, B or H, remains disputed. The Democrat's published prices for 1833 put coffee at 17 cents a pound and tea at 87 cents to $1.25.

== narrative provisions
Purchase something to drink, or preserve the purse for other necessities.

== label provisions coffee
Coffee and sugar — 50¢

== consequence provisions coffee
The morning coffee pot is adequately provided for.

== label provisions tea
A paper of tea — 40¢

== consequence provisions tea
Tea for the evenings, with slightly more money remaining in the purse.

== stop the-druggist
Return east to Philo Carpenter's, the druggist at the Sign of the Golden Mortar, who began selling drugs in 1832 in "a small log house standing near the river", then built here and expanded into salt, sugar and hardware. His notice of 27 June 1835 lists cream of tartar, lemon syrup, black lead and tooth powder.

== narrative the-druggist
Select one inexpensive item from the druggist's extensive inventory, or pass it by entirely.

== label the-druggist tartar
Cream of tartar for the baking — 15¢

== consequence the-druggist tartar
A small paper of it, placed conveniently on the cupboard shelf.

== label the-druggist tooth-powder
Tooth powder for the washstand — 20¢

== consequence the-druggist tooth-powder
A box of it, positioned conveniently beside the washbasin.

== stop the-corner
Finish at Peck's corner of LaSalle and South Water, a few steps from where you began. His store was the village's first two-story frame building, constructed of lumber "brought from Detroit by a boat". His printed advertisement lists dry goods, hardware, groceries and crockery, and a December 1834 notice adds flannels and calicoes.

== narrative the-corner
Complete the basket, or keep whatever remains, then carry it around to the boarding house.

== label the-corner crockery
A crockery cup and plate — 30¢

== consequence the-corner crockery
Your own individual cup and plate at an otherwise shared table.

== label the-corner flannel
A length of flannel — 75¢

== consequence the-corner flannel
Warmth for the chilly nights, although it consumes most of what remains in the purse.

== leg 0
Begin by purchasing whatever the list requires most urgently.

== leg 1
Something inexpensive for the cupboard shelf or the washstand.

== leg 2
One final purchase, and the basket will be sufficiently full to carry home to the boarding house.

== ending table-laid
Something to drink and a cup of your own from which to drink it: your place at the boarding-house table has begun, and the cupboard along with it.

== ending room-kept
Flannel for the nights and tooth powder beside the basin, so your share of the room is beginning to feel genuinely your own.

== ending purse-kept
You purchased relatively little and preserved most of the purse, because a household can begin with patience, and the block's stores will still be there tomorrow.

== ending basket-begun
The basket does not entirely match the list with which you set out, which is how most first days proceed, and the cupboard is begun, although certainly not yet finished.
