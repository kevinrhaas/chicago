== premise
A quiet outing past the town's two churches and its log meeting house, concluding with a call at the Sauganash.

== opening
JAUNT: A SUNDAY CIRCUIT. A spring Sunday morning. Three years ago this town possessed no church at all; then, in a single season, 1833, they arrived simultaneously: a Catholic priest said mass in one of Mark Beaubien's log cabins, a Presbyterian minister preached in the fort's carpenter's shop, and the Methodists gathered in a log room at Wolf Point. Soon the town's trustees will even prohibit "the sale of liquors on Sunday".

A neighbor expects you at the Sauganash, and along the way you will pass every place the town worships. Look from the road and observe how much three years have accomplished.

== stop st-marys
St. Mary's, on Lake Street west of State, is the town's first Catholic church. Father St. Cyr "celebrated his first mass in a log cabin belonging to Mark Beaubien" in May 1833, and that August Augustine D. Taylor raised this frame, twenty-five by thirty-five feet, its lumber transported by scow across the lake from St. Joseph at $12 a thousand. It cost about $400. Inside are rough benches for pews; outside there is no paint, no steeple and no tower.

== narrative st-marys
The plainest church in town.

== stop presbyterian
Two blocks west, at Lake and Clark, stands the Presbyterian church, dedicated on 4 January 1834. Its pastor, Jeremiah Porter, "the pioneer Protestant minister of Chicago", arrived with the troops from Fort Brady and "preached a sermon in the carpenter's shop of the garrison on the 19th of May, 1833". Until the church was built he lived and studied upstairs in Peck's store. It also serves as a meeting room: School District No. 4 is called here for 7 July.

== narrative presbyterian
Church and meeting room combined.

== stop walker
Across the South Branch at Wolf Point, Wau-Bun remembered "a small square log building, originally designed for a school-house, but occasionally used as a place of worship whenever any itinerant minister presented himself". A pioneer account credits Father Jesse Walker, the Methodist missionary, with building it, and his own log house "served the purpose of a parsonage, parlor, kitchen and audience-room". A frontier preacher made a single room accomplish everything.

== narrative walker
One log room, wherever it actually stood.

== stop the-call
Back over the bridge to Lake and Market and the Sauganash, white with blue shutters in Wau-Bun and newly fitted up according to the Democrat of 10 June 1835. Your neighbor is waiting. On a Sunday the landlord's fiddle ought to remain quiet, although Moses and Kirkland remember Mark Beaubien as "an ardent, if not always devout, Catholic", and "a liberal contributor" to St. Mary's.

== narrative the-call
Arrive where your call is expected.

== leg 0
Two blocks west, from the priest's plain frame to the minister's church.

== leg 1
Leave the town's frame churches behind for its oldest place of worship.

== leg 2
Cross once more, to the call for which you set out.

== ending among-neighbors
You reach the Sauganash's door with the entirety of the town's religious life behind you: a priest's plain frame, a minister's church that doubles as a meeting hall, and a log room at the forks that served for both school and worship. Three years ago none of it existed.
