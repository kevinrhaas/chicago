== premise
Depart town with a practical kit, reliable equipment and sufficient money for the road.

== opening
JAUNT: OUTFIT FOR THE WEST. Daybreak in spring, and the roads west are drying out. Chicago is not the end of your journey; for thousands it is a doorway, since "Many immigrants passed through the town with a view to locating farther west", arriving "by land and by water", in prairie schooners, "on horseback and on foot".

You have fifteen dollars and a considerable distance to travel. Compose your list at the Green Tree, have harness and iron attended to at Lake and Canal, then cross the river for provisions and tools. Spend carefully, because the prairie has no stores.

== stop the-list
Begin outside the Green Tree, the public house at Lake and West Water, "so called from an oak tree standing close by". Old settlers recalled Chester Ingersoll keeping it from 1834 to 1837. Travelers bound west gather in houses like this one, exchanging news of the roads and of the land offices ahead.

== narrative the-list
Draw up a short list: something to carry the load, a few tools, provisions for the road and dependable ironwork.

== stop carrying
A block west along Lake Street, at Canal, Goss and Cobb advertised saddles, harness, bridles and trunks in 1833, the year Silas B. Cobb arrived. The partners separated in February 1835, and Cobb's card announces that he will continue the business. Everything you own must travel somehow.

== narrative carrying
Determine first how the load will travel, or keep your money.

== label carrying pack
A pack and straps — $3

== consequence carrying pack
Inexpensive, although the load rides on your own back.

== label carrying harness
Harness mended for a team — $6

== consequence carrying harness
More expensive, but a hired wagon carries considerably more than you can.

== stop ironwork
At the same crossing stands Asahel Pierce's smithy, opened in October 1833. Andreas records him ironing the first Chicago-to-St Louis stage line and manufacturing "Bull" ploughs from 1834 onward, the kind of craftsman a westbound traveler should consult.

== narrative ironwork
Have the ironwork examined before the long road, or trust it as it stands.

== label ironwork repair
Have the ironwork checked — $2

== consequence ironwork repair
Tires, hooks and chains inspected before the long road.

== stop provisions
Across the river on South Water, a Jones grocery and provision store advertised in 1833, and Andreas adds forwarding and commission services by 1834. The Democrat's first numbers that year quoted flour at $6 a barrel, coffee at 17 cents and tea at up to $1.25 a pound. Even Jones's initial remains disputed.

== narrative provisions
Stock up now, or plan to purchase along the way.

== label provisions provisions
Flour, pork and coffee — $5

== consequence provisions provisions
Sufficient provisions for the first days on the road.

== stop tools
A block east, Peck's store occupies the corner of South Water and LaSalle. His own advertisements, from December 1833 through 1834, list dry goods, hardware, groceries, crockery, boots, shoes and hats, everything a departing traveler might have forgotten.

== narrative tools
Last on the list: tools for the road, or trust what you already own.

== label tools tools
An axe and a few tools — $4

== consequence tools tools
Hardware from the merchant's shelves rather than the smith's forge.

== leg 0
Before purchasing anything, decide how the load will travel.

== leg 1
Harness and ironwork are attended to at the same corner.

== leg 2
With the carrying settled, fill the load on the east bank.

== ending prepared
Your list is crossed off and the load is packed. You take the western road prepared, with whatever remains of the purse and a town diminishing behind you on the flat horizon.

== ending light
You depart lightly equipped, with money in hand and gaps in the kit. The road will have to supply the remainder, and the road is not famous for its stores.
