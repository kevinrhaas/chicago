== premise
Make a short trip across the river between the parts of town, and learn why the place where the river splits mattered.

== opening
JAUNT: ACROSS WOLF POINT. It is dawn in spring, and the river is running high. This is where Chicago began. It began at the forks, where the North and South Branches of the river meet and flow east to the lake as one. Before there were streets, there were inns here. There were the first bridges, and a ferryman who also played the fiddle.

Cross the river the way the town did. Start at the inn that gave Wolf Point its name. Go down the west bank past the Green Tree inn, over the South Branch bridge, and up to the Sauganash. There are four stops and nothing to buy, and you can see the whole early town.

== stop the-fork
Start on the west bank at the inn that named Wolf Point. It is part log and part boards. An army officer made its sign from a piece of a box, with a wolf painted on it. People are not sure if it still takes guests. One story says William Walters ran it until 1836. Another says it closed in 1834. Look east. The two branches of the river meet off this bank and flow to the lake as one river.

== narrative the-fork
Look at the fork before you cross it.

== stop west-bank
Walk south along the bank to the Green Tree, at Lake and West Water Streets. James Kinzie built it in 1833. It was the first house here built to be a hotel. It got its name from an oak tree standing close by. Chester Ingersoll ran it from 1834 to 1837. It lasted longer than any other inn in town. Sixty years later it was still standing, moved to a new street.

== narrative west-bank
Face east. The south side is where you are going.

== stop the-crossing
Go a few steps south to the South Branch bridge. At first, people said, it was just floating logs tied together. Later it stood on log posts, about ten feet wide and six feet above the water. That was high enough for wagons to pass under it on the ice in winter. People paid for it together. It cost $486.20. An old history says "the Indians of the vicinity contributed $200".

== narrative the-crossing
This is the crossing. Stay on the bridge, not in the water.

== stop south-side
Get off the bridge and go north to Lake and Market Streets. Here is the Sauganash, white and two stories tall, with bright blue wooden shutters. It was fixed up new in June 1835. People called it the nicest inn in the city. Its owner, Mark Beaubien, played the fiddle, ran the inn, sold goods, rowed the ferry and loved horse races. In winter the town raced horses on the frozen river you just crossed.

== narrative south-side
Down the west bank, over the bridge, north to this corner. Now you know the way.

== leg 0
See the two branches first. Then walk down the one you will cross.

== leg 1
The South Branch is the water between you and the south side.

== leg 2
Arrive on the south side at the town's best-known house.

== ending crossed
You crossed between the parts of town the way the town did, on the bridge and not through the water. You went from the oldest inn corner to the most famous one. Come back when the river freezes, for the horse races.
