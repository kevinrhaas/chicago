== premise
Take a quiet walk past the town's two churches and its log meeting house. End with a visit at the Sauganash.

== opening
JAUNT: A SUNDAY CIRCUIT. It is a spring Sunday morning. Three years ago this town had no church at all. Then, in just one year, 1833, churches came all at once. A Catholic priest held a church service in one of Mark Beaubien's log cabins. A Presbyterian minister preached in the fort's carpenter shop. And the Methodists met in a log room at Wolf Point. Soon the town's leaders will even ban selling alcohol on Sundays.

A neighbor is waiting for you at the Sauganash. On the way you will pass every place the town worships. Look from the road and see how much has changed in three years.

== stop st-marys
This is St. Mary's, on Lake Street west of State Street. It is the town's first Catholic church. Father St. Cyr held his first church service in a log cabin that belonged to Mark Beaubien, in May 1833. That August, a builder put up this wooden church. Its boards came by boat across the lake from St. Joseph. It cost about four hundred dollars. Inside are rough benches. Outside there is no paint, no steeple and no tower.

== narrative st-marys
This is the plainest church in town.

== stop presbyterian
Walk two blocks west to Lake and Clark Streets and the Presbyterian church. It opened on 4 January 1834. Its minister, Jeremiah Porter, came with soldiers from another fort. He gave his first church talk in the fort's carpenter shop on 19 May 1833. Until the church was built, he lived and studied upstairs in Peck's store. The church is a meeting room too. A meeting about schools will be held here on 7 July.

== narrative presbyterian
A church and a meeting room.

== stop walker
Cross the river to Wolf Point. Juliette Kinzie remembered a small square log building here. It was first meant to be a school house. Sometimes it was used for church whenever a traveling preacher came to town. One old story says Father Jesse Walker, a Methodist preacher, built it. His log house was his home, his living room, his kitchen and his church all at once. A frontier preacher made one room do everything.

== narrative walker
One log room, wherever it stood.

== stop the-call
Walk back over the bridge to Lake and Market Streets and the Sauganash. It is white with blue shutters, and it was fixed up new in June 1835. Your neighbor is waiting. On a Sunday the owner's fiddle should be quiet. A history book says Mark Beaubien was a Catholic who cared a lot, even if he did not always act very holy. He gave money to help St. Mary's.

== narrative the-call
Arrive where your neighbor is waiting.

== leg 0
Walk two blocks west, from the priest's plain church to the minister's church.

== leg 1
Leave the town's wooden churches for its oldest place of worship.

== leg 2
Cross the river once more, to the visit you set out for.

== ending among-neighbors
You reach the Sauganash's door. Behind you are all of the town's churches. You saw a priest's plain wooden church, a minister's church that is also a meeting hall, and a log room at the forks used for both school and church. Three years ago none of it was here.
