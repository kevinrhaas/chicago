== premise
Conduct several imagined social calls: present a letter of introduction from Brown's boarding house, interpret the Peck household from its card, locate where the town convenes at the Exchange, and deposit a calling card.

== opening
JAUNT: CALLING ON NEIGHBORS. You have secured a room in town this summer and carry two letters of introduction, which in 1835 constitute the conventional mechanism by which a stranger becomes a neighbor. Chicago is emphatically a community of strangers, and the Democrat found them "crowding every room of our public houses": everybody is newly arrived, everybody is introducing themselves, and everybody deposits a card.

Present a letter, interpret a merchant's household, discover where the town convenes, and leave your card. Come winter, this town dances, and at one ball Charles Hoffman observed "a veteran officer in full uniform balancing to a tradesman's daughter", a mingling of ranks characteristic of the frontier, but in summer it attends to commerce. Observe the proprieties.

== stop the-room
Commence behind Peck's store at Mrs Rufus Brown's log boarding house, which Andreas identifies among the first-class boarding houses of the town. You lodge here, carrying two letters of introduction from home. In a settlement where nearly every resident arrived within the previous three years, a letter from a respectable correspondent back east is how the community establishes who you are and whether you merit its confidence. Determine which to present first.

== narrative the-room
One letter first; the other can wait.

== label the-room merchant-letter
A letter from a merchant back east

== consequence the-room merchant-letter
It introduces you to the town's commercial establishment.

== label the-room minister-letter
A letter from your minister at home

== consequence the-room minister-letter
It introduces you to the town's religious congregations.

== stop the-merchant
Several steps around to Peck's corner of South Water and LaSalle. P. F. W. Peck arrived in July 1831 with a stock of merchandise, and when the Reverend Jeremiah Porter came in 1833 Peck provided him "a lodging place and study in the second story of his store building", where "one of the earliest Sunday-schools organized in the town" convened, an illustration of how commerce and congregation shared quarters in the young settlement. This is also the year Peck marries Mary K. Wythe of Philadelphia. Interpret the household from its card.

== narrative the-merchant
Interpret the household at its card, not at its door.

== stop the-exchange
West to Lake and Wells and the Exchange Coffee House, operated by Mr and Mrs Murphy since August 1834. The town's Democrats convened here in April and again in June 1835 to nominate their candidates, and in 1834 the dancing master J. A. Marshall requested that prospective pupils deposit their addresses here. A frontier public house functions simultaneously as post office, notice board and assembly hall. Deposit your card, or carry it onward.

== narrative the-exchange
Where the town convenes, a card is discovered.

== label the-exchange card-at-exchange
Leave your card at the Exchange

== consequence the-exchange card-at-exchange
Deposited where the 1834 notice requested addresses be left, although no caller is documented.

== label the-exchange card-kept
Keep it for the Sauganash

== consequence the-exchange card-kept
The card accompanies you to the concluding call.

== stop the-sauganash
Along Lake Street to Market stands the Sauganash, newly refurbished in June and, according to Moses and Kirkland, "the most attractive hostlery the city possessed". A week after the refurbishment the Democrat found strangers crowding every room in town, and each of them, like you, exists socially as merely a name on a card. A card remaining in your pocket is deposited here.

== narrative the-sauganash
A community of strangers, perpetually introducing themselves.

== leg 0
Letter in hand, around to the front of the block.

== leg 1
With one household interpreted, proceed to where the town assembles.

== ending merchant-at-the-exchange
You have presented a merchant's letter and deposited your card at the Exchange, where the Democrats convene and the dancing master compiled his names. By next week the appropriate people will know precisely who you are.

== ending minister-at-the-exchange
You have presented a minister's letter and deposited your card at the Exchange, where the town assembles. With fortune, a congregation will call upon you before Sunday.

== ending card-at-the-sauganash
Your card is deposited at the Sauganash, among a summer's multitude of strangers. Within a year most of them will be established neighbors, and a fortunate few will be wealthy.

== keepsake text
A fictional calling card from July 1835. No authentic introduction, caller or reply is inscribed upon it, and no named person is encountered.
