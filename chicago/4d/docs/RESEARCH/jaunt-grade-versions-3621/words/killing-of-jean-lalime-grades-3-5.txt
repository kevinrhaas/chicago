== premise
17 June 1812, sunset, at the fort gate: the first killing written down in Chicago's history. The man who died was Jean Lalime, who spoke for the fort with its neighbors. The man who killed him was John Kinzie. Nine stops.

== opening
JAUNT: CHICAGO'S FIRST MURDER. Chicago in 1812 is a small, crowded place. There is a fort with about fifty soldiers and their leader, Captain Nathan Heald. There is a government trading house, and a few houses on the north side of the river. Everyone knows everyone. Two of these men have not gotten along for years. They argued over a house and over selling to the soldiers. They also argued over how to treat their neighbors, as a war with Britain came closer.

There are nine stops. We start with the sad ending. Then we go back to see how it happened. People told this story in three different ways. You will hear all three.

The story starts outside the gate of Fort Dearborn, at sunset on 17 June 1812.

== stop cold-open
It is 17 June 1812, at sunset, outside the gate of Fort Dearborn. Jean Lalime lies still on the ground. He was the fort's interpreter. That means he helped people who spoke different languages understand each other. Lalime is dead. The man walking away, hurt in the shoulder, is John Kinzie. He is the trader who sells to the soldiers. Later, books would call him the Father of Chicago. Everyone agrees that Kinzie killed Lalime. But why he did it is still argued. To see how it came to this, go back eight years, across the river, to the best house in town.

== stop du-sable-house
It is May 1804, in the morning. This is the best house in Chicago. Jean Baptiste Point du Sable built it. He was the trader who settled here first. In 1800 he sold it. The paper for the sale says the buyer was Jean Lalime, using money from the man both Lalime and Kinzie worked for. John Kinzie signed that paper as a witness. Now Kinzie moves his own family in and makes the house his. Nobody has found any paper saying Lalime sold it to Kinzie. Lalime did not leave town. He moved a few doors west, close enough to watch Kinzie live in the house.

== stop lalime-house
It is 26 May 1811, in the afternoon. Years before, Lalime had spent a winter in the big house with the fort's first doctor. The doctor called him a very good man and a good friend. When Kinzie took the house, Lalime moved here, to a smaller house down the river bank. He became the fort's interpreter. Today he writes a letter to the governor in St. Louis. He passes on news that horses were stolen from farms to the south. A history writer said his letters show a smart, well-taught man. Across the river, something new is about to make Kinzie richer, and Lalime his enemy.

== stop factory
It is 10 March 1812, in the morning, at the factory. This is the government's own trading house. Matthew Irwin runs it, and he sells things at fair prices. In January, Captain Heald let Kinzie and his partner be the only men allowed to sell goods to the soldiers. Today Irwin writes to the leaders in Washington. He says Kinzie and his partner offered money to get that job. Two men take Irwin's side: the young doctor, Isaac Van Voorhis, and Lalime. One army officer once wrote that Kinzie had a temper he could not control. Soon an argument about money turns into angry threats.

== stop bad-blood
It is 16 April 1812, at the officers' rooms in the fort. Ten days ago, two farm workers were killed up the river. Now everyone is afraid. Irwin writes that some men at the fort said they wanted to harm their peaceful neighbors. He names Kinzie and Kinzie's son-in-law, Lieutenant Linai Helm. Ensign George Ronan, the youngest officer, threatens Lalime. Helm makes terrible threats against Irwin. In May, Captain Heald arrests Helm for planning to harm six Sauk men who came in peace. Then he lets him go. By June, everyone at the fort has picked a side.

== stop the-quarrel
It is 17 June 1812, about six in the evening, inside the fort. Tomorrow, far away in Washington, the country will go to war with Britain. The news will not get here for a month. On the open ground inside the fort, Helm shouts against Irwin's trading house. He says he wishes it would close. Kinzie takes his son-in-law's side. Lalime speaks up for Irwin's store. He says it is honest and fair. The voices get louder. Then Kinzie and Lalime walk out of the gate together. The gates will soon close for the night. Only one of the two men will walk back.

== stop the-gate
It is 17 June, at sunset, back outside the main gate. Victoire Porthier was a girl inside the fort. Years later she remembered it. It was sunset, when they shut the gates. She heard Helm call out to Kinzie to watch out, because Lalime had a pistol. Then she heard the pistol go off and saw the smoke. The two men fell down together. Kinzie got up, hurt in the shoulder. Lalime did not get up. Irwin saw it all from close by. The doctor said Kinzie had planned it. Kinzie's family said he was only defending himself.

== stop the-flight
It is 17 June, late at night. Irwin said one officer told Kinzie to run. He said Helm hid Kinzie in the fort for hours, then walked him to the river, shook his hand and let him go. Kinzie's family told it another way. They said he came home and his wife took him into the woods. She cared for his wound and hid him for days. Victoire said her father took Kinzie away to Milwaukee that night. That is three stories about one night. At the gate, Lalime still lies where he fell.

== stop the-grave
It is 18 June, in the morning. The townspeople want a public meeting to find out how Lalime died. But Irwin wrote that Helm and a group of soldiers took Lalime's body away instead. They buried him on this bank, near the Kinzie house, inside a little fence. He could be seen from the house he had once owned on paper. In 1891, workers digging under a building found an old wooden coffin with bones inside. The bones went to the Chicago Historical Society.

== ending verdict
That night Captain Heald told Irwin to help arrest Kinzie. Irwin would not help, so he was sent out of the fort. Later Heald wrote that Kinzie had killed a man right in front of Irwin. Irwin left in July and was taken prisoner by the British. The doctor, Van Voorhis, died that August. Kinzie lived. He came back in 1816, and his family told their side of the story for a hundred years.

So was it murder? People who study history still disagree.

Some say Kinzie was guilty. Irwin and the doctor said Kinzie was behind a lot of dishonest dealing at the fort. A newspaper in 2018 said the story that Kinzie was only defending himself was not true.

Some say Kinzie was not to blame. His family said Lalime was jealous and attacked first. One book says the officers decided the killing was not a crime. Victoire said Kinzie was not to blame. Then she added, that is what they all said.

For a long time, only the story told by Kinzie's family and friends got heard. Lalime's bones are still in a box at the Chicago History Museum. You have stood where he fell. Decide for yourself.

== keepsake text
In 1913 a history writer named Milo Quaife decided this: it is clear that Kinzie killed Lalime, but from what we know, nobody can say who was to blame.
