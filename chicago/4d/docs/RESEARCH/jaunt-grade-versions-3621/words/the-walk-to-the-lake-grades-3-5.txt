== premise
At dawn a widow wakes at 1906 Prairie. Eight years ago her husband walked out this door at six in the morning and never came back. Follow his last walk to the lake.

== opening
JAUNT: THE WALK TO THE LAKE. It is dawn on a Monday in November 1904, at the south end of Prairie Avenue.

There are four stops, going north toward the lake. The Keiths are a steady family. Three brothers live in three houses, and they run one big store business. But one Monday morning eight years ago, at about this time, the kindest brother got up after a night with no sleep. He put on his hat and coat and walked out his front door.

Start at his door. His wife still lives here.

== stop door
This is 1906 Prairie, at dawn. Mrs. Edson Keith lives here. Eight years ago her husband, Edson, left this house at about six in the morning. He was 63. He had come to Chicago in 1854 and sold goods for a living. For ten years he had hardly slept. That last night he walked up and down the halls again. At two in the morning his butler found him, and he did not seem to know where he was. By six he was gone. Look around. Hilda Johnson, the maid, is up first. Paul Rasmussen, the coachman, is crossing the yard. Mrs. Keith comes to the door to see the weather. Walk north to his brother's house next door.

== caption door
Mrs. Keith opens her door at first light and looks up the street toward the lake. Her maid stands behind her.

== stop brother
This is 1900 Prairie, the house of his brother Elbridge. It was built in 1870, in the Second Empire style. Elbridge and his wife saw trouble coming. A few weeks before that Monday, Edson's wife told them she was afraid. He had been sick so long that his mind was slipping. His helper at work said Edson never said a mean word about anyone. He was the gentle brother, the one who never shouted. Elbridge is up early today too. He still walks past his brother's door. Walk north up Prairie, the way Edson went.

== caption brother
Elbridge Keith comes down his steps in the early light. His wife watches him go from the door.

== stop firm
This is 1808 Prairie, the house of another brother, Osborn Keith. In 1858 he helped start the family business on Lake Street. It grew huge. By the 1880s it sold four and a half million dollars of goods a year. Hundreds of people worked for it. In 1904 Stanley Field lives here, but people still call it a Keith house. Edson walked past it in the dark that Monday, going north to the lake. It is a long walk for a sick man. This house will be torn down in 1968. Keep going north to Sixteenth Street.

== caption firm
A milkman's boy runs past 1808. No one else is on the street.

== stop lake
This is Sixteenth Street, the north end of the avenue. Past the railroad, to the east, is the lake. The street ends here. Edson Keith kept going. That Monday morning someone found a hat and a coat on the beach. Fishermen told what they had seen. People searched for him all day. The servants only learned what had happened on Tuesday, from the newspaper. Stand here and look east at first light. The lake is grey and flat and very big.

== caption lake
A man in a hat and coat walks east along Sixteenth Street toward the lake. He does not look back.

== ending dragged
The walk ends here. The news hit the family very hard. People searched the lake for him. Edson Keith had left his money to his wife, Susan, and their two sons. His wife stayed on at 1906 Prairie, and his brother stayed next door. The street remembers him as the gentle brother, who never said an unkind word. One Monday morning he walked out his own front door and went to the lake.
