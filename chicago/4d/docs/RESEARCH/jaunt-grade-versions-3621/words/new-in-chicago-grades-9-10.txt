== premise
Establish your bearings, secure a bed and identify a practical next step on an imagined first day in town.

== opening
JAUNT: NEW IN CHICAGO. This is your first morning in a town incorporated less than two years ago, when it counted twenty-eight legal voters; this summer it supports two newspapers, a land office and more strangers than beds, and you are one of them.

Five stops lead from the forks to South Water Street: a landmark to steer by, the old mail corner, the store everyone patronizes, a map of the town, and a bed for tonight, if one can be found. The houses of 1834 "were always full", and full meant three in a bed.

== stop arrival
Begin outside the Sauganash, a landmark for every newcomer. Recalling 1831, Juliette Kinzie described a white two-story house with blue shutters. Moses and Kirkland call it "the most attractive hostlery the city possessed", and its landlord Mark Beaubien, "with his merry fiddle, the most popular of hosts". Every newcomer learns this corner before any other.

== narrative arrival
Let that landmark serve as your starting point: while you are finding your bearings, the town is finding room for you.

== stop mail-corner
This intersection is the old mail corner, where John S. C. Hogan, appointed postmaster in 1832, distributed letters "in a room partitioned off for that purpose" in his log store. Andreas reports that the office moved around July 1834, and Hogan's card now offers goods "one door below the Post Office". Inquire for your correspondence there rather than here.

== narrative mail-corner
Retain the old corner as a landmark on your route, but do not mistake it for the town's current mail counter.

== stop store
At Peck's store, on South Water at LaSalle, note a reliable supply shop. It was the village's first two-story frame, constructed of black walnut lumber "brought from Detroit by a boat". His own advertisements list dry goods, hardware, groceries, crockery, boots, shoes and hats. Upstairs, Peck provided the young minister Jeremiah Porter "a lodging place and study".

== narrative store
Remember where the everyday necessities of a newcomer are sold.

== stop newspaper
This building retains the Democrat's original address, although by May 1835 the paper was printing above Jones and King's hardware store. That spring it was still running Kinzie and Forsyth's notice of lithographed town maps, which could also be obtained at the paper's office.

== narrative newspaper
A town map is a practical next step, so read the notice carefully.

== stop lodging
Finish at Brown's boarding house, where Andreas identifies Mrs Rufus Brown as a boarding-house keeper and places her log house behind Peck's store, which you passed a block ago. He reports that the houses of 1834 "were always full; and full meant three in a bed sometimes, with the floor covered besides". Under those circumstances, a space on the floor qualifies as a bed.
