== premise
Follow goods back to the lake. See a store house on the river, the Dearborn Street bridge, the light at the river's mouth, and the two piers still growing into the lake. Leave with a map note.

== opening
JAUNT: ALONG THE WORKING HARBOR. It is a summer morning, and the lake is the town's highway. Three years ago Chicago had no harbor at all. The river was blocked by a big bank of sand. Then the army cut a path through the sand. Long piers went out into the lake. In 1834 a big sailing ship called the Illinois sailed into the river, and the people cheered. That year one hundred and eighty ships unloaded here.

Follow the goods back the way they came. Start at a store house on the river. Go under the Dearborn Street bridge and past the lighthouse, out to the piers. Both piers are still growing into the lake.

== stop the-forwarding-house
Start at Newberry and Dole's warehouse on the river. In the paper on 1 July 1835, they said they stored goods and sent them on. Goods come off the lake ships at houses like theirs. Then they go on to every store in town. When the ship Illinois came up the new channel in July 1834, people cheered her at this firm's dock. Nobody is sure which side of the river the warehouse stood on. But everyone agrees on the work they did.

== narrative the-forwarding-house
What the town cannot make, the lake brings.

== stop the-draw
Go east to Dearborn Street and the town's first drawbridge. A ship builder named Nelson Norton built it in 1834. It was about three hundred feet long. Its middle part opened so ships could pass through. Ships kept bumping into it, so it had to be fixed again and again. This week the paper lists sailing ships coming in, some with loads of lumber. Ships like them tie up at the docks and go out to the lake through this bridge. Stay on the south bank.

== narrative the-draw
This is where people cross the river and ships pass by.

== stop the-light
Walk on along the south bank to the light at the river's mouth. It was the first lighthouse on Lake Michigan. The first tower fell down in 1831, before it was finished. A builder put up this one in 1832. It is forty feet high, with a shiny mirror in the lamp room on top. Here is a surprise. Mark Beaubien, the town's fiddle player and inn keeper, was a keeper of the lighthouse too.

== narrative the-light
A light shows ships where the river is.

== stop the-piers
Go out to the start of the south pier. Across the water stands the north pier. It keeps the sand out. One hundred and eighty ships unloaded here last year. Congress has given more money for the harbor. Both piers grow longer all summer. Some ships are too deep to get over the sand. They stop out in the lake. In May, men in small boats carried one ship's people and goods to shore. Choose a mark for your map note.

== narrative the-piers
This is where the harbor meets the lake.
