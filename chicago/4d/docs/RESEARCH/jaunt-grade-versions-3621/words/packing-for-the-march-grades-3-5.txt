== premise
Play a child who lives by Fort Dearborn in August 1812. The soldiers are leaving. Do your jobs, and pack for the walk.

== opening
JAUNT: PACKING FOR THE MARCH. It is August 1812, and you are twelve. Your family lives in a log house near Fort Dearborn. The fort is on the south side of the Chicago River.

There is a war. A general far away has told the soldiers to leave the fort. In two days they will walk out, and your family will walk with them.

The places are real. The facts are real. The child is you. You will not talk to the captain or the chief. Watch, carry things, and pick your jobs.

== stop the-order
It is Thursday, 13 August 1812. It is morning. You stand on the north side of the river. Here is the house and trading store of Mr. Kinzie. The fort is across the water. On the 9th, Captain Heald got an order. It said to leave the fort. The order was read out loud to the soldiers. The officers wanted to stay. Heald will obey. On the 15th, at nine, everyone will march away. Your family must sort what it owns. What will you carry? What will you give away? Your mother has two jobs for you across the river.

== narrative the-order
Which job first?

== label the-order to-factory
Go to the trading house

== consequence the-order to-factory
You will see what the fort gives away.

== label the-order to-gate
Go to the gate for news

== consequence the-order to-gate
You will hear who is coming.

== stop factory
It is afternoon. This log house is the factory. It is the government's trading house. Today the goods go out. There are blankets, cloth, calico and paints. Heald gives away food too, more than the soldiers can carry. The general's order said the goods could go to the Indians who would walk with them. They could go to the poor of the fort too. Your family is poor. Next door is the agent's house. Its porches are boarded up, with holes cut for looking out. You carry home some calico, and nothing else.

== narrative factory
What the fort cannot carry, it gives away.

== stop the-gate
It is afternoon at the main gate. The fort is a square of buildings. It has two tall blockhouses and two rows of tall fences. Riders come in. One is Captain William Wells from Fort Wayne. Heald says he brought about thirty Miami men to help the soldiers on the walk. A book called Wau-Bun says he came a day later, and too late. Wells is the uncle of the captain's wife. Nobody stops to tell you all this. You listen, and you carry the news home.

== narrative the-gate
News comes in at the gate first.

== stop covered-way
It is evening by the river corner of the fort. A covered path runs from here down to the water. Tonight the soldiers are busy. They drop the gunpowder and the whiskey down a well. They break the guns and drop them in too. They pour the rest of the whiskey into the river. Heald does not want anyone to use it. It is meant to be secret. But people hear the noise, and the next morning the water tastes of grog. Your family gets its water from this river.

== narrative covered-way
Get water tonight, or wait?

== label covered-way draw-water
Fill the water keg upstream now

== consequence covered-way draw-water
Your family will have clean water for the walk.

== label covered-way keep-watch
Stay near the fort tomorrow

== consequence covered-way keep-watch
You will see who comes on the last day.

== stop river-mouth
It is Friday, 14 August, in the morning. Here the river meets the lake. It is half a mile from the fort. Long ago a captain drew the Kinzie house on a map of this place. Wau-Bun says Mrs. Kinzie's boat will come down to here tomorrow. Everyone else will walk. Your water keg is full. Today you pack the rest: a blanket each, the pot, and the cloth or the news. Some things must stay behind. Tomorrow the soldiers will walk south on the beach.

== narrative river-mouth
The last day is for packing.

== stop the-medal
It is Friday, 14 August, in the evening. You are inside the fort, near the captain's house. Black Partridge has come. He is a Potawatomi chief from the Illinois River. Wau-Bun says he went into the captain's house and gave back his peace medal. He said, "I will not wear a token of peace while I am compelled to act as an enemy." Two men of the fort wrote down his warning. You only see a door close. You hear about the medal later, like everyone else.

== narrative the-medal
A warning on the last evening.

== leg 0
Over the river, then past the fort.

== leg 1
Round to the gate where the news comes in.

== ending ready-to-walk
It is the night of the 14th. Your bundle is tied. The calico is under the blankets. The water keg is next to the pot. You did both jobs, and your water is clean. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens. The soldiers and families will walk south on the beach. The jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn tells what happened that morning.

== ending the-warning
It is the night of the 14th. You brought home news from the gate. Tonight you bring home the story of the medal. Everyone is talking about it. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens. The soldiers and families will walk south on the beach. The jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn tells what happened that morning.

== ending the-gift
It is the night of the 14th. The calico is on top of your bundle. You watched the last evening at the fort. You know what was given away, and why. Tomorrow at nine the gate opens. The jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn tells what happened on the beach that morning.

== ending packed
It is the night of the 14th. The water keg is full. Your bundle is tied. You know who rode in at the gate. At nine the gate opens. The jaunt The Battle of Fort Dearborn tells what happened on the beach that morning.
