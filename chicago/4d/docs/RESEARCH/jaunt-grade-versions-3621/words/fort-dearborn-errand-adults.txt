== opening
JAUNT: FORT DEARBORN ERRAND. A winter morning at the river mouth. The lake is iron grey, the boats have stopped, and 'during the long six months of winter the inhabitants, were cut off from the rest of the world'. 'There was nothing coming in, and there was no means of sending anything out of the town.' No letter, no doctor, no news until the ice goes. Out on the prairie, 'Wolves were in great plenty all around the town', and the men who hunted them called the chase 'as exciting as it was at times full of peril'. Inside the pickets, the fort keeps its stores, and the stores are all there is.

You carry a note: choose a small supply at the sutler's, check the parcel against the note at the store-house, and take it out to the fort's shop by the lake.

== stop errand
The fort's south gate, held by troops since June 1832. Juliette Kinzie, living inside in 1831, wrote of high pickets and large gates north and south. Hubbard put the guard-house and store-house either side of this gate in 1827: the lock-up and the larder, side by side. In a frozen season, what is in the stores is all there is until spring, and a short count means hunger.

== stop supply
East of the north gate stands the sutler's store, the post trader's shop, selling what the ration did not supply, at whatever price a man with a monopoly on a frozen post could ask. Hubbard placed it here in 1827; an 1855 photograph key still letters it the Sutler's. Its 1835 keeper is not known, but a soldier's pay ended up in his till.

== stop account
East of the south gate is the store-house. The Army's 1834 beef notices call it the subsistence store house: fresh beef came in quarters, up to three times a week, probably 400 pounds weekly, a whole garrison's meat by contract to whoever bid lowest. A shortfall in the count is a shortfall on someone's plate, and the man who signs for it answers for it. Check your parcel against the note.

== stop deliver
Outside the pickets toward the lake, the 1830 plan draws a building lettered Shop. No trade is named, so this is a workshop, not a smithy. Perhaps it was this one: Jeremiah Porter, 'the pioneer Protestant minister of Chicago', 'preached a sermon in the carpenter's shop of the garrison on the 19th of May, 1833', among sawdust and tools, to soldiers far from home. Set the parcel down.

== ending accounted
Supply chosen, parcel tallied and set down at the shop. The fort is a working neighbour at the river mouth, with stores to keep and a long winter to keep them through, and before the end of 1836 its troops will be gone for good.

== ending unfinished
Part of the note goes back with you, and someone will go without. The fort's service buildings will be here tomorrow, and so will the rest of the list, and so will the snow and the wolves.
