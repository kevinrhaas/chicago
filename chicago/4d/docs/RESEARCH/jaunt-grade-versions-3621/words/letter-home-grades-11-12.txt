== premise
Compose an imagined letter home, examine the newspaper's register of uncalled letters and determine where to post your own correspondence.

== opening
JAUNT: A LETTER HOME. You have resided in Chicago for several days, and you owe the people you left behind a letter, because the mail constitutes the settlement's essential lifeline. In winter, Moses and Kirkland recall, "There was nothing coming in, and there was no means of sending anything out of the town", and whenever the mail finally arrived the entire community assembled to hear it proclaimed from "a dry goods box".

Determine what purpose your letter serves, procure paper and ink, understand how the town administers its correspondence, and establish where to post it.

== stop purpose
Commence at Mrs Rufus Brown's log boarding house, which Andreas situates behind Peck's store. Seat yourself at the common table, should you find an unoccupied corner, and determine initially what the letter is intended to accomplish: a concise assurance of your safe arrival, an elaborate description for anyone contemplating following you westward, or a page of inquiries requiring a reply capable of locating you here.

== label purpose safe
Tell them you arrived safely

== consequence purpose safe
A concise letter describing the journey, the settlement and your continued health.

== label purpose prospects
Describe the town's prospects

== consequence purpose prospects
A lengthier letter, intended for anyone at home contemplating emigration.

== label purpose news
Ask for news from home

== consequence purpose news
A letter of inquiries, which will require a reply capable of locating you.

== stop paper
In front stands Peck's store, whose printed advertisements enumerate dry goods, hardware, groceries and crockery. For stationery, however, the Chicago American of 20 June 1835 indicates an alternative: Frederick Thomas's new drug store on Water Street advertised letter paper, wafers, wax, and black and red ink. A wafer or a drop of wax secures the folded sheet, since envelopes have not yet come into use here.

== narrative paper
Stationery from the druggist, then.

== stop notice
At the corner where the Democrat originated, examine its notices; by May its office had relocated above Jones and King's hardware store. On 27 May 1835 it published a List of Letters remaining in the Chicago post office on 31 March, an inventory of names nobody had claimed. Every name represents somebody who departed, never arrived, or never considered inquiring, a reminder of how transient this population remains.

== narrative notice
Request that your family address their reply plainly and legibly.

== stop mail-corner
Conclude at the original mail corner, where Lake and South Water converge. John S. C. Hogan was appointed postmaster in November 1832 and distributed the mail from "a room partitioned off" within his log store, forty-five feet by eighteen. Andreas locates the office here until approximately July 1834. This spring Hogan's advertisement offers merchandise "one door below the Post Office", and the Democrat continues to identify him as Postmaster.

== narrative mail-corner
The counter is one door along, so where will you seek it?

== label mail-corner south-water
Look for it beside Hogan's store

== consequence mail-corner south-water
You will inquire along South Water Street, one door below Hogan's establishment.

== label mail-corner keep
Keep the letter until someone shows you

== consequence mail-corner keep
You fold the draft away until a knowledgeable neighbor can direct you to the counter.

== leg 0
Convey your purpose around to the street side of the block.

== ending safe-and-well
Your letter reports your safe arrival and continued health. It is folded, sealed with a wafer and prepared for the counter on South Water Street.

== ending come-west
Your letter describes a bustling, muddy, booming settlement and its considerable prospects. It will be read aloud at home, and somebody will inevitably be aboard the next vessel.

== ending write-back
Your letter requests news from home, and now you comprehend precisely what becomes of a letter nobody claims. It is prepared for dispatch.

== keepsake text
A fictional folded letter, composed in town and not yet posted.
