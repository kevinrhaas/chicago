# T-0442 — identity research pilot for 75 real named residents

## Scope and method

This pass reviewed 75 of the 848 attested or inferred named people in the resident
layer (8.8%; 75 of the roughly 750 originally estimated). It excludes every
reconstructed person. The fixed cohort is 5 established profiles, all 20 richer
unplaced newspaper profiles, and 50 stratified post-office letter-list names (25
recorded present and 25 uncertain). `tools/select_resident_research_pilot.py`
re-derives that selection and refuses a reconstructed or missing person.

Queries combined the exact printed name with Chicago, Illinois, 1835, occupation,
and genealogy terms, then tested spelling/OCR variants where the newspaper form was
suspect. Search surfaces included the repository's newspaper corpus, government and
institutional biographies, edited documentary projects, digitized county histories,
historical encyclopedias, and broad genealogy indexes as finding aids only. The
ranked source policy and all identity rules are committed in
`data/research/residents/source_hierarchy.json`.

## Result

| outcome | people | meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| corroborated enrichment | 4 | an independent source adds or corroborates a fact and identity is resolved by more than name alone |
| candidate identity | 7 | useful profile lead retained beside the person, explicitly not merged |
| no corroboration | 64 | no reviewed record could be attached safely; not evidence that the person did not exist |
| **total** | **75** | every selected person has a dated outcome and retained queries |

The strongest resolved case is **J. H. Collins**. The edited Papers of Abraham
Lincoln record gives James H. Collins's profession and his 1834 Chicago partnership
with John D. Caton—the same distinctive partner and trade carried by the newspaper
record—so it does more than expand initials by name similarity. Caton, Dole and
Carpenter receive independent institutional corroboration. These findings are shown
on the card as research results; this ticket deliberately does not rewrite generated
household facts.

## Candidates and conflicts

| resident record | candidate | verdict | why it was not merged |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Garrett | Augustus Garrett | strong | matching auction trade, city and period, but no reviewed source explicitly expands the newspaper initial |
| J. Curtiss | James Curtiss | strong | matching attorney, city and period, but the record gives only an initial |
| B. S. Morris | Buckner Stith Morris | strong | unusual exact initials and surname, but no occupation or full-name bridge |
| David Brookins | early Chicago carriage seller | possible | exact name and regional chronology, but the county history does not identify the letter return |
| James McFadden | Dayton/Galena settler | rejected | exact name, but the source's geography conflicts with a Chicago identity |
| J. W. Fell | Jesse Weldon Fell | rejected | institutional chronology places Jesse in Vandalia and Clinton in 1835 |
| Alanson B. Vaughan | later Mower County settler | possible | uncommon exact name, but no migration link back to Chicago |

The negative Fell and McFadden results are as important as the tempting matches:
they prevent a familiar biography from swallowing a thin record. Surname form was
used to locate variants only; no heritage, ethnicity, immigration origin, kinship,
marriage, birth or household claim was inferred from a surname.

## Limits and next research

This was a broad deep-search pass, not an archival vital-record order. The 64 no-find
reviews are reproducible records of where this pass looked, but individual court,
church, probate, land, naturalization and manuscript collections may remain
unindexed. The next efficient pass should adjudicate the three strong initialed
candidates with period city directories, Cook County legal/land records and image-
level newspaper searches, then follow the uncommon letter-list names through
naturalization and county marriage indexes. Common-name profiles should not advance
without two independent discriminators.

The public payload is `data/residents/research_pilot.json`. It is generated by
`tools/compile_resident_research_pilot.py`; the renderer joins it to a person by stable
person id. Keeping it separate is intentional: candidates can be read and cited
without becoming asserted household data.
