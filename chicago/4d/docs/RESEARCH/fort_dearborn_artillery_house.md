# Fort Dearborn — artillery house

> Part of the Fort Dearborn complex. The plan source, the scale derivation, the garrison chronology, the wrong-fort guards and the three corrections to `docs/research/04-structures-south.md` are all in **`docs/RESEARCH/fort_dearborn.md`**, which this memo does not repeat.

## The thinnest record in the complex

One mention, in one sentence, about the fort **fifteen years after the army left it**: Robert
Fergus, describing the 1850 view, puts "the building where the artillery was housed, the
soldier's barracks, and the storehouse" on the east of the parade. Nothing reached names an
artillery house in 1827, in 1830 or at any date before 1850.

## Why it is built at all

The same continuity argument `data/structures/hogan_store.json` runs forwards, run backwards:
the post was abandoned as a military station on 29 December 1836 and nothing reached records
anything being **built** inside it afterwards, so a building standing in 1850 is most likely a
building of the garrison years. **That is an argument and not a citation.** If it is wrong this
record belongs in `data/exclusions.json` rather than in the scene.

## What is ours

The position (the 1830 plan has already filled the east side with the barracks, so this stands
in the one part of it the plan leaves empty), the size, the construction, the storey count, the
wall height and the roof — **L42** and **L43**.

## The guns are not modelled

"Three pieces of light-artillery" belongs to the pre-1812 fort; see
`data/exclusions.json` → `fort_dearborn_artillery_pieces_1812`. Nothing reached says what
ordnance stood at this fort in 1835, and no gun is drawn anywhere in the scene.
