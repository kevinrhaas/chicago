# Botsford's two addresses, and where Graves' Tavern stood — reconciliation

**Ticket:** T-0324 · **Records touched:** `data/structures/mansion_house.json` ·
**Corpus:** `data/research/newspapers/extracted/` (the *Chicago Democrat*, 1833-11 to 1835-08;
the *Chicago American*, 1835-06 to 1835-08)

J. K. Botsford advertised two addresses in the *Chicago Democrat* of 25 February 1834, eighty
lines apart in the same column run — `Corner of Dearborn an[d] Lake-sts.` and `next.door to
Graves' Tavern.` T-0311 refused to decide between them and the refusal was right on the February
evidence alone. Reading forward and backward through the whole corpus settles two of the three
readings, corroborates the third, and — unexpectedly — answers a question the Mansion House
dossier had put on its own "what would resolve what" list.

**The short answer.** The two addresses are **one frontage** (reading 1). A move (reading 3) is
refuted outright. Two premises (reading 2) loses the trade split it rested on. And Graves' Tavern
needs no new placement record, because it already has one: it is the **Mansion House**, held in
this reconstruction since 2026-08-11.

---

## 1. Every printing of Botsford's two addresses

All *Chicago Democrat*. "copy" is the advertisement's own date line.

and thereafter the corner alone, unbroken to the end of the year: 1834-04-30 c005, 06-04 c018,
07-02 c058, 07-09 c026, 08-13 c015, 09-24 c009, 10-01 c006, 11-12 c006, 12-03 c019, plus
Fullerton & Botsford's pork at the same corner (06-04 c024, 07-09 c027).

**The last "next door to Graves' Tavern" in the corpus is 1834-04-01 c009.**

## 2. Reading 3 — a move between 17 and 25 February — is refuted

It requires the older copy to be a stale insertion the advertiser forgot to pull. Two things in
the table above make that impossible.

1. **Both addresses run in ONE issue on 1 April 1834**, c009 and c010, six weeks after the
   supposed move.
2. **The corner advertisement is being RE-SET with current dates while the other keeps running.**
   Its date line advances Feb 4 → Feb 24 → Apr 22. A man who was re-dating one advertisement
   through March was not failing to notice the other.

## 3. Reading 2 — two premises — loses its motivation

The two-premises reading rests on the trades being incompatible: a grocery counter in one notice,
a tin *manufactory* in the other. **In February there is no manufactory.** The corner
advertisement is a dealer's — tin ware "JUST RECEIVED, EX]PRESS FROM DETROIT" — and it promises
the manufacturing as future work:

> [The subsc]riber would inform the pub[lic that at the openi]ng of navigation, he will
> c[ommence the man]ufacturing of Tin, in all its [branches].
> — 1834-02-25 c001

The manufactory opens on **22 April 1834**, and it opens at the corner: "he has commenced the
above business", `C[OR]NER OF DEAR[BORN and Lake streets]` (1834-04-23 c003). In that same issue
the **groceries** — brandy, St. Croix rum, wine, soap — are advertised at the corner too
(c002). So the trade split does not exist before April, and from the week the manufactory exists
both trades share one address. Two premises is still *possible*; nothing in the corpus asks for
it any more.

## 4. Reading 1 — one frontage — and what fixes which side

The east neighbour is the piece nobody had used. G. Spring's standing For-Sale notice ran six
times between June and November 1834:

> For Sale, **LOT No. 7, in block No. 16, one lot east of Haddock's Tavern, on Lake street**, in
> the town of Chicago. There is on said lot **a large Dwelling-House and fine well**.
> — 1834-06-18 c006; also 1834-09-03 c005, 1834-10-15 c006, 1834-11-19 c010

Haddock's Tavern is this same house (§5). **The lot immediately east of the tavern carried a
dwelling-house, not a shop.** So if "next door to Graves' Tavern" and "corner of Dearborn and
Lake" are one frontage, Botsford stood on the tavern's **west** side — which is the Dearborn
corner — and the tavern stood on the second lot from Dearborn rather than on the corner lot
itself.

That is consistent with everything already committed. `data/structures/mansion_house.json` places
the house on the north side of Lake immediately east of Dearborn and says in its own position
note that "'near Dearborn' is not 'at the corner', so the house may have stood one or two lots
further east". One lot is about 24 m, inside the ±20 m georeference residual plus the lot-width
allowance the record already carries. **The coordinate is therefore NOT adjusted on this
reading** — moving a building inside its own declared uncertainty, on an inference, would buy
precision the evidence has not paid for. The reading is recorded on the record and here.

**The usable constraint for the storefront tickets** (T-0263, T-0306): Botsford's corner is the
**north-east** corner of Lake and Dearborn. The Tremont House holds the north-west corner
(documented, three period texts), the tavern and its neighbours are on the north side of Lake,
and "next door" is adjacency on one block face.

## 5. Graves' Tavern is Haddock's Tavern is the Mansion House

The ticket's premise — "the tavern is doing the work of a survey point without having a location
of its own" — is **not true**, and the corpus now nails the chain shut.

- Andreas, p. 635: Graves "sold it to a Mr. **Haddock**, his son-in-law, who kept it until 1835".
- The *Democrat* names the house as Haddock's, by both names, in the summer of 1834: "H.
  Haddock's Mansio[n House]" (1834-06-25 c002), "at the Mansion House, **E. H. Haddock's**"
  (1834-07-30 c003), "Haddock's Man[s]ion-House" (1834-08-13 c007).
- And the *Democrat* dates the handover Andreas leaves at "about this time". The Board of
  Trustees adopts ordinances "at the house of Dexter Graves" on 1833-12-04 and 1834-03-05; an
  employment notice of **1834-04-01** (c017) says "enquire of **Mr. Graves, at his tavern**"; the
  ordinances of **1834-06-06** are adopted "at the house of E. Haddock" (1834-06-18 c002, reset
  as "E. H. Haddock" on 1834-07-02 c017); the house is "H. Haddock's" by 1834-06-25.
  **The house changes hands between 1 April and 25 June 1834**, and probably before 6 June.
  Dexter Graves commences the Baking Business on South Water Street over a copy date of
  1834-03-25 (1834-03-25 c003), which is the same spring.

  *Caveat, stated because it is the weak link:* the trustees' venue is not by itself a tenancy —
  the same board met at Starr Foot's house, the Presbyterian church, the Tremont House and "the
  Exchange" later in 1834. The bracket rests on Graves's own advertisement and on the house being
  named as Haddock's; the venue agrees with it rather than proving it.

**"Maddock" is settled.** Spring's notice reads "Haddock's Tavern" in four legible settings and
"Maddock's Tavern" in exactly one (1834-07-09 c024); a sixth is cut to "[…]k'n Tavern"
(1834-07-02 c053). The reading pass of 1834-07-30 named that as the condition for closing it —
"the two anchors stay separate until an issue prints both spellings in a way that decides it" —
and the later printings of the same notice are it. **The gazetteer cannot record the merge**: it
mints both tavern names as *persons*, and the person rule refuses two entries with a matching
surname and differing initials, correctly and for a different reason. `T-0359` is that defect.

## 6. Graves' Tavern was NOT renamed the Tremont House

The ticket's other question. Matthias Mason & Co.'s standing blacksmith advertisement — **one
dateline, "Chicago, Nov. 26, 1833", unchanged in every setting** — re-anchors mid-1834:

| settings anchored on **Graves' Tavern** ("nearly opposite", "opposite", or cut) | settings anchored on **the Tremont House** |
|---|---|
| 1833-11-26 c020, 1833-12-24 c003, 1834-01-07 c008, 1834-02-18 c010, 1834-02-25 c006, 1834-03-04 c015, 1834-04-01 c012, 1834-06-11 c013 | 1834-09-10 c013, 1834-10-15 c007, 1834-11-19 c008, 1834-12-10 c007 |

Neither a rename nor a second house across the street. **The landlord's name went stale.** The
corpus's last "Graves' Tavern" is 1834-06-11; its first use of "Mansion-House" as this building's
own name is 1834-09-03 c010, a militia election "held at the Mansion-House in Chicago" — one week
before Mason's advertisement first says "the Tremont House". An advertiser whose landmark was
named for a keeper who had left re-anchored on the largest name at the crossing. Both houses were
standing throughout: the first Tremont was built in 1833 by Alanson Sweet on the north-west corner
and burned in 1839 (`data/structures/tremont_house_1.json`), and Andreas has the Mansion House
"almost opposite the old Tremont House" — diagonally, across Dearborn. A forge on the south side
of Lake at that crossing is "nearly opposite" the one and "opposite" the other **without
moving**, which is what the ticket required of any answer.

**This answers the open question in T-0345**, which recorded the dated anchor change and said
"nothing here says WHICH changed … either the shop moved, or the building opposite it changed
hands and name". It changed hands and name, and the paper dates that independently (§5b). **One
correction to that ticket's bracket:** it gives the last Graves-anchored printing as 1834-07-16,
but the committed extractions carry no Mason printing that week — `chicago_democrat_1834_07_16`
c019 is Alanson Sweet's lost saddles. The last is **1834-06-11 c013**, so the bracket is
1834-06-11 → 1834-09-10, thirteen weeks rather than eight.

That also disposes of Andreas's aside that the house "according to some authorities had no name":
the town's own newspaper is calling it the Mansion House by September 1834, ten months before the
scene date.

## 7. Main-street is NOT identified, and this run argues harder for leaving it alone

The ticket forbids identifying Main-street with a platted street on this evidence, and the corpus
supplies a new reason to keep that refusal. **The street word in Mason's advertisement is
unstable across settings of one advertisement**: four read "Main-street" and the setting of
1834-10-15 (c007) reads "on Mar[k]et street, opposite the Tremont House". The same standing copy,
the same dateline, two different street names. Whatever "Main-street" was, this advertisement is
not a reliable witness to it, and the placement of Mason's forge continues to rest on the
**anchor** — a building this model holds — and not on the street word. That is the position
`data/reconstruction/1835_inferred_household_programme.json` and the 1834-12-10 c007 claim note
already take; nothing here changes it.

## 8. What is still open

1. **Reading 1 against reading 2.** Reading 1 is what the corpus supports and it is an inference,
   not a proof. No source puts the tavern on a corner directly, and "next door" can be loose.
2. **Which lot.** "Block No. 16" cannot be resolved: nothing committed carries the Thompson plat's
   block numbering (`T-0358`). Until it does, the most precise placement statement in the whole
   corpus — a lot, a block, a street, an offset, a building and a well — cannot be put on the
   ground.
3. **Who kept the house on 1835-07-01.** Untouched by any of this. Haddock or Markle, and the
   record still declines to choose.

## Postscript (T-0773): why this house stays on the outranked report

`tools/measure_placement_silence.py` lists `business_j_k_botsford` under *"a printed address
outranked by a later printed address"* — its live placement is the `relative` reading *"next
door to Graves' Tavern"* and the `corner` readings of Dearborn and Lake outrank it by class
rank. **That is a rank artefact, not an unresolved question.** This note already ruled the two
addresses one frontage, and the corner is the corner the tavern stands on, so no reordering is
owed and the ground does not move either way.

No `anchor_changes` rule may be written here even if one were wanted, and the mechanism is
right to refuse it: the tavern reading runs 1834-02-18 to 1834-04-01 and the corner reading
starts 1834-02-25, so the two anchors were printed in **overlapping weeks**, and guard 6 holds
that a change is a change only where one anchor stops before the next starts. Overlapping
anchors are two standing descriptions of one house — which is what this note found them to be.
See `docs/RESEARCH/outranked_printed_addresses.md` § 2.
