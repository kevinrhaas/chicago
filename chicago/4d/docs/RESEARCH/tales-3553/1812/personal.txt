# Personal tales: told by one named person of 1812, in their own voice, from what their
# records and the accounts they cite say of them. The telling is reconstructed.

## p12-kinzie-advice
for: kinzie_john_elder
title: I told the captain not to go
meet: heald_nathan

When General Hull's letter came, I told Captain Heald what I thought. Stay inside the walls, I said. You have provisions for six months and powder enough. Or go at once, tonight, before the camps fill. But do not wait, and do not promise the chiefs what you will not give.

He waited. He promised. And then, on my own advice, he poured away the liquor and the powder he had promised, because I would not have the young men armed and drunk at our backs. So I have helped to make the anger I warned him against. That is the trouble with good advice given halfway. Tomorrow I will walk with the soldiers, and my wife and children will go in the boat. Ask the captain what he makes of me now.

## p12-kinzie-lalime
for: kinzie_john_elder
title: Lalime

You will have heard about Lalime. Everyone has. On the seventeenth of June, at sunset, outside the gate. I will not tell you what words passed between us. That is between me and him and God. I will tell you that I came away with a wound in my neck and he did not come away at all.

A friend took me up the shore to Milwaukee that night, and I waited there until word came that the officers thought it was a fair fight and I could come home. He is buried in front of my own house, inside a fence. I look at it every morning. A man ought to look at what he has done.

## p12-heald-order
for: heald_nathan
title: My orders
meet: kinzie_john_elder

I am a soldier, and a soldier obeys. General Hull wrote that I am to evacuate the post if it is practicable, and give the factory's goods to the Indians, and march for Fort Wayne. I have read the letter twenty times. If practicable. Two words, and my whole command hangs on them.

Mr. Kinzie thinks I should stay. Some of my officers think so too, and say it to my back. But I have five days of argument behind me, and four hundred warriors outside, and orders in my pocket. I have chosen to march. I will not make a speech about it. Tomorrow, at nine o'clock, the gate opens. I pray I am right.

## p12-heald-wife
for: heald_nathan
title: Rebekah
meet: heald_rebekah

I married Rebekah Wells in Kentucky last May, and we rode here together on horseback with her servant and the servant's child, through a month of forest. I had told her it was a hard post. She said she was a Wells, and the Wells family did not choose easy places.

This spring we lost our son. There was no one here who knew what to do. She has not complained once, not of the baby, not of the fort, not of me. Tomorrow she will ride beside the column with her uncle in front. If you meet her, you will understand why I am more afraid for her than for any man in the company.

## p12-rebekah-ride
for: heald_rebekah
title: I will ride
meet: wells_william

My uncle William came yesterday from Fort Wayne with his Miami to take us home. I have not seen him since I was a girl. He looks older and harder, and he hugged me in front of the whole garrison as if I were still twelve.

Nathan wanted me to go in the Kinzies' boat. I said no. I came here on a horse and I will leave on one. My uncle smiled at that, the first smile I have seen on him. He told me to keep my horse close to his. I will. If you find him, tell him I am ready. He worries, though he would rather die than say it.

## p12-wells-face
for: wells_william
title: Why I came
meet: heald_rebekah

I came because my niece is here. That is the whole of it. When I heard Hull had ordered the post abandoned, I knew what the road along the lake would look like with four hundred young men watching it. I got thirty Miami who would come and I rode.

I was a Miami before I was anything else, and I know what they are saying in the camps tonight, because I have said it myself, long ago, about other forts. I would have stayed behind the walls. The captain has made his decision. So I will lead the column out, in front. Tomorrow I am going to paint my face. You will know what that means, if you know the old ways. If you do not, it is better that you do not ask.

## p12-black-partridge
for: black_partridge
title: The medal
meet: heald_nathan

Seventeen years ago at Greenville, General Wayne hung this medal on my chest, and I promised peace with the Americans. I have kept that promise every day since. I have held back my young men. I have fed the soldiers when they were hungry.

Tonight I am going to the captain's house with the interpreter, and I will give him back his medal. I will tell him the truth: the linden birds have been singing in my ears. The young men will not be held, and I will not wear the sign of a friendship I cannot keep. It is the hardest thing I have ever done. But a man who wears a lie on his chest is worse than an enemy. Go to the captain's quarters tonight. You will see me there.

## p12-winnemeg
for: winnemeg
title: The letter I carried
meet: kinzie_john_elder

I am Winnemeg, the Catfish, of the St. Joseph River. Two years ago I kept my people out of Tecumseh's confederacy, because I believed peace was the stronger road. So when General Hull wanted a messenger to Chicago, he chose me. I walked here with his letter, and I gave it to the captain, and I told Kinzie what I thought.

Go now, I said, at once, tonight, before the others come. Leave everything. Or stay behind your walls and do not come out. Do not wait. Do not give away the guns. Do not promise. The captain did all of those things. I have done what a friend can do. Ask Kinzie. He heard every word.

## p12-topenebe
for: topenebe
title: My old friend's family
meet: kinzie_john_elder

I am Topenebe, chief of the St. Joseph people. I have known John Kinzie since before this fort was built. My sister married the trader Burnett. Our families have eaten from the same kettle for twenty years.

I cannot stop the young men. No one can now. But I can tell my friend what I hear, and I can stand near his boat when the time comes. Keep your family on the water, I told him. Do not let them go with the soldiers along the shore. He listened. He is a stubborn man, but he is not a fool. Go and ask him if I am right. He will not say. But he will put his family in the boat.

## p12-waubansee
for: waubansee
title: A small band on the Fox River
meet: kinzie_john_elder

I lead a small band on the Fox River, thirty men in the spring, fewer now that some have gone south to the British. We are not a great people. We hunt and plant and trade with Kinzie, who has always been fair with us.

My young men want to be part of what is coming. I understand them. When the whole world is moving, a young man cannot stand still. But I have told them: Kinzie and his family are not to be touched. Whatever happens on the beach, the trader's house is ours to guard. Some of them laughed. I did not laugh. They will see.

## p12-keepotah
for: keepotah
title: The boat at the river mouth
meet: kinzie_eleanor

I am Kee-po-tah. I have visited the Kinzies many times. I was at their house when the American general Harrison came through and we met, years ago. I like Mrs. Kinzie. She speaks to me as an equal, and she was a captive herself once, raised among the Senecas, so she understands more than the others.

Tomorrow, when the soldiers march, I will be at the mouth of the river with another man, standing beside their boat. Nobody will touch it while I am there. Go and tell Mrs. Kinzie not to be afraid. She will pretend she was not. But she will hear it.

## p12-naunongee
for: naunongee
title: The officers' wives at play
meet: hays_otho

In the spring I watched the officers' wives playing games on the parade, running and laughing like girls. I said to the interpreter: they are amusing themselves very much now. It will not be long before they are hoeing in my cornfield. He went white and told everyone.

I meant it, and I did not mean it. That is what men say in a hard year. And yet there is a sergeant in that fort, Hays, who has been kinder to me than my own cousins. He mended my gun. He gave me a blanket. I do not know what tomorrow will make of the two of us. Go and find him. Tell him Nau-non-gee remembers the blanket.

## p12-blackbird
for: blackbird
title: The war leader
meet: heald_nathan

The French call me Letourneau. My people call me Black Bird. In the spring I was with Pepper's band on the Au Sable, and last year I sat in council with the Americans at Peoria, and I listened to their promises, and I watched them take the Wabash.

The young men look to me now. That is a heavy thing. A war leader does not choose the war. The war chooses him, and then he must decide how it will be fought, and how it will end. Whatever happens tomorrow, I have told the young men this: when it is done, it is done. A man who has given up is not to be harmed. Remember I said it. Tell the captain, if you see him.

## p12-fox-running
for: main_poc_runner
title: The red belt

I am a runner. I have come a long way from Main Poc with this belt and with news. Mackinac belongs to the British. The Americans' general Hull has crossed into Canada and run back again like a frightened deer. Detroit will fall. The British father has said to all his children: now is the time.

I have told this at every fire tonight. The old men look at the belt and say nothing. The young men touch it. A belt does not make a war. It only says, the war is here, will you come? Every man must answer for himself. Most of them already have.

## p12-nuscotnemeg
for: nuscotnemeg
title: The Mad Sturgeon

They call me the Mad Sturgeon. The Americans in the Illinois settlements blame me for killings two summers ago, and they have asked the chiefs to give me up. The chiefs have not. My daughter is married to Billy Caldwell, who is half Irish and rides with Tecumseh now. So you see, everybody in this country is related to everybody else.

I do not pretend to be a peaceful man. I have seen too much of what peace with the Americans brings: a medal, a treaty, and the land gone. Tomorrow I will be on the beach. Do not look for me afterwards. If I live, I will be somewhere on the Des Plaines, and you will not find me unless I want you to.

## p12-margaret-stay
for: helm_margaret
title: I will not leave him
meet: helm_linai

My mother wants me in the boat with her and the little ones. I told her no. I married Linai Helm last year, and I am going where he is going. I am seventeen and I can ride as well as any soldier in this fort, and I can shoot.

My mother looked at me for a long time and then she kissed me and said, then ride well. That is all. She knows the camps better than anyone and she did not tell me I was safe. Go and find my husband. He will pretend to be cross with me for not going in the boat. He is not. He is proud. So am I.

## p12-helm-lieutenant
for: helm_linai
title: The second lieutenant
meet: helm_margaret

I came here from Detroit as second in command and married the trader's stepdaughter, and some of the officers have not forgiven me for being happy. Margaret is the bravest girl in the Northwest. She has told me she will ride beside the column tomorrow, and I have stopped arguing.

I disagreed with the captain about the march. I said so once, properly, and then I held my tongue, because that is what an officer does. Tomorrow I will march where I am ordered and fight if I must. If you see Margaret, do not tell her I am afraid. Tell her I said she rides better than I do. It is true.

## p12-eleanor-captive
for: kinzie_eleanor
title: I was a captive once
meet: helm_margaret

When I was a little girl in Pennsylvania, the Senecas came to our farm and carried me away. I lived among them for years. The old chief adopted me as his own daughter and I loved him, and when my family finally found me and took me home, I cried for him.

So I do not look at the camps the way the officers do. I know there are kind people out there, and grieving people, and angry people, the same as in any town. Tomorrow I take my little ones in the boat. My Margaret will ride with her husband. I have told her all I can. Go and find her. Tell her that her mother is watching from the water.

## p12-johnny-kinzie
for: kinzie_john_h_1812
title: I am nine
meet: kinzie_eleanor

I am nine and I am the eldest, so I have to help Mama with the little ones in the boat tomorrow. Ellen is six and Maria is four and Robert is only two, and he screams if you take his spoon away.

Papa says I must be the man of the boat until he comes back, because he is going to walk with the soldiers. I said I would. I have my knife and a piece of maple sugar in my pocket. Josette sewed pockets into all our clothes with corn and sugar inside, in case we get hungry. Mama does not know. Do not tell her.

## p12-chandonnai
for: chandonnai_jean_baptiste
title: Between the lodges and the books
meet: kinzie_john_elder

I am Mr. Kinzie's clerk. I keep his books, I count his furs, and I carry his words to the camps and their words back to him. My mother's people are Potawatomi and my father's people are French, and so I am welcome everywhere, and trusted nowhere entirely.

Tomorrow Mr. Kinzie has asked me to take care of his family in the boat. I have told him I will. I have also told my cousins in the camps that this family is under my protection. Some of them listened. Whatever happens tomorrow, I will spend the day walking between the two sides with my hands open. It is the only thing I know how to do.

## p12-josette
for: laframboise_josette_1812
title: The children's nurse
meet: kinzie_john_h_1812

I am Josette. My mother's people are Ottawa, from up the lake, and my father was a French trader, so I speak to the Kinzie children in three languages and they answer me in a fourth that is all their own.

Tomorrow I will sit in the boat with Mrs. Kinzie and the little ones while the soldiers march. I am frightened, but children can smell fear, so I am not allowed to be. I have sewn food into their clothes and taught Johnny a word to say if a stranger comes near the boat. Go and ask him to say it for you. He is very proud of it.

## p12-cicely
for: cicely
title: My son

My name is Cicely. That is the only name anyone has ever written for me. I came here with the captain and his wife from Louisville, on horseback, with my baby in my arms. I keep the captain's house. I cook and wash and sew and keep my mouth shut, which is what is wanted of me.

But I will tell you about my son, because you asked. He is just beginning to talk. He says no to everything and laughs when he says it. He holds onto my finger so tight in the night that I wake with it numb. Tomorrow I will carry him. Whatever else the world has decided I am, I am his mother. Nobody can write that away.

## p12-black-jim
for: black_jim
title: The Kinzie boat
meet: kinzie_john_h_1812

They call me Black Jim. I work for Mr. Kinzie, and whether the law calls me free or something else is a thing I keep to myself. I do the heavy work: wood, water, the boats. Tomorrow I will be in the bow of the family boat.

I have checked every plank. I have put an axe under the seat where my hand can find it. The children will be behind me, and Mrs. Kinzie, and the nurse. If anybody comes at that boat, he will have to come past me first. Go and tell young Johnny that Jim is ready. He will feel better. So will I.

## p12-hays
for: hays_otho
title: A friend in the camps
meet: naunongee

I signed on in April of last year and I am a sergeant now. I am not a clever man, but I have always believed that if you live beside people you ought to be decent to them. So I have been decent to Nau-non-gee of the Calumet village. I mended his gun. I gave him a blanket in the cold.

This summer he does not meet my eye. Somebody told me what he said about the officers' wives and his cornfield. I do not believe he meant it. I believe he is a man caught in a hard year, like me. Go and find him if you can. Tell him Sergeant Hays has not forgotten him either.

## p12-burns
for: burns_thomas
title: Sergeant of the militia
meet: burns_mrs

I served my time in this fort and then I stayed, because I liked the country and I liked a widow named Mrs. Cooper, and she liked me well enough. Now I have a house on the north bank with a cow in it, and a wife, and a houseful of her children who are mine now, and a new baby of our own born the night before we all ran into the fort.

The captain made me sergeant of the twelve. We are farmers with muskets. Tomorrow I will march with them beside the wagons that carry my family. Go and see my wife. Tell her I will be close by. Tell her the cow will be fine.

## p12-mrs-burns
for: burns_mrs
title: Carried in my bed
meet: burns_thomas

I had my baby the night before the alarm in April. The next evening the Kinzie men came running and said the farm had been attacked and everyone must go into the fort. I could not stand. So they picked up my bed, with me in it and the baby in my arms, and carried it down to the boat and across the river and in through the gate.

I have been in the fort ever since, with all my children, and the youngest of them was a day old when we came. Tomorrow we go in the wagon. My husband marches beside it. Find him. Tell him the baby has stopped crying. It has not. Tell him anyway.

## p12-lee
for: lee_charles
title: My farm on the South Branch
meet: kelso_john

I came here about seven years ago and took up land on the South Branch, and I made the first real farm in this country. Wheat, cattle, hogs. I fed this fort. My family lived down by the river mouth and my men worked the farm.

In April a party of strangers came to the farm friendly and then turned on my men. Liberty White and the Canadian Cardin were killed. My boy got away, running all the way down the river to the fort with John Kelso. I have not been back. Go and find Kelso. Thank him for me. I have tried, and the words do not come out right.

## p12-kelso
for: kelso_john
title: The run
meet: lee_charles

I was done with the army in December of 1810. A free man, working where I pleased. In April I was at Lee's farm when the strangers came. Before sunset they turned on the farm. I took Lee's boy by the arm and we ran.

We ran down the river bank with the light going, through the brush, through the water when we had to. We did not look back. At the gate I could not speak, so the boy spoke for both of us. A month later I put my name on the army's paper again. A man who has run once does not want to run again. Find Lee and tell him his boy was braver than I was.

## p12-van-voorhis
for: van_voorhis_isaac
title: A young doctor's prospects
meet: heald_nathan

I came here from Fishkill on the Hudson, a young doctor, and last year I wrote home that this country would one day be the garden of the world. I still believe it. The soil is so black and so deep that you could plant a walking stick and get a tree.

Since Mr. Irwin left I have kept the keys of the factory, and this week I have given away every bolt and kettle in it, on the captain's order. I told the captain what I thought of the march. He listened politely, which is how captains listen to young doctors. Tomorrow I will ride with the wagons and the sick. If you see the captain, tell him the medicine chest is packed.

## p12-ronan
for: ronan_george
title: Straight from West Point
meet: heald_nathan

I came here from West Point in March of last year, the youngest officer at the post. I have read every manual on infantry tactics ever printed, and I have learned that none of them were written for a fort on a sandbar with four hundred warriors outside.

The older officers think I am green. They are right. But I have said what I think of the march, out loud, in front of the captain, and he did not like it. I may have been rude. If I was, I am not sorry. Tomorrow I will march where I am told and I will not run. Tell the captain I said so.

## p12-griffith
for: griffith_william
title: A settler turned sergeant
meet: black_partridge

I was a settler here until April. When the killings came at Lee's farm, I walked into the fort and put my name down. By June I had stripes. That tells you how many men here can read.

A chief came to me this summer, a friend to the fort, and said the linden birds had been singing in his ears. I did not understand until the interpreter explained it: he had heard warnings he could not say aloud. I think it was Black Partridge, though he did not say so. If you find him, tell him Griffith understood.

## p12-corbin
for: corbin_fielding
title: I came back for her
meet: corbin_mrs

I did my five years and went out, and then I came back and signed on again, because my wife would not live anywhere but here, and a soldier's pay is better than none. She does the washing for the company. She is expecting our child.

She has told everyone that she will never be taken. I believe her. I asked the sergeant to let me march beside the wagon tomorrow, and he said yes before I finished. Go and see her. Tell her I have the blankets packed, and her good shawl. She will say I packed them wrong. She will be right.

## p12-ouilmette-stay
for: ouilmette_antoine
title: I am staying home
meet: ouilmette_archange

I came here in 1790, before the fort, before the Americans, when this was a river and a few houses and the lake. I have carried goods over the portage for Mr. Kinzie for years. My wife Archange is Potawatomi, and her people are my people.

Everybody is leaving tomorrow. The captain asked me to join the militia, and I said no, I am not a soldier. Tomorrow when they march I am going to stay home and mind my garden. When it is over, whatever it is, I will still be here. Go and meet my wife. She will feed you. She feeds everybody.
