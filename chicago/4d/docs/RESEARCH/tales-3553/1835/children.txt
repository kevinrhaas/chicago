# Tales told by the children and young people of the town, 1 July 1835.

## s35-ch-schooner
roles: child, youth
sex: m
title: I saw the schooner come in

When a schooner comes in, all the boys in town run down to the river mouth to watch. You can see her from far out, her sails like a white bird on the lake, and then she comes in between the piers, and the sailors throw ropes, and the men on the wharf pull her in.

I want to be a sailor. My father says sailors drown, and drink, and never come home. I do not care. Last week a sailor let me climb up the rigging to the first crosspiece and look out over the whole town. You could see everything, all the roofs and the fort and the prairie going on forever. I am going to be a sailor. Do not tell my father.

## s35-ch-mud-boots
roles: child
title: I lost my boot

Yesterday I was walking to school on Clark Street and I stepped in the mud and my foot went in up to the knee. When I pulled it out, my boot stayed in. I could see the top of it, but I could not reach it, and every time I tried I sank deeper.

A man came by with a board and laid it across the mud and fished my boot out with a stick. He said that was the third boot he had rescued that week. He said one day he is going to open a shop selling boots that people lost in the mud. I went to school with one muddy boot. The teacher made me sit by the door.

## s35-ch-indians
roles: child
title: The Indian boy and the bow

There is a Potawatomi boy who comes into town with his father to trade, and he can shoot a bird out of the air with a little bow. He showed me how. You hold it like this, and pull back to your ear, and let go smooth, not jerky. I tried and the arrow went straight into the ground.

He laughed so hard he fell over. Then he gave me the bow. I gave him my best marble, the blue one. My mother says his people are going away soon, far west across the big river. I asked him about it with my hands and he nodded and pointed at the sun going down. I am keeping the bow forever.

## s35-ch-wolf
roles: child
title: I heard a wolf

Last winter, on a very cold night, I woke up and heard a wolf howling, out on the prairie past our house. It went up and up and then down, and then another one answered, and another, until there were lots of them all singing together.

I got into my sister's bed and we pulled the quilt over our heads. In the morning my father showed us the tracks in the snow, right up to our pig pen. The pigs were all fine. My father said the wolves were only singing to the moon. I asked him what the moon sang back. He did not know.

## s35-ch-school
roles: child
sex: m
title: School in a church

I go to school in the back of the Baptist church, with thirty other children on long benches. We have slates and one book for every three children. The teacher has a long stick and she can reach anybody on any bench without getting up.

There is a French girl who speaks no English, and an Irish boy who speaks it so fast nobody understands him, and twin girls from Vermont who say they are going to be teachers too. I am going to be a lawyer, like my father. Or a sailor. I have not decided. The teacher says I should decide to learn my letters first.

## s35-ch-sauganash
roles: child, youth
title: Mr. Beaubien's fiddle
meet: beaubien_mark

Mr. Beaubien at the Sauganash has more children than anybody in Chicago, and they are all my friends. On Saturday nights we sit on the stairs and watch the grown-ups dance, and Mr. Beaubien plays the fiddle and stamps his foot and sings in French, and everybody spins round and round until they fall over.

He saw me on the stairs once and winked and played a tune just for me, a fast one, and all the grown-ups had to dance faster. Go and find him. If you are a child he will give you a piece of sugar. If you are a grown-up he will make you dance.

## s35-ch-fort-drum
roles: child
sex: m
title: The fort's drum

Every morning the drum at the fort wakes up the soldiers, and it wakes me up too, because our house is near the fort. Rat-a-tat-tat. Then the fife. Then the soldiers shout.

I sneak over to watch them drill sometimes. They march up and down and turn all together, and the sergeant shouts things nobody can understand, and a little boy my age beats the drum. He is a drummer boy. I asked him how you get to be a drummer boy and he said you have to run away from home. I am thinking about it.

## s35-ch-ice
roles: child
title: Skating on the river

In the winter the river froze so hard you could drive a wagon across it. Everybody went skating, grown-ups and children, up and down the river from the fort to the forks. My brother made me skates from old knife blades bound to wooden soles with leather.

I fell down a hundred times. Then I did not fall down. Then I could go fast, faster than my brother. He said it was because I am lighter. I said it was because I am better. We had a race to the forks and I won. He says I cheated. I did not cheat. I am just faster.

## s35-ch-berries
roles: child
title: Strawberries on the prairie

In June the prairie is covered with wild strawberries, little red ones hidden in the grass, sweeter than any garden strawberry. My mother sends us out with baskets and we come back with red mouths and red hands and half a basket.

You have to watch for snakes. My friend saw a rattlesnake once, coiled up in the grass, shaking its tail like a baby's rattle. We ran all the way home. My mother said that was why we only brought back half a basket. It was not why. But we let her think so.

## s35-ch-new-house
roles: child
title: Our new house

We used to live in one room behind my father's store, all six of us. Now my father has built a real house, with two rooms downstairs and two upstairs and a real glass window in the front. I have to share a bed with only one sister now instead of two.

It took my father and two carpenters six days to build it. They nailed it together out of thin boards, very fast, and my grandfather, who came from Connecticut, said it would blow away in the first storm. It did not. The storm came and the house just creaked a bit. My grandfather said it was a miracle.

## s35-ch-steamboat
roles: child, youth
sex: m
title: The steamboat's whistle

The first time I heard a steamboat whistle, I thought it was a monster. It came from out on the lake, a long scream, and then black smoke, and then the boat came in, bigger than the fort, with wheels on its sides going round and round, splashing.

My father lifted me up on his shoulders to see it. People came off it all day, more people than I had ever seen. Now I am not afraid of steamboats. I know every one by its whistle. That one is the Michigan, she has a deep one. I want to be a steamboat captain. Or a sailor. Or a lawyer.

## s35-ch-ague
roles: child
title: When I had the shakes

I had the ague all last August. Every afternoon at two o'clock I shook so hard the bed rattled, and my teeth went chatter chatter chatter, and I was so cold even with three quilts. Then I got hot and my mother put wet cloths on my head.

Every morning I felt fine, and I went out to play, and then at two o'clock I had to come home and shake. The doctor gave me quinine, which is so bitter it makes you cry. My mother mixed it with molasses and it was still bitter. In September it went away. My mother says everybody gets it. I hope I never get it again.

## s35-ch-letter-list
roles: child
title: My name in the newspaper

My father's name was in the newspaper last week, in the list of letters waiting at the post office. He did not even know there was a letter. My mother read the list out loud at breakfast, and there it was.

He went to Mr. Hogan's store and paid a whole quarter dollar and got the letter. It was from my grandmother, and it said she was coming to live with us next spring. My father read it twice and went very quiet. My mother laughed and laughed. I like my grandmother. She brings peppermints.

## s35-ch-indian-camp
roles: child
title: The camp by the river

Every summer Potawatomi families camp on the edge of town near the river, in houses made of mats and bark. The women sell baskets and berries and maple sugar, and the men sell venison and fish. My mother buys from them.

I used to be scared of them. Then a girl from the camp showed me how to make a doll out of corn husks, with a little husk dress and husk hair. Now we play together when they come. This summer she told me with her hands that they are going far away and will not come back. I made her a doll out of rags with button eyes, to take with her. She gave me a husk one. I sleep with it.

## s35-yo-apprentice
roles: youth, mechanic
sex: m
age: -19
title: The printer's devil

I am the printer's devil at the newspaper, which means I do every dirty job there is. I sweep the floor and ink the type and fetch the water and run the papers round town. My hands are black from Monday to Sunday.

But I can read faster than anybody in Chicago, because I read every word of the paper before anybody else does, backwards, while I am setting it. I know all the news first. I know who has died and who is getting married and whose lot has been sold. When I am grown I am going to own a newspaper. I am going to call it something better than the Democrat.

## s35-yo-courting
roles: youth
sex: f
title: Walking out on Sunday

There are four young men in Chicago for every young lady, so the young ladies can be very particular. I have been walking out on Sunday afternoons with a carpenter's apprentice, and also with a clerk from Lake Street, and also, once, with a lieutenant from the fort.

My mother says I must choose. My father says I must not choose a lieutenant, because the army will take him off to some fort at the end of the world. My aunt says I must choose the clerk, because clerks become merchants. I am going to choose the carpenter. He makes me laugh, and he built my mother a cupboard for nothing. That is better than the end of the world.

## s35-yo-land
roles: youth, clerk
sex: m
age: -19
title: I bought a lot

I am sixteen and I own a lot on the North Side. I saved my wages from the store for a year, forty dollars, and a land agent sold it to me on credit for two hundred, forty down and the rest when I sell. He says it will be worth five hundred by Christmas.

I have walked out to see it twice. It is a swamp with a dead tree in the middle. But everybody's lot is a swamp with a dead tree in the middle. That is what Chicago is. When the canal comes, the agent says, the swamp will be the middle of a great city. I believe him. I have to. I have forty dollars in it.

## s35-yo-runaway
roles: youth, labourer
sex: m
age: -19
title: I ran away to Chicago

I ran away from my uncle's farm in New York state when I was fifteen. He beat me and worked me like a horse. I walked to Buffalo and worked my passage on a schooner, scrubbing decks and hauling ropes, and the captain put me ashore at Chicago with a dollar in my pocket.

That was last year. Now I drive a dray for a merchant, and I have a bed in his warehouse, and I eat at his table. Nobody here asks where you came from. Nobody cares. If you can work, you can eat. I have written to my mother to tell her I am alive. I have not told her where I am, in case my uncle comes looking.

## s35-yo-girl-school
roles: youth
sex: f
title: I want to be a teacher

I am fourteen and I help at the little school, minding the youngest ones and hearing their letters. The teacher says I have a gift for it. When I am eighteen she says I could have a school of my own, in one of the new towns going up on the prairie.

My mother wants me to marry. My father wants me to help in the store. I want to teach. There are so many children coming into this country, and so few teachers. A girl can do that. A girl can go out to a new town and be somebody. I would like to be somebody.

## s35-yo-irish-lad
roles: youth, irish
sex: m
age: -17
title: Carrying water

I carry water for my mother's laundry, two buckets on a yoke from the lake, fifteen trips a day. I am fourteen. My father died on the boat coming over, in the fever, and they put him over the side in a sheet. My mother and me and my sisters came on alone.

Now I carry water and run errands for the stores, and my mother washes, and we have a room of our own. On Sundays I go to Mass and then I go down to the river and watch the schooners. One day I am going to be on one of them, going back east, with money in my pocket, to show the old country what a Chicago man looks like.

## s35-tod-mud
roles: toddler
title: The mud

There is mud outside. Lots and lots. It goes squish. I jumped in it.

My mother said oh no. My dress is all brown now. My shoes are gone. My mother says the mud ate them. I think the mud is hungry. Do not jump in the mud.

## s35-tod-boat
roles: toddler
title: The big boat

A big boat came. It had white sails. It was bigger than our house. Everybody ran to see.

My father put me on his shoulders. I could see everything. The men on the boat waved. I waved. When I am big I am going on the boat.

## s35-tod-kitten
roles: toddler
title: Kitty

We have a kitty. She is grey. She sleeps by the stove. She is soft.

I pulled her tail. She scratched me. Here, look. It is a red line. My mother kissed it. Now it is better. I do not pull the kitty's tail any more. Not very much.

## s35-tod-drum
roles: toddler
title: Boom, boom

At the fort there is a drum. Boom, boom, boom. It wakes me up. Then the soldiers march.

I have a drum too. It is a pot. I hit it with a spoon. Boom, boom. My mother says not inside. So I go outside and march, like the soldiers. Boom, boom.

## s35-tod-sister
roles: toddler
title: My sister

My sister is big. She is six. She knows all her letters. She can carry the water.

She carries me too, when my legs are tired. She tells me stories at night. When she is gone I cry. Then she comes back. She always comes back.

## s35-tod-snow
roles: toddler
title: The snow

Once there was snow, all white, everywhere. It was so cold. My nose was red.

My brother made a snow man. It had a stick nose. I hugged it and it was cold. Then the sun came and the snow man went away. Where did he go? Nobody knows.

## s35-tod-horse
roles: toddler
title: The horse

There is a horse that pulls the wagon. He is very big. His feet go clop, clop.

The man let me sit on the horse. Up high. I held on to his hair. The horse did not mind. He is a good horse. I gave him some grass. He ate it all up.

## s35-tod-candy
roles: toddler
title: Sugar

At the store there is sugar. It is brown and sweet. The man gave me a little piece.

I ate it fast. Then I wanted more. The man laughed. He said come back tomorrow. I will come back tomorrow. I will come back every day.
