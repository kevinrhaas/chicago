## 2. Every empty field, and the policy it stands on

A field that runs empty with no policy behind it is the finding this table is for.
Every one below has one.

| field | empty | why a record may carry nothing here |
|---|---:|---|
| `type` | 5 | `not_stated` where the notice prints neither a trade nor an article to read a class off (trade_class_rulings.json's own ruling, boundary B6); never an empty list. |
| `trade` | 4 | The notice prints no trade. The class ruling records the same absence as `not_stated`. |
| `occupation` | 12 | No single occupation word the register will stand behind for this house. |
| `goods` | 80 | The notice names no article. A lawyer, a school or a physician sells no goods; a store that names none is a gap, and T-1404 works the trades from the research. |
| `firm_styles` | 168 | The house is printed under one style only, so there is no second style to keep. |
| `proprietors` | 85 | The notice names no keeper — an unsigned advertisement, or one signed by a firm style alone. A null here is a finding about the paper, not a person left out. |
| `partners` | 152 | A sole keeper, or no keeper named at all. `compile_businesses` files a lone name as a proprietor and only a firm of two or more as partners. |
| `staff` | 111 | THE PAPERS NAME OWNERS AND ALMOST NEVER A CLERK, so empty on a record the register compiled is a true reading of the register and not an omission. What is no longer empty is the 84 houses T-1433's seating seated a reconstructed hand in: T-1462 lays those 124 seats on as `staff` rows at their own tier, each with its seed and its basis. |
| `proprietor_community` | 83 | No keeper this record names has a community on their town card, so there is nothing to read a house's community off. Never inferred from a surname. |
| `customers` | 196 | The register carries no customer claim at all; T-1189 is where a workplace gets its people. |

## 3. Identity: the layer against `identity.json`

A merge is HONOURED when the merged-away spelling is not a second house or a second
person. It may still stand on `firm_styles[]` or `also_printed_as[]` of the record it
merged into — the typography is evidence and the layer keeps it there deliberately.
A refusal is honoured when the layer has not quietly folded the two together anyway.

| proprietor merges | 12 | 12 |
| refused firm merges | 22 | 22 |

the rest were never printed as a standing house in this window and so have nothing to

## 5. The four houses that moved

The register brackets a change of printed anchor for four businesses: `after` is the last
issue that sets the old one, `before` the first that sets the new, and the move falls
between them. **That pair is the bracket of the MOVE and not the span of either siting**,
and it was being written onto the superseded location as `from: after, to: before` — which
dated an address to exactly the window in which it was being replaced, and carried it up
to and including the first printing of its replacement. T-1402 re-dated both rows off
their own printings; `compile_businesses.locations_for` carries the reasoning.

| record | live anchor (register's rule) | superseded / later anchor | move bracketed | live row | other row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `biz_chicago_democrat_printing_office` | Messrs. Jones & King's hardware store | the corner of South Water and Clark streets (earlier) | 1834-01-07 → 1835-05-20 | street_only 1834-01-07 → — | 1834-01-07 → 1834-01-07 |
| `biz_g_spring` | the Tremont House | the corner of Franklin and South Water streets (earlier) | 1834-11-26 → 1835-05-20 | anchored 1835-05-20 → — | 1833-12-17 → 1834-11-26 |
| `biz_matthias_mason_co` | the Tremont House | Graves' Tavern (earlier) | 1834-06-11 → 1834-09-10 | premises 1833-11-26 → — | 1833-11-26 → 1834-06-11 |
| `biz_the_chicago_democrat` | the corner of South Water and Clark streets | Jones & King's hardware store (later) | 1834-12-03 → 1835-08-05 | anchored 1833-11-26 → — | 1835-08-05 → — |

`the_chicago_democrat` is why the direction is read off `live_anchor` and never assumed:
its move to Jones & King's is first printed 1835-08-05, AFTER the scene date, so the
register keeps the corner live and the LATER siting is the second row. Read as an
“earlier” anchor like the other three, that record carried the corner twice — once live,
once as its own predecessor — and the move to Jones & King's appeared nowhere on it.

largest standing gap and is not fixed by an audit.

## 8. What this audit does not settle

- **Goods on a store that names none** — T-1404, which works the in-window trades from
  the research and raises a business for each.
- **Staff** — a staff row on 85 of 196 records; a `staffing` block on 88. The
  papers name proprietors and almost never a hand, so an empty list is a true reading
  of the register and not an omission. T-1183 rules the staffing model; T-1422 laid it
  over the schools and the printing offices as `staffing`, which names nobody; T-1462
  lays the 124 reconstructed seats T-1433 drew onto the 84 houses that took them, each
  with the shortfall that house still stands at against the model's typical band.
- **Secondary premises** — § 6. T-1404 and T-1405.
- **Black-owned and Native or Métis-run houses** — T-1403.
- **The class counts against the census's own** — `tools/trade_census_1835.py`.
