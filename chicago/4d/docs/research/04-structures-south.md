## 1. Fort Dearborn (second fort, 1816) and the Government Reservation

### 1.1 Garrison status on 1835-07-01 — **OCCUPIED**

| Dates | Commandant | Source |

| 1832-06-17 → 1833-05-14 | Maj. William Whistler, 2nd Inf. | `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 181; [drloihjournal](https://drloihjournal.blogspot.com/2018/06/history-of-the-first-and-second-fort-dearborn-in-Chicago.html) |
| 1833-05-14 → 1833-06-19 | Capt./Bvt. Maj. John Fowle, 5th Inf. | `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 619, 627 |
| 1833-06-19 → 1833-10-31 | Maj. George Bender, 5th Inf. | `[DOC]` drloihjournal; Andreas I, scan p. 487 |
| 1833-10-31 → 1833-12-18 | Capt. DeLafayette Wilcox, 5th Inf. | `[DOC]` drloihjournal |
| **1833-12-18 → 1835-09-16** | **Maj. John Greene, 5th Inf. — in command on 1835-07-01** | `[DOC]` drloihjournal |
| 1835-09-16 → 1836-08-01 | Capt. DeLafayette Wilcox, 5th Inf. | `[DOC]` drloihjournal |
| 1836-08-01 → **1836-12-29 (final evacuation)** | Bvt. Maj. Joseph Plympton, 5th Inf. | `[DOC]` drloihjournal; [chicagology prefire052](https://chicagology.com/prefire/prefire052/) |

- **Correction to the brief's premise:** troops did *not* return in 1832 after a 1823 gap. Sequence is: garrison withdrawn **May 1823** (Andreas I, scan p. 183: *"In 1823 the fort was evacuated, and remained for several years without a garrison"*); re-garrisoned **Oct 1828** after the Winnebago scare (Andreas I, scan p. 193); **troops removed to Green Bay, May 1831** ([chicagology prefire276](http://chicagology.com/prefire/prefire276/)); re-garrisoned **June 1832**, Black Hawk War (Andreas I, scan p. 181: *"In June the fort was once more garrisoned. Major William Whistler being assigned to the command"*); continuously held thereafter to **1836-12-29**. `[DOC]`
- Garrison strength in 1833 was **two companies of U.S. infantry** (Andreas I, scan p. 235). `[DOC]` Strength in mid-1835 not directly attested → model **one to two reduced companies, ~50–90 men**. `[INF]`
- Lt. J. L. Thompson "came June 26, 1833, and remained until December, 1836" — a continuously-resident junior officer, confirming unbroken occupancy through 1835. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 281.
- The fort was also functioning as a **general hospital** during the 1832 cholera episode; by 1835 that use has lapsed. `[DOC]` chicagology prefire052.

### 1.2 Buildings inside the palisade

Best interior description is Andreas' **1827** inventory (scan p. 549), which should be treated as the baseline for 1835 absent evidence of demolition:

- **Palisade:** high wooden pickets; **double wall of wooden palisades** in the 1816 rebuild. Bastions at alternate angles; **large gates opening north and south**. `[DOC]` Juliette Kinzie's 1831 eyewitness account (quoted in [Wikipedia: Fort Dearborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Dearborn) and drloihjournal): *"The fort was inclosed by high pickets, with bastions at the alternate angles. Large gates opened to the north and south."*
- **Block-house at the SOUTHWEST corner**; **bastion at the northwest corner**. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 181, 549. **Guard:** the "two blockhouses at SE and NW corners" description (Andreas I, scan p. 171; Britannica; Currey) belongs to the **FIRST fort (1803–1812)** — do not apply it to the 1816 fort. `[DOC]`
- **Brick magazine**, positioned *between the western guard[house] and the block-houses*. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 549.
- **Officers' quarters — brick buildings** on the **west** side (Andreas I, scan pp. 181, 549). The 1855 photo key gives: **"A" Commandant's Quarters, brick, ~25 × 50 ft**; **"B" Officers' Quarters, wood, ~30 × 60 ft**, both at/near the northwest corner. `[DOC]` chicagology prefire052 / prefire276 / drloihjournal. **Conflict:** Andreas says brick officers' quarters; the 1855 key says the officers' quarters were wood and only the commandant's was brick. Model the commandant's quarters as brick and flag the officers' quarters material as **contested**.
- **Soldiers' barracks — log**, on the **east** side; separate **log quarters for voyageurs**. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 181, 549.
- **Parade ground ~80 × 200 ft**, with a **sun-dial on a timber post**. `[DOC]` chicagology prefire052.
- **Sutler's building** (key "D"), **storehouse(s)**, **artillery house**, **quartermaster's quarters**, **hospital**. `[DOC]` chicagology prefire052.
- **Sally-port / subterranean passage** from the parade ground north to the river. `[DOC]` chicagology prefire052 (attested for the 1816 fort's north side).
- **North gate** (key "E"). `[DOC]` chicagology prefire052.
- **Kitchen garden at the southwest corner** inside; **company gardens** outside, *"well filled with currant-bushes and young fruit-trees"* (Kinzie, 1831). The garrison garden with shrubbery and young fruit trees extended **south toward Madison Street**. `[DOC]` [chicagology prefire273](https://chicagology.com/prefire/prefire273/).
- **Condition 1835:** no source reports dilapidation before the late 1830s; the fort was still an active, maintained post in 1835 and only "in decay" language appears after the 1837 conversion to harbor-works use. Model as **serviceable, weathered, whitewashed/unpainted log-and-brick.** `[INF]`
- **Fate (for the timeline, not 1835):** buildings deeded/turned over after 1837; principal structures demolished **summer 1856** for river widening; fire 1857; remnants gone by 1871. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 337; chicagology prefire276; Wikipedia.

### 1.3 The Reservation

- **75.69 acres**, the **southwest fractional quarter of Section 10, T39N, R14E, 3rd P.M.** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 183.
- Extended from the river mouth south along the lake; **Michigan Avenue** was its western/lakeward frame in the platted grid, and **State Street** was the town's eastern boundary on the south side until the 1835 extension. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 339 ("the land upon the east side of Michigan Avenue, in Block 5, between South Water Street and the lots reserved"); [chicagology prefire278](https://chicagology.com/prefire/prefire278/).
- **Live event at the epoch:** **Jean Baptiste Beaubien's pre-emption certificate for the whole reservation, dated 1835-05-28, recorded 1835-06-26, price $94.61** — i.e., about five weeks old on 1835-07-01. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 183, 185, 337. Voided by *Wilcox v. Jackson*, 38 U.S. 498 (1839). `[DOC]` [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/38/498/)
- **Guard:** *"the land whereon the block-house stood, and the northern half of Block 5, was not parted with"* — retained by Treasury. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 341.
- The reservation was **unplatted** in 1835 — no streets crossed it; South Water Street was ordered "pitched" only **from the United States Reservation to Randolph Street** (Aug 1833 order, to be done by April 1834). `[DOC]` [chicagology/southwaterstreet](https://chicagology.com/chicagostreets/southwaterstreet/)

### 1.4 The Indian Agency / "Cobweb Castle" — **NORTH SIDE, not south**

- **Guard for the model:** the U.S. Indian Agency house nicknamed **"Cobweb Castle"** stood on the **NORTH bank** of the river, at/near the corner of **North State (Wolcott) and North Water Streets**, near the later Galena & Chicago Union freight house. Do **not** place it inside or beside the fort. `[DOC]` [chicagology prefire276](http://chicagology.com/prefire/prefire276/): the Indian Agency occupied *"the log house, on the north side"*, *"familiarly known as 'Cob-Web Castle.'"* Andreas I, scan pp. 207, 1233 place the agency-house explicitly "on the North Side."
- It was **a log building of two large rooms** in Dr. Alexander Wolcott's era (Andreas I, scan p. 191); nicknamed for the cobwebs of his bachelor residency (scan p. 193). Left unoccupied at Wolcott's death, late fall **1830** (scan p. 237). `[DOC]`
- **Status 1835:** standing and in civilian occupation — **Col. Richard J. Hamilton** resided in "the old Indian agency-house" by 1833, and John Watkins taught school beside it that spring. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 305, 429.
- **Guard:** the two-story log agency house *"at the west of the fort and fronting north on the river"* (Andreas I, scan p. 171) is the **1803–1812** agency, burned in 1812. Not present in 1835. `[DOC]`
- A blacksmith shop stood **"near the Agency House, at the foot of State Street"** (north side). `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 1161.

## 2. The Lighthouse (1832)

- **Authorized** by Act of Congress 1831-03-03; **$5,000 appropriated March 1831** after the engineers' harbor plan. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 499; [lighthousefriends](https://www.lighthousefriends.com/light.asp?ID=234)
- **First tower collapsed 1831-10-30**, ~10 months after completion. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 499.
- **Second tower: built and completed by "Mr. Jackson" in 1832; forty feet high; conical stone/masonry.** First on the Great Lakes. **This is the structure standing on 1835-07-01.** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 499; drloihjournal; lighthousefriends.
- **Location:** on the reservation, immediately adjacent to the fort at the river mouth — Andreas: *"Fort Dearborn and the light-house are placed at the angle thus formed"* (scan p. 263), i.e. at the bend of the old river channel, on the south bank west/southwest of the fort proper. `[DOC]` for adjacency; exact offset `[CONJ]` — model ~50–100 yds from the stockade, riverward.
- **Keeper:** **Samuel S. Lasby** first keeper (Andreas I, scan p. 499; drloihjournal). Keeper on 1835-07-01 not directly attested — William M. Stevens is named for 1836. `[INF]` Lasby or an interim keeper.
- **Guard:** the **pier-head light at the end of the North Pier is a LATER structure** (Andreas I, scan p. 339: *"The Government had erected a new light-house at the end of the North Pier"*; the skeletal iron pier light dates 1859). Do **not** put a light on the pier in 1835. `[DOC]`
- **Keeper's dwelling:** salary $350/yr with free quarters `[DOC]`; a small keeper's house adjacent to the tower is `[CONJ]` for 1835.

## 4. The Dearborn Street drawbridge (1834)

- **Builder:** **Nelson R. Norton**, ship-carpenter; arrived Chicago 1833-11-16, cut lumber over the winter, **construction commenced March 1834**. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 283; [chicagology Dearborn Street Bridges](https://chicagology.com/harbor/riverbridges/dearbornstreetbridges/)
- **Completed** by Norton's claim **1834-06-01**; **formally accepted by the trustees in August 1834**. `[DOC]` chicagology.
- **Dimensions:** *"about three hundred feet long"*, with *"the opening for the passage of craft about sixty feet."* `[DOC]` chicagology. A design premium of **$25 had been offered for the best plan, the draw to be forty feet** — the built draw was larger. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 301.
- **Design:** **"gallows pattern"** — a tall timber A/gallows **frame at either end of the draw**, from which the leaf was hoisted by chains/tackle. Andreas' source: *"for five years, the frames, one at either end, stood like instruments of death to frighten the timid stranger at night."* `[DOC]` chicagology. Model as a **long low timber trestle causeway with a single hoisted draw leaf at midstream, flanked by two tall gallows towers.**
- **Location:** in line with **Dearborn Street**, crossing the main river; the approach land "adjoining Michigan Avenue" is later Pearsons property. `[DOC]` chicagology. Practically: south abutment at the foot of Dearborn on South Water Street. `[INF]`
- **Condition at 1835-07-01:** already troublesome — struck repeatedly by vessels; **"The bridge was repaired in 1835 and 1837."** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 415. The city had already paid **$166.67 for repairs in September 1834**. `[DOC]` chicagology. On one occasion the raised draw jammed **48 hours**. `[DOC]` Model as **weathered, patched, sagging**.
- **Fate:** Common Council ordered removal July 1839; a crowd chopped it to pieces before dawn the next day. `[DOC]` chicagology; Andreas I, scan p. 1319 ("Dearborn-street bridge demolished").
- **Related:** the drawbridge was a **landmark address** in 1835 — the *Chicago American* office was *"on Water Street, near the draw-bridge."* `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 767.

## 5. South Water Street commerce, mid-1835

South Water was the **first street** and, with Lake, one of *"the two principal thoroughfares of the village"*; both were early turnpiked and graded, and **plank sluices were built across Clark Street** to drain toward the South Branch. Grading order July 1834 required the street to shed water from each cross street into the river. `[DOC]` chicagology/southwaterstreet. **South Water lots were 55 ft wide** (Andreas I, scan p. 497). `[DOC]`

### Date/location guards for South Water

- **Newberry & Dole's forwarding warehouse was on the NORTH side of the river.** Andreas: *"It was made by Newberry & Dole, whose warehouse was on the North Side, immediately east of where Rush-street bridge now stands."* `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 1139. **Do not put it on South Water Street.** Their **wharf** (where the *Illinois* docked 1834-07-12) is the same north-bank frontage. `[DOC]` scan p. 503. Separately, **George W. Dole's 1832 warehouse and slaughter yard** were on the **south side**, *"close to the present site of the Tremont House"* (Lake & Dearborn) — model that one south of the river. `[DOC]` scan p. 261. In **1834 Newberry and Dole built a slaughter-house on the South Branch**. `[DOC]` scan p. 1151.
- **G. S. Hubbard had NO South Water Street warehouse in 1835.** In **1834 Hubbard packed "in the old bank building, corner of Lake and La Salle streets"**; his Kinzie Street packing house is 1837–38; **his South Water Street house (Clark–LaSalle) dates 1840**. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 1151. "Hubbard's Folly" does not appear in Andreas at all. Treat any 1835 Hubbard South Water warehouse as **unsupported**.
- **P. F. W. Peck's brick house is 1837, not 1835** — *"the first brick house erected in Chicago"*, later a police station, demolished 1868. `[DOC]` chicagology. **Exclude from 1835.**
- **Frink & Walker's stage office** appears only in later material (Andreas I, scan pp. 65, 1305). In 1835–36 stage seats were taken **at Markle's Exchange Coffee House** (Lake & Wells) per Dr. Temple's stage-line advertisement. `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 945. Model the stage office as **not yet a separate building** in mid-1835.
- Andreas records **250 vessel arrivals in 1835, 22,500 tons** — the wharves along the south bank should be busy. `[DOC]` scan p. 303.

## 6. Lake Street by mid-1835 — was it overtaking South Water?

**Answer: not yet, but the shift is visibly underway.** `[INF]` South Water still had the wharves, the forwarding houses, the post office and both newspapers; Lake had the hotels, the churches and the courthouse block. The decisive tilt comes **1836–37** with the Saloon Building, the City Hotel and Steele's brick blocks. `[INF]` chicagology/lakestreet asserts Lake "once was the queen of the city" but dates the tipping to the late 1830s.

### Lake/Randolph date guards — **NOT built by 1835-07-01**

## 7. Civic buildings and the Public Square (Clark/Randolph/Washington/LaSalle)

- **"Estray pen"** — Chicago's **first public building**, erected on the **southwest corner of the public square** (Randolph/Clark/Washington/LaSalle). Andreas: *"there arose upon the southwest corner of the square, the so-called 'estray-pen'"*; the chronology entry reads *"(March) — First public building (the 'Estray[-pen]')"*, i.e. **March 1833** (some accounts say 1832). Used for stray livestock and, faute de mieux, prisoners. **Standing 1835-07-01.** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 33, 365, 1315; Cook County Sheriff's Office history
- **Log jail** — *"During the fall of 1833, the citizens felt that something more metropolitan was required than the 'estray pen'"*; a log jail was built on the **northwest corner of the court-house square**. **Standing 1835-07-01.** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 367, 1243
- **First Cook County court-house** — **1835**, generally placed at the **southwest corner of Clark & Randolph** / on the public square; a small wooden stockade-type building. `[DOC]` [Illinois Court History PDF](https://www.illinoiscourthistory.org/resources/858a0c70-b542-438b-88d3-087d86e131dd/Cookcountyhistory.pdf); Andreas I, scan p. 373 heading *"THE FIRST COURT-HOUSE."*
  - **Date guard, important:** no source I found fixes a **month** in 1835. On **1835-07-01 it may have been unbuilt, under construction, or just finished.** Model it as **under construction / frame going up**, and flag. `[INF]`
  - **Guard:** the well-documented **"court-house on the northeast corner of the public square, basement and principal story, about thirty by sixty feet, court-room and jury rooms on the principal floor, clerk's and recorder's offices and vaults in the basement, front ornamented with a four-column Doric portico of wood work"** is the **1837** description. **Do not use its portico/Doric elevation for 1835.** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 1035
  - **County clerk's office:** R. J. Hamilton, Clerk of the County Court, was issuing estray notices in 1834 — the clerk's office in 1835 was most likely **rented rooms**, not a purpose-built structure. `[DOC]` on Hamilton; `[INF]` on housing
- **Public square in 1835:** otherwise open, unimproved, fenced or unfenced prairie block. `[INF]`
- **Fire apparatus:** **the first fire engine was purchased 1835-12-01 from Hubbard & Co. for $894.38** and Engine Company No. 1 organized then — so **no engine house on 1835-07-01**. **EXCLUDE.** `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan pp. 299, 463

## 9. Schools

Its precise siting is usually given as near the reservation's western edge, i.e. **near State Street between South Water and Lake** — `[CONJ]` on the exact lot. ## 11. Landscape / setting notes for the model

- **Population:** ~3,265 in the 1835 count (Andreas chronology, scan p. 1317). `[DOC]` A parallel figure of 3,279 circulates. Flag.
- **Lakefront:** the **beach and standing sand hills lay east of Michigan Avenue**; *"between Michigan Avenue and the beach of Lake Michigan. The sand hills near the shore were still standing."* `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 69 (describing 1836 — apply to 1835). Model **low dune ridges** between Michigan Ave and the water, with the fort/lighthouse on the made angle at the river mouth.
- **Streets:** unpaved; South Water and Lake turnpiked/graded first, **fully turnpiked only by winter 1836**. `[DOC]` chicagology/southwaterstreet. In July 1836 there was still *"a pond of water on Lake Street, corner of La Salle, inhabited by frogs."* `[DOC]` Andreas I, scan p. 403 — model **standing water and mud at LaSalle & Lake**, and a **slough in front of the Presbyterian church at Lake & Clark**. `[DOC]`
- **Town boundary (from 1833-11-06):** Jackson St south, Jefferson/Cook west, Ohio north; east bounded on the south side **by State Street** (reservation beyond). **Extended 1835-02-11** east to the lake shore and out to Chicago Ave / Twelfth St. `[DOC]` chicagology prefire278; chicagology/southwaterstreet
- **Building stock:** overwhelmingly **frame and log**; **no brick building of consequence yet on the south side** — the first brick house is 1837, the first three-story building (Saloon) 1836. Model everything except the fort's magazine/commandant's quarters as **timber**. `[DOC]`/`[INF]`

## 12. Block-by-block sketch — South Water Street, forks → fort, mid-1835

North side of the street = the **river bank, wharves, and small landings**; south side = the **store fronts**. Lots 55 ft wide. `[DOC]`

```
                          C H I C A G O   R I V E R  (main stem)  →→ to lake
 ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
   wharves / landings / lumber piles along the whole north face of the street
 ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────── S O U T H
      W A T E R   S T R E E T   (unpaved, graded to shed water to the river)
 ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
 [MARKET]      [FRANKLIN]   [WELLS]    [LaSALLE]  [CLARK]   [DEARBORN]  [STATE]
```

**A. Forks / Market to Franklin** — the west end, thin. Wharfage and the approach to Wolf Point across the South Branch. `[INF]` No named south-side structure firmly attested in this block for 1835; drloihjournal's placement of "Hogan's store at South Water and Market" **conflicts** with Andreas' "near Franklin and South Water" — prefer Andreas. `[DOC]`

**B. Franklin to Wells** — the civic-religious cluster of the west end.
- **Temple Building** (1833), corner Franklin & South Water: two-story frame, ~$900; **First Baptist worship below, Sproat's boys' school above**; used in turn by Methodists, Presbyterians and Baptists. `[DOC]`
- **Post Office + Brewster, Hogan & Co. store**, one building partitioned in two, near Franklin. Hogan postmaster; John Bates, Jr. deputy. `[DOC]`

**C. Wells to LaSalle**
- **Philo Carpenter's small store** (built summer 1833 on his lot in this block) — drugs. `[DOC]`
- Remainder: scattered frame stores/dwellings, gaps. `[CONJ]`

**D. LaSalle to Clark** — the commercial core.
- **SW corner South Water & LaSalle: P. F. W. Peck's two-story frame store** with unfinished loft. `[DOC]`
- Mid-block: the **militia rendezvous ground** between Clark and LaSalle. `[DOC]`
- **Corner of Clark & South Water: Harmon & Loomis's store/building.** `[DOC]` (height in 1835 uncertain)

**E. Clark to Dearborn** — the print block.
- **SW corner South Water & Clark: the *Chicago Democrat* office** (John Calhoun), in a building that was *unfinished* when he took it in Nov 1833. `[DOC]`
- **South Water near the drawbridge: the *Chicago American* office**, four weeks old on 1835-07-01, and **Frederick Thomas's shop two doors from it**. `[DOC]`
- **West side of Dearborn between Lake and South Water: John Bates, Jr.'s wooden auction room** (1833/34), fronting Dearborn but anchoring this block. `[DOC]`
- **The Dearborn Street drawbridge** lands here: ~300 ft of timber causeway with a 60-ft draw and **two tall gallows frames**, patched from its 1835 repairs. `[DOC]`

**F. Dearborn to State** — thinning toward the reservation.
- **SW corner South Water & Dearborn: Madore Beaubien's two-room log house** (1831). `[DOC]`
- Otherwise sparse; State Street was the town's eastern edge on this side until Feb 1835. `[DOC]`

**G. Across the reservation, State Street → the lake** — no platted street, no lots.
- Open reservation ground, garrison **gardens and orchard extending south toward Madison**. `[DOC]`
- **Beaubien homestead cluster** at South Water & Michigan Avenue (corner contested). `[DOC]`
- **Fort Dearborn** at the river mouth: picket palisade, SW blockhouse, NW bastion, brick commandant's quarters, officers' quarters, log barracks, brick magazine, sutler's store, storehouse, hospital, north and south gates, sally-port to the river, 80 × 200 ft parade with sun-dial — **garrisoned, Maj. John Greene, 5th Infantry**. `[DOC]`
- **The 40-ft 1832 lighthouse** at the angle beside the fort. `[DOC]`
- Beyond: the **north and south piers** driving out into the lake — north pier the longer, mid-extension toward its 1,260-ft end-of-season length; **no light on the pier yet**. `[DOC]`/`[INF]`
- East of Michigan Avenue: **beach and standing sand hills**. `[DOC]`
