# Spanish jaunts — the translator's glossary

The Spanish telling of a jaunt is the same walk in another language (tools/jaunts_es.py has the
file format). These rules keep every jaunt's Spanish in one voice.

## Voice

- Neutral Latin American Spanish, the Spanish most Chicago students hear at home. Address the
  visitor as `tú`; groups as `ustedes`, never `vosotros`.
- Translate; do not rewrite. No fact, name, date, number, hedge or explanation enters that the
  English does not have, and none is dropped. Keep the paragraph breaks.
- Keep the English telling's reading level: a grades 3-5 telling stays short and plain, an 18+
  telling stays as frank as its English.
- Storytelling stays storytelling: "according to legend" is `según la leyenda`, with no extra
  hedging. Never mention translation, the app or how anything was built.
- Choice labels are buttons: short, an imperative or a noun phrase, as the English is.

## Names and terms

| English | Spanish |
|---|---|
| Jaunt / Jaunts (and the opening's `JAUNT:` prefix) | paseo / Paseos (`PASEO:`) |
| Attested · Inferred · Reconstructed | Atestiguado · Inferido · Reconstruido |
| Sources · Liberties · Confidence view | Fuentes · Licencias · Vista de confianza |
| Chicago River · the river | el río Chicago · el río |
| Lake Michigan · the lake | el lago Míchigan · el lago |
| Fort Dearborn · the fort | el fuerte Dearborn · el fuerte |
| a block (of a street) | una cuadra |
| feet · miles · yards | pies · millas · yardas |
| 10 June 1835 | 10 de junio de 1835 |

- People, businesses, ships, hotels, houses and streets keep their own names: Lake Street,
  South Water Street, Prairie Avenue, the Sauganash, John Kinzie, Glessner House. A street
  named in passing may take `la calle`: `la calle Lake`, `en Lake y Market`.
- Nations keep their own names, written as Spanish writes a people (lower case): los
  potawatomi, los ho-chunk, los odawa, los ojibwe. Use the word the English uses and no other.
- Titles: Spanish sentence case (`Un abrigo decente`).

## Quotations

A quotation from a source is given in Spanish, inside the same quote marks the English uses,
covering the same words and no more. The jaunt panel tells the visitor, in Spanish, that
quotations are translated from English. Commas and full stops stay outside the closing mark
where the English has them outside.
