# The owner's brief of 2026-08-18 — reference views, and a standing ruling

Eleven reference images supplied by the project owner in the session of 2026-08-18,
plus one screenshot of the current render. **The image binaries are not yet committed** —
they were supplied in-session and this file is the written record of what each shows,
made so the queue tickets that consume them (T-0062 … T-0079) are self-contained.
Most are identifiable published plates; T-0075 owns identifying each against
chicagology/Andreas plate numbering and creating proper source records, after which
the binaries (or better scans of the same plates) belong in this directory.

**These are TIER 5 PICTORIAL sources** unless a record says otherwise — retrospective
views drawn decades after 1835. They may drive **massing, roof form, fenestration,
materials, furniture and setting** as `inferred`, and they may never drive a coordinate
or a footprint outline. Several depict people: **L1 stands — the people in these images
are reference for scale and use only and are never depicted in the scene.**

## The standing ruling that came with them

The owner, verbatim: *"in general i think you are being too cautious on adding items to

below quotes the owner overriding a conservative reading (two attested docks, signs only
where attested, wagons only at attested yards), the override is the rule.

## The images

1. **The first Cook County jail** (engraving; Andreas-family plate). A low log jail —
   squared timber, near-flat roof — behind it and along the street: **board fences lining
   the street frontage and plank sidewalks** at the fence line. Consumed by T-0070 (the
   jail itself) and T-0069 (the streetscape it demonstrates).
2. **The Dearborn Street drawbridge, view one** (engraving). The 1834 **gallows-frame
   drawbridge**: twin timber A-frames/gallows posts above the deck, chains to the leaves,
   timber approaches. **Sailing vessels (schooner rig) moored close by**; small boats on
   the water. Consumed by T-0071 and T-0063.
3. **The Dearborn Street drawbridge, view two** (engraving). Same bridge from farther
   out: masts crowd the reach below the bridge; a light/beacon structure near the mouth;
   low warehouses on the banks. Consumed by T-0071, T-0063, T-0062.
4. **The Wolf Tavern** (engraving). A one-and-a-half-storey **log** tavern, and beside it
   a **very tall pole with a cross-arm at the top, flying a board sign painted with a
   wolf** — read by the owner as "almost like a flag". Barrels by the wall. Consumed by
   T-0072.
5. **The Tremont House street scene, 1833** (the dense street view; owner reads it as
   **looking southwest from the corner of Lake and Dearborn**). A continuous two-storey
   storefront row: shared party lines, signboards across the fronts, awnings, plank
   walks. The owner: *"you can reconstruct most of the main downtown area from this
   diagram. there should be more and denser buildings. this is important."* Consumed by
   T-0077/T-0078/T-0079 (the T-0073 split) and T-0066.
6. **The Green Tree Inn — 1838** (Braunhold engraving, Andreas 1884; the existing
   2026-08-11 set holds a sibling as plate "11"). Two-storey clapboard inn, even 6-over-6
   bays, end chimneys; **post-mounted hanging signboard** at the corner; **plank sidewalks
   with board crossings**; a lower gabled wing at the left with a wide (carriage) door;
   **ship masts rise directly behind the roofline** — the river is that close. Consumed by
   T-0042, T-0066, T-0069.
7. **The Green Tree Inn** (Trowbridge drawing). Same inn from the other quarter: the
   hanging "GREEN TREE" sign on its post, a **bench of sitters against the front wall**,
   an **open-sided wagon shed attached at the left with a covered wagon under it**, farm
   wagons in the yard, a dirt road with **grass verges**, brush and small trees at the
   fence lines. Consumed by T-0042, T-0064, T-0067.
8. **"Sauganash Hotel. 1831"** (C. E. Petford watercolour, Chicago History Museum
   family). The clapboard two-storey block with its **log wing** at the left; **green
   (moss/shingle-dark) roof**, **brick chimneys**; a small flat-hooded **entrance
   frontispiece/porch** on the front door; **plank sidewalk with a board crossing over
   the road**; two **posts** (hitching/corner posts) at the road edge; a **vertical-board
   fence** running off to the right; trees behind the fence. Consumed by T-0043, T-0069.
9. **"The Sauganash Hotel"** (Braunhold engraving, © A. T. Andreas 1884). Same building:
   log wing with its shed-roofed porch over the door, plank walks on both frontages,
   **hitching posts**, board fence at the right, the clapboard block's wide entrance
   surround. Consumed by T-0043.
10. **The Sauganash** (Trowbridge drawing). Adds: **louvred shutters on the sash**, the
    tall board fence enclosing the rear yard, a saddled horse at the post (L1: reference
    only), the log wing's door direct to grade. Consumed by T-0043.
11. **"South Water Street in 1834 — now Wacker Drive"** (© Chicago Historical Society
    postcard of a retrospective painting). The **south bank river frontage as a
    continuous working row**: roughly ten one-storey log and frame buildings shoulder to
    shoulder facing the river, two two-storey frame stores anchoring the east end; an
    **ox-drawn covered wagon train** on the river street; **rowboats** on the water and
    at the bank; a single large tree at the west; grassy bank between street and water.
    Consumed by T-0078, T-0062, T-0063, T-0064.
12. **Chicago circa 1833, looking east** (coloured retrospective view; lake on the
    horizon). Scattered frame houses, and the ground plane is the lesson: **split-rail
    and board fences line both sides of the roads and enclose every property**; inside
    the fences the ground reads as **garden, orchard rows, dooryard green — not prairie**;
    **trees and bushes stand close around the houses**, kept deliberately; a covered
    wagon on the road. Consumed by T-0068, T-0067, T-0069, T-0074.

### What each identification rests on

**3 — the drawbridge.** The plate at `chicagology.com/harbor/riverbridges/dearbornstreetbridges/`
carries the three things the brief gives image 3 and not image 2: masts crowding the reach
below the bridge, a tapering light structure on the far shore near the mouth, and low
warehouses along the bank. It also draws the bridge as image 2 describes it — twin gallows
frames, chains to the leaves, a railed deck. **Image 2 is a different plate and is not
claimed by this record.**

**5 — the Tremont row.** The signature was read off the plate at 5× (`N. Roswell Gifford`);
the row, the party lines, the lettered boards and the board walk are all on it. **Awnings are
not**, at the resolution published, and the record says so rather than nodding the brief
through.

**7 — the Green Tree.** Every feature the brief lists is on the plate: the GREEN TREE board
on its post, the bench of sitters, the attached wagon shed with a covered wagon under it, the
farm wagons, the grass verges, the brush at the fence lines. Signed and dated by the artist.

**10 — the Sauganash.** Louvred shutters, the tall board fence behind, the saddled horse at a
post, the log wing's door direct to grade — all four, plus the road-edge posts. The signature
carries a two-digit year that is **not legible** at this resolution, so the record dates it
"undated" and says the Green Tree companion is signed 1902.

## Identifications — T-0136, 2026-09-13: Andreas at page-image level

T-0075 left eight of the twelve open and named the route out for the two Braunhold plates:
**go to Andreas volume I at page-image level rather than to another aggregator.** That route
was taken. It resolves three of the eight, not two, and it settles their rights at the same
time — which was the second half of the reason for taking it.

**How the plates were found, so nobody has to find them again.** The volume prints its own
**LIST OF ILLUSTRATIONS** (§ VIEWS), and it gives the printed page of every plate in the book.
Read at the page image it names, among others, *Wolf Point in 1830* (630), *Dearborn-street
Drawbridge, 1834* (631), *Sauganash Hotel* (632), *Green Tree Hotel* (634) and *Frink &
Walker's Stage Office* (636) — five plates in seven pages, because Andreas gathers the early
taverns into one chapter, "Wolf Point and Early Hotels". Nobody had opened that list.

Two facts about the archive.org scan, both measured and both non-obvious, are recorded on each
new source record's `access_notes` as well as here:

- the derivative served by `/download/historyofchicago01andr/page/n<leaf>_large.jpg` is
  **mirrored** — flip it horizontally before reading anything;
- in this stretch of the volume **leaf index = 2 × printed page + 33**, verified against six
  plates and portraits (pages 629, 630, 631, 632, 634, 636). The arithmetic is **local**: leaf
  361 is not printed page 164. Re-derive the offset from a known plate before trusting it
  elsewhere. The item's `_page_numbers.json` derivative carries no page numbers at all, and
  `_scandata.xml`'s `<pageNumber>` disagrees with the BookReader leaf index; neither is usable.

### What resolved

- **Image 9, the Sauganash (Braunhold).** Andreas vol. I p. 632, plate captioned *"THE
  SAUGANASH HOTEL."*, with a copyright line lettered **inside the plate border at the lower
  left**: `A. T. Andreas`, year reading `188` with the fourth digit not crisp. The clapboard
  block, the log wing with its door direct to grade under a shed roof, the wide entrance
  surround and the plank walk are all on it. The brief's **hitching posts and right-hand board
  fence are not resolvable** in this scan and are therefore not claimed.
  → `andreas_1884_sauganash_hotel_plate`.
- **Image 6, the Green Tree (Braunhold).** Andreas vol. I p. 634, plate captioned *"THE GREEN
  TREE HOTEL."* — **no year on the caption**, so the brief's "1838" is the brief's and this
  record does not carry it. Identified on the two features nothing else in the volume's VIEWS
  draws: a signboard hanging from a **tall post clear of the wall** at the corner, and **ship's
  masts rising directly behind the roofline**. It is an 1884 witness to the post-mounted sign,
  eighteen years older than and independent of `trowbridge_green_tree_1902`, and it agrees.
  → `andreas_1884_green_tree_hotel_plate`.
- **Image 2, the closer Dearborn drawbridge view** — which was not on this ticket's priority
  list and fell out of the same chapter. Andreas vol. I p. 631, *"DEARBORN STREET DRAWBRIDGE,
  BUILT IN 1834."*: two braced gallows frames with chains, a railed deck on cribbing, a trestle
  approach, and **one schooner under sail immediately alongside**. It carries none of image 3's
  three markers — the crowded reach, the beacon, the bank warehouses — which is exactly the
  distinction `dearborn_street_bridge_1834_engraving` drew when it declined to claim this view.
  → `andreas_1884_dearborn_drawbridge_plate`.

**And the rights question closed with them.** All three are `public_domain` on the volume's
1884 imprint. Every plate this brief had a record for until today is `check_required`, because
every one of them was cited to a blog. That is the difference the page-level route was expected
to make, and it made it.

### The five still open, and the NEW searches that were run

Not one of these repeats the four routes T-0075 recorded.

- **Image 4, the Wolf Tavern.** *Ruled out against Andreas, positively.* The volume's only Wolf
  Point view is **p. 630, "WOLF POINT IN 1830"**, and it was fetched and read: it is a distant
  landscape across the water — the far bank, a cloud-filled sky, marsh grass in the foreground —
  and **not** the close view of a log tavern with barrels at the wall the brief describes. There
  is no other tavern plate in the volume's list of VIEWS. Worth recording anyway: a tall pole
  does stand beside the buildings on the far bank in that plate, and Andreas's text on the
  facing pages argues at length about **who painted the wolf sign and when** — so the sign is
  attested in Andreas even though the brief's plate is not.
- **Image 1, the first Cook County jail.** *Ruled out against Andreas, positively.* The list of
  ILLUSTRATIONS was read end to end. Volume I reproduces *The First Court-House* (177) and *The
  Second Court-House* (176, 181) and **no jail at all** — so the brief's jail engraving is not
  an Andreas volume I plate, and the next attempt should go to volume II or to the Chicago
  Historical Society's own print holdings, not back here.
- **Image 8, the Petford watercolour.** A real advance short of the accession number. The
  **Chicago History Museum Prints and Photographs Collection is reachable as `chm_pp` on
  CARLI's ContentDM API** (`collections.carli.illinois.edu/digital/api/…`), and it was searched.
  It holds exactly one Petford: **"Kinzie mansion in 1883", ICHi-01222, artist "Petford, C. E.",
  dated 1893, "No known copyright restrictions"**. So the artist is confirmed in the CHM
  collection, the shape of the eventual record is confirmed (an `ICHi-` number and a
  no-known-restrictions line), and — the part that matters for grading — **the Petford hand is
  a hand of the 1890s**, which makes the brief's "Sauganash Hotel. 1831" a retrospective of some
  sixty years and not a contemporary record. The Sauganash watercolour itself is **not in the
  digitised subset**: `chm_pp` returns two hits for *Sauganash* and both are something else (a
  Mark Beaubien portrait, and the 1860 Wigwam).
- **Image 11, the CHS postcard of "South Water Street in 1834".** Searched in the same `chm_pp`
  collection; the nine hits for *South Water Street* are all twentieth-century photography
  (Hedrich-Blessing and later). Its rights line still cannot be read off the object, so it still
  gets no record.
- **Image 12, "Chicago circa 1833, looking east".** Not an Andreas volume I plate: the volume's
  general views are *Chicago in 1779* (frontispiece), *Chicago in 1830* (164) and *Chicago in
  1845* (149), none of them c. 1833, and all of them wood engravings where the brief's image is
  **coloured**. `chm_pp` has nothing for it either.

**The obstacle, stated so it is not re-hit.** The Chicago History Museum's own catalogue at
`images.chicagohistory.org` and the Chicago Collections aggregator at
`explore.chicagocollections.org` both **refuse an automated client** from this runner — a flat
403 from the first and a Cloudflare interstitial from the second — and HathiTrust returns 403 as
well. CARLI's ContentDM API answers normally and is the way in to CHM material from here. That
is why image 8 ends with an artist and a collection rather than an accession number.

**Still true of all eight, and of the five that remain: none of them blocks anything.** What the
three new records change is that the claims resting on them cite a numbered page of a
public-domain volume instead of a line in this file.


### 2026-10-23 — T-2380 jail attribution / rights correction (text only)

The detailed jail artwork remains distinct from Andreas's courthouse plate. A matching earlier copy was actually read in the UCR/Calisphere Keystone-Mast catalogue, item 1996.0009.L54254, creator Francis Dare, Date Created and/or Issued September 19, 1930; the separate newspaper reproduction is credited 10 June 1947. The combined copy date does not prove original publication in 1930, and original engraving/publication remain unknown. The textual source `calisphere_ucr_first_jail_catalogue_1930` records the credit and unresolved rights (`check_required`, `text_only`). The inherited image inventory's pre-1930/public-domain assertion is withdrawn; no new asset derivation or geometry is authorized. Required credit: Keystone-Mast Collection, UCR/California Museum of Photography, University of California at Riverside.

The earlier statement that Andreas volume1 has “no jail at all” refers to the absence of a separately titled detailed jail plate; it should not be read as absence of a jail in the illustrations. The actual First Court-House plate at printed p177 shows the jail ensemble in its background, and the identical artwork at Cook County p353 is explicitly captioned FIRST COURT HOUSE AND JAIL. Both carry 1884 copyright credits, establishing that artwork's separate public-domain basis. Chicago p175 distinguishes original jail/later separate oaken cell; p176 dates the courthouse to fall 1835. Thus this plate does not establish the exact July 1835 cell shape or completion of the whole enclosure. It is not a substitute licence for the detailed drawing.

## T-3055: verified 1902 hotel plates supersede earlier rights uncertainty

Actual full-page inspection found the exact Trowbridge Sauganash drawing at Internet Archive `reminiscencesofe00ingale/page/n49` (facing printed p. 31), and the exact Green Tree drawing at `page/n55` (facing p. 35). The book’s actual title and copyright pages establish 1902 US publication and illustrator **W. E. S. Trowbridge**. Their prior `check_required` artwork holds and the older undated-publication language above are superseded; new unmodified page copies and hash manifests live in each source’s asset folder. The 1835 captions remain retrospective, and no historical fitting date, original colour, metric dimension, independent corroboration or image match is created by this verification.
