# Glessner House — 1904 reconstruction brief

Start with the source-linked facts in `data/library.json` and `research/glessner-dossier.json`. The dossier distinguishes verified custodian descriptions, indexed catalog records, downloaded images and unread archive leads.

The useful sequence is original design (1885), construction/occupation (1886–December 1887), first published views (1888), documented electrification and redecoration (1892), the 1904 target, 1911 Sanborn, the 1923 family memoir/photographs, and later HABS/restoration records. Do not combine these into an undated idealized house.

Priority objects: six HABS measured drawings; 27-page report/caption list; 17 photos; Harvard Richardson drawing collection; Frances Glessner journals and George Glessner photographs; full 1923 *Story of a House*; original specifications and restoration investigation reports. Initial HABS retrieval failures were resolved for the report and all six measured sheets; the full photograph series remains an acquisition gap. The 1963 first-floor plan gives a 161 ft 3 in by 74 ft envelope; the circa-1955 service stair is explicitly later and must be excluded from 1904. Sheet 6 elevations use a local DOOR datum, not Chicago City Datum.

The 1888 period exterior/plan plate, circa-1888 porte-cochere photograph and 1885 Richardson sketch were acquired. The sketch is design intent, not an as-built measured plan. Modern restored doors and finishes must be tied back to historic photographs. The 1892 electrical conversion and room finishes matter to 1904; the later 1923 fixtures cannot all be backdated. Granite facing thickness conflicts between museum pages and remains unresolved.

Keep a separate service-space model: kitchen, service circulation, basement work, courtyard access, carriage/stable areas and staff rooms are not incidental. The basement silverwork bench dates to 1904 but not yet to a month. Furniture positions require a dated photograph or journal entry. No human depiction is proposed by this library.
