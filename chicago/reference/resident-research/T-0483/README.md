## Method

The frozen `pass_09_75_cohort.json` manifest was treated as authoritative. No person was added or removed. The cohort contains 37 scene-date postal-list names and 38 earlier/uncertain postal-list names.

Every name received an exact-name/variant open-web and repository-corpus sweep oriented toward 1833-1836 Chicago, Cook County, northern Illinois, the Chicago-Great Lakes migration corridor, and period-appropriate records. Strong follow-ups used institutional collections, contemporary/near-contemporary maps and records, and published county/local histories. Search-name normalization is a **search aid**, not an asserted correction.

A waiting post-office letter is evidence that someone expected a person of that name to be reachable through Chicago; it is not proof that the person lived in Chicago or was physically present on 1 July 1835. Exact-name or surname similarity alone was never used to assert kinship, ethnicity, occupation, residence, household membership, or migration.

## Limitations

Open-web indexing is incomplete and many probate, church, land, naturalization, and genealogical collections are paywalled, unindexed, or image-only. This work does **not** claim to have examined inaccessible records. Negative outcomes mean only that the completed searches did not produce a sufficiently discriminating corroboration.

No canonical household/person fields are promoted by this cohort. The candidates and corroborated facts are staged for the later corpus-wide adjudication sequence so ambiguity is preserved rather than forced.
