# Chicago Democrat, November-December 1833: Validation Notes

## Scope and method

The source PDF contains five consecutive four-page issues of the *Chicago Democrat*, Volume I, Numbers 1-5. The transcriptions were produced with page-specific column segmentation, independent Apple Vision and Tesseract recognition passes, enlarged visual inspection, and a second-reviewer correction pass. Reading order is source page by source page and left-to-right by printed column.

The release contains 111,921 word tokens, 20 source-page sections, and 121 column sections. The first issue has 25 column sections because its third source page contains seven narrow advertisement columns; the remaining source pages have six.

## Editorial policy

- Historical spelling, capitalization, and punctuation are retained where legible.
- Obvious OCR substitutions were corrected only when supported by the image or strong recurring evidence.
- Square brackets identify editorial uncertainty or physical loss. Common forms are `[uncertain: ...]`, `[illegible]`, and `[missing at edge]`.
- Repeated publication terms and agent rosters were compared across issues, but genuine printed variants were retained.
- External name or place evidence was used only for corroboration; the printed page remains the authority.

## Issue-specific residual risks

### November 26, 1833 - Volume I, Number 1

The opening article on source page 1, column 1 has genuine left-edge loss. Surviving fragments are retained and extensively marked rather than reconstructed. Decorative advertisements and small outer-edge type on source pages 3-4 also remain high-risk. The school-land figures were visually confirmed as $38,700, $1,163, and $1,969, totaling $41,832; the locations are River Du Page and Hickory Creek.

### December 3, 1833 - Volume I, Number 2

The outer tear on source page 5 and small advertisements on source pages 7-8 account for most remaining uncertainty. Marriage notices, academy matter, advertisers, estray notices, and recurring publication terms received targeted second review.

### December 10, 1833 - Volume I, Number 3

Publication terms, the agent roster, Goss & Cobb and W. Kimball advertisements, Clinton County political matter, names, and locations received targeted manual reconciliation. Edge-damaged tables and small advertisements remain the principal risk. The Bloomington agent's surname remains bracketed because the impression does not securely resolve it.

### December 17, 1833 - Volume I, Number 4

Recurring standing matter, advertisements, legal notices, names, and place names received a separate cross-check. Remaining uncertainty is concentrated in damaged outer-edge type and continuous prose where column-rule interference removed letters.

### December 24, 1833 - Volume I, Number 5

Source page 19 is skewed and blurred, and the final outer-edge advertisement is physically clipped. Unsupported recognition debris from that clipped advertisement was removed and replaced with an explicit uncertainty note. The agent roster, local advertisements, academy notices, legal material, and place-name jurisdiction headings received targeted second review. The Bloomington agent remains `[uncertain: Wm. H. Efner]`.

## Quality assurance

All five TXT files passed UTF-8, metadata, source-page, and column-marker checks. All five DOCX files were rendered to 240 page images and reviewed in full contact sheets, with additional full-page inspection. No clipping at document boundaries or unintended blank pages was detected. Red italic text in the DOCX editions marks uncertainty for rapid review.

These are high-accuracy, OCR-assisted research transcriptions, not fully diplomatic editions. Before publishing a quotation from a bracketed passage, return to the corresponding source page and column.
