# Prairie Avenue, 1904: the Sanborn 1911 sheets and the Robinson 1886 plate

Received by the owner on **2026-09-23** from **William Tyre, Glessner House**, who is working with
the project on the Prairie Avenue scene. Committed 2026-09-26 so the Prairie Avenue tickets
(band 7, SOUTH THROUGH TIME: T-1250–T-1252, T-0474–T-0477) can read them. These are source
**images only**. Each ticket that spends one writes its own `chicago/4d/data/sources/*.json`
record in the house format: citation, locator, tier, `what_it_supplies` and
`what_it_does_not_supply`. Nothing here is a source record yet.

## The target year, and why 1911 sheets serve it

**The Prairie Avenue model targets 1904** (the owner, 2026-09-26, with Glessner House). The
Sanborn sheets are the **1911** edition. Glessner House's reading, passed on with the sheets, is
that on these three sheets only **two** things changed between 1904 and 1911:

1. **1609–1611 Prairie Avenue.** The two rowhouses that stood here in 1904 had been razed by
   1911 and replaced by a commercial building. On sheet 20 this is the brick building at the
   north-east corner of the Prairie/16th block, labelled "Auto Repairing 1st, Wind Shield Mfg.
   Abv.", with automatic sprinklers.
2. **1620 Prairie Avenue.** The house standing here in 1904 had been razed by 1911, and the lot
   was left empty.

For both, **Robinson 1886 plate 10** supplies the outlines of what stood there. It is less
detailed than a Sanborn but enough to "drop in basic blocks to represent the lost buildings"
(Glessner House's wording). Everything else on sheets 20, 28 and 35 is read as standing in 1904.
That is the correspondent's reading, recorded as such. It is not a claim this file has checked.

**Provenance printed on the sheets.** Each Sanborn sheet is stamped "Fire Insurance Map
Collection, Geography and Map Division, Library of Congress" and headed "Chicago Vol. 3". The
parenthesised numbers under each sheet number, e.g. "(239-240)", point to the corresponding
sheets of the **1891** edition (the Key says so). Both works are public domain. Before a record
cites a sheet, find it in the Library of Congress catalogue and record the item URL. It is not
known yet, and this file does not guess it.

## What these can and cannot carry (for the tickets to state properly)

- **Can:** footprints and their lot positions; material by colour; stories and basement ("2B",
  "3SB"); dwelling, flat and store use; outbuildings (barns, garages, stables, green houses);
  street widths ("66'"); lot frontages and addresses; the IC right-of-way and the lake edge as
  drawn in 1911; water mains and hydrants.
- **Cannot, on their own:** architecture above the footprint (elevations, rooflines beyond the
  roof-covering symbol, ornament), trees, fences, or street furniture. Nor any building's
  *construction* date. A 1911 sheet shows only that a structure stood in 1911, and the 1904
  presence of everything except the two changes above rests on Glessner House's reading.
- **Garages** on the 1911 sheets are the clearest risk of a post-1904 anachronism. A motor
  garage is plausible in 1904 on this street but not certain for each one. The tickets should
  rule, building by building, whether a "Garage" was a carriage house or stable in 1904, rather
  than build 1911 garages into a 1904 scene by default.
